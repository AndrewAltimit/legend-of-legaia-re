Player FAQ

Short answers to what players ask most about the port: which disc image it needs, where saves go, how to play the original game with no enhancements, controllers, the scene selector, changing your party, expansion packs and other languages, how the port differs from the PlayStation game, what to try when something goes wrong, and what to put in a bug report. Each answer links to the page with the full detail. The same engine runs in two places, the desktop download and the browser play page, and the answers name both where they differ.

The disc image

What do I need to play?

Your own copy of Legend of Legaia for the PlayStation, USA release (SCUS-94254), dumped to a raw .bin file (Mode 2/2352, every sector whole). The desktop apps also take the .cue sheet beside it; the browser page takes the .bin itself. Nothing from the game ships with the port: no data, no music, no executable. Every program reads the disc image you supply, on your machine, and uploads nothing.

I have the CD but no image file. How do I make one?

With a PC and a CD or DVD drive: ImgBurn's Create image file from disc on Windows, or cdrdao read-cd --read-raw on Linux and macOS. The steps are in Before you start: a disc image.

My image is refused. Why?

You haveWhat to do
A 2048-byte .isoDump again as a raw .bin: an .iso has no room for the movies and voices.
.chd, .pbp, ECM or a zipUnpack it to a .bin first (chdman extractcd for .chd).
A PAL or Japanese discThe game runs on the USA release only. Legaia Explorer still opens other releases to browse.
A randomized or translated copy of the USA discThat works: it still boots the USA executable.

The launcher, Legaia Explorer and the play page check the file when you pick it and say which of these it is.

Saves

Where do my saves live?

Browser play pageA memory card kept in that browser's storage for the site

On the desktop, the launcher's Saves folder button opens the folder; options and key bindings sit beside the saves (where saves and settings live). A play-window started from a terminal keeps them in the current directory instead, unless you pass --save-dir.

In the browser, the card stays as long as the browser keeps the site's data: clearing site data, a private window, or another browser or computer starts with an empty card. Use the Saves card's Export to keep a copy, or to carry a game to another machine.

Can I use my saves from an emulator?

Yes. The desktop and the browser both read emulator memory cards (.mcr, .mcd, .srm, DexDrive .gme, single-save .mcs). On the desktop, drop the card on the launcher or right-click Saves folder: it becomes card 2 on the game's Load and Save screens, and saving writes back into it (saves from another emulator). On the play page, import it on the Saves card or put it in a card slot. The other way works too: memcard1.mcr is an ordinary PlayStation card image, and a card exported from the play page loads in an emulator.

Where can I save?

Where the original lets you: save points and the world map, through the game's own Save screen. The Save anywhere tweak (off by default) lets the pause menu's Save row work in every scene.

The original game, and the enhancements

How do I play exactly like the original?

The port starts with its enhancements on. One switch turns them all off for a session and leaves your own settings as they were:

WhereSwitch
Desktop launcherFirst row, Look and feel: Original (adds the PlayStation pixel look), or the Original game only row alone
In the desktop gameF12 opens the settings panel on Look and feel; one Right press plays the original, the next gives your mix back
Terminallegaia-engine play-window --disc <bin> --boot-ui --retail
Browser play pageVisual enhancements panel: Look and feel, or the Retail-faithful box

Original mode also brings back the original's short loading pauses: about a second on a held or black screen while the pause menu or a shop loads, and again on the way back to the map, and three to four seconds of black behind each door while the next scene loads (longer when the music changes), five to seven on the way back from a battle. The default mode skips them. It also plays the original's timing on the field and the world map: a button press acts two screen refreshes after it lands (a very short tap can go unread), your character moves a whole step once a game frame, and the L1 map draws twelve frames a second. The default reads a press at once and moves both every refresh, at the original's speeds.

Can it look like a PlayStation picture?

Yes: PlayStation pixel look on the launcher and in the F12 panel (--psx-render), or PSX rasterisation on the play page. It draws the console's own 320x240 picture, magnified, with its wobbling vertices and colour dithering.

Controls

Does a controller work?

Yes, on the desktop game, the launcher, Legaia Explorer, the play page and the minigames page. It plays by button position: bottom / right / left / top face buttons are Cross / Circle / Square / Triangle, the bumpers L1 / R1, the triggers L2 / R2, back / start Select / Start, and both the D-pad and the left stick walk. Plug it in at any time; the notice line says Controller connected. A browser sees a controller only after its first button press. One the browser does not map in the standard way still plays, with its buttons guessed; Key Config with that pad fixes the rest. On a phone or tablet the play page draws a touch pad over the picture.

Every key and button can be moved with Key Config (Select on the pause menu's Options screen, on both hosts) or the launcher's Controls page. The mouse turns the camera: drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, double-click for the game's own framing.

How do I open the menu?

Triangle, the original game's menu button, or Start. F12 (or Select + Start on a controller) is a different thing: the port's own settings panel, with the game held still under it. On the desktop, Esc pressed twice quits; save in the game first.

I rebound a key and now cannot reach the menu.

Put every key and button back: on the desktop, the last row of the launcher's Controls page, or the F12 panel's Keys page (Delete pressed twice); on the play page, Reset keys and buttons in the Controls card.

The scene selector

Can I jump straight to a town or dungeon?

Yes. On the play page, pick it on the Scenes card (the box above the list filters it). In the desktop game, F12 then Tab (or L1 / R1) to the Scenes page takes you there with your party and bag. From a terminal, legaia-engine list-scenes --disc <bin> lists the names and play-window --scene <name> starts in one. You arrive where the game's own doors put you. With no save loaded, a picked scene starts with Vahn, Noa and Gala at level 1, a convenience of the port; a real New Game starts with Vahn alone.

Why did a cutscene start when I picked a scene?

Because the story opens that scene on one: a town's welcome, a dungeon's first line, the Uru Mais dreams, frozen and thawed Buma, the Bio Castle's last room and Noa's dream among them. The selector seats the party where the beat plays and raises the story flags the door in would have raised, so it plays as it does when you walk in. With a save already past that point, nothing fires, as in the original. Some picks are cutscene-only scenes that the game only ever passes through (the second form of Zora's castle is one); they play that passage and move on. More in Pick a scene.

The selector says no. Why?

It refuses on the title screen, in the pause menu, in a fight, a movie or a minigame, and says which. Go back to the map and pick again.

Your party

Can I change who is in my party mid-game?

Yes, as a port tool the original does not have. In the desktop game, F12, then Tab (or L1 / R1) to the Tools page: the Party row picks who fights and in which order (Vahn alone, Vahn and Noa, all three in any of a few orders, Noa or Gala alone), Left / Right to choose and Cross to seat it. On the play page, the Battle tools box takes the names in battle order (noa,vahn,gala) and Apply party seats them; from a terminal, play-window --party noa,gala starts with that party. Up to three fight. Terra is not on the desktop list: in the original she is never a party member on the field, only the computer-controlled companion who joins Noa's fights while the story calls for her, and the game seats her that way by itself. Naming her on the play page or with --party (noa,terra) seats her the same way: last, computer-controlled, in battle only, beside one or two others. That raises her companion flag, which stays up until the story lowers it. A character who has not joined yet in your game joins with the stats the game gives them when they first join; one who has keeps their level, equipment and arts. Only the party changes: story flags stay as they are, so the story does not know who joined early.

The scene selector is the other way the party changes: a picked scene keeps your party, and with no save loaded it starts Vahn, Noa and Gala at level 1 (above). Levels, money and items are on the cheats pages.

Expansion packs and other languages

What is an expansion pack, and where do I get one?

New content made for the port - maps, quests, fights, trading cards, game modes - that plays beside your own disc and carries none of its data. Every release includes the sample pack. On the desktop, the launcher's Expansion pack... button picks one; on the play page, the Expansion pack card loads an .lxpack once the disc is up. With a pack on, Select in the pause menu opens its Journal. The steps are in Playing an expansion pack; making one is Making expansion packs.

Can I play in another language?

Yes, by translating a copy of your disc: the ROM patcher page's Language box applies one of the community translations (Spanish, French, German, Italian, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese) or your own pack, in the browser, and gives you a patched image to play. Your own disc is not changed. Writing or improving a translation: Translating the game.

How the port differs from the original

Is it the same game?

The rules are the original's: damage, drops, encounter rates, AP costs, story flags and saves are ported from the game's own code and checked against recordings of it. The whole game plays, New Game to the credits. The picture is where the distance is, most of all in the middle of a battle: it is close, not frame-identical. The engine page has the measured figures.

What is different on purpose?

  • The enhancements above start on. Original mode turns them off.
  • No loading pauses around the pause menu, shops, doors and battles, except in Original mode.
  • Smoother input and motion: a press acts the moment it lands, and walking and the L1 map move every screen refresh rather than once a game frame, at the same speeds. Original mode plays the original's timing.
  • Start opens the pause menu as well as Triangle, and leaves any minigame, so no minigame can trap you.
  • A few original softlocks are closed. The battle freeze guard, a guard on ledge hops that could box you in, and the slot machine, where pressing Cross and Circle together on the rules screen spins the reels forever in the original and does not here.
  • Additions with no original counterpart: the scene selector, the F12 panel, cheats, the saved-chain Arts list, custom game modes, expansion packs and online play. Each is off or out of the way until you use it.

Why each one is the way it is: fidelity and enhancements and gameplay tweaks.

Something behaves differently from how I remember it.

Some of what feels off is the original's own behaviour. Two common ones: one Run in battle is one escape roll for the whole party, and when it fails the party's turn is spent and the monsters still act; and a healing spell or item opens its target cursor on the character casting it. Both are as on the PlayStation. If you are not sure, try it with Original mode on; if it still differs from the original, it is worth a report.

Desktop or browser?

Both run the same engine and the same saves format. The desktop game has every option on the command line, hosts online rooms, and reloads an expansion pack from a folder as you edit it; the browser needs nothing installed, runs on a phone, and does VR. What a tab cannot do: what a browser cannot do. Downloads for Windows, macOS and Linux (PC and ARM) are on the Releases page; the first start on each system is in Desktop apps.

When something goes wrong

Which version am I running?

The desktop launcher names it at its top right (engine port and the release number); every program's --version prints it (legaia-engine --version among them), and so does Legaia Explorer's About box. The play page always runs the site's current build. Both hosts put the version in a copied bug report (below).

The desktop programs will not start.

On Windows, unpack the .zip with Extract All... first; a program run from inside the zip window cannot find the engine. A Windows protected your PC or Apple could not verify warning is the first start of a program that is not signed by Microsoft or notarised by Apple; Windows and macOS say how to let it run. On Linux the game needs ALSA for sound and udev for controllers (Linux).

The game stopped with an error.

Started from the launcher, the launcher comes back and says how the game ended and why; the whole log is legaia-engine-game.log in the saves folder. Copy the report from there for an issue.

The play page has no sound.

A browser opens no audio until you click or press a key on the page; the sound starts with that press. Check the page's own volume slider and F2 (mute) as well.

The play page is slow, or the lighting went off by itself.

The page runs everything on one thread, so on a slow machine it steps its quality down to hold the frame rate: it turns enhancements off and draws a smaller picture for the session, and says so beside the picture, with a Put it back button. It never changes a saved setting. The desktop game has more headroom on the same machine.

Reporting a bug

Open an issue on the project's GitHub issues page. Both hosts gather most of what a report needs in one press, with no game data in it:

WhereCopy the report
Desktop game, still runningF12, Tools page, Copy report (written to legaia-report.txt in the saves folder where there is no clipboard)
Desktop game that stopped with an errorThe launcher comes back: Ctrl+C, or a click on its help box; the full log is legaia-engine-game.log in the saves folder
Legaia ExplorerCopy report on its error banner
Browser play pageCopy report beside the picture

The report carries the version, your system (and in the browser, the browser and graphics chip), the scene and the settings. Add to it:

  1. What happenedWhat you did, what you expected, and what you saw instead.
  2. Original modeWhether it still happens with Original mode on. That tells an enhancement's side effect apart from the game itself.
  3. Your discThe plain USA disc, or a randomized or patched copy (and its seed or patch).
  4. A picturePrint Screen saves a screenshot on both hosts; a short video is better for anything that moves. For a "looks different" report, footage of the original at the same moment helps most.
  5. A save near itDesktop: Export session on the F12 Tools page writes an .lgsf into the saves folder. Browser: Export save on the Saves card.

Never attach the disc image or files extracted from it; the project cannot take Sony's data.
