Translating the game

A guide for translators, no programming needed: export the game's text from your own disc into a plain text file, write your translation next to each line, and apply the file back onto a copy of your disc - in your browser or on the command line. This page covers what you need, both paths, how much room each line has, and why a line can stay English. The technical details live in the translation reference.

What you need

  • Your own copy of Legend of Legaia (USA) (SCUS-94254) as a .bin image (Mode 2/2352), or its .cue sheet. Packs are keyed to the USA disc, so they do not apply to the European or Japanese discs.
  • A text editor that saves UTF-8 - Notepad++, VS Code, Kate, or TextEdit in plain-text mode. The pack is a YAML file: plain text with indentation.
  • A way to apply the pack: the ROM patcher page, which runs in your browser and uploads nothing, or the legaia-patcher program from a release archive (getting started).
  • An emulator to play the result.

How a translation works

The game's text is spread across the disc: item and spell names in the main program, dialog inside each scene's data, menu labels inside the menu code. The tools collect all of it into one file, a language pack. Each piece of text becomes one entry:

  - key: 'scus:str:0x80012260'    # where the text lives on the disc
    context: 'item 0x79'          # a hint for you
    source: 'Healing Berry'       # the English text
    translation: ''               # you write here
    budget: 13                    # how many bytes fit here

You only ever edit translation:. Leave key, source and budget alone. An entry you leave empty stays English, so a half-finished pack is always playable.

The disc was never meant to be edited, so every piece of text has a fixed amount of room - its budget. How strict that is depends on the kind of text; how much room a line has explains it.

Path A · in your browser

The ROM patcher page does everything in the tab. Your disc is read on your machine and never uploaded.

  1. Choose your disc under Disc image.
  2. Open the Language section at the top of the form.
  3. Export a starter pack. With Language set to None, press Export a starter pack from my disc. You get legaia_en.working.yaml: the whole game, every translation: empty. To continue an existing translation instead, choose that language (or Import my own pack) first - the button becomes Export a working copy of this pack, with that pack's lines filled in and the English beside each.
  4. Edit the pack in your text editor. Change the language: line near the top to your language code (fr, pt-BR, ...), add yourself under contributors:, and fill in translation: fields. The markup table lists what to keep.
  5. Load your pack: set Language to Import my own pack (.yaml) and pick your file.
  6. Check pack against my disc. This runs the whole import in memory and reports, per section, how many lines would land and why the others would not. Nothing is written.
  7. Download skipped lines (.csv) appears when some lines did not land: one row per line with its key (search for it in your pack), a short reason and the full message. The troubleshooting table explains each one.
  8. Patch. Optionally tick Give translated dialog more room (below), then press Patch & download. The language patch combines with any randomizer options you also chose. The patched disc image is for your own play only.
  9. Make a shareable pack (.yaml, no English) when you are ready to share. The downloaded file holds only your translations - that is the file to pass around.

Path B · on the command line

The same work in six steps with legaia-patcher. A .cue works wherever a .bin does.

# 1. Export the disc's text into a working pack (once).
legaia-patcher translate export --input "Legend of Legaia (USA).bin" -o legaia_en.yaml

# 2. Make an empty pack for your language.
#    Add --resume site/lang/fr.yaml to start from a published pack.
legaia-patcher translate init --lang fr --from legaia_en.yaml \
    --contributor "Your Name" -o legaia_fr.yaml

# 3. Fill in translation: fields. Working in a team?
#    `init --chunk 500` splits the pack into pieces; merge puts them back.
legaia-patcher translate merge --base legaia_fr.yaml \
    --pack legaia_fr.001.yaml --pack legaia_fr.002.yaml -o legaia_fr.yaml

# 4. Check it against your disc (nothing is written).
legaia-patcher translate stats --pack legaia_fr.yaml --input "Legend of Legaia (USA).bin"

# 5. Make the shareable pack.
legaia-patcher translate strip --pack legaia_fr.yaml -o fr.yaml

# 6. Apply it to a copy of your disc, and write a PPF patch as well.
legaia-patcher translate import --input "Legend of Legaia (USA).bin" \
    --pack legaia_fr.yaml --output legaia_fr.bin --patch legaia_fr.ppf

stats and import print skipped lines grouped by reason; --verbose lists every line with its key. The tool never writes to your original image: --output is a new patched image for your own play, and --patch is a small PPF file that turns a clean disc into the translated one - a PPF is safe to share. How much room does a line have?

The budget: field is the number of bytes a translation may take in place. A plain letter is one byte; a markup token such as {c1:00} is two. What happens when you go over depends on the kind of text.

Names

Item, item-type, spell, art and accessory names (and their descriptions) sit one after another, each in a slot rounded up to a multiple of 4 bytes. So the spare room depends on how long the English happens to be: Potion has one spare byte, Antidote three, Medicine none. That is why two names of the same length can have different budgets.

A name longer than its slot moves automatically: the tools pack the names tighter, collect the bytes shorter translations give up, and place the longer names there. Nothing to switch on. It fails only when the whole table has run out of room (no free run) - then shorten that name or others in the same table.

Monster names grow their record instead, up to 15 bytes, the longest name the game already uses. Party names (9 bytes) and world-map place names (31 bytes) are fixed fields. A name that fits in bytes can still be too wide on screen: an item list has a fixed quantity column.

Menu and battle labels

Menu commands, battle messages and system prompts sit in the game code and cannot move. Their budget is a hard limit, and it is tight: a translation can be shorter than the English but rarely much longer. Abbreviate.

Dialog

For dialog the budget on each line is only a hint. The real limit is the scene: each scene's dialog is stored compressed, and on the USA disc each compressed scene fills its space exactly.

So one line longer than the English usually still fits - the tools move the rest of the scene along to make room. What cannot grow is the scene as a whole, and translated text compresses a little worse than English, so a scene can overflow even when no line got longer. When it does, the tools put some of its lines back in English, starting with the ones that cost the most room, and report each. Shorten them (or other lines of the same scene) and check again.

Giving dialog more room

Give translated dialog more room on the site, --allow-relayout on the command line, removes the scene limit: an overflowing scene gets extra 2048-byte sectors and the rest of the disc shifts along, so every line fits at full length. The official European discs were mastered the same way. It affects only dialog, and it costs three things:

  • The disc image grows, usually by a few KB.
  • No PPF. A PPF only describes same-size changes, so you get the patched image only.
  • Save states break. A save state made on another layout no longer matches; start from a fresh boot or a memory-card save.

Text markup quick reference

Most text is written as-is. A few special forms keep the game's formatting working:

WriteMeansRule
|a new line inside the text boxmove it to where your sentence should break
{c1:00}a character's namekeep it; it may move within the sentence
{c2:..} {c3:..} {c5:..}an item / magic / art namekeep it
{cf:..}a colour changekeep it, around the same words
{ce:..} and other {xx:yy}spacing and iconskeep it
{xx}one special byte ({01} = item icon)keep it
{7b} / {7d}a literal { / }use these instead of typing braces

The substituted names come from the tables you are also translating, so write sentences that stay grammatical whichever name appears.

No accents. The game's font has only plain English letters, digits and common punctuation (printable ASCII): no é, ñ, ß or ç, and no Cyrillic, Greek or CJK. Write Epee, not Épée. Adding letters to the font is a separate, much larger project (font patch).

Automatic folding. A few characters word processors and AI tools like to insert are replaced for you: curly quotes become ' and ", en and em dashes and the minus sign become -, the ellipsis becomes ..., and a non-breaking space becomes a plain space. Anything else outside the font is reported, character by character.

Testing your translation

  • Start from a fresh boot or a memory-card save. An emulator save state holds the memory of the disc it was made on, text included. Loaded onto your patched disc it shows the old text, or breaks if the disc was relaid out.
  • Test on a copy. Keep your original image untouched; the tools never write to it.
  • Read a scene's lines together. A text box shows up to three lines, and consecutive dialog entries in the pack are consecutive rows of the same box.
  • Watch lists and labels. Byte room is not screen width; a long item name can run into the quantity column.

Glossary

Language pack
the YAML file holding the text and your translations
Working pack
a pack with the English source: text; for your own use only
Shareable pack
only your translations and keys; safe to share (also called a distributable pack)
Key
where a piece of text lives on the disc, such as man:4:0x210
Budget
how many bytes a translation may take in place
Byte
one letter, digit or punctuation mark; a {xx:yy} token is two
Scene / footprint
one area of the game, and the fixed space its compressed dialog occupies on the disc
Relayout
growing a scene by whole sectors, shifting the rest of the disc
PPF
a small patch file that turns a clean disc into the patched one
Token
a {...} code standing for a name, colour, icon or special byte

FAQ

Is there a flag to let names grow?
No - there is no --relocate-names. A name longer than its slot moves automatically on every import.
Can I test with a save state?
No. Boot the patched disc fresh, or load a memory-card save.
What may I share publicly?
The shareable pack and a PPF patch. Never a working pack, a lifted pack, or a patched disc image - they contain the game's own text and data.
Russian, Greek, Japanese, Chinese, Korean?
Not with the retail font, which has only plain English letters; it would need a font patch these tools do not do.
Can I start from someone else's pack?
Yes: choose it and press Export a working copy of this pack, or pass --resume their-pack.yaml to translate init.
With the randomizer?
Yes - pick a language and any randomizer options and patch once. Dialog is applied before the randomizer and names after it, so neither disturbs the other.
My line is fine but still reported?
Dialog is limited per scene, not per line; another line of the same scene may have used the room.
The European disc?
Packs are keyed to the USA disc. If you own a European or fan-translated disc, Translation from another disc I own turns its text into a pack for the USA disc (translations from another disc).
The title-screen logo?
Text drawn as a picture needs replacement artwork (text baked into textures).
