Translation / language packs

Legend of Legaia only ever shipped in English, French, German, Italian and Japanese. A language pack lets a community translate the game's text - item and spell names, menus, dialogue - into any Latin-script language and play it on the retail disc. The pack is a plain YAML file you edit in any text editor; the patch that applies it never changes the size of anything on the disc.

At a glance

  • What you get - a translated game on your own disc, applied in the browser patcher (pick a shipped pack or import your own) or with the legaia-patcher translate CLI. It composes with every randomizer option.
  • What is covered - the name tables (items, spells, arts, accessory passives, party names), the whole NPC / cutscene / narration dialogue corpus, and the pause-menu, shop and battle system labels. Not covered: text baked into textures, and accented or non-Latin scripts, which the retail font has no glyphs for.
  • The one rule - every edit is same-size in place. A translation has a byte budget; a line over budget is reported and left English rather than corrupting the disc.
  • Sharing - a pack is safe to publish only once its source: text is stripped; what remains is your new text keyed by disc coordinates, and carries none of the original script.
  • Already own a PAL disc? The official French / German / Italian text can be lifted onto the USA disc - in the browser, from your own two discs.

Workflow

Six steps take a disc to a distributable pack. Only the first needs the disc; the middle ones are pure text editing.

1exportdump the disc's own text into a working pack (local only - it holds the game script)translate export 2initmake a skeleton for your language; --resume seeds it from an already-published packtranslate init 3fillwrite the translation: fields - editor, script or AI draft; --chunk / merge split the workany editor 4statscheck coverage, encodability and budgets; add --input to dry-run against a real disctranslate stats 5stripdrop the source text - the result is the shareable, committable packtranslate strip 6importapply to a scratch copy and/or emit a PPF patchtranslate import 
# 1. Dump the source text into a working pack (once).
legaia-patcher translate export --input "Legend of Legaia (USA).bin" -o legaia_en.yaml

# 2. Make a skeleton for your language (fr de es it pl pt-BR ja ru zh ko ...).
legaia-patcher translate init --lang fr --from legaia_en.yaml \
    --contributor "you" [--resume site/lang/fr.yaml] -o legaia_fr.yaml

# 3. Fill `translation:` fields. --chunk N splits the skeleton into N-entry
#    files for a parallel bulk fill; recombine them with `translate merge`.

# 4. Check coverage + encodability/budget (--input dry-runs against a disc).
legaia-patcher translate stats --pack legaia_fr.yaml [--input DISC.bin]

# 5. Publish: strip the source to make the distributable pack.
legaia-patcher translate strip --pack legaia_fr.yaml -o site/lang/fr.yaml

# 6. Apply to a scratch copy (and/or emit a shareable PPF).
legaia-patcher translate import --input "Legend of Legaia (USA).bin" \
    --pack legaia_fr.yaml --output legaia_fr.bin --patch legaia_fr.ppf

A partially filled pack is always playable: entries with an empty translation: are left byte-identical. Import is idempotent (re-running the same pack over a patched image applies nothing) and incremental (fill more entries, re-import onto a fresh copy). When a scene's dialogue no longer recompresses into its footprint, import rolls back that scene's longest lines one at a time rather than dropping the whole scene; stats and import summarise skipped entries per reason and --verbose prints the per-key list.

What may / may not be committed

  • Distributable packs may be committed - they are new authored text plus a coordinate table; the shipped site/lang/*.yaml packs are tracked, and a disc-free test fails the build if any tracked pack still carries a source: field.
  • Working packs must not - they carry the game's script. /translations/ and legaia_*.yaml stay gitignored.
  • No disc / exe / asset bytes, ever.

On the site

The in-browser ROM patcher offers the shipped packs from a language dropdown (default None), plus import my own pack, export a starter pack from my disc, and official translation from my own PAL disc. It validates a chosen pack against your disc before patching, applies it in two phases around the randomizer passes, and shows a per-section coverage report afterwards - applied / skipped counts plus a skip-reason breakdown (over budget, scene does not recompress, not on this disc, not encodable). Nothing is uploaded; the packs are static assets fetched from site/lang/.

Two pack shapes

A pack comes in two shapes that share one schema. The difference is whether it still carries the game's own text.

Shapesource:HoldsLives
workingyesthe disc's own text (for the translator to read)your machine only - never redistributed
distributablenoonly the new translated text, keyed by disc coordinatesshareable / committable

translate strip turns a filled working pack into a distributable one: it drops every source: and context: field and every unfilled entry, leaving a pure key → translation lookup plus the byte-budget hint. The key (scus:str:0x<va>, scus:party:<n>, man:<prot>:0x<off>, raw:<prot>:0x<off>, ui:<prot>:0x<va>) is a disc coordinate, not text, so a distributable pack carries none of the original script. That is the only shape it is safe to commit or publish.

A distributable pack has no source to self-check against, so its budget is only a hint: import (and translate stats --input) re-measures every target on the disc being patched and rejects any entry that does not fit, or whose on-disc length disagrees with the hint. Same-size in place is enforced from the disc, never from the pack.

Ordering: dialogue before the randomizer, names after

A pack and a randomizer run can share one patch. Because some randomizer passes move bytes around and one of them reads item names, the pack is applied in two phases:

  • Dialogue sections (man: / raw: keys) go first. A dialogue edit is same-size inside the decompressed MAN, keyed by a byte offset into it, whereas the door, location-rename and starting-bag passes relocate records - moving every byte after the splice. Applied first, the translated text simply rides along with any later relocation.
  • Name sections (scus: and ui: keys) go last. The equipment-bonus-drop pass classifies gear by matching the disc's item names against curated English names; with the item table already translated its pool comes back empty and the pass aborts. Nothing in the randomizer relocates a SCUS or overlay string, so translating the name tables after every pass is always safe.

The randomizer otherwise reads structure - records, tables, item ids - never text, so translated strings never perturb it. A standalone translate import applies everything in one pass.

YAML schema

A working pack, the shape a translator edits:

format: 'legaia-text-pack-v1'
language: 'fr'
game: 'Legend of Legaia (USA) SCUS-94254'
contributors: ['...']
notes: '...'
sections:
  items:               # one list per section, fixed order
  - key: 'scus:str:0x80012260'   # stable provenance key
    context: 'item 0x79'          # human context, not machine-read
    source: 'Healing Berry'       # US text, markup form
    translation: ''               # fill me
    budget: 13                    # max encoded bytes for the translation

A distributable pack is the same document with source: and context: removed and only filled entries kept. Each section maps to one place on the disc and one patch mechanism:

item_typesshared item "type" stringsscus:str:0x<va>same

Strings pointer-shared by several table slots export once; interior pointers clamp the budget. The SCUS name pools are 4-byte aligned, so each string's budget also claims the 0..3 bytes of zero padding after its terminator - about 1.5 extra bytes on average, which is what lets the tightest name tables translate at all. The dialogue sections are line-granular: the pager packs up to three consecutive segments into one box (MES), so consecutive entries in the pack are consecutive rows on screen - translate them as a group and keep each row inside its own budget.

Text markup + encoding

Game text is mostly plain letters with a few control tokens for names, colours and line breaks. The glyph atlas is indexed by byte with 0x20..=0x7E as plain ASCII (dialog font), so a pack's markup is mostly literal text:

  • printable ASCII maps to itself; | is the in-game newline glyph (0x7C);
  • {xx:yy} is a 2-byte token: {c1:00} = character-name substitution, {c2:79} = item-name substitution, {c3:..} magic, {c5:..} art, {cf:0n} colour change, {ce:..} spacing/icon escape - keep these in the translation wherever the source has them;
  • {xx} is a bare byte ({01} item-icon prefix, high glyph tiles); literal braces are written {7b} / {7d}.

encode (string → game bytes) is the exact inverse of the exporter's decode and reports per-character errors: anything outside printable ASCII is not in the retail glyph set. Common typographic lookalikes (smart quotes, en/em dashes, ellipsis, NBSP) are folded automatically. Accented Latin, Cyrillic and CJK are not encodable - the retail font simply has no such glyphs. A full non-Latin translation needs a font patch (new glyph tiles + width table), which this pipeline does not attempt; French/Italian/etc. must be written unaccented (Epee not Épée).

Budgets (the same-size constraint)

Nothing on the disc moves, so a translation's encoded length is capped by its budget. What that budget is depends on where the text lives:

  • SCUS strings: the original string's byte span (shorter is fine - the string is re-terminated).
  • Dialogue segments: same-size in place is the fast default - shorter translations are space-padded so the 0x1F ... 0x00 framing never moves. When a line overflows its own span, the importer tries the generalised rewriter: it grows every filled segment in that scene MAN to full length and relocates all crossing references, verified as the same program by re-walking both buffers. The budget then becomes the MAN's own footprint, not each string. See MAN relocation.
  • A whole scene's edits must additionally recompress into the MAN's original LZS footprint at the same LBA. Text compresses well, and the repack falls back to an optimal-parse LZS encoder when the fast greedy parse just misses. The retail scene entries are sector-aligned with zero compressed slack, though, so a scene that still overflows falls back to same-size and rolls back its longest lines one at a time, each with a per-key diagnostic - shorten the reported lines and re-run.

translate stats checks all of this offline. On import each target is also verified against the pack's source; a mismatch (wrong disc revision, or a conflicting randomizer patch that moved the text) skips the entry with a per-key warning rather than writing blind.

The dialogue-carrier gate (raw: writes)

The 0x1F <text> 0x00 dialogue framing is short enough to occur by coincidence all over the disc's binary asset banks: sequenced music, VAB sample banks, the battle-character mesh/animation packs, monster archives, and every scene's first ANM slot all contain runs that read as a two- or three-letter "segment". Overwriting such a coincidental hit corrupts the asset with a same-size write that passes every framing/budget check yet freezes the game. Both export and import therefore gate every raw: write on a per-entry dialogue-carrier check: a PROT entry is a real raw text carrier only if it carries enough multi-word prose segments. Across the retail disc the two populations separate with a wide margin, so the gate keeps every real carrier and refuses every binary bank. Import re-runs the check on the disc it is patching and skips a non-carrier entry with a per-key diagnostic. SCUS name-table (scus:), scene-MAN (man:) and overlay (ui:) writes are unaffected - those targets are structurally addressed, not scanned.

Coverage + limitations

Covered: the SCUS name tables (items, item types, spells, Tactical Arts, accessory passives, party names); the 0x1F-segment dialogue corpus (scene bundles + raw event-script carriers) - NPC dialogue, cutscene dialogue and narration, picker labels, chest flavour text; and the overlay-resident UI strings (ui_menu): the pause-menu / options / shop / equip / status command labels and the in-battle system messages, which are NUL-terminated C strings in the menu (PROT 0899) and battle (PROT 0898) overlay data segments, pinned by disc-coordinate VA windows (menu pool 0x801CE81C.., battle pool 0x801F4B98.., both under load base 0x801CE818; see field menu). Those pools are tight - a short label can be shorter than English but rarely much longer - and the in-battle Attack / Arts / Magic / Item command ring is drawn as icon sprites, so there is no string to translate there.

Not covered: textures with baked-in text (below), and dialogue lines the conservative segment scanner declines to export (they simply stay English). Junk entries the scanner does export (dev-debug strings) are harmless: leave them untranslated. The dialogue exports raw source lines including substitution escapes, so translations must keep grammatical agreement working around the substituted names.

Official PAL translations

Sony shipped French, German and Italian discs (SCES_019.44 / .45 / .46). If you own one, its official text can be lifted onto the USA disc's coordinates and applied like any other pack - from your own two discs, in the browser or on the CLI. The official text is yours; it is never bundled with this site.

The PAL discs are structurally 1:1 with the USA disc (same 1233 PROT entries, same block boundaries), and their name tables and dialogue corpus align id-for-id and line-for-line. translate lift-official --from PAL.bin --target USA.bin -o pack.yaml detects the source language from the disc, locates each name table, and re-keys the text positionally into a filled working pack. --fold-accents (the browser default) folds the PAL accent glyphs onto plain ASCII (é → e, ß → ss), because the USA font has no cells for them - unfolded accents encode fine but draw blank until the font is patched.

Not all of it fits, and the reason is sector alignment, not string length. The USA scene entries are packed to the sector with zero compressed slack, so any line that grows overflows; the PAL discs solved this at mastering by giving most scene entries one extra sector. In place, roughly a third of the dialogue corpus lands (name tables 36-60%, dialogue lines about half per-string); the rest is reported as over-budget or non-recompressing rather than dropped quietly. The CLI's --allow-relayout import grows the image to close that residual (an --output image only - a relayout is not a same-size PPF); the browser never relays out. The overlay-resident ui_menu labels are not lifted either - the PAL overlays place their pools elsewhere - so those stay English.

Measurement tools: translate diff-disc (alignment counts between two discs) and translate fit-report (per-string vs per-MAN fit), both text-free. Recommended path to a distributable pack: lift → fit-report → import with relayout → font patch (separate deliverable) → strip to site/lang/<lang>.yaml. Full structure, encoding tables and fit numbers: tooling/pal-localizations.md.

Textures with baked-in text

Some UI text is not a string at all - it is pixels in a texture. Replacing one means authoring new glyph art at the exact same TIM footprint (identical width / height / bpp / CLUT layout) so the same-size in-place write applies; the ROM patcher's texture replacement and the tim crate's PNG encoder make that mechanically possible. The blocker is art authoring (and, for logos, rights), not the pipeline.

A font patch - new glyph tiles + width table in the menu glyph atlas (see boot and dialog font) - is the separate, larger effort that would lift the printable-ASCII-only limitation across all text. The official PAL discs already carry such an atlas, with the accent cells laid out CP437-style.

AI example packs

Machine-translated packs built with this pipeline are examples / starting points only - the tooling exists so communities can produce and iterate on real human translations. Prefer community packs; treat any AI-filled pack as a draft to correct, and credit editors in contributors.
