Gate replay

A pad recording made on the port cannot be played blind on the original game for long. The two sides reach a door, or close a page of dialogue, a few frames apart; every later press then lands somewhere else, and the two runs part for good. The gate replay plays the port's own pad on retail one beat at a time and lines the two back up at every beat's end, so a disagreement stays inside the beat that caused it and can be measured there.

Beats and gates

A gate is a change of state both sides can see in their own memory: a script taking hold of the player, a battle opening, a dialogue page finishing its typing. A beat runs from one gate to the next. The engine's run is cut into beats first; retail then plays each beat's pad and the probe waits for the same gate before the next beat starts.

GateOn the engineOn retail
engaged=0/1A script context holds the playerBit 0x80000 of the player record's flags at +0x10
battle=0/1The world's mode is battleThe mode byte 0x8007B83C reads 0x15. The battle context pointer outlives the fight, so it says nothing

page=NThe open box's window reaches state 0x19The pager state _DAT_801F2734 reaches 0x19; pages are counted, not timed
pos=X/ZA turn in a walk: the d-pad changes to another held direction, settled for 8 ticks, at least 96 units from the last turnThe player comes within 24 units of the spot, passes it, or is engaged first
flow=XX:N, act=XX:NThe Nth entry into a battle command surface, or the action startThe same bytes, ctx[+0x06] and ctx[+0x07]
  • Walks are gated on position, not time. A retail walk and an engine walk start a pass of position apart, and a door or a ledge fires on where the player stands. Each turn is therefore a gate on the spot, and the next leg's pad starts where retail really is.
  • Levels, not edges. Where a gate is a level (engaged, battle, scene), retail matches the level, so two gates the sides reach in the other order read as a zero-length beat and not a stall. Pages, prompts and action starts are ordinals, so a page only one side shows costs one beat and not the rest of the run.
  • A door holds retail's engaged bit from its trigger to its landing, where the engine's word stays clear. A retail engaged interval that contains a warp is that hold and does not satisfy an engaged=1 gate.
  • Field gates are not cut inside a battle, where the field player record says nothing. An engaged run shorter than three ticks is a script pulse and is ignored (--min-dwell).
  • The press that moves a battle surface closes its own beat. The engine's flow byte changes in the row of the press that caused it, so such a gate ends one tick later and keeps the press: it is what the beat repeats if it does not land.

Replaying a beat

Each beat's pad rows are timed from its opening gate. A press is held at least two ticks (--min-press), because a one-vsync press can fall between two of retail's game passes and be lost, and a widened press keeps one released tick after it so a run of pulses stays separate edges. What happens at the closing gate depends on who got there first:

Synthetic input is input the engine's log did not supply. Every one is a row in gate_events.csv with the press and what it found, so a run that needed help says where. Nothing is pressed while the action state machine owns the frame, or on a flow byte no surface reads the pad on.

A beat with a small gap and a matching position says the two sides agree there, whatever came before it. That is what the resynchronisation buys. A second table, page waits, has a row per page gate: when each side's pager finished the page, their difference (which carries the drift of the beats before it) and the part this page added.

What the Rim Elm run shows

The first run replays the walk to Tetsu, the talk and the sparring fight from a Rim Elm free-roam state. With every surface gated, the fight replays to its end.

  • Engaged holds. Retail holds the player's engaged bit across a door's whole trigger-to-landing run, and across the talk to Tetsu from its first page to its last.
  • The fight's opening. Retail's command flow walks 0xFD, 0x00, 0x0A, 0x0B, 0x14, 0x1E before the first round prompt. The action state machine passes through 0x5A after every action, between two actors of one round as well as at a round's end, so the prompt is gated on the command flow's 0x1E.
  • One-vsync presses get lost. With one-tick presses two of the talk's page turns wait on a synthetic Cross and the first round prompt ignores six logged pulses; with two-tick presses the same turns close on the logged presses.
  • The ring. Up in the command ring opens the item window on retail as on the engine. Both sides ignore the ring for about 16 ticks after entering it, and a mash merged into one solid hold gives retail a single edge that lands before the ring shows.
  • Every surface holds the pad for its glide. The engine dispatches on the eighteenth tick after the flow byte moves and retail about two vsyncs later, on its two-vsync pass:

  • Where the actions differ. A round's actions run the same states on both sides with the same dwell except at the close of each: 0x51 holds 62 ticks on the engine against 76 vsyncs on retail, and retail then walks 0x5A where the engine walks 0x00. The item band and the Spirit band are shorter on the engine.
  • The boxes that close themselves. Retail's first-round box and its sign-off box wait for a press and otherwise close on their own hold: with no Cross offered, retail spends 364 vsyncs in 0x14 and 427 in 0xC8 before the fight closes. A press skips the long hold on both sides.
  • The hits are one kernel. The thirteen melee hits of this fight, fed through the port's physical_rolls with retail's stats and retail's own rand() draws, give retail's raw and guarded damage on every hit. The two sides' damage differs only where the rolls and swing counts differ, and those follow the random stream, which parts in the first round.
