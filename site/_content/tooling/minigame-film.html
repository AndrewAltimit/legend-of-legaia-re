The minigame film: one minigame, both sides, game frame by game frame

The retail comparison corpus scores a minigame state on one frame, which cannot see a rhythm game's timing: when the beat clock starts against the music, which game frame judges a press, how the camera track steps. The minigame film holds a pad schedule on one retail minigame state on both sides and compares a named set of the minigame's words game frame by game frame. It reports and asserts nothing; its output reads retail RAM, so it stays under captures/.

At a glance

scripts/ci/minigame-film.py minigame_dance_retail_ready --set dance --frames 3300
scripts/ci/minigame-film.py minigame_dance_retail_ready --set dance --frames 2000 --shift 3 \
    --pad 130-133:SQUARE,144-147:CIRCLE,407-410:TRIANGLE
scripts/ci/minigame-film.py minigame_dance_retail_ready --set dance --no-retail   # engine side only

A word is name=ADDR:KIND, or name=*PTR+OFF:KIND through a pointer. The driver writes film-score.tsv beside the two films: each column's agreement and its first diverging frame. The engine side's seed is the corpus's minigame state warp.

Reading it

  • Pairing. Both sides are cut into game frames and paired on the minigame's own key. For the dance that is the READY counter through state 3, the GO! accumulator through states 4 and 5, the lead-in count through states 6 and 7, then the song's game frames by their ordinal from the first. A vsync of entry latency on either side therefore does not read as a divergence.
  • The offset. The driver prints the vsync offset of every pair; a constant offset is the save state's own vsync phase.
  • Pressed runs. A save state's RAM is the vsync before the film's first row, so the engine's row 1 stands a few vsyncs ahead of retail's. A hands-off run prints that offset; pass it as --shift on a pressed run, so the engine holds each press on the vsync that matches retail's.

What the dance film pins

Hands off, from minigame_dance_retail_ready to the results: the count-in states, the beat phase and song timer, both rivals' scores, gauges, triangle stock, chain cursors and banked combo slots, and the camera key, segment timer, angles and eye, on every game frame of the song. With a press schedule, the human's score, gauge, stock, chain, latch and miss counter too. The words the engine does not keep past their phase (the READY counter after state 3, the song timer past its limit) are the columns that stay apart. The minigame itself: Noa's dance.

What the hub film pins

--set hub from minigame_muscle_dome_retail_welcome, hands off: the contest hub's arm byte 0x801D1A78 and its counter family (0x801D1A70, 0x801D1A7C..0x801D1A8C) agree on every game frame from the Welcome hold through the ROUND card's fade-out, and the hub hands the leg over on the same vsync. The engine's own scene mode rides along as an engine-only emode column: retail spends the vsyncs after the hand-over in game mode 0x14 before the fight's 0x15, while the engine enters the fight on the hand-over tick.

The fishing replay

--set fishing --replay records a catch from minigame_fishing_retail_shore with Cross driven closed-loop on the fishing globals (LEGAIA_FILM_POLICY=fishing_catch: tap on the shore, lock the meter at 0xF00, pump the cadence, then hold under the tension band). The engine side then ticks the pond once per retail game frame on that frame's step and pad words instead of running its own clock (LEGAIA_RC_MINIGAME_FILM_REPLAY), then runs the venue's actor frame on the same step, whose re-target roll and catch trail draw from the pond's rand() stream.

  • A game frame is a move of the frame driver's counter _DAT_8007B798; the step it runs on is read a vsync after the move, its pad words on the move, and its results on the next move.
  • Two harness seats stand the pond on the capture: its rand() stream on the state's BIOS seed, and the pad pump's free-running auto-repeat clock _DAT_8007B940 on the capture's phase.

Over a catch, the rand() word and the point total agree on every game frame through the close-up and the result plate. The state column keeps 0x1E past the close-up, since the port holds the showcase states 0x20..0x22 in the venue camera, not the session.

The replay compares rules, not pace. The pace question is the venue's game tick, in the fishing state machine.

The Baka Fighter film

--set baka from minigame_baka_fighter_retail_attract, START at vsync 30 and Cross every 20 vsyncs: the cabinet's state, its clocks, both fighters' HP, wins, chosen attacks and combo counters, and the rand() word, the cabinet's stream seated on the capture's (a harness seat). Map each side's rand() words to their index in the BIOS stream to read which exchange took which draw; the two sides then agree exchange for exchange through both rounds.

The cabinet's opponent install (state 0x1E) blocks on its CD load for 26 vsyncs in the film and costs the port one; that is drive time, not game logic.

Adding a minigame

Add the words to SETS in the driver (address, kind, a key per pre-play phase and the columns that mark a play frame) and the same names to columns / values in crates/parity/src/retail_compare/minigame_film.rs. A minigame whose play phase reads rand() needs the stream seated at the aligned tick before the film can pair past its first roll.
