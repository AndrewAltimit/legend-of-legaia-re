ROM patcher (in browser)

Run the randomizer on your own Legend of Legaia disc image, entirely in your browser. Pick a preset (or fine-tune), choose a seed, and download a patched image. The disc bytes never leave your machine - the patch runs as WebAssembly locally, nothing is uploaded.

Language translation patch (optional)

Off by default; composes with any randomizer options. The retail font is plain ASCII, so the shipped packs are ASCII-folded (accents dropped) and cover the item / magic / arts / menu tables plus recurring system messages - the bulk story dialog is not translated yet.

If you also own an official European release, this transfers its translation onto your USA disc. Both discs are read here in your browser and neither is uploaded - same as the disc above. The official text is yours, from your own disc; it is never bundled with this site.

Not all of it will fit. The USA disc's scenes are packed to the sector with no spare room, so the shorter lines transfer and the rest stay English. The coverage report after patching says exactly how many landed and why the others did not.

Prices & names fishing prizes, location names, Earth Egg

Choose your disc image above and the current prices and names appear here, read from your own disc. Leave a box empty to keep the disc's value.

Fishing prize prices ?The fishing exchanges at Buma and Vidna sell these prizes for fishing points. Set a new point price in any row; the price is also the “appears once you can afford it” gate, so a cheaper prize also shows up sooner. The prize list itself is fixed on the disc (which items, and how many rows) - the patcher can only reprice a row, not add, remove or swap prizes. A prize sold at both venues is one row here: the new price applies to both.

Earth Egg coin threshold ?The Sol Tower “Prize Counter” offers the Earth Ra-Seru Egg once you hold this many casino coins (retail 100000), and debits exactly that many on purchase. It is a hidden scripted exchange, not a row in the casino prize table, so the casino editors can't reach it. Range 1 - 8388608.

Tactical-Art overrides AP cost, AP refund, damage per art

Pick any Tactical Art and change what it costs in AP, make it give AP back, or change its damage. ?AP: set what an art costs in AP (Spirit), or flip it to give AP back - a giving art becomes usable at any AP level and adds the amount you choose, up to the 100 cap, every time it lands in a combo. Either way the amount is 1..100: the injected setting is one signed byte per art and 0 is its “leave alone” value, so a completely free art is not offered - use 1. AP changes are per character, and the pause-menu arts list is rewritten to match; a giving art shows as 0 AP there - the in-game marker for “this one pays you”. Damage: pick a per-hit damage tier, or turn its damage off; damage is keyed by the button combo rather than by character, so another character with the same combo changes too (each row's note says which). AP overrides are mutually exclusive with Shiny Seru (both use the same injected-code arena).

Advanced: raw combo=value lists (CLI syntax)
Rebalance a party Tactical Art's damage ("arts power-down"). One or more combo=value pairs, comma-separated - the combo is the art's input (L/R/D/U, e.g. RDLDL = Vahn's Burning Flare) and value is a power byte: 0x0C..0x1F is a damage tier (a defence facet plus one of the multipliers 12/18/20/22/28, so a lower value powers the art down), or 0 disables its hits. Every per-strike power byte of the matched art is set to value. The CLI legaia-patcher arts lists every art's combo and current power tiers. Merged with the rows above; listing the same art both ways is an error.
Make a party Tactical Art grant AP (Spirit) instead of costing it. One or more [character:]combo=amount pairs, comma-separated - the combo is the art's input (L/R/D/U, e.g. Vahn:RDLDL) and amount (1..100) is the AP added per use; the art becomes castable at any AP level and adds that much, clamped at the 100 cap, without polluting the end-of-turn refund, and its menu AP number is rewritten to 0. The setting is keyed by (character, art row), so nothing spills onto another character's art; leave the character: prefix off to hit every character holding that combo, each in its own slot. legaia-patcher arts lists each art's combo. Use the picker rows above for the matching cost side. Mutually exclusive with Shiny Seru - both reuse the same verified-dead SCUS regions, so enable only one.
Texture replacement swap any texture for your own PNG

Replace any texture on the disc with your own image: scan your disc to browse every texture, download one as a PNG, edit it, and upload your edit. ?The replacement must keep the texture's exact pixel size; its color count must fit the texture's palette (or check the fold-colors box); alpha becomes PSX transparency (fully transparent, semi-transparent, opaque). raw textures always apply; lzs ones live inside compressed data and apply only when your edit re-compresses into the original's footprint - the preview tells you before anything is written. Everything runs in this tab; the disc never leaves your browser.

Two files download: the patched .bin and a matching .cue. Keep them together and point your emulator at the .cue; to reuse your original .cue, rename the patched .bin to match its FILE line.

  
Heads up: a full disc is ~470 MB, so patching needs a desktop browser with enough RAM and the download is the same size. The patched image contains Sony data: it is for your own local play, do not redistribute it - sharing a run is what the CLI's seed + PPF patch are for.






  
What it does

  
This is the same randomizer / disc patcher the CLI runs, compiled to WebAssembly. Each option is a deterministic, seeded edit; every randomizable category offers Shuffle (redistributes the existing set, difficulty preserved) or Randomize (draws fresh from the whole valid pool). Per-feature mechanics, table locations, and the code-injection details live on the randomizer page.

  
    
    
  • Monster drops - reassigns the item each monster drops (and its chance).
    • 
    
  • Random encounters - reassigns the monster ids in each scene's formations; Monster pool sets how wide a net is cast (Scene / Kingdom / World). Boss and scripted fights are never touched, and Solo strong fights makes an out-of-area heavy hitter show up alone rather than in a pack.
    • 
    
  • Treasure chests - reassigns the item each chest gives. Quest / key / story items are kept static so progression items stay where the player expects them.
    • 
    
  • Town shops - reassigns what each town store sells.
    • 
    
  • Casino prizes - reassigns prize-exchange items; coin price and progression gate kept.
    • 
    
  • Steal items - reassigns what each monster yields to the Evil God Icon; the steal chance is always preserved.
    • 
    
  • Arts button combos - reassigns each Tactical Art's directional combo; input count kept, each character's combos stay unique, Miracle Arts left alone.
    • 
    
  • Monster stats - redistributes each monster's combat stats (HP / MP / ATK / DEF↑ / DEF↓ / INT / SPD); the AGL action gauge is left alone.
    • 
    
  • Special-attack power - redistributes the per-move power of enemy specials & Seru magic (not party Arts); animations, effects, and sound stay put.
    • 
    
  • Element affinity - scrambles the 8×8 element matrix; Shuffle keeps the same number of weaknesses & resistances.
    • 
    
  • Spell MP costs - redistributes the costs of the named, costed spells; free enemy-tier entries never gain a cost.
    • 
    
  • Equipment stat bonuses - redistributes gear stat bonuses within each slot type; equip restrictions and accessory passives never move.
    • 
    
  • Equipment drops - a low per-battle chance (default 5%) of one extra random weapon / armor / accessory, via an injected code hook on top of the untouched normal drop.
    • 
    
  • Experience on the run - a code hook banks 5% of a fled fight's experience into the party (vanilla awards nothing for fleeing).
    • 
    
  • Enemy ally - a 20% chance per battle that one enemy starts charmed and fights its own side (not a controllable party member); multi-enemy fights only, so scripted solo fights can't softlock. How: code in the rodata gap.
    • 
    
  • Shiny Seru - a 2% chance per battle that a capturable enemy spawns as a translucent shiny with +35% stats; the Seru captured from it keeps a permanent +35% damage boost (captures after patching only). How: code in the rodata gap.
    • 
    
  • Weapon specialty - permutes which weapon class each character favors; an off-class weapon widens that character's Arms command. The Astral Sword stays always-wide.
    • 
    
  • Doors / exits - reassigns where scene-transition doors lead, re-paired (two-way) or independent (one-way). A few big overworld hubs whose data can't grow in place keep their original doors.
    • 
    
  • House doors (experimental) - shuffles intra-town interior/exit door targets; the op is shared with NPC movement, so some actor positions scramble too.
    • 
    
  • Starting items - replaces the new game's fixed Healing Leaf ×5 with distinct random consumables, sharing the seed's slot capacity with the convenience toggles.
    • 
    
  • Starting level - starts the party at the chosen level (up to 14) with stats and XP thresholds recomputed from the disc's own growth curves - real level-N stats, not a relabel.
    • 
    
  • Tactical-Art overrides - pick any art by name and change what it does: set what it costs in AP, make it give AP back instead (your amount, 1..100, up to the native 100 cap), set its per-hit damage tier, or turn its damage off. AP changes are per character and update the pause-menu arts list too; damage is keyed by the button combo, so another character with the same combo changes with it - each row's note lists exactly which.
    • 
    
  • Fishing prize prices / location names / Earth Egg threshold - structured value edits over the disc's own tables: reprice any fishing-exchange prize (the prize list itself is fixed on the disc), rename any of the 16 world-map location slots (31-char ASCII), and set the Sol Tower Earth-Egg coin threshold. Current values are read from your disc, in the browser.
    • 
    
  • Texture replacement - scan the disc for every texture (the flat TIM catalog plus the ones inside LZS-compressed sections), download any as a PNG, and patch an edited PNG back: same size / bit depth / palette layout enforced, VRAM placement preserved, alpha mapped to PSX transparency, and compressed textures applied only when the edit re-compresses into the retail footprint. Same coordinates and rules as the CLI's tim-list / tim-export / tim-replace.
    • 
    
  • Unused enemies - brings back two monsters the shipped game never places, “Comm” and the Evil Bat; needs Randomize encounters.
    • 
    
  • Unused items - adds “Something Good” and the no-name accessory the randomizer calls “Seru Bell” to Randomize fills.
    • 
  

  
The same seed always produces the same patched image, and the post-patch summary is spoiler-safe. Edits that can't be re-packed into their original footprint are skipped and reported rather than corrupting the disc.
