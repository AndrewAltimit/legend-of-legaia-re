Player battle files (data\battle\PLAYER1..4) Confirmed

Equip a new sword and, in the next battle, your character is holding it - every weapon, armour piece and Ra-Seru form has its own look. These four files are where that comes from: one per playable character (Vahn, Noa, Gala, Terra), each holding everything that character needs in battle - mesh pieces, textures, the battle animations and the palette. The elegant part is how the mesh pieces are keyed: the file is divided into sections, one per equipment slot, and each section's entries are labelled with item ids. At battle load the engine matches your equipped items against those ids and splices the winners into one mesh, which is why the battle model is assembled per character rather than stored whole.

What these files are - and are not

Each file is a self-contained container: a header, an LZS-compressed record[0] (the palette chain and the character's battle animations), a 12-byte-entry descriptor table, and a region of per-slot LZS streams that each decompress to [32-byte header + Legaia TMD + texture pool]. Several other battle containers live near it on the disc and have been conflated with it; this format is distinct from:

  • the monster stat archive (extraction 0867, retail monster_data),
  • the standalone TIM-pack used by some other PROT entries,
  • the DATA_FIELD streaming format used by scene bundles,
  • the field-pack and effect-bundle containers.
History: the “16 MB container at PROT 0865” reading

An earlier reading described a custom 16 MB battle_data container at PROT 0865. The figure was extraction 0865's TOC-indexed window under the superseded entry-size expression (7811 sectors, 0xF41800): it covered Gala's own 222-sector file, all of Terra's, and 7542 of the monster archive's 7760 sectors, and the analysis never noticed the boundary. Every structure on this page sits inside each file's own footprint, and the slot region tiles that footprint exactly (data_base + last_offset + last_size = footprint in all four files), which is what confirms the footprint as the true file size. The same over-read produced the “Vahn = PROT 0861” attribution (the 1-sector stub entries 0859..0862 precede the real file, and 0861's extended window reached Vahn's bytes at offset 0x1000) and the “battle_data 0865 vs monster archive 0867” conflation. The entry-size correction and the CDNAME −2 correction dissolve all three; catalogued in do-not-re-walk § containers.

Descriptor table - one section per equipment slot

OffsetSizeFieldMeaning
+0x004idEquippable item id (the item-name table space); 0 marks a section boundary and supplies that section's default slot.

The chain invariant offset[i+1] == offset[i] + size[i] holds across every entry and an all-zero entry terminates the table. Entries group into sections of descending ids separated by id = 0 entries - Gala's (0865) reads 57 56 55 54 53 | 00 | 42 41 40 3f | 00 | 21 20 27 … 2e | 00 | 19 … 13 | 00 | 69 68 67 66 | 00; Terra's (0866) carries only five id = 0 entries and no variant slots.

The five sections are the character's five equipment slots, in the order of the character record's equipped-item bytes at +0x196..+0x19A (live record base 0x80084708, stride 0x414). Vahn's file carries body (0x43 Hunter Clothes … 0x4A), head, weapon (0x22 Survival Knife … 0xBA), Ra-Seru weapon (0x01..0x09 Meta tiers) and footwear sections, each with its id = 0 fallback. The sections are not in the same order for every character: Vahn's section 2 is his weapon and section 3 his Ra-Seru; Noa's are the other way round. Both take the equipment table's Weapon slot, because a character's Ra-Seru is their weapon arm. Live proof: with Vahn in Hunter Clothes / Survival Knife / Ra-Seru Meta, the assembled battle mesh's vertex pools byte-match exactly the 0x43, 0x22 and 0x01 sections plus the defaults - see character mesh § provenance.

Every section is bone geometry - the item is not its own object

Equipment is not modelled as a separate object that gets attached. A section's slot carries whole skeleton bone objects, and selecting it replaces the bare object outright with a re-authored one that happens to include the gear. Vahn's right-hand bone across three of his weapon ids:

Armour works the same way and can make an object smaller: Hunter Clothes takes Vahn's torso from 79 vertices to 64. It is a re-sculpt, not a layer, and a full endgame set re-authors ten of his fifteen bone objects. Two readings that look right and are not:

  • A positional set-difference does not find the added geometry. The equipped hand shares as few as one vertex position with the bare hand. What answers “what did this change” is a radius envelope (the bare object's centroid plus its reach), which still leaves 4-31 primitives straddling the boundary per weapon - honestly, since they belong to both. The characters page's diff highlight uses exactly that (battle_char_assembly::equip_diff).
  • The 200+ extras are not weapon meshes. They are usually byte-identical duplicates of their attach bone, reached only through the equipment-variant track; the 100+ extras are ordinary geometry drawn on the preceding bone's channel.

The item is still separable - by palette, not by geometry

“Not its own object” is not “inseparable”. The separator is the primitive's CLUT word: a weapon is drawn from its own palette column, and across all 81 weapon / Ra-Seru records no primitive mixes the two. Geometry alone would have missed it - connectivity splits Gala's and most of Noa's weapons off cleanly but not Vahn's, which weld to the fist at the grip. What tells the buckets apart is the joint: a TMD object is authored about its own bone origin, so the flesh half always reaches the origin and the held item never does (battle_char_assembly::equip_item).

A welded cut is exact at primitive level, but the exported item has an open grip: the shaft inside the closed fist was never modelled. Sections 0/1/4 (body, head, feet) have no boundary to cut on - no surplus object, and a body-versus-trim palette split - so every one of their 51 records exports fused: the whole piece with the body part it is sculpted onto, labelled as such. Completeness over purity: over all 132 equipment records the split is 6 / 21 / 53 / 52 and nothing yields nothing.

Two further layers sit on top of the exact cut, both opinionated and both labelled in their output. The item-alone export (equip_isolate, rule table crates/asset/data/equip-isolation.toml) answers “give me just the axe” by policy: headgear and held items use a colour diff against the bare counterpart, body and footwear an identity test, both backed by a skin-tone test and a per-record override table for the cases the heuristic cannot know (Vahn's circlet Seals, Noa's hair strands in the torso column, Gala's neck-capping plates). The grip repair (equip_repair) bridges the open grip by pairing the two rims of a straight shaft on the same object and lofting a tube between them - nine of the 81 held items take a bridge, all Vahn's; rims facing away, lone rims and cross-object pairs are deliberately refused. Every result says which rules touched it.

The 200+ surplus is a duplicate, except when it is not

Most 0xFF (tag 200+) surplus objects are byte-copies of their attach bone, reached only through the equipment-variant track at +0xA4, so drawing one alongside its host z-fights a limb. Sixteen are not copies: all of Noa's section-2 records, four of her section-3 ones and three of Gala's section-3 ones - and in six of those the surplus is the bare hand while a 0xFE extra holds the weapon alone. Neither “always skip” nor “always draw” is right; the test is a byte comparison against the attach bone (AssembledCharacter::duplicate_objects).

Slot region + decompressed slot layout

At data_base + entry.offset each slot is [u32 decompressed_size][LZS stream]. The decoder stops on the output count, not the input length - hand it a generous source slice rather than truncating to entry.size. Decoded, every slot has the same three-part shape: a 32-byte header, the loader frame wrapping a Legaia TMD, and a texture pool.

The assembled objects are not only drawn - they are effect-read: the Spirit charge's streamer effect samples authored vertex indices of specific objects (on Noa's retail assembly, the hair part's 49 vertices and the 0xFE weapon extra's 56). A rebuild that ships a zero-vertex object in one of those slots feeds the reads from whatever bytes follow, and the trail fan anchors off-model; a prim-less object with a real vertex pool (all vertices at the origin) satisfies the reads while drawing nothing.

History: earlier readings of the slot header

+0x04 / +0x08 were first read as nested-section end offsets (“a multi-mesh slot” - a Gala slot whose u32[1] = 0x3310 was in fact its swing record, a 15-part / 17-frame stream) and +0x10 as the low half of a flag word; the byte-match corpus read the loader frame's attach-count + bone-id bytes at u32[5..6] as “texture format tags”. All superseded by the FUN_80052FA0 disassembly. Full ledger: settled threads.

Texture-pool VRAM placement

Where each character's textures land in video memory is pinned. FUN_80052FA0 runs once per present party member, with p the member's 0-based ordinal (the band selector - not the character id; a Noa + Terra party bands Terra at her ordinal like anyone else). Per member it issues up to seven upload blocks through FUN_80053B9C: the two record[0] image blocks at clut_a_off / clut_b_off with inline rects (0x20, 0x80, 0x20, 0x80) and (0x60, 0x00, 0x20, 0x80), plus one block per flagged equipment section with the rect from the static SCUS_942.54 table at 0x800775B8:

FUN_80053B9C reads the block's [clut_x][clut_n] prefix and issues two LoadImages: the CLUT at (clut_x, 0x1E1 + p, clut_n, 1) with the STP bit OR'd onto every non-zero colour, and the pixels at (x0 + 0x200 + p*0x80, y0 + 0x100, w, h). Battle animations (record[0])

The player files are also the party's in-battle pose source, not just their meshes. record[0]'s head is a u32 action-offset table (12 populated disc slots at +0x00..+0x2C; the loader widens it at runtime) whose entries are the character's battle action-animation records - the same per-action entry family as the monster archive's, with the packed [u8 parts][u8 frames][9-byte TRS] keyframe stream at entry +0xAC (monster entries keep theirs at +0x8C), the rate byte at +0x78, and the first byte the action tag (identity with the slot index here: 0 idle, 1 walk, 2..5 hit reactions, 7/8/9 ready / recover / defeat, 0x0B block). parts = the skeleton bone count (15 / 16 / 15 / 17): channel i drives assembled object i, equipment extras ride their attach bone. Frame 0 of the idle is the combat-stance rest pose that sockets the assembled mesh - live-pinned byte-exact against a full-party capture's anim contexts. Each entry's +0x14..+0x53 region is the action's effect script (per-footfall dust, impact flashes, per-swing sparks - monster-animation § effect-script records). Parsers: battle_char_assembly::{decode_record0, battle_animations, idle_battle_animation}.

Only the region below clut_a_off is battle-persistent. Member init decodes record[0] into one 0x19000-byte allocation, uploads the two image blocks, then decodes the five sections sequentially into the same buffer from clut_a_off on. Anything that must stay readable per frame - the action table, its entries, the art bank - lives below clut_a_off; an edit that adds such data must grow that region (shifting clut_a_off / clut_b_off / budget and the paired +0x5C word), never borrow space above it.

The runtime action table (rebased copies at 0x801C9360 + slot*4) is wider than the 12 disc words: slots 0xC..0xF take the swing records spliced from the equipment sections, and slots 0x10/0x11 are dynamic art-anim slots the commit FUN_8004AD80 materialises for any staged id >= 0x10.

Two container encodings, one pose format

Inline in record[0] a stream is raw: exactly 2 + parts * frames * 9 bytes, sized by its own head, laid end to end with no size field or codec - the retail splice FUN_800557B8 copies (parts*frames*9 + 5) >> 2 words after the 0xAC header. An art clip's stream is a "ME" archive body in readef.DAT whose size word carries a bit-15 compression flag, set on every retail player-art body, so it is delta-coded per 12-bit channel by FUN_8002A9CC. Both decode to the same bytes; the trap runs the other way - a raw inline stream can be rewritten in place if parts and frames hold, whereas a "ME" body must be re-encoded and its length is a property of the content.

Swing records (equipment sections → slots 0xC..0xF)

The four direction-command swings are per-equipment animations: each selected section's payload carries a standard action entry at its +0x04 word (section 4 a second at +0x08), and FUN_80052FA0 installs the copy at action-table word 0x28 + section*4 (slot 0xC + section − 2; section 4's second record at slot 0xF), pointing the entry's +0x88 stream pointer at entry+0xAC. Sections 0/1 carry 0 in both words; every section-2/3/4 slot carries a valid record with parts = the skeleton bone count (up to +2 channels on slots with attach objects). The record's first byte is not the runtime slot: it sits in the head the art bank uses for the per-strike power run, and its band (0x0E..0x1F) lies inside that encoding (art-data). Attach-object records are matched by their +0x07 key against action entries' +0x77 bytes (then the art bank's +0x9B) and linked into the entry's +0x04/+0x08 pair by FUN_80055854 - a word-granular memcpy whose extent two nested signed counts define ([word][word][u32 outer] then outer × ([word][word][u32 pairs] + pairs × 2 words)). Parser: battle_char_assembly::swing_battle_animations.

Art-animation bank (record[0] +0x58)

The self-relative word at +0x58 locates [u32 count] then count 0xD0-stride records: a 0x24-byte arts-matcher head (+0x00 direction-command combo, +0x0A stream-source index into the character's "ME" archive, +0x10 inline art name) followed by a standard 0xAC-byte action entry whose first bytes are the per-strike power run (art-data). The bank keeps its keyframe streams out-of-file; a materialised art clip installs through the record's embedded entry (FUN_8004AD80 installs record +0x24 as the slot 0x10/0x11 pointer).

“ME” stream archives (readef.DAT)

The art keyframe streams resolve through compressed “ME” archives at the head of readef.DAT's side-band slots (summon-readef; parser legaia_asset::me_archive; every retail art stream decodes length-exact). The main-vs-base archive pick is per battle phase: the initiative scheduler FUN_801DABA4 stages the acting character's group each turn, leaving the main archive resident, while the battle-end arms (FUN_801DABA4's no-living-enemy branch, FUN_801E295C's victory arm) request slot 3×char+2, so the win poses (ids 0x11..=0x18) decode from the base archive. No "ME" archive exists anywhere in a player file - a disc-wide magic sweep over all four footprints and every decoded stream finds none, with readef.DAT as the positive control (exactly the eight documented archives validate).

Facial animation tracks (entry +0x8C / +0x98)

Two further entry-header fields are per-clip facial keyframe tracks - eyes at +0x8C, mouth at +0x98 - consumed by the per-frame facial animator FUN_8004C7B4, which MoveImage-stamps the selected face frame over the head section's texture rows in step with the clip. An art clip's tracks sit at bank-record +0xB0/+0xBC (ArtAnimRecord::face; Terra's are all empty). During the victory celebration (DAT_8007BD71 == 0xFE with FUN_8004E568 running) a member whose staged anim id is a win pose (0x11..=0x18) takes its mouth records from a sixteen-record static SCUS table at 0x80077E80 (char*0x180 + staged_id*0x30), clocked by the global victory counter gp+0x9EA >> 1: the win-quote mouth flap.

Equipment-variant track (entry +0xA4)

The entry header's last 8 bytes are a third track: two 2-byte [start, end] frame windows per variant pair, consumed by FUN_8004CCD4 - a mesh swap, not a stamp. The 0xFE extras sort in as extra animated channels; the 0xFF variants sort past every drawn channel and render only when the pass swaps their pointer onto the attach-bone channel of the render node's per-channel model table (*(node+0x44)+4) - inside a +0xA4 window, or unconditionally during the extra-channel swings. The arts motion-trail renderer FUN_80049348 re-runs the pass per after-image ghost. Live windows exist only in Noa's file; the other three carry all-zero windows.

Editing: carrying a weapon into another character's file

The loader selects a section by the equipped item id, and every retail file only carries records for the items its character can equip - so a weapon handed to another character through the SCUS owner mask alone lands on that character's id = 0 weapon default at battle load: the bare hand. Giving them the model means giving their file a record, and a record is built, not copied (legaia_asset::equip_transplant; the patcher's --equip-owner does it for every weapon it enables):

  1. The weapon's own primitives are cut out of the donor record with the item-alone cut above (retail prims verbatim, CBA columns remapped onto the target section's CLUT columns).
  2. They are merged into the target's weapon-section default record channel by channel - donor bone k maps to target bone k, the held sections being the same three arm bones on every file, only numbered per skeleton (Vahn 3 4 5, Noa 7 8 9) - and re-seated: the skeletons' arm-bone frames differ (the same Short Sword runs along -Y in Vahn's hand, -Z in Noa's, (0, +0.5, +0.85) in Gala's), so each channel goes through a rigid transform calibrated from the weapons both files carry (legaia_asset::equip_hand_frame: principal frame + far tip fitted, roll by consensus, translation by ICP, averaged per channel) - per weapon class, since a club is swung along a different line than a blade is pointed. Leave-one-out over the shared blades lands within 4-9 GTE units (verbatim copying: 22-55). The 0xFF variant is a byte copy of the armed hand on every retail weapon record, so the transplant emits one armed hand and aliases the variant onto it.
  3. The donor's tile rides along as the pool (unused texels blanked) under the weapon's palettes on the target section's columns; sections 2 and 3 share texpage 0x16 and differ only by tile row, so a cross-section transplant shifts v by 128 and nothing else.
  4. The target default's header, loader frame, attach list and swing records survive whole, with the swing record's +0x74 set to the donor weapon's cost.

The record is appended to the target's weapon section and the file re-packed with 0x800-aligned slots. Size is the constraint: Vahn's Astral Sword record for Noa comes out at 7 sectors, and the retail files tile their footprints exactly - the optimal LZS parse frees 4 / 2 / 5 sectors in Vahn's / Noa's / Gala's files and Terra's none - so the patcher moves the boundaries between PROT entries 863..865 (a file that grows into its neighbour's former sectors needs nothing but the TOC word) and, past that pool, parks the records outside PROT.DAT.

Parking the records in DMY.DAT

The loader never bounds a player file by its TOC span: FUN_8003E8A8 sets the CD position from the start LBA and returns the span, a value the caller discards, and everything after the prologue read is the gap-and-seek streaming above. Two consequences: the offsets are 32-bit and can reach anywhere later on the disc, and because the gap is shifted as an unsigned value a selected slot must never sit before the previous one.

So a rebuilt file that outgrows the pool keeps its first 0x8000 bytes in the PROT entry and puts its whole slot region, in chain order, into DMY.DAT (developer fixtures no retail code loads - 18,054 Form 1 sectors at the end of the disc), with every table offset displaced by the byte distance from the entry's data base to the annexed region. The old record sectors stay where they were and nothing reads them. legaia_asset::player_file_annex tells an annexed table from a retail one (the chain starts at a non-zero sector-aligned offset) and materialises the retail-shaped file back for every existing parser; DiscPatcher::{annex_player_file, read_player_file, patch_player_file} are the disc side, with a bump allocator whose marker (LGAX, version, sectors used) lives in DMY.DAT's last sector. The image keeps its size, so a PPF carries it. Verified in PCSX-Redux from a pre-battle save on the patched disc: Noa and Gala both draw the Astral Sword with their records streamed from DMY.DAT.

What this does not unlock: a Ra-Seru level form, a body / head / footwear record is the donor's whole re-sculpted limb or torso on the donor's skeleton, not an item to seat on the target's bone (see every section is bone geometry), so those slots still fall through to the target's default; and Terra's file carries no equipment sections at all.

Not the monster archive

The monster stat archive (legaia_asset::monster_archive, retail monster_data = define 869 → extraction 0867) shares the [u32 dec_size][LZS] → mesh + texture pool general shape but is a different container with no shared structures:

  • Archive slots are fixed-stride 0x14000 bytes keyed by 1-based monster id, with no descriptor table; player-file slots are variable-size, reached through the 12-byte descriptor table.
  • The archive's decoded head is the monster stat record (+0x00 name_offset, +0x0C HP, +0x4C action-offset array - monster-animation); the player-file slot head is the 32-byte header above, with the TMD inside the loader frame.
  • The action entries are siblings - same header family, keyframe stream at +0x8C in the archive versus +0xAC here.

Parser status

  • legaia_asset::battle_char_palette implements the runtime-pinned framing (header words, descriptor chain, record[0] + sub-record palette assembly; byte-exact vs live battle VRAM).
  • legaia_asset::battle_data_pack (the TMD-slot walker) reads the descriptor table in the [id, offset, size] frame: detection validates the chain invariant (entry 0 at offset 0, sector-aligned sizes, all-zero terminator) plus the header-word ordering (clut_a < clut_b < budget), accepting all four retail player files - including Terra's all-default 0866 - and rejecting every other PROT entry.
  • legaia_asset::battle_char_assembly is the assembler port: splice, retag, sort, relocate_tsb_cba, texture uploads, record[0] and swing animations, the equipment cuts.
History: the 4-byte-shifted descriptor frame

An earlier revision of the walker read the table through a 4-byte-shifted frame; its observations (“the table is sized to a maximum and zero-padded”, “0866 has a zero count in the canonical position”, “the last 0865 slot over-runs the footprint”) were artifacts of that frame - under the correct frame 0866 parses like its siblings and all four files tile their footprints exactly.

VRAM byte-match corpus

The tool that first pinned the texture pools is byte-matching: slide a 32-byte halfword-aligned window over each decoded slot's post-TMD bytes and search a mednafen-captured VRAM blob for exact matches; each hit yields (slot, slot_offset, fb_x, fb_y). Driver: mednafen-state clut-trace; analysis API battle_data_pack::find_clut_in_vram. Over a four-save corpus of Gala's file (town, town, pre-battle, active battle) the equipment slots land in the battle band at fb_x = 768 and the last-section defaults in the town band at fb_x = 864, with consecutive slot offsets stepping 0x40 per +1 in fb_y - the post-TMD pool uploads as a 32-halfword-wide (128 px at 4bpp) contiguous block. The placement is now fully pinned by the static rect table rather than by search.

What is not in these files: the NPC palettes at row 479

The town NPC CLUTs at row 479 byte-match no decoded slot of any player file (nor any raw PROT entry or SCUS_942.54). They are plain PSX TIMs in each scene's own scene_tmd_stream entries, uploaded by FUN_8001FE70 at battle init - see npc-palette. The engine consequence (field scene-loads exclude these packs from VRAM) is wired through SceneResources::SceneLoadKind.

Vestigial field: record[0] +0x5C

The word is rebased self-relative→absolute at load by FUN_80052FA0 back to back with the +0x58 art-bank pointer (800532b4..800532e4), but unlike +0x58 (read by FUN_8004AD80) it has no traced reader. The negative is word-wise exhaustive over SCUS_942.54 (one non-sp load at 0x5c in the whole executable - the relocation itself) and over the 15 extracted overlay images (two hits, both a scratchpad block copy); 11 overlay images remain dump-only, so the honest statement is “no reader in SCUS or the extracted overlays”. Its target is clut_a_off − 4 (zero on disc); the CLUT upload uses file+0x04/+0x08, not this field. The earlier “it points at the art "ME" archive” hypothesis is separately disc-refuted (the archives live in readef.DAT).
