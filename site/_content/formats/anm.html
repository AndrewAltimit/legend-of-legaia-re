ANM animation container Confirmed

Every time Vahn walks across Rim Elm, a villager shifts on their feet, or a house door swings open, the motion comes from an ANM clip: a short list of per-bone positions and rotations, one set per frame, that the field engine plays back over a TMD mesh. It is the animation format of the field - player characters, NPCs and scene props - and it matters because it is the only thing standing between a static model and a living town. Technically it is a small offset-table container of frame-major keyframe records, asset type 0x06 in the asset-type dispatcher; each (bone, frame) entry is 8 bytes.

It is not the only animation format on the disc, and picking the wrong one is the usual mistake. Battle monsters use a separate per-object keyframe stream inside the monster archive (monster animation), and the party's battle poses come from the player battle files' own streams rather than from here (character mesh).

Two families of clips

The same record layout reaches the runtime from two places, and they are never mixed: the party's walk and idle clips ship once for the whole game, while every town carries its own pool of NPC and prop clips. A field actor's +0x4C record pointer points into one or the other, never both.

Bone i drives TMD object i one-to-one. The consuming actor's object-pointer table at actor[+0x44] ([u32 count][ptr × count], built by FUN_80024D78 straight off the pool TMD) must have count equal to the record's bone count or FUN_8001B964 skips the draw. For the party meshes that count is the runtime-capped 10 - the disc pack ships nobj = 12, but groups 10 / 11 are equipment-swap templates that are never rendered (character mesh).

A held-pad "run" does not exist in the locomotion bank: fifteen seconds of continuous walking plus direction + button chords never moves the pointer off the walk record, and the select byte at player+0x5C reads only 1 (walk, record 0) / 2 (idle, record 1) - the same id = record + 1 shape as the NPC bundle. Whatever triggers the other bank slots, it is not plain field locomotion.

The battle-form sibling - PROT 1203

PROT 1203 (other5) is the same container shape with 30 records: three 9-record banks (Vahn 0..=8 15-bone, Noa 9..=17 16-bone, Gala 18..=26 15-bone) plus three 10-bone-rig records 27..=29. Its bone counts match the PROT 1204 pack's object order - the Baka Fighter / viewer configuration - and the duel's display-anim ids pin the slot roles: the combat tick stores base + k at actor+0x5C (base = fighter_id * 9) and the player resolves it through the container header (word 0 is the count, word d is offsets[d-1]), so display id base + k lands on record k - 1: 0 idle, 1..=3 attacks, 4 special, 5..=7 hit / knockdown, 8 win flourish (legaia_asset::baka_opponents::action_slot_label).

It is not what a real battle poses the assembled party meshes with. The in-battle pose source is each character's own TRS streams in record[0] of their player battle file (battle-data-pack § battle animations); no 1203 record is resident in a mid-battle capture.

Container and record layout

The container is an offset table followed by records. Offsets are absolute byte offsets into the LZS-decoded buffer (the legaia_anm::parse convention).

OffsetSizeFieldMeaning
+0x004countNumber of records
+0x044 × countbyte_offsets[]Absolute byte offset of each record; offsets[i+1] - offsets[i] = record size
offsets[i]variesrecords[]Per-record bodies (below)

Each record opens with an 8-byte header, then the frames, then 8 zero bytes of padding. The frame-stream size obeys record_size = 16 + 8 × (a & 0xFF) × b byte-exact across every record of every scene bundle in the corpus, which is what the find_in_entry detector validates before declaring a bundle parsed.

Those header fields are exactly what the playback advancer FUN_800204F8 reads: bone count at +0, scaling flag at +1, frame count at +2, divisor at +6 (ghidra/scripts/funcs/800204f8.txt).

The 8-byte (bone, frame) entry

Each entry is one bone's translation and rotation for one frame, decoded by FUN_8001BE80 (ghidra/scripts/funcs/8001be80.txt; port player_anm::BoneTransform::decode). Translations are nibble-packed signed 12-bit values; rotations are one byte per axis.

OffsetSizeFieldMeaning
+01low8(T0)Translation X, low byte
+11low8(T1)Translation Y, low byte
+21high4(T1) << 4 | high4(T0)The two high nibbles (sign bits live here)
+31low8(T2)Translation Z, low byte
+41? << 4 | high4(T2)T2's high nibble is the low nibble of byte 4 (andi 0xf at 0x8001BF38); the high nibble is never read
+51rot Xu8, shifted left 4 to a 12-bit PSX angle
+61rot Ysame
+71rot Zsame
  • T0..T2 are signed 12-bit (if (v & 0x800) v |= 0xFFFFF000) joint offsets in actor-local space. The runtime pushes them through the GTE MVMVA with the actor's rotation matrix and loads the result as the per-object world translation.
  • The three angles compose the GTE rotation matrix in Z, Y, X order (post-multiplication) through the PsyQ-shape builders FUN_8004638C / FUN_8004629C / FUN_800461A4, each reading the global sin / cos tables at DAT_80070A2C / DAT_8007122C.
  • Frame 0 of an idle clip is the rest-pose assembly transform - it places each TMD object at its joint with its rest orientation. Vahn's field form (nobj 12, 10 bones) decodes to a bilaterally symmetric humanoid.
  • The renderer (FUN_8001B964) poses object i with a flat R·v + T about the object origin - there is no bone hierarchy to accumulate.
History: the "byte 4 high nibble" phrasing

Prose elsewhere in the repo once placed high4(T2) in the high nibble of byte 4. The instruction is lbu v0,0x4(t3); andi v0,v0,0xf; sll v0,v0,0x8 at 0x8001BF30..0x8001BF3C - unambiguously the low nibble - and the code was never wrong: the disc-gated unit test bone_transform_decode_signed_12bit (town01 record 17) pins the byte-exact decode and would have failed the moment anyone "corrected" bytes[4] & 0x0F to match the prose. Other falsified readings live in do-not-re-walk.

Playback - the frame cursor and the blend

FUN_800204F8 is the per-frame clip driver, called from the actor tick FUN_80021DF4. It binds the clip (actor+0x4C = pack_base + offsets[id], rebinding whenever the requested id actor+0x5C differs from the bound id actor+0x5E, which also resets the cursor and selects draw kind 1), then advances the frame cursor actor+0x68.

  • The cursor is in 1/16-frame units: FUN_8001B964 poses from frame = (i16)(actor+0x68) >> 4, and the clip's last position is frame_count * 16 - 1.
  • The per-tick step is actor+0x6A, through the header scaling above.
  • The mode word actor+0x62 carries hold (0x0002), clamp (0x0008, clear = loop), reverse (0x0080), an end latch (0x0100) the tick sets on reaching either end, and a restart request (0x0200) the next tick consumes.
  • Scripts drive all of it through the field-VM ops 0x2B / 0x2C / 0x2D (set / clear / spin on a bit of actor+0x62), 0x4C nibble-4 sub-1 (the rate) and nibble-3 sub-5 / sub-6 (the two pose snaps); 0x22 <id> is SET_ANIM. This is what swings a town's house doors open - field locomotion. Engine port legaia_engine_core::field_env::PropAnim.

The interpreter interpolates - the decoder does not

FUN_8001BE80 is not a pure per-entry decoder. It reads two entries and blends them: the sub-frame fraction is *(u16*)(actor + 0x68) & 0xF, and the blend runs only when the gate *(u8*)(a2 + 1) & 1 is set - otherwise the current frame is emitted as-is. Translations lerp as a + (((b - a) * frac) >> 4); the angles go through the wraparound-aware interpolator FUN_8001D088. Results land in scratchpad 0x1F8002C0 (T at +0x0..0x6, angles at +0x8..0xE) before the GTE load.

BoneTransform::decode models only the un-interpolated arm, which is the right shape for a format decoder and what the site's character viewer needs; a consumer chasing frame-exact parity adds the blend itself. The angle half is available as legaia_asset::player_anm::lerp_angle_12: (to + ((from - to) * frac >> 4)) & 0xFFF, with the pair first brought onto the short arc by two sequential guards (0x8001D090..0x8001D0B8) - add a turn to to when from - to >= 0x800, then to from when the updated to - from >= 0x800. Inputs exactly half a turn apart fire both guards, which cancel, so that case resolves forward. Retail also accumulates |from - to| into _DAT_8007BD28 and journals the pair into the slot table at 0x800891A8; neither affects the transform and the port omits both.

The WASM LegaiaViewer::player_anm_record_pose_frames emits per-frame absolute transforms in the BoneTransform shape for the characters and NPCs viewers.

Entry points - how a clip gets bound

FUN_80024CFC (play_anm_by_id(id, actor, ?) in SCUS) primes an actor for playback:

  1. Calls FUN_80020DE0 (actor allocator).
  2. Reads the per-record offset from the ANM payload at _DAT_8007B7C8 + (id*4) + 4.
  3. Stores (anm_base + record_offset) in actor[+0x4C].
  4. Writes 0xB to actor[+0x56] (animation state byte) and 100 to actor[+0x68] (frame counter).

The actor tick FUN_80021DF4 then reads actor[+0x4C] whenever actor[+0x5A] is 2 or 6. The field/event script VM opcode 0x34 sub-op 3 is the sibling path: it indexes an ANM container and hands the entry to func_0x800252EC, landing the same actor[+0x4C] slot.

Allocator preamble

When the dispatcher (FUN_8001f05c case 6) loads ANM data, the malloc'd buffer at _DAT_8007B7C8 carries a 16-byte allocator preamble before the payload; the on-disc form has none, and crates/anm::peel_preamble strips it.

The type byte is a documented quirk: the scene bundle's section 2 is tagged 0x05 ("MOVE" in AssetType) while the loader that allocates _DAT_8007B7C8 is the labelled ANM dispatch (ghidra/scripts/funcs/8001f05c.txt, anm_malloc_err). The runtime case selector indexes asset bytes differently than the enum's display label suggests.

The other body layout - opcode-6 keyframe tables

Records consumed via animation opcode 0x06 carry a different body: a per-bone src/dst keyframe table the actor tick FUN_80021DF4 (block 0x80022ec4..0x80023040) interpolates in place, indexed by a bone count from the actor's mesh context.

OffsetSizeFieldMeaning
+0x008headera, b, marker_1, marker_2
+0x088 × Noutput slotsPer-bone packed pos + rot deltas, written by the tick
+0x08 + 8N24 × Nkeyframe dataPer bone 12 little-endian i16: src_pos.xyz, dst_pos.xyz, src_rot.xyz, dst_rot.xyz, read by the tick

Total size 8 + 32N. The tick multiplies the (dst - src) deltas by actor[+0x22] (the per-actor interpolation factor, driven from the field-VM frame counter) and writes the packed result back into the output slots. crates/anm::KeyframeReader is the typed accessor; the bone count is caller-supplied, and KeyframeReader::infer_bone_count recovers it from the record size when the equation fits.

The per-frame interpreter for non-opcode-6 records is partially overlay-resident. A handler in the town overlay (FUN_801DE840, overlay 0897) reads actor[+0x4C] at 801e260c via a 16-entry sub-dispatch table at 0x801CEF88 (routed by opcode & 0xF):

  1. Guard: reads actor[+0x5C]; skips the whole handler if ≤ 0.
  2. Calls FUN_800204f8 (a0 = actor) - the clip driver above.
  3. Loads s6 = actor[+0x4C].
  4. Calls FUN_80056798 (BIOS vector 0xa0/0x2F) while advancing the field VM PC by 2 in the delay slot.
  5. The 40-byte body at 801e2630..801e2670 completes the frame selection; that window is in the overlay dump but not yet traced.

This path gates on actor[+0x5C]; the opcode-6 path gates on actor[+0x5A] == 6. See ghidra/scripts/funcs/overlay_0897_801de840.txt line ~3389.

Battle-side anim state (the actor record)

In battle the party and monsters are posed by a different consumer, but the per-actor state still lives in a small named region of the 0x2D4-byte battle actor record (slot-0 base 0x800EC9E8, one record per +0x2D4). A pre-action / mid-action save pair (a quiet frame vs an in-flight somersault) pins it:

The pointer (shadowed into the render context at +0x4C by FUN_80049348) names a runtime per-record control struct, not a bytecode program. The per-frame consumer is the ladder in FUN_8004AD80 (ghidra/scripts/funcs/8004ad80.txt), which dispatches on the struct's first byte:

The 24-byte control block observed at the captured pointer (u32 len = 0x18, two reserved words, then field_C / field_10 / field_14 / field_18) is the head of this same struct: its leading byte is kind 0x18, the "other" arm, so the somersault is a raw-playback record.

Nested per-frame data (battle)

The buffer at consumer[+0x88] carries the bone keyframes the battle renderer interpolates each tick, validated against FUN_8004AD80, FUN_80048A08, FUN_8004998C and FUN_80047430:

OffsetSizeFieldMeaning
+0x001bones_per_frame (B)Inner render-loop bound (FUN_80048A08)
+0x011frame_count (N)FUN_8004AD80 stamps (N - 1) * 16 into the frame-counter cap; FUN_80047430 divides per-frame velocity by it, so zero is a runtime divide-by-zero
+0x02N × B × 9keyframesFrame stride B * 9; FUN_8004998C reads base + frame_index * B * 9 + 2

Each 9-byte bone block packs six sign-extended 12-bit values as two [i16; 3] vectors:

byte[0] | (byte[2] & 0x0F) << 8   → vec_a.x
byte[1] | (byte[2] & 0xF0) << 4   → vec_a.y
byte[3] | (byte[5] & 0x0F) << 8   → vec_a.z
byte[4] | (byte[5] & 0xF0) << 4   → vec_b.x
byte[6] | (byte[8] & 0x0F) << 8   → vec_b.y
byte[7] | (byte[8] & 0xF0) << 4   → vec_b.z

Adjacent low bytes share a high-nibble byte; bit 11 set ORs in 0xF000 (FUN_8004998C). The two vectors are runtime angle / pose deltas whose renderer-side naming is lost in compilation, but the byte layout is exact.

Frame counter / sub-frame interpolation

actor[+0x68] is a u16: bits [4..15] = frame index, bits [0..3] = sub-frame factor 0..=15. With a non-zero factor, FUN_8004998C lerps each bone toward the next frame as dst = a + (b - a) * frac >> 4, where "next" is the loop target +0x85 (when frame_index == +0x86 - 1 and actor[+0x21B] != 0), frame_count - 1 on the terminal frame, or frame_index + 1 otherwise.
