Player-character mesh packs Confirmed

Vahn, Noa and Gala have two bodies. Walking around a town you see the small, low-detail field form; the moment a battle starts you see the tall, high-detail battle form, wearing exactly the armour and weapon you have equipped. The field form is five whole models in one archive entry. The battle form exists on disc only as parts: at battle setup the engine assembles each character from per-equipment-slot pieces, so the model you fight with may exist nowhere on the disc as a single file. Both forms are live on the characters page.

Field form - PROT 0874

PROT 0874 is a three-section LZS container (parse_player_lzs(buf, 3) shape): §0 the mesh pack, §1 the party locomotion ANM bundle, §2 the textures. Section 0 decompresses to a canonical pack with five Legaia TMDs, shared by every field scene:

The runtime byte count is what the size-bounded LZS decode produces (~46 KB total), so slot 4 receives only its TMD prefix. Each body is a standard Legaia TMD: 12-byte header, then nobj × 0x1C group descriptors.

10-group cap + equipment swap

In the field, equipping a weapon changes one thing: a single group toggles between two pre-baked variants. The field player loader overwrites the three party TMDs' group count to 10 after install; disc groups 10 and 11 are never drawn directly - they are the templates the per-frame patch loop copies over the visible group:

The swap is binary and never changes the object count; item identity in the field comes from the texture atlas, not the mesh. Clean-room port: character_pack::equipment_swap::apply.

Rest pose (§1)

The field TMDs' vertices are object-local - each group models one body part about its own joint - so drawing a character needs a per-object rigid transform. That comes from the locomotion ANM bundle in section 1 (23 records: three 7-record character banks + savepoint + aux). Bank slot 1 is the standing idle; frame 0 of that clip is the field rest pose. Bone i drives group i one-to-one, which is why the 10-group cap matters: 12 disc groups minus 2 templates leaves exactly the 10 objects the 10-bone clips animate. Each 8-byte (bone, frame) entry decodes to a flat R·v + T.

Textures (§2)

Section 2 is a pack of eight standard PSX TIMs (parser field_char_textures), byte-exact against a live field-scene VRAM dump. Three per-character atlases (Vahn / Noa / Gala) tile horizontally to fill the 4bpp texpage at (832, 256); their CLUTs land on row 478 as flat horizontal strips, 64 columns per character. Three shared 256-colour pages and two atlas extensions complete the set (entry table).

  • Only about a third of a field character's primitives are textured; hair, vest and boots carry per-vertex RGB in the TMD. A texture-only renderer leaves holes - details.
  • The textures are not in extraction PROT 0876, whose filename label reads player_data only because of the CDNAME −2 shift.

Battle form - assembled from the player files

A main-game battle renders no disc TMD directly. At battle setup the engine builds each party member's mesh from that character's player battle file, picking one section per equipment slot by the character's equipped item ids (character record +0x196..+0x19A), and installs the merged TMD into pool slots [0..=2]. A section is not an accessory bolted on: it carries whole re-sculpted bone objects (a fist with the sword modelled into it), so equipping something swaps limbs, not attachments.

Where each rest pose comes from

Every battle TMD is a set of object-local pieces posed with a flat per-object transform v_world = R·v_local + T - no skeleton hierarchy, no pivot subtraction. The transforms come from the character's own action-animation streams in record[0] of the same player file: a u32 action-offset table at the record head, slot 0 the idle loop, with a [parts][frames][9-byte TRS] stream at entry +0xAC (the monster archive keeps its at +0x8C). parts = the bone count (15 Vahn / 16 Noa / 15 Gala / 17 Terra); channel i drives sorted object i, and the extras ride their attach bone's channel. Frame 0 of the idle is the combat-stance rest pose.

The two battle sources are not interchangeable: 1204's object order differs from the assembled blob's sorted order (Vahn and Gala permute head / torso / limb triples; Noa happens to coincide), so 1203 must never pose the assembled mesh and a player-file stream must never pose 1204. Posed at frame 0, a correct assembly stands with feet within a few units of y = 0 and faces +Z toward the monster seats (per-source table + measured extents).

Load-time texpage / CLUT relocation

The texpage and CLUT words on disc are an authoring layout (the one Baka Fighter renders directly). A normal battle rewrites every textured primitive into a per-party-slot band: CLUT row 481 + slot, texpages (512 + 128*slot, 256) and the page beside it - on the player-file meshes a uniform +3 texpage / +0x40 CLUT-id rewrite. Textures come from the player file itself, loaded into the band at static per-section rects (placement); the palette is rebuilt at battle load from the file's CLUT structs, one 16-colour sub-CLUT per object, with the STP bit set on every non-zero colour (the disc source is bit-15-clear). Port legaia_asset::battle_char_palette.

PROT 1204 + 1205 - the default-equipment sibling pack

The Baka Fighter minigame lets you play as Vahn / Noa / Gala, so it loads pre-assembled copies of the same characters with default equipment. PROT 1204 is a flat streaming container with five TMD2 chunks (Vahn / Noa / Gala + two extra fighters); PROT 1205 holds eight character atlases with CLUTs at rows 490..497 (the authoring palette a normal battle never uses); PROT 1203 is the matching ANM rig. Its default-section geometry is byte-shared with the player files, which is what makes it look like the battle source. The engine keeps it as the per-member fallback; play-window assembles the real thing. Chunk table in the details; parser battle_char_pack.

Details

Editing trap: the battle loader reads PROT 0874's header words as VDF pointers

PROT 0874 has a second reader with a different shape expectation (its extraction label is vdf for a reason). At every battle load the loader state machine FUN_800520F0 (state 0xc) walks the raw entry as a flat pack and registers words 1..3 - the header word 0x2CBA0, type<<24|size0, offset0 - verbatim as battle-VDF pointers. The header word is the offset of a VDF data tail past the LZS payload in the same entry.

An editor that rebuilds the container and recomputes that word, or changes §0's decoded size, points the effect system at garbage: the field walks fine and the corruption detonates at the next battle load. An in-place edit must keep the first four words byte-exact (§0's decoded size pinned at 46 236 bytes) and leave the bytes past the LZS payload untouched. legaia_asset::party_swap::fieldize implements that contract.

Field texture pack - the eight TIM entries

The upload writes (clut_x, clut_y, w*h, 1), which is why a character spans several CBA columns of one row instead of the declared CLUT rect. The row-479 NPC CLUTs are a separate, STP-set upload. Kept resident across transitions by FIELD_SHARED_BLOCKS.

Hybrid render: a third of the field prims are textured, the rest carry vertex colour

Only face, eyes, skin and parts of the clothing sample the atlas. Hair, vest and boots are untextured flat / gouraud prims whose (cba, tsb) is (0, 0), so a texture-only renderer samples empty VRAM and leaves holes. The colour block sits at the start of the prim, before the vertex indices, with length vertex_offset: flat prims carry one RGB, gouraud one per corner at a 4-byte stride. legaia_tmd::mesh::tmd_to_vram_mesh_field_hybrid returns the per-vertex shading alongside the mesh; the characters page's field form draws it that way.

Why a live VRAM dump can differ from the TIM

Each strip carries authored alternate-face frames (blink / mouth variants) below the live texel rows. Cutscene scripts stamp them over the live cell with the field-VM op 4C 60, a literal-operand MoveImage whose six u16 operands sit at arbitrary byte parity (read through FUN_8003CE9C; handler at 0x801E1B28..0x801E1B90 inside FUN_801DE840). A script that stamps once and never stamps back leaves the cell parked on the alternate frame until the band is re-uploaded. The engine queues the same stamps (World::queue_script_vram_move / apply_script_vram_moves). The actor wrap-scroll (FUN_80021DF4, installed by move-VM opcode 0x1E) scrolls water and ambient cells but never the character atlas.

Per-character animation is otherwise not in the pack: the actor tick consumes a record pointer at actor[+0x4C] installed by FUN_80024CFC, and battle actions feed a parallel struct at actor[+0x234] - see ANM.

Battle form: byte-verified provenance and party gating

On a full-party save, DAT_8007C018[0] = 0x80165E38 = the assembler's ctx+0x50 blob + 0x18 exactly; the assembled TMD reads nobj=17, bone-id bytes [0..14, 200, 201], attach array [5, 8]. With Vahn equipped Hunter Clothes / Survival Knife / Ra-Seru Meta, every one of the 17 object vertex pools byte-matches a PLAYER1 section, equipment-selectively: body only in the id 0x43 section, weapon objects only in id 0x22, the Ra-Seru extra in the Meta tiers, defaults elsewhere. The five equipped-variant objects appear nowhere in PROT 1204; the other 12 default-section objects are byte-shared with it. The full party installs 0x80165E38 / 0x8017A908 / 0x8018D550 into slots 0..2 (game_mode 0x15).

The install loop in FUN_800513F0 is gated per slot by DAT_8007bd10[i], the active-member ID (1 Vahn / 2 Noa / 3 Gala / 0 empty) - not a 0/1 flag. A Vahn-solo fight ([1,0,0,0]) installs only slot 0 there and FUN_800542C8 fills the rest (per-member loop bounded by *(rec+0x4a)). The loader FUN_800520F0 separately registers PROT 0x36a (etmd.dat) into the effect window [3..], never the party slots.

The inline record[0] streams are raw (2 + parts*frames*9 bytes); the character's art clips carry the same decoded bytes as channel-delta-coded "ME" bodies in readef.DAT - two container encodings, one pose format.

Palette port accuracy against the reference capture: Vahn byte-exact, Gala 100 %, Noa ~98 % (late-game equipment patches in the capture). Texture placement reproduces live battle VRAM at 99.7-100 % per member. The 1204 atlases match the live band only at 73-98 % (default-equipment texels).

History: readings that were wrong
  • “Battle reuses the field pack” and “battle renders PROT 1204 directly, posed from 1203” - the partial byte match was the default-section geometry both sources share; the equipped-variant objects are in no 1204 chunk, and no 1203 record is resident in battle RAM.
  • “Seven atlases inside 1204, the last truncated” - an over-reading entry size ran 1204 into 1205; there are eight whole atlases in 1205.
  • “Nominal CBA, no relocation, the palette is scene residue at rows 490..497” - those rows hold the scene environment palette; the party palette is uploaded to 481..483 by the battle loader.
  • “The per-frame patch loop adds the equipment groups” - it is a pose toggle and never changes the object count. The “+2-row scroll phase” reading of a Noa strip variant was a cutscene blink stamp.
