Player-character mesh packs Confirmed

Vahn, Noa and Gala have two bodies. Walking around a town you see the small, low-detail field form; the moment a battle starts you see the tall, high-detail battle form - wearing exactly the armour and weapon you have equipped. The field form is simple: five whole Legaia TMDs stored in one PROT entry and kept resident every field scene. The battle form is the twist: it exists on disc only as parts. At battle setup the engine assembles each character from per-equipment-slot pieces in that character's player battle file, so the exact model you fight with may exist nowhere on the disc as a single file. Parsers: legaia_asset::character_pack (field) and legaia_asset::battle_char_assembly (battle). Both forms are live on the characters page.

Field form - on-disc layout (PROT 0874 §0)

PROT 0874 is a parse_player_lzs(buf, 3)-shaped container with three LZS-compressed sections: §0 the mesh pack, §1 the party locomotion ANM bundle, §2 the textures. Section 0 decompresses to a canonical pack with five Legaia TMDs:

The “runtime body bytes” column is what the LZS-bounded decode produces: the descriptor's compressed-size hint caps the decode at ~46 KB total, so slot 4 receives only its 1 048-byte TMD prefix even though its stream would expand further into zero padding. Byte-equality verified against the live DAT_8007C018[4] allocation (world-map-overlay § disc-side source of [0..4]). The pack is shared across every field scene; only the trailing field-pack at [5..] changes per scene.

Dual consumer - the battle loader reads the header words as VDF pointers

PROT 0874 has a second, battle-side reader with a different shape expectation (its extraction label is vdf for a reason). At every battle load FUN_800520F0 state 0xc walks the raw entry as a flat pack and registers words 1..3 - the header word (0x2CBA0), type<<24|size0, offset0 - verbatim as battle-VDF pointers. The header word is the offset of a VDF data tail past the LZS payload inside the same entry. An editor that rebuilds the container and recomputes it, or changes §0's decoded size, points the effect system at garbage: the field walks fine and the corruption detonates at the next battle load. An in-place edit must keep the first four words byte-exact (pinning §0's decoded size at retail's 46 236 bytes) and leave the bytes past the LZS payload untouched - legaia_asset::party_swap::fieldize implements that contract.

Field form - 10-group cap + equipment-conditional swap

In the field, equipping a weapon changes almost nothing about the mesh - one group toggles between two pre-baked variants. FUN_8001E890 overwrites the three party TMDs' entry[+0x08] (group count) to 10 after install, so the last two disc groups are never drawn directly; they are the templates the per-frame patch loop FUN_8001EBEC picks between:

  1. Read the equipment toggle byte at the character record's per-slot offset.
  2. Non-zero: source the group-10 template at TMD+0x124. Zero: the group-11 template at TMD+0x140.
  3. Overwrite the visible group descriptor at the slot's patched group index with that 28-byte template.

They are not in extraction PROT 0876 (a VAB + empty TIM_LIST + SEQ trailer) - that entry was only searched because its filename label says player_data; the retail player_data define names extraction 0874 itself under the CDNAME −2 correction.

Hybrid render - a third of the prims are textured

A field-form character is not fully textured. Only about a third of its primitives sample the atlas (face, eyes, skin, parts of the clothing); the rest are untextured flat / gouraud prims (hair, vest, boots) carrying per-vertex RGB in the TMD. A texture-only renderer drops those (their (cba, tsb) is (0, 0), so they sample empty VRAM), leaving holes where the body should be. The colour block sits at the start of the prim, before the vertex indices, with length vertex_offset: flat prims carry one RGB, gouraud one per corner at a 4-byte stride. legaia_tmd::mesh::tmd_to_vram_mesh_field_hybrid returns the per-vertex shading alongside the mesh; the characters page's field form draws it that way.

Why a live VRAM dump can differ from the TIM

“Resident” is not “immutable”. Each strip carries authored alternate-face frames (blink / mouth variants) below the live texel rows, and cutscene scripts stamp them over the live cell with the field-VM op 4C 60 - a literal-operand MoveImage whose six u16 operands sit at arbitrary byte parity (read through FUN_8003CE9C; handler at 0x801E1B28..0x801E1B90 inside FUN_801DE840). A script that stamps once and never stamps back leaves the cell parked on the alternate frame; a later re-upload of the band restores the disc bytes. The engine queues the same stamps (World::queue_script_vram_move / apply_script_vram_moves). A second writer, the actor dispatch-4 wrap-scroll (FUN_80021DF4, installed by move-VM opcode 0x1E), scrolls texture rects for water and ambient cells but does not touch the character atlas.

History: the “+2-row scroll phase” reading of the Noa strip variant

A three-word variant at VRAM row 271 in Noa's strip was read as a parked wrap-scroll phase, then as a pause-menu upload. Both are falsified: the pause walk issues no image transfers, and a 2-row shift would move most rows of the strip. The writer is town01's opening timeline record stamping two Noa blink cells once, and the row-273 equality is frame-content coincidence. Full entry in do-not-re-walk § field and settled threads.

Field form - rest pose (PROT 0874 §1)

The field TMDs' vertices are object-local - each group models one body part about its own joint origin - so a drawn character needs a per-object rigid transform. That comes from the party locomotion ANM bundle in PROT 0874 section 1 (23 records: three 7-record character banks + savepoint + aux). Bank slot 1 is the standing idle, and frame 0 of that clip is the field rest pose. Bone i drives group i one-to-one: FUN_80024D78 fills the actor object table straight off the pool TMD and FUN_8001B964 requires the counts to match - which is why the 10-group cap matters: 12 disc groups minus 2 templates leaves exactly the 10 objects the 10-bone clips animate. Each 8-byte (bone, frame) entry decodes through FUN_8001BE80 to a flat R·v + T.

Per-character animation is otherwise not in the pack: the actor tick FUN_80021DF4 consumes a record pointer installed by FUN_80024CFC (actor[+0x4C]), and battle actions feed a parallel struct at actor[+0x234] - see ANM § per-actor anim state offsets.

Battle form - assembled from the player files

This is the part that defeats a naive “extract the model” approach. A main-game battle renders no disc TMD directly: at battle setup the engine builds each party member's mesh from that character's player battle file (data\battle\PLAYER<n>, extraction 0863..0866 - format on the player battle files page), picking one section per equipment slot by the character's equipped item ids (char record +0x196..+0x19A), and installs the merged TMD into DAT_8007C018[0..=2]. A section is not an accessory bolted on: it carries whole re-sculpted bone objects (a fist with the sword modelled into it), so equipping something swaps limbs, not attachments.

Battle pose source - where each rest pose comes from

Every battle TMD is a set of object-local pieces posed with a flat per-object transform v_world = R·v_local + T - no skeleton hierarchy and no pivot subtraction; placing a piece anywhere other than its local origin pulls the joints apart. The (T, R) come from the character's own action-animation streams in record[0] of the same player file: a u32 action-offset table at the record head, slot 0 the idle loop, with the monster-format [parts][frames][9-byte TRS] stream at entry +0xAC (the monster archive keeps its at +0x8C). parts = the skeleton bone count (15 Vahn / 16 Noa / 15 Gala / 17 Terra); channel i drives assembled object i (post-sort, object index == bone tag) and the extras ride their attach bone's channel, which is why the duplicate weapon pieces coincide with their host instead of drawing twice. Frame 0 of the idle is the combat-stance rest pose. Live-pinned: a full-party capture's anim contexts (node +0x4C, consumed by FUN_80047430 → FUN_8004AD80) point at record0 + action_table[0] + 0xAC and the stream byte-matches the disc decode; no PROT 1203 record is resident in battle RAM.

The two battle sources are not interchangeable: 1204's object order differs from the assembled blob's sorted bone-tag order per character (Vahn / Gala permute their head / torso and limb triples; Noa happens to coincide), so 1203 must never pose the assembled mesh and a player-file stream must never pose 1204. 1203 stores u8 << 4 angles where the player streams are full 12-bit, so the two idles agree only up to quantisation. The inline record[0] streams are raw (2 + parts*frames*9 bytes); the character's art clips carry the same decoded bytes as channel-delta-coded "ME" bodies in readef.DAT - two container encodings, one pose format.

Posed at frame 0, a correct assembly has a measurable shape (Y down, battle-world units): Vahn 202 × 425 × 339 with the head at −423 and feet at +3; Noa 217 × 390 × 296; Gala 208 × 503 × 340. Vertical extent dominates, feet land within a few units of y = 0, and all three face +Z toward the monster seats. Terra's file (0866) is a quadruped - long in Z by design - and has no runtime texture band, so it is not drawn as a party member. Oracle: crates/asset/tests/battle_pose_orientation_real.rs.

Provenance (byte-verified, full-party save)

DAT_8007C018[0] = 0x80165E38 = the assembler's ctx+0x50 blob + 0x18 exactly; the assembled TMD reads nobj=17, bone-id bytes [0..14, 200, 201], attach array [5, 8]. With Vahn equipped Hunter Clothes / Survival Knife / Ra-Seru Meta, every one of the 17 object vertex pools byte-matches a PLAYER1 section, equipment-selectively: body only in the id 0x43 section, weapon objects only in id 0x22, the Ra-Seru extra in the Meta tiers, defaults elsewhere. The five equipped-variant objects appear nowhere in PROT 1204; the other 12 default-section objects are byte-shared with it.

Install sites + party gating

The party-mesh install into [0..=2] is static SCUS, pinned by a DAT_8007C018[0..2] write-watchpoint across a live field→battle transition: FUN_800513F0 (lead / active actors - tmd_register(*(actor+0x50)+0x18) in a while < 3 loop right after the FUN_80052FA0 palette decode) and FUN_800542C8 (additional members - per-member loop tmd_register(*(*rec+4))). Both are dispatched indirectly as battle state-handlers, so a static 0x8007C018 cross-reference finds no writer. Installed pointers byte-match the battle form (Vahn → 0x80165F48). The captured loader FUN_800520F0 separately registers PROT 0x36a (etmd.dat) into the effect window [3..], not the party slots.

FUN_800513F0's loop is gated per slot by DAT_8007bd10[i], the active-member ID (1 Vahn / 2 Noa / 3 Gala / 0 empty) - not a 0/1 flag. A Vahn-solo fight ([1,0,0,0]) installs only slot 0 there with FUN_800542C8 filling the rest; a full party ([1,2,3,0]) installs all three through the loop (confirmed on the full-party battle saves: game_mode 0x15, DAT_8007C018[0..2] = 0x80165E38 / 0x8017A908 / 0x8018D550). Probe: scripts/pcsx-redux/autorun_battle_party_mesh_install.lua; dumps ghidra/scripts/funcs/800513f0.txt + 800542c8.txt.

Battle render - load-time TSB/CBA relocation

The texpage and CLUT words stored on disc are an authoring layout (the one the Baka Fighter minigame renders directly). A normal battle rewrites every textured primitive into a packed per-party-slot band: FUN_80053A28, called by FUN_800513F0 per slot, sets the CBA row to 0x1E1 + slot (column preserved) and the TSB texpage to 0x18 + 2*slot for authoring page 0x15, else 0x19 + 2*slot. The assembled player-file meshes all author at texpages 0x15/0x16 and CLUT row 480, so on them the pass is a uniform +3 texpage / +0x40 CLUT-id rewrite. Port: legaia_asset::battle_char_assembly::relocate_tsb_cba (dump ghidra/scripts/funcs/80053a28.txt).

Textures come from the player files themselves - the equipped sections' post-TMD pools plus the two record[0] image blocks, each LoadImaged into the band at a static per-section rect (SCUS_942.54 table 0x800775B8, banded by the party ordinal) - reproducing live battle VRAM at 99.7-100 % per member (placement). Palette is built fresh at battle load: FUN_80053B9C copies each source CLUT struct [u16 base][u16 count][BGR555] from the LZS-decoded records into a 0x1E0-stride per-character block (15 sixteen-colour sub-CLUTs, one per disc object), OR-ing the STP bit onto every non-zero colour, then DMAs it to row 481 + slot. The disc source is bit-15-clear, the runtime copy bit-15-set - the detail that made every early byte-search miss. Port legaia_asset::battle_char_palette: Vahn byte-exact, Gala 100 %, Noa ~98 % (late-game equipment patches in the reference capture).

History: palette and relocation readings that were wrong

“Nominal CBA, no relocation, the palette is scene VRAM residue at rows 490..497” is falsified - those rows hold the scene environment palette shared between field and battle, which is why they matched; the party palette is uploaded to 481..483 by the battle loader. “LZS-decompressed from the town0c scene bundle” caught the scene bundle's decode into the shared work arena, not the palette. A “sub-record decode diverges past ~0x1C00” residual was a scratch decoder ignoring FUN_8001A55C's output-byte budget. The 0x8007BEC0 texpage→row table is the scene renderer's and plays no part here. Catalogued in do-not-re-walk § battle.

PROT 1204 + 1205 - the Baka Fighter / default-equipment sibling pack

The Baka Fighter fist-fight minigame lets you play as Vahn / Noa / Gala, so it loads a pack of pre-assembled copies of the same characters with default equipment (overlay_baka_fighter loads data\field\other5.lzs + PROT 1205/1206). It is a shared battle / minigame pack whose default-section geometry is byte-shared with the player files, which is what made it look like the battle source. PROT 1204 is a flat streaming-format container (no LZS wrapper) with five chunks of asset type 0x09 (TMD2) plus a terminator, exactly its own 75 sectors (0x25800); PROT 1205 holds the eight character atlases as type-0x00 TIM chunks at 0x8224 stride; PROT 1203 is the matching ANM rig.
