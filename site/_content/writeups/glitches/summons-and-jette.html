Ra-Seru levels, Orb and Jette's Clone

Three community-reported oddities, read against the game's own code: why Meta, Terra and Ozma can arrive at Palma's level, what in the Orb summon could misbehave, and why Jette's Clone sometimes hands him a full MP bar in a different seat. Everything here comes from the disassembly and disc data of the USA release; nothing was captured on an emulator, and each section says what is proven and what is only predicted.

Reading the addresses
Addresses are runtime addresses on the USA disc (SCUS_942.54). A character's record is 0x80084708 + character * 0x414 (Vahn 0, Noa 1, Gala 2; see the save record). In battle, ctx is the context the word at 0x8007BD24 points to, and the actor table at 0x801C9370 holds eight seats: 0-2 the party, 3-6 monsters, 7 the summon creature (see battle).

Meta, Terra and Ozma inherit Palma's level

Reported as
Getting Meta, Terra or Ozma sets their level to Palma's level (community report)
Cause
The three Ra-Seru summons are inserted from the same per-character "stash slot 0" that stores Palma's level when the Earth Talisman is taken off
Where
FUN_80042558 in SCUS_942.54: three clear a2 at 0x800427AC, 0x800427CC, 0x800427E8
Affects
Meta, Terra, Ozma only; the donor is always Palma, on the same character
Status
Explained - from the disassembly; not captured live

Summons that come from equipment are not learned for good. Every time the game rebuilds a character's stats it looks at the eight equipment bytes and makes sure the matching summon is in the spell list. When a Talisman comes off, its summon is removed and its level and experience are parked in a small per-character stash, so they return when the Talisman goes back on.

Each summon has a stash slot number, written as a literal in the code. Palma uses slot 0, Mule 1, Horn 2, Jedo 3 and the Evil Talisman's summon 4. The three arms that add Meta, Terra and Ozma also pass slot 0.

So when a Ra-Seru reaches its final form and its summon is inserted, the level and experience come out of the stash that only Palma ever fills. The summon arrives with whatever Palma had on that same character, the last time the Earth Talisman was taken off them. If that never happened, the stash is still zero and the insert routine turns level 0 into level 1 - the ordinary result.

That makes the precise statement narrower than the report: the summon inherits the stashed Palma level of the same character, not the live level of a Palma currently equipped on someone.

Instruction-level walkthrough

The grant scan in FUN_80042558 loops over the eight equipment bytes (lbu v1,0x196(v0) at 0x80042718). Each arm first asks FUN_800431FC whether the spell is already in the list, and only if not falls to the shared jal 0x800432bc at 0x8004287C. The Meta arm:

80042794  jal 0x800431fc      ; delay slot: li a1,0x9e
8004279c  bne v0,zero,0x80042884
800427a4  li a1,0x9e
800427a8  j 0x8004287c        ; delay slot: clear a2   (stash slot 0)

FUN_800432BC(character, spell, slot) inserts at the head of the list, taking level and experience from the slot:

800432f4  lbu t1,0x2b5(v0)    ; level = stash byte +5
8004331c  bne t1,zero,0x80043328
80043324  addiu t1,t1,0x1     ; level 0 becomes 1
80043378  sb t3,0x13d(t0)     ; id
8004337c  sb t1,0x161(t0)     ; level
80043380  sw t2,0x8(t0)       ; experience
8004338c  sb v0,0x13c(t0)     ; count + 1

The only writer of a stash slot is FUN_80042DBC (level through lbu v0,0x161(v0) at 0x80042E64 and sb v0,0x2b5(v1) at 0x80042E6C). Its one caller is the revoke scan of the same function (jal at 0x80042978), which dispatches id - 0x99 through a five-entry jump table at 0x800151D0, bounded by sltiu v0,v1,0x5 at 0x800428BC. The table stops at 0x9D, so Meta, Terra and Ozma are never removed and never write a stash.

Two things are inference rather than proof. That nothing else touches the stash rests on a search of the dumped functions, not of every overlay byte. And that the story grant is the equipment byte changing to the final Ra-Seru form follows from this scan being the only code found that inserts these three spells; the script that upgrades the Ra-Seru is not traced.

What else follows

  • Experience is copied with the level, so the summon is also as far from its next level as the parked Palma was.
  • It is per character: a Palma levelled on Vahn does nothing for Noa's Terra.
  • It needs a Palma removal first. A Palma that is still equipped has not been parked.
  • It is one-way. Meta's progress never flows back to Palma, and Mule, Horn, Jedo and Evil Seru Magic keep their own slots.

Verifying it

  1. Equip the Earth Talisman on Vahn, raise Palma, unequip it. 0x800849BD now holds Palma's level.
  2. With Meta not yet listed, set one of Vahn's equipment bytes (0x8008489E..0x800848A5) to 0x09. After the next stat rebuild 0x80084845 reads 0x9E and 0x80084869 equals the stash byte.
  3. Control: zero 0x800849B9..0x800849BD first, and Meta arrives at level 1.
  4. A breakpoint at 0x8004287C shows a1 = 0x9E, a2 = 0.

Open: which script writes the final-form item id, and whether the Japanese and PAL executables use separate slots.

Orb's animation can glitch

Reported as
Orb's animation can glitch sometimes (community report)
Cause
Not established. The spell's module has five state-dependent spots; none is shown to produce a visible fault
Where
Orb cast module, PROT entry 0911, loaded at 0x801F69D8
Affects
Unknown - the report does not say what the glitch looks like, or whether it means the spell Orb (0x89) or the enemy Orb
Status
Not confirmed - candidates listed; a capture is needed

This section reads the spell's cast module and lists what in it depends on anything outside itself. It does not claim to have found the reported glitch.

The module spawns four parts and then sixteen light motes at random offsets around the caster, pulls the motes in over a fixed countdown, drops the Orb creature in from above, and heals the party row. Most summons let each part move under its own script. Orb is different: the module itself repositions the sixteen motes every pass, through sixteen pointers it saved when it spawned them.

The candidates

  1. Unchecked failed spawnsThe pool spawn FUN_80050ED4 returns 0 when its 96-word pool at 0x801C90F0 or the actor allocator is full. Orb stores the result without a test (0x801F6EC0, 0x801F7064) and later writes through all sixteen. A failed mote would be missing, and the writes would land at addresses 0x14, 0x16, 0x18. Whether the pools can be that full during a cast is not established.
  2. Random by designEach pair of motes rolls four random numbers for its start offset (0x801F6CDC, 0x801F6D08, 0x801F6D38, 0x801F6D70), so no two casts look alike. Variation, not a defect - but possibly what was seen.
  3. Stale mote pointersWould matter if a mote retired early. Its script at 0x801F8194 waits 0xFFF frames before halting, far longer than the pull-in, and the pointers are unused afterwards. Reads as safe.
  4. The pool flush overshootsFUN_80050E74 clears 0x80 words where the allocator fills only 0x60, so any non-zero word in 0x801C9270..0x801C92EF is treated as an actor and halted. What lives there is not identified, and every module that flushes shares this.
  5. Leftover scratchThe countdown (0x801F8558) and both tables sit in the module image, zero on disc, and are written before they are read. Reads as safe.
Module walkthrough

The tick is FUN_801F69D8; its phase byte is ctx[+0x279].

  • Phase 2 (0x801F6BB8): four parts through FUN_80021B04 (records 0x801F804C, 0x801F8098, 0x801F80E4, 0x801F8130), then sixteen motes (sltiu v0,s2,0x10 at 0x801F7068) on record 0x801F8190. X is rand % 511 + 0x100 with a random sign, Y is -(rand % 511), Z is rand % 255 + 0x300. Offsets go to 0x801F8560 + i * 8, pointers to 0x801F85E0 + i * 4.
  • Phase 3 (0x801F707C): loads each pointer (lw a0,0x0(a3) at 0x801F7210) and writes the position (+0x16 at 0x801F7230, +0x18 at 0x801F72CC, +0x14 at 0x801F7368), then flushes the pool and places the creature in seat 7.
  • Phase 4 lowers the creature; phase 5 jumps to 9 (sb s7,0x279(s5) at 0x801F7780), which flushes again and heals.
  • The same unchecked shape recurs with the object pointer at ctx[+0x102C]: stored at 0x801F76B0, dereferenced at 0x801F78D0..0x801F78E4.

The capture that would settle it

First, a description of the symptom: missing sparkles, a mis-posed creature, a stall - and which Orb. Then, with the spell loaded in a debugger:

  • Break at 0x801F6EC0 and 0x801F7064 on v0 == 0. A hit means a mote failed to spawn.
  • Write-watch 0x00000014..0x00000019 during the cast.
  • At entry to 0x80050E74, look for non-zero words in 0x801C9270..0x801C92EF.

A cast where one of these fires, recorded with what the screen shows, tells which candidate - if any - is the glitch.

Jette's Clone refills his MP and moves him

Reported as
Jette restores full MP when using Clone, and the real Jette moves to a new slot (community report)
Cause
Clone builds a brand-new monster from Jette's record, copies only his current HP onto it, then a coin flip decides which of the two becomes the 1 HP decoy
Where
FUN_801F78B8 in PROT entry 0940; coin at 0x801F8134..0x801F8140
Affects
Jette only for Clone; the Divide spell shares the "fresh copy" half
Status
Explained - from the disassembly; not captured live

The two observations are one event. Clone does not copy Jette. It builds a new monster in the next free seat from Jette's monster record, exactly as the game does when a battle loads: full HP, full MP, fresh stats. Then it overwrites only the newcomer's current HP with the caster's current HP.

Then a coin flip picks which of the two becomes the decoy, and the loser is set to HP 1, MP 0, attack 1 and a small action gauge.

So the MP refill happens exactly when, and because, the real Jette changes seat. It is not a separate restore, and it does not happen on the even roll.

On the "real" Jette after an odd rollResult
MP (+0x150)Record maximum, 1000 - a new actor, not a restore
HP (+0x14C)Not reset: copied from the caster at 0x801F809C. Maximum HP is the record's 34567
Ailments (+0x16E)Empty; they stay on the old seat, now the decoy
Attack, defences, intelligence, speed, action gaugeRecord values plus the usual battle-load boost; changes made during the fight stay on the decoy
SeatSeat 3 to seat 4 on the first Clone, to seat 5 on the second

When Jette picks Clone

The choice is made in Jette's arm of the monster AI, FUN_801E9FD4 in the battle overlay (PROT entry 0898), at 0x801EB894..0x801EB90C. All four must hold:

  1. It is JetteMonster id byte equals 0xAA (0x801EB8A0).
  2. Fewer than three monstersctx[+1] is not 3 (0x801EB8B8) - so at most two Clones per fight.
  3. One in threerand() % 3 == 0 (0x801EB8C4..0x801EB8EC).
  4. MP is not zero+0x150 != 0 (0x801EB8F4..0x801EB8FC) - so a decoy, whose MP is 0, can never Clone.
Instruction-level walkthrough of the cast

Clone (0xAE) and Divide (0x50) share FUN_801F78B8. s2 is the caster.

801f7e08  lbu v0,0x1(v1)      ; s3 = ctx[+1], old monster count = new seat index
801f7e1c  sb v0,0x1(v1)       ; ctx[+1] + 1
801f7e3c  sb v0,0x0(v1)       ; monster id copied to the new seat
801f7e70  sw v0,0x0(a2)       ; record pointer copied to the new seat
801f7f18  jal 0x80054cb0      ; full monster init of the new seat

FUN_80054CB0 is the normal battle-load initialiser. Its copy block 0x8005516C..0x8005520C fills HP (+0x14C / +0x14E / +0x172), MP (+0x150 / +0x152 / +0x174) and the other stats from the record. It does not write the ailment word +0x16E. The only caster-to-newcomer copy is HP:

801f8090  lhu v0,0x14c(s2)    ; caster's current HP
801f8098  sh v0,0x172(v1)     ; newcomer HP mirror
801f809c  sh v0,0x14c(v1)     ; newcomer current HP

The Clone-only tail (li v0,0xae / bne at 0x801F80F0..0x801F80F4) names the new seat from the string at 0x801F8644, then flips the coin:

801f8134  jal 0x80056798      ; rand
801f813c  andi v0,v0,0x1
801f8140  beq v0,zero,0x801f8164
801f8148  sh v0,0x14c(s2)     ; odd: caster HP = 1
801f814c  sh v0,0x158(s2)     ;      caster attack = 1
801f8154  sh zero,0x150(s2)   ;      caster MP = 0
801f8158  sh v0,0x156(s2)     ;      caster action gauge = 0x20
801f8160  sh v0,0x154(s2)

The even branch at 0x801F8164 makes the same five stores on the newcomer (0x801F816C, 0x801F8178, 0x801F8184, 0x801F8190, 0x801F819C).

The new seat starts clean because battle init FUN_80055B6C zero-fills the whole actor arena (0x80055F14..0x80055F24) and seats are only ever appended. That nothing else dirtied the unused record earlier in the fight is inference.

What else follows

  • Divide uses the same routine, so every Divide copy is also a fresh actor with record MP and stats carrying the caster's current HP. Divide has no coin and no weakening.
  • Clone has exactly one user: no monster record lists 0x50 or 0xAE among its magic-attack bytes; both are chosen by AI code.
  • Each Clone has an even chance of refilling MP and shedding stat changes and ailments. Over the two possible casts, the chance of at least one refill is three in four.

Verifying it

  • In the Jette fight, break at 0x801F8134 and step to 0x801F8140: v0 = 0 makes the new seat the decoy, v0 = 1 the caster.
  • For seats 3 and 4, read the actor pointer p from 0x801C9370 + 4 * seat, then p + 0x14C (HP), p + 0x150 (MP), p + 0x158 (attack), p + 0x16E (ailments), before and after. Predicted odd result: seat 3 reads 1 / 0 / 1; seat 4 reads the old HP, 0x03E8, the boosted record attack, 0.
  • Replacing the andi v0,v0,0x1 at 0x801F813C with a constant forces either outcome.
  • With Jette's MP at 0, the gate at 0x801EB8FC never selects Clone.

Open: whether any targeting or display state still points at the old seat after an odd roll; whether the reset is an intended part of the shell game or a side effect of reusing the monster initialiser (the code reads as the latter, but intent is inference); and the Japanese and PAL releases, which are not examined.
