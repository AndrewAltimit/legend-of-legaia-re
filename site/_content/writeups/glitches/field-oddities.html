WORK ERR, missing encounters and script slips

Four community-reported oddities that live in the field and battle runtime rather than in one attack or item: a debug counter that reaches the screen on Mt. Rikuroa, the random battles that stop in Jette's Absolute Fortress, the camera orbit that ends the Gaza rematch, and three small slips in the English script. Each is explained from the game's own code and data on the USA disc, with the addresses to check.

How this page was made
Everything here is static analysis: disassembly of SCUS_942.54 and its overlays, and data decoded from the disc. Nothing on this page was captured on an emulator. Where a statement is a prediction rather than something read from the instructions, the text says so, and each section ends with the debugger check that would confirm it. PROT numbers are extraction indices.

WORK ERR on Mt. Rikuroa

Reported as
The debug text “WORK ERR2” appears on screen on Mt. Rikuroa (community report)
Cause
The scene asked for more actors than the fixed 143-slot pool holds; the number is how many requests have failed
Where
SCUS_942.54: print in FUN_80016444, counter 0x8007B8A0, spawn FUN_80020DE0
Affects
Any scene, minigame or battle stage that runs the pool dry
Status
Partially explained - the message and its counter are proven; which Mt. Rikuroa event overflows the pool is a prediction

Everything that moves in a scene - the player, each townsperson, each door and prop, each part of a visual effect, each attached light, each running cutscene script - is an actor, and all of them come out of one fixed pool of 143 slots. When something asks for a slot and the pool is empty, the request fails, an error counter goes up by one, and from then on the frame loop prints WORK ERR followed by that counter on every frame.

So “WORK ERR2” is not error number two. It says that two actor requests have failed since the current stage was set up. There is no separate “WORK ERR1” message: the digit is a %d.

Mt. Rikuroa starts with 93 of the 143 slots already taken, a heavy load but not an overflowing one. The remaining 50 have to be used up by something that happens during play.

The chain from text to cause

  1. The printThe frame driver FUN_80016444 loads the signed 16-bit counter at 0x8007B8A0 (lh a1,0x588(gp) at 0x800164B0) and, when it is non-zero, calls the dev text formatter FUN_8001A068 with the string at 0x800100F4. Unlike the two prints above it, it is not behind the development switch gp+0x3B8.
  2. The counterCleared by stage init FUN_8001E1B4 at 0x8001E304; incremented in exactly one place, 0x80020E0C..0x80020E1C.
  3. The failing spawnThat place is the failure arm of the actor spawn FUN_80020DE0: it calls the pop FUN_80020454, and when the pop returns null it bumps the counter, logs to the dev console and returns 0.
  4. The poolFUN_800203EC seeds a free stack of 143 slot pointers, 0x8007C5AC + i * 0xD8. The pop refuses when the top index at 0x8007C348 is negative. Stage init takes 7 slots as list sentinels, leaving 136.

What Mt. Rikuroa asks of the pool

The scene is rikuroa: its field map is PROT entry 153 and its story-state script container is the variant MAN in PROT entry 157 (13 / 29 / 64 records in its three partitions).

Only six of the disc's field maps bind more objects than Mt. Rikuroa's 54. The 50 slots left over serve party followers, one actor per running cutscene record (the scene carries 64 such records), one per attached light (20 script sites) and the effect trees its scripts install (23 sites, each able to fan out into several actors). The scene's two longest cutscene records both install effects, and an overflow during one of them is the likely cause. That last step is inference: static analysis cannot say which request was the 144th.

A failed spawn returns null. Some callers test for that and some write through the result unchecked, so beyond the text an overflow can also cost a prop or an effect part. That is not traced per caller.

Verifying it

Put a write breakpoint on the two bytes at 0x8007B8A0 and play through Mt. Rikuroa. A hit from 0x80020E1C is a failed spawn: the return address names the caller, and the first argument on entry to FUN_80020DE0 is the template of the actor that did not get a slot. The word at 0x8007C348 shows the load directly: 0x8E is an empty pool, -1 is exhaustion.

No encounters in Jette's Absolute Fortress

Reported as
Sometimes no random battles happen in Jette's Absolute Fortress (community report, repeated but unconfirmed)
Cause
Story-gated encounter tables: one flag switches the Fortress off after Jette, and much of the interior is zero-rate even before that
Where
Roll FUN_801D9E1C in PROT entry 0897; tables in the scene MANs of PROT entries 606 and 706
Affects
Both Fortress scenes; two of the smaller contributors affect every dungeon
Status
Partially explained - three mechanisms are in the data; which one a given report met cannot be told without the save

Random battles in a dungeon are not a flat chance per step. The map is divided into rectangles, each with its own rate, and the rectangles are grouped by story state: the game picks one group according to which story flags are set, and only that group counts. Inside the group the first rectangle that contains the player wins. A rate of zero means that stepping there never moves the encounter counter.

The Fortress is two scenes, the entrance (deroa) and the transport-system interior (chitei2). Their tables hold three ways to walk without a battle:

  • The collapse flag, 0x521. Set by the scene that follows Jette's defeat, never cleared by anything on the disc. Both scenes then select an all-zero group, for the escape and for good. This is the designers switching the dungeon off.
  • Silent boxes in front of the rooms. In the interior's default table, zero-rate boxes are listed ahead of boxes that roll and partly cover them. About 46% of the interior grid by area never counts a step.
  • A borrowed flag, 0x15. It heads both Fortress tables but is only ever set and cleared inside Zora's Floating Castle. If a save left the castle with it set, the Fortress would be silent from the first step. No way of doing that was found; this one is a prediction about what the data permits.

How the roll reads them, step by step
  1. A step is a change of the player's 128-unit tile by at most one on each axis (0x801D9ED4..0x801D9F0C).
  2. The condition list is walked in order (0x801D9F30..0x801D9FD8): the first record whose flag is 0xFFFF or set wins.
  3. The first region of that group whose box contains the tile wins (0x801D9FE8..0x801DA054).
  4. The step does nothing while the player's engaged bit is up, while 0x8007B6B4 is non-zero, while the warp timer 0x8007B6B0 is positive, or while the Incense window 0x8007B600 is non-zero (0x801DA130..0x801DA180).
  5. The rate is scaled by the setting byte 0x8007B5F8, the High and Low Encounter accessories, and system flags 0x1D (doubled) and 0x1E (halved) (0x801DA198..0x801DA204).
  6. The result is subtracted from the counter 0x8007B5FC; at zero or below a battle starts (bgtz at 0x801DA218) and the counter is reset to between 488 and 1460.

Flag 0x521 is written by partition-2 records 15 and 19 of the interior's MAN (each opens with 55 21). Flag 0x15 is set by partition-2 record 12 of the Floating Castle's variant MAN in PROT entry 373 (50 15) and cleared as the last flag op of record 29 (60 15).

Smaller contributors

  • The strange water in Rogue's Tower. Two drinkable props there set system flags 0x1D (rate doubled) and 0x1E (rate halved), and nothing on the disc clears either. With the halving flag and a Low Encounter accessory, rate 18 becomes 4, and the counter lasts 122 to 365 steps.
  • A top-up on every scene entry. When a scene loads with the counter below 487, FUN_8003AB2C adds half of a fresh roll (0x8003AC7C..0x8003AC90). Moving between the two Fortress scenes keeps refilling a low counter.
  • Incense suspends the roll outright while its window is open.

Checked and not found: a counter that wraps (it is a signed word tested with bgtz), and a script op in either scene that turns encounters off without a matching op that turns them back on.

Verifying it

In the Fortress, read the flag bytes above, the Incense window and the counter. Then break at 0x801DA208: register s0 holds the final per-step rate and s2 points at the region record that won. If that breakpoint never fires while the player walks, either a gate is holding the step or the winning region has rate 0; the branch at 0x801D9FC8 shows which group was selected. A save made after Zora's Floating Castle with flag 0x15 still set would confirm the third mechanism.

Gaza 2 softlock

Reported as
In the Gaza rematch the battle stops and the camera circles the party forever (community report)
Cause
A monster walking to its target is carried only by its Move animation; a leftover flinch flag swaps that animation for idle and the wait never ends
Where
Battle action state machine FUN_801E295C, PROT entry 0898, state 0x19
Affects
Nearly every monster, bosses included, when a melee directly follows a flinch
Status
Explained - traced in full on its own write-up; the key addresses re-read here

This one has a full write-up of its own, with the evidence behind it and the patch. In short: a monster that starts a melee attack out of reach has to walk to its target. Almost no monster has a dedicated approach animation, so the game plays the ordinary Move loop and waits in a state that only re-measures the distance. That state has no movement code and no timeout.

If the monster is still flinching from a hit when its attack begins - a summon is the usual cause - a leftover “go back to idle when this clip ends” flag is still set. The Move loop plays one cycle, the flag swaps it for the idle pose, the monster stops short, and the wait never ends. The camera's idle sweep runs on its own, so the screen keeps orbiting.

  1. FlinchThe hit-reaction stager in FUN_800402F4 queues the flinch clip and raises bit 2 of actor+0x1DC (ori v0,v0,0x4 at 0x80042140).
  2. Attack setup, state 0x14Out of reach, no tag-0x20 walk action (jal 0x80050e2c at 0x801E3268, miss tested at 0x801E327C): the Move clip is staged at 0x801E32B0 and state 0x19 stored at 0x801E32D0.
  3. Mid-clip commitThe Move clip is installed by the path that clears bits 0..1 only (andi v0,v0,0xfc at 0x80047A38). Bit 2 survives.
  4. First natural endFUN_80047430 tests bit 2 (0x80047B38) and queues idle over the Move clip (sb zero,0x1da(s2) at 0x80047B44).
  5. ParkedState 0x19 re-checks the range every frame at 0x801E3594 and falls to the stall label 0x801E35D0, forever.

Verifying it

In a stalled battle the context state byte (*0x8007BD24 + 0x07) reads 0x19, the attacker's +0x1D9 and +0x1DA are both zero and its position does not change. To provoke it, set bit 2 of a monster's +0x1DC while it is in state 0x14. The fix is the Approach-softlock fix toggle in the ROM patcher.

Dialogue inconsistencies

Reported as
“Van Saryu” against “Bon Saryu”, “Moustache” against “Whiskers”, and the bath scene in Usha's Research Center (community report)
Cause

Where

The bath scene in Usha's Research Center

The scene is partition-2 record 4 of doman (the variant MAN in PROT entry 401), at MAN offset 0x923E. It plays once: its header blocks it when system flag 0x3FB is set, and the record ends by setting that flag (53 FB at 0x9BCB). Three things in it do not line up.

  • The choice box answers for the researcher. After Noa's question a two-option picker (27 32 00 D2 00 at 0x97BB) offers two excuses. Everything else on that side of the conversation is the researcher's - the surrounding ops drive his channel, 0x25, and he gives both excuses again himself at 0x9AD3..0x9B2D - while the game's convention is that a choice box holds the silent hero's replies. The structure is read from the disc; who is meant to be speaking is inference.
  • The researcher has no name label. His lines carry no speaker prefix while Noa's and Gala's all do, so the exchange reads as if Noa were answering herself.
  • The words for the place disagree. Noa says hot spring (four lines, 0x9332 to 0x9526); Gala and the researcher say bath; and Gala's line at 0x9487 calls the place floating, although the scene's own later lines have the Research Center reached by a wound elevator. Whether that is an error or characterisation is a judgement.

The record has no branch on who is in the party. Its only branch is the choice box, and both arms rejoin at script offset +0x0711.
