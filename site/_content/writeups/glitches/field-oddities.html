WORK ERR, missing encounters and script slips

Four community-reported oddities that live in the field and battle runtime rather than in one attack or item: a debug counter that reaches the screen on Mt. Rikuroa, the random battles that stop in Jette's Absolute Fortress, the camera orbit that ends the Gaza rematch, and three small slips in the English script. Each is explained from the game's own code and data, with the addresses to check.

How this page was made
The base of every section is static analysis of the USA disc: disassembly of SCUS_942.54 and its overlays, and data decoded from the disc. Two sections also rest on emulator captures, and each says which kind. The Mt. Rikuroa capture is on a poked state: a retail cold boot from a memory-card save, with story flags cleared and the player teleported. The Fortress captures are one played memory-card save and one edited copy of it. The bath scene is read against the Japanese disc (SCPS_100.59) as well. Where a step is inferred rather than read or observed, the text says so. PROT numbers are extraction indices; P1[n] and P2[n] are records of a scene's script partitions 1 and 2.

WORK ERR on Mt. Rikuroa

Reported as
The debug text “WORK ERR2” appears in the top-left corner on Mt. Rikuroa right after the Golem fight, as the cave collapses, and stays (community reports)
Cause
The rock and dust burst after the Golem fight fills the fixed 143-slot actor pool; the number is how many requests arrived while it was full
Where
SCUS_942.54: print in FUN_80016444, counter 0x8007B8A0, spawn FUN_80020DE0, effect stager FUN_80021B04
Affects
Any scene, minigame or battle stage that runs the pool dry
Status
Partially explained - the message is proven and the event that fills the pool is captured; the failed request itself was never caught

Everything that moves in a scene - the player, each townsperson, each door and prop, each rock and dust puff of a visual effect, each open dialogue box - is an actor, and all of them come out of one fixed pool of 143 slots. When something asks for a slot and the pool is empty, the request fails, an error counter goes up by one, and from then on the frame loop prints WORK ERR followed by that counter on every frame.

So “WORK ERR2” is not error number two. It says that two actor requests have failed since the current stage was set up. There is no separate “WORK ERR1” message: the digit is a %d.

Mt. Rikuroa uses 97 of the 143 slots just standing still. When the Golem is beaten the game does not rebuild the scene: it restores the mountain exactly as it was before the fight, every actor included, and at once plays the cave collapse, which throws up to 40 rocks and dust puffs at the same moment a dialogue box opens. Replayed from a memory-card save, that moment leaves between four slots and no slots to spare. One or two more actors alive on that frame and their requests fail: the counter reads 1 or 2 and stays on screen until the next screen change. Nothing else breaks; a rock or two never appears.

The chain from text to cause

  1. The printThe frame driver FUN_80016444 loads the signed 16-bit counter at 0x8007B8A0 (lh a1,0x588(gp) at 0x800164B0) and, when it is non-zero, calls the dev text formatter FUN_8001A068 with the string at 0x800100F4. Unlike the two prints above it, it is not behind the development switch gp+0x3B8.
  2. The counterCleared by stage init FUN_8001E1B4 at 0x8001E304; incremented in exactly one place, 0x80020E0C..0x80020E1C.
  3. The failing spawnThat place is the failure arm of the actor spawn FUN_80020DE0: it calls the pop FUN_80020454, and when the pop returns null it bumps the counter, logs to the dev console and returns 0.
  4. The poolFUN_800203EC seeds a free stack of 143 slot pointers, 0x8007C5AC + i * 0xD8. The pop refuses when the top index at 0x8007C348 is negative; free slots = top index + 1. Stage init takes 7 slots as list sentinels, leaving 136.
  5. The battle return keeps the poolStage init always clears the counter, but skips the reseed and the seven pops when the field resumes from a battle: resume word 0x8007B8B8 == 2 and next mode 2 (bne at 0x8001E308, beq at 0x8001E31C to 0x8001E36C). The pool block is parked on the way into the fight and copied back, so every actor alive before the fight is alive after it.
  6. The collapseThe Golem record P2[47] is respawned by the system script when the field returns, and its post-battle arm installs two effect trees (34 30 0D, 34 30 07). Their parts are spawned by FUN_80021B04 (jal 0x80020de0 at 0x80021B78), two to seven per frame at the peak.

What the pool holds, measured

The scene is rikuroa: its field map is PROT entry 153, its walk-on triggers PROT entry 154 and its story-state script container the variant MAN in PROT entry 157 (13 / 29 / 64 records in its three partitions). The Golem record P2[47] sits on tiles (69,97) to (69,99): it sets flag 0x285 and starts a scripted battle against formation row 16 (3E FF 10).

The stager tests the result (bnez s0 at 0x80021B84), so a failed part is simply a rock or dust puff that is not drawn.

The number on screen is then a derivation, and it is inference: the counter goes up once per request made while the stack is empty, the stager asks for two to seven slots a frame at the peak, and parts free themselves shortly after. A run one or two actors heavier than the replay fails one or two requests in a single frame and recovers. What supplies those last actors in a player's run is not identified. Three candidates are tested and ruled out on the same set-up: finished cutscene records do not accumulate, ordinary random battles do not leak slots, and nothing survives a real scene change.

Verifying it

Put an execute breakpoint on 0x80020E0C and play the climb from the mountain entrance through the Golem, from a save made before the Golem tile (flag 0x284 clear). A hit is a failed spawn: the caller's return address is at sp+0x38 and s1 is the template of the actor that did not get a slot. The word at 0x8007C348, logged every frame, shows the load directly: 0x8E is an empty pool, -1 is exhaustion. That played, unpoked run is the one measurement still missing.

No encounters in Jette's Absolute Fortress

Reported as
In some files Jette's Absolute Fortress has no random battles at all, before Jette is fought; it survives a reset and a memory-card load, and Incense and the encounter accessories make no difference (community reports, one first-hand: Reddit, r/LegendofLegaia, “Jette's fortress no random encounter”)
Cause
Escaping Zora's Floating Castle with under a minute on the clock leaves its “no battles” flag set for good, and the Fortress's encounter tables still obey it
Where
Clock actor 0x801D2EBC and roll FUN_801D9E1C in PROT entry 0897; scripts in PROT entry 373; tables in PROT entries 606 and 706
Affects
Both Fortress scenes, in any file that had less than 1:00 left during a successful castle escape
Status
Explained - a real defect; the flag write and the silent Fortress are captured on an edited save, and the castle escape itself was not played to its last minute

The cause is in Zora's Floating Castle, hours earlier. When the castle is falling the game gives six minutes to get out, and it switches random battles off for the last minute by raising a story flag. The record that ends a successful escape stops the clock but never lowers the flag, so a file that reached the exit with less than a minute showing keeps it for good.

In the castle that no longer matters. But the Fortress's two encounter tables were built from the castle's and still begin with the same “this flag means no battles” group. In that file the whole Fortress is silent, and the Door of Light is greyed out there as well. No item, accessory or reset changes it, because the flag is saved with the file. A file that escaped with more than a minute to spare is unaffected.

Check and fix

How
Check, no toolsIn the Fortress, open Items: the Door of Light is greyed out. In a healthy file it is usable there

Avoidreach the Floating Castle's exit with more than 1:00 on the clock

The clock is not gentle: the scene loader takes 20 seconds off a running clock on every scene entry, and the castle is two scenes, so each crossing between them costs 20 seconds on top of the walking.

The cause, step by step

  1. The countdown opField-script op 4C D3 arms a countdown kept in the saved game state: two flag ids, a duration and a threshold (handler 0x801E2BDC..0x801E2C38 in PROT entry 0897), and spawns a clock actor.
  2. The clock actor0x801D2EBC takes the play-clock delta off the remaining time each tick. On expiry it sets the first flag (0x801D2F60); whenever the remaining time is below the threshold it sets the second (0x801D2F84). The flag ids come from RAM, not from constants in the code.
  3. The castle arms ittaiku P2[29] (PROT entry 373), the Mist Generator scene, clears flag 0x15 (60 15 at +0x1508) and then arms the clock at +0x1533: expiry flag 0x396, warning flag 0x15, 21600 ticks (6:00), threshold 3600 ticks (1:00). The castle's encounter table opens with a flag-0x15 group that is one whole-map rate-0 row: the last minute has no battles by design.
  4. Nothing clears itThe success record taiku P2[16] issues the same op with duration 0, which stops the clock, and never issues 60 15. The whole disc has one script clear of flag 0x15 - the one that runs before the clock is armed - and no native call clears it by constant id.
  5. The Fortress reads itFlag 0x15 heads the condition list of exactly three scenes: the second castle scene, and the two Fortress scenes deroa and chitei2. Their first row is the castle's row carried over: whole map, rate 0, both Doors blocked.

How the roll reads them, step by step
  1. A step is a change of the player's 128-unit tile by at most one on each axis (0x801D9ED4..0x801D9F0C).
  2. The condition list is walked in order (0x801D9F30..0x801D9FD8): the first record whose flag is 0xFFFF or set wins.
  3. The first region of that group whose box contains the tile wins (0x801D9FE8..0x801DA054).
  4. The step does nothing while the player's engaged bit is up, while 0x8007B6B4 is non-zero, while the warp timer 0x8007B6B0 is positive, or while the Incense window 0x8007B600 is non-zero (0x801DA130..0x801DA180). The roll is not reached at all while a menu or scripted submode is open (bit 0x8000 of 0x1F800394, raised at 0x801F1360, dropped at 0x801F1684).
  5. The rate is scaled by the setting byte 0x8007B5F8, the High and Low Encounter accessories, and system flags 0x1D (doubled) and 0x1E (halved) (0x801DA198..0x801DA204).
  6. The result is subtracted from the counter 0x8007B5FC; at zero or below a battle starts (bgtz at 0x801DA218) and the counter is reset to between 488 and 1460. The counter is not saved: after power-on it holds 974, so the first battle after a cold load in a rate-18 room comes on step 55.

Things that thin battles without stopping them: the two drinkable-water props in Rogue's Tower set system flags 0x1D (rate doubled) and 0x1E (rate halved) and nothing clears either, so with a Low Encounter accessory rate 18 can become 4; a scene load with the counter below 487 adds half of a fresh roll (0x8003AC7C..0x8003AC90); Incense suspends the roll while its window is open. None of them produces a silent Fortress.

Verifying it

Gaza 2 softlock

Reported as
In the Gaza rematch the battle stops and the camera circles the party forever (community report)
Cause
A monster walking to its target is carried only by its Move animation; a leftover flinch flag swaps that animation for idle and the wait never ends
Where
Battle action state machine FUN_801E295C, PROT entry 0898, state 0x19
Affects
Nearly every monster, bosses included, when a melee directly follows a flinch
Status
Explained - traced in full on its own write-up; the key addresses re-read here

This one has a full write-up of its own, with the evidence behind it and the patch. In short: a monster that starts a melee attack out of reach has to walk to its target. Almost no monster has a dedicated approach animation, so the game plays the ordinary Move loop and waits in a state that only re-measures the distance. That state has no movement code and no timeout.

If the monster is still flinching from a hit when its attack begins - a summon is the usual cause - a leftover “go back to idle when this clip ends” flag is still set. The Move loop plays one cycle, the flag swaps it for the idle pose, the monster stops short, and the wait never ends. The camera's idle sweep runs on its own, so the screen keeps orbiting.

  1. FlinchThe hit-reaction stager in FUN_800402F4 queues the flinch clip and raises bit 2 of actor+0x1DC (ori v0,v0,0x4 at 0x80042140).
  2. Attack setup, state 0x14Out of reach, no tag-0x20 walk action (jal 0x80050e2c at 0x801E3268, miss tested at 0x801E327C): the Move clip is staged at 0x801E32B0 and state 0x19 stored at 0x801E32D0.
  3. Mid-clip commitThe Move clip is installed by the path that clears bits 0..1 only (andi v0,v0,0xfc at 0x80047A38). Bit 2 survives.
  4. First natural endFUN_80047430 tests bit 2 (0x80047B38) and queues idle over the Move clip (sb zero,0x1da(s2) at 0x80047B44).
  5. ParkedState 0x19 re-checks the range every frame at 0x801E3594 and falls to the stall label 0x801E35D0, forever.

Verifying it

In a stalled battle the context state byte (*0x8007BD24 + 0x07) reads 0x19, the attacker's +0x1D9 and +0x1DA are both zero and its position does not change. To provoke it, set bit 2 of a monster's +0x1DC while it is in state 0x14. The fix is the Approach-softlock fix toggle in the ROM patcher.

The bath scene in Usha's Research Center

A researcher refuses to share the bath with Noa. Noa turns to Vahn and, in the Japanese script, asks him whether “this person” dislikes her; the player picks Vahn's answer, either that the man is embarrassed or that the bath is too small. The English changed her question to “You don't like me?” and the first answer to “I'm too shy”, so it reads as if Vahn were the one refusing. The rest of the scene is translated straight, which is why Noa's next line - said while turning back to the researcher - and Gala's scolding no longer fit. The line should be “He's too shy”.

The scene is P2[4] of doman (the variant MAN in PROT entry 401): MAN offset 0x923E on the USA disc, 0x9BC7 in the same entry of the Japanese disc (SCPS_100.59). It plays once: its header blocks it when system flag 0x3FB is set, and the record ends by setting that flag (53 FB at 0x9BCB). The structure is the same on both discs: the same nineteen face-turn ops in the same order, one choice box, the same rejoin, and no branch on who is in the party.

At the choice Vahn is the speaker and Noa the listener, as in every choice box; the two face-turn ops just before the question are the evidence of who is addressed. Read from the English alone, it is tempting to conclude that the choice box speaks for the researcher. It does not: the box is in the same place and belongs to the player on both discs, and only the pronouns are wrong. Reading the subjectless Japanese line as “he” is inference, but it is forced by the question it answers and by Noa's reply.

Three other things in the English scene look odd and are not errors, because the Japanese script has them too: the researcher's lines carry no name tag; Noa calls the place a hot spring where Gala and the researcher call it a bath; and Gala's “floating” place renders a Japanese phrase for a building up in the air.
