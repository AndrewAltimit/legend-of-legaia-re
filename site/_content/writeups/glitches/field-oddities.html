WORK ERR, missing encounters and script slips

Five community-reported oddities that live in the field and battle runtime rather than in one attack or item: a debug counter that reaches the screen on Mt. Rikuroa, the random battles that stop in Jette's Absolute Fortress, the freeze after a Spikefish Flute escape, the camera orbit that ends the Gaza rematch, and three small slips in the English script. One section is a mechanic rather than a fault: the two springs in Rogue's Tower that change the encounter rate. One is a freeze that needs an emulator to trigger it: the heap running out in Conkram's mirror scene when the emulated CPU is too fast. Each is explained from the game's own code and data, with the addresses to check.

How this page was made
The base of every section is static analysis of the USA disc: disassembly of SCUS_942.54 and its overlays, and data decoded from the disc. Several sections also rest on emulator captures, and each says which kind. The Mt. Rikuroa capture is on a poked state: a retail cold boot from a memory-card save, with story flags cleared and the player teleported. The Fortress captures are one played memory-card save and one edited copy of it. The bath scene is read against the Japanese disc (SCPS_100.59) as well. Where a step is inferred rather than read or observed, the text says so. PROT numbers are extraction indices; P1[n] and P2[n] are records of a scene's script partitions 1 and 2.

WORK ERR on Mt. Rikuroa

Reported as
The debug text “WORK ERR2” appears in the top-left corner on Mt. Rikuroa right after the Golem fight, as the cave collapses, and stays (community reports; one recorded playthrough shows it, and two viewers of that recording report the same text at the same place). Also reported on a console and in DuckStation, both relayed rather than recorded, so it is not one emulator's artefact
Cause
The rock and dust burst after the Golem fight fills the fixed 143-slot actor pool; the number is how many requests arrived while it was full
Where
SCUS_942.54: print in FUN_80016444, counter 0x8007B8A0, spawn FUN_80020DE0, effect stager FUN_80021B04
Affects
Any scene, minigame or battle stage that runs the pool dry
Status
Partially explained - the message is proven and the event that fills the pool is captured; the failed request itself was never caught, and a played run is several actors heavier than the replay for a reason not yet measured

Everything that moves in a scene - the player, each townsperson, each door and prop, each rock and dust puff of a visual effect, each open dialogue box - is an actor, and all of them come out of one fixed pool of 143 slots. When something asks for a slot and the pool is empty, the request fails, an error counter goes up by one, and from then on the frame loop prints WORK ERR followed by that counter on every frame.

So “WORK ERR2” is not error number two. It says that two actor requests have failed since the current stage was set up. There is no separate “WORK ERR1” message: the digit is a %d.

Mt. Rikuroa uses 97 of the 143 slots just standing still. When the Golem is beaten the game does not rebuild the scene: it restores the mountain exactly as it was before the fight, every actor included, and at once plays the cave collapse, which throws up to 40 rocks and dust puffs at the same moment a dialogue box opens. Replayed from a memory-card save, that moment leaves between four slots and no slots to spare. A few more actors alive at the peak and the next requests fail: the counter reads 2 in every report and stays on screen until the next battle or scene load. Nothing else breaks; a part or two of the collapse never appears.

A recorded playthrough shows the text arrive at the moment the replay's pool is fullest, but with the dialogue box already closed, which the replay survives with three or four slots free. A played run is therefore several actors heavier than the replay, and which actors those are is not known.

The chain from text to cause

  1. The printThe frame driver FUN_80016444 loads the signed 16-bit counter at 0x8007B8A0 (lh a1,0x588(gp) at 0x800164B0) and, when it is non-zero, calls the dev text formatter FUN_8001A068 with the string at 0x800100F4. Unlike the two prints above it, it is not behind the development switch gp+0x3B8.
  2. The counterCleared by stage init FUN_8001E1B4 at 0x8001E304; incremented in exactly one place, 0x80020E0C..0x80020E1C.
  3. The failing spawnThat place is the failure arm of the actor spawn FUN_80020DE0: it calls the pop FUN_80020454, and when the pop returns null it bumps the counter, logs to the dev console and returns 0.
  4. The poolFUN_800203EC seeds a free stack of 143 slot pointers, 0x8007C5AC + i * 0xD8. The pop refuses when the top index at 0x8007C348 is negative; free slots = top index + 1. Stage init takes 7 slots as list sentinels, leaving 136.
  5. The battle return keeps the poolStage init always clears the counter, but skips the reseed and the seven pops when the field resumes from a battle: resume word 0x8007B8B8 == 2 and next mode 2 (bne at 0x8001E308, beq at 0x8001E31C to 0x8001E36C). The pool block is parked on the way into the fight and copied back, so every actor alive before the fight is alive after it.
  6. The collapseThe Golem record P2[47] is respawned by the system script when the field returns, and its post-battle arm installs two effect trees (34 30 0D, 34 30 07). Their parts are spawned by FUN_80021B04 (jal 0x80020de0 at 0x80021B78), two to seven per frame at the peak.

  • The run. Noa alone with Terra following, in Noa's opening chapter, after ordinary random battles and a menu visit on the climb. The player does nothing unusual, and two commenters report the same text at the same place: in a played run this is the common outcome.
  • The platform. An emulator at a raised internal resolution, by inference from the picture; the uploader names none.

The recording contradicts the replay on one point, the dialogue box. In the replay the pool only fills when the three-actor box is open on the peak frame; with the box closed, three or four slots stay free. In the recording the box has been closed for about a second when two requests fail. A played run therefore holds at least five more actors at the peak than the replay. The replay is a different party on a poked state (Vahn alone, teleported between trigger tiles); the reported runs are Noa with Terra, walked. Which of those differences supplies the actors is not measured.

The number on screen is then a derivation, and it is inference: the counter goes up once per request made while the stack is empty, the stager asks for two to seven slots a frame at the peak, and parts free themselves shortly after. A run that reaches the peak with the pool already full fails the requests of one frame and recovers. Every report reads 2; nothing in the instructions fixes that number, so its constancy says a played run is the same few actors over the limit each time. The failing caller is most likely the stager creating a part of the collapse, since the dialogue box is closed when the text appears. Three sources of extra actors are tested and ruled out on the replay's set-up: finished cutscene records do not accumulate, ordinary random battles do not leak slots, and nothing survives a real scene change.

Verifying it

Put an execute breakpoint on 0x80020E0C and play the climb from the mountain entrance through the Golem, from a save made before the Golem tile (flag 0x284 clear) in Noa's opening chapter, with Terra following. A hit is a failed spawn: the caller's return address is at sp+0x38 and s1 is the template of the actor that did not get a slot. The word at 0x8007C348, logged every frame, shows the load directly: 0x8E is an empty pool, -1 is exhaustion. That played, unpoked run is the one measurement still missing. Without a debugger, the recording gives the check: beat the Golem, dismiss both of Terra's boxes, and the text appears about a second later, before the screen fades; it is gone after the next battle.

Open questions

  • The failed spawn is not captured. The recording shows that two requests fail and when, not which caller made them.
  • What makes a played run at least five actors heavier than the replay at the peak: Terra's own actors, parts left by trigger records the replay skips, and actors a walked route leaves behind are all untested.
  • Why the count is 2 in every report and never 1 or 3.
  • The dev text formatter FUN_8001A068 is not traced for a gate of its own. The recording shows it drawing in an emulator; that it draws on a retail console rests on a relayed report.

No encounters in Jette's Absolute Fortress

Reported as
In some files Jette's Absolute Fortress has no random battles at all, before Jette is fought; it survives a reset and a memory-card load, and Incense and the encounter accessories make no difference (community reports, one first-hand: Reddit, r/LegendofLegaia, “Jette's fortress no random encounter”)
Cause
Escaping Zora's Floating Castle with under a minute on the clock leaves its “no battles” flag set for good, and the Fortress's encounter tables still obey it
Where
Clock actor 0x801D2EBC and roll FUN_801D9E1C in PROT entry 0897; scripts in PROT entry 373; tables in PROT entries 606 and 706
Affects
Both Fortress scenes, in any file that had less than 1:00 left during a successful castle escape
Status
Explained - a real defect; the flag write and the silent Fortress are captured on an edited save, and the castle escape itself was not played to its last minute here. A player's own test confirms the outcome: leaving the castle with under a minute left gives a Fortress with no encounters and no Door of Light (community report, first-hand)

The cause is in Zora's Floating Castle, hours earlier. When the castle is falling the game gives six minutes to get out, and it switches random battles off for the last minute by raising a story flag. The record that ends a successful escape stops the clock but never lowers the flag, so a file that reached the exit with less than a minute showing keeps it for good.

In the castle that no longer matters. But the Fortress's two encounter tables were built from the castle's and still begin with the same “this flag means no battles” group. In that file the whole Fortress is silent, and the Door of Light is greyed out there as well. No item, accessory or reset changes it, because the flag is saved with the file. A file that escaped with more than a minute to spare is unaffected.

The clock is not gentle: the scene loader takes 20 seconds off a running clock on every scene entry, and the castle is two scenes, so each crossing between them costs 20 seconds on top of the walking.

The fix

The patcher's --fortress-encounter-fix (a checkbox in the browser patcher; reference) closes it on the disc, and it heals a save that already carries the flag. In both Fortress scenes it rewrites the leading group of the encounter table - 14 bytes of each scene's data, same size - into a duplicate of the scene's own collapse group: the first condition tests flag 0x521 instead of 0x15, and its row becomes a copy of that group's whole-map row. The Fortress then never reads flag 0x15. A healthy file sees the same rooms and rates as before, and once the Fortress collapses the same quiet one.

The castle is not touched: its last minute stays quiet and the flag is still left set afterwards, with nothing left to read it. The missing op itself, a 60 15 in the escape-success record, is not added - the castle's script ships as a raw chunk of a streamed file, where a two-byte insertion cannot be made in place.

The cause, step by step

  1. The countdown opField-script op 4C D3 arms a countdown kept in the saved game state: two flag ids, a duration and a threshold (handler 0x801E2BDC..0x801E2C38 in PROT entry 0897), and spawns a clock actor.
  2. The clock actor0x801D2EBC takes the play-clock delta off the remaining time each tick. On expiry it sets the first flag (0x801D2F60); whenever the remaining time is below the threshold it sets the second (0x801D2F84). The flag ids come from RAM, not from constants in the code.
  3. The castle arms ittaiku P2[29] (PROT entry 373), the Mist Generator scene, clears flag 0x15 (60 15 at +0x1508) and then arms the clock at +0x1533: expiry flag 0x396, warning flag 0x15, 21600 ticks (6:00), threshold 3600 ticks (1:00). The castle's encounter table opens with a flag-0x15 group that is one whole-map rate-0 row: the last minute has no battles by design.
  4. Nothing clears itThe success record taiku P2[16] issues the same op with duration 0, which stops the clock, and never issues 60 15. The whole disc has one script clear of flag 0x15 - the one that runs before the clock is armed - and no native call clears it by constant id.
  5. The Fortress reads itFlag 0x15 heads the condition list of exactly three scenes: the second castle scene, and the two Fortress scenes deroa and chitei2. Their first row is the castle's row carried over: whole map, rate 0, both Doors blocked.

The quiet states that are intended

  • The collapse flag, 0x521. This is not a “Jette is beaten” flag: beating Jette changes nothing about encounters, and the walk from his room to the Mist Generator rolls exactly as the walk in did. The flag is set later, in the Mist Generator room after the Cort fight, as the Fortress starts to collapse (chitei2 P2[15] and P2[19]), and it is never cleared. Both scenes then select an all-zero group. This is the designers switching the dungeon off; the steps below show how little of it a player can see.
  • Safe rooms. In the interior's default table each zero-rate box outlines one room: Jette's room, the antechamber before it, the council chamber and Mist Generator room, the two elevator shafts and the bridge. Every ordinary room and corridor rolls at rate 18. Measured over enclosed floor tiles, 39% of the interior and 8% of the entrance is rate 0.

How the roll reads them, step by step
  1. A step is a change of the player's 128-unit tile by at most one on each axis (0x801D9ED4..0x801D9F0C).
  2. The condition list is walked in order (0x801D9F30..0x801D9FD8): the first record whose flag is 0xFFFF or set wins.
  3. The first region of that group whose box contains the tile wins (0x801D9FE8..0x801DA054).
  4. The step does nothing while the player's engaged bit is up, while 0x8007B6B4 is non-zero, while the warp timer 0x8007B6B0 is positive, or while the Incense window 0x8007B600 is non-zero (0x801DA130..0x801DA180). The roll is not reached at all while a menu or scripted submode is open (bit 0x8000 of 0x1F800394, raised at 0x801F1360, dropped at 0x801F1684).
  5. The rate is scaled by the setting byte 0x8007B5F8, the High and Low Encounter accessories, and system flags 0x1D (doubled) and 0x1E (halved) (0x801DA198..0x801DA204).
  6. The result is subtracted from the counter 0x8007B5FC; at zero or below a battle starts (bgtz at 0x801DA218) and the counter is reset to between 488 and 1460. The counter is not saved: after power-on it holds 974, so the first battle after a cold load in a rate-18 room comes on step 55.

Things that thin battles without stopping them: a Low Encounter accessory makes rate 18 into 9, and into 4 if system flag 0x1E is also set. Flags 0x1D and 0x1E come from the two springs in Rogue's Tower, which is reached after the Fortress, so in a normal file neither is set here; they have their own section. A scene load with the counter below 487 adds half of a fresh roll (0x8003AC7C..0x8003AC90); Incense suspends the roll while its window is open. None of them produces a silent Fortress.

Rogue's Tower has two springs side by side. Examining one shows “It's strange green water!” or “It's strange blue water!” and asks whether to drink. Either one fully restores the party's HP and MP. When the field comes back after a battle, the game clears both water flags. Both springs also set a third flag, 0x1C, which nothing ever clears. It is the switch for the clean-up: the battle-return code clears the two water flags only when 0x1C is set, and 0x1C is only ever set together with one of them. A file that has drunk once keeps 0x1C for good, so the byte reads 0x08 after any later battle.

The effect is real but easy to miss even inside its one battle. The step count between battles is re-rolled over a three-to-one range after every fight, so the bands for “normal” and “green” overlap, and so do “normal” and “blue”. A fair test is to drink, count the steps to the next battle, and repeat many times, or to read the rate in a debugger.

The scaling code

The rate is what one step takes off the step counter, so a larger rate means more battles. The counter restarts between 488 and 1460 after each battle, 974 on average, and a battle comes on step ceil(counter / rate).

801da1f4  jal  0x8003ce64 ; li a0,0x1e      ; test system flag 0x1E

801da204  srl  s0,v0,0x1                    ; set: rate / 2

801da214  subu v1,v1,v0                     ; counter -= rate
801da218  bgtz v1,0x801da360                ; still positive: no battle

The two tests are independent and run in that order. With both flags set the rate is shifted left and then right by one bit, which returns the same number for every rate, odd or even. There is no cap and no floor other than the integer shift.

The same block applies the accessories first: High Encounter (Bad Luck Bell, Nemesis Gem) multiplies by four and Low Encounter (Good Luck Bell, Evil Talisman) halves. Everything stacks by plain shifts. The roll is the same function for every field scene and for the overworld, so while a flag is set the scaling is not local to the tower. It does nothing in rooms whose rate is 0, to scripted fights, or while Incense holds the roll.

The flute's attack shows a caption with the attack's name, “Blowfish”. The flute's escape skips the end of its own attack, so the caption is never taken down. It stays on screen through the escape and keeps pointing at its text after the battle has freed that memory. When the game reads the field's data back into the freed memory, the caption's “text” becomes picture data. Drawing it as text can send the text routine into a copy that never ends, and the game locks up on the black screen after the escape. The mechanism

  1. The caption outlives the attack (capture). While the flute's attack runs, the retained widget list FUN_80031D00 draws a text widget every frame: jal 0x80036888 at 0x800321E0, string pointer at widget +0x18. In this fight the string is "Blowfish" at heap address 0x800B995C. Arm 8 of FUN_801F6A00 in PROT entry 0925 moves the action state machine to the run wait 0x65 and never removes that widget. The caption is still drawn every frame through states 0x65, 0x66 and 0x67, beside the “successfully escaped” banner.
  2. Its text is overwritten (capture, disassembly). At state 0x67 the teardown in FUN_8004E568 frees the enemy record buffers and the side-band stream buffer, and the loader-B id 0x8007BC4C goes from 0x1E (PROT 0925) to 0x53 (PROT 0978, the field-back-read module). PROT 0978 re-reads the scene's own data from disc into the largest free heap block, which now covers the caption. From then on the widget's string at 0x800B995C reads f8 f2 f2 ff fa ef da ..., which is jouind's PROT entry 681 at offset 0xE4BC, with no zero byte nearby.
  3. The text routine never comes back (capture, interpreter core). The renderer FUN_80036888 hands that “string” to the substitution expander FUN_80036514. The expander's first pass rewrites each 0xFF in the source to 0xCF in place, so it also corrupts the freed memory it reads. The copy loop at 0x800367E4 then follows a substitution code whose source pointer t0 lands in the expander's own output buffer (from 0x800740EC). The read cursor trails the write cursor by 0x1FE bytes and never meets a zero. The copy runs on through 0x80074723, 0x80075E93 and 0x80079543 (logged every 2000 passes), overwriting everything after the buffer. Under the fast core the emulator stops with “Unrecoverable error while running recompiler” at pc 0x80036868, the loop's tail. On hardware the game hangs.

A plain escape never installs the caption, so it cannot hit this. The water flags are read only after the battle has ended, by which time the copy has already locked up.

Why one fight and not another

The outcome is a fixed function of the scene and the formation's distinct monster species. Three links make it so.

Where the caption text sits (capture). The caption string is the attack name inside the Spikefish's actor record. That record is streamed into the side-band slot at the bottom of the enemy stager buffer, so the text sits at 0x800B990C + 0x50 = 0x800B995C. The stager buffer (0x2E390 bytes) sits at 0x800B9900 in every captured fight, in both scenes.

How much the field-back read loads (disassembly, capture). PROT 0978's routine 0x801F6B24 works in three steps:

  1. It takes the largest free heap block (FUN_8002B790, at 0x801F6F08), counts whole sectors in it, and caps the count at 0xC5.
  2. It allocates that many sectors less 4 bytes (FUN_80017888, at 0x801F6F74). The best-fit allocator FUN_8002B468 cuts the allocation from the top of the block, so the buffer ends at 0x800E7C9C, where the freed stager buffer ended.
  3. It reads that many sectors from 32 sectors into the scene's first PROT entry (0x801F726C..0x801F7298), the scene's own field data.

After the teardown the largest free block is the stager plus the monster blocks allocated directly below it. There is one monster block per distinct species, each the species' battle-heap cost (block[+0x08] of its PROT 867 record) plus a 12-byte header:

sectors = min((0x2E390 + 0xA8 + sum(cost + 12 over distinct species)) / 0x800, 0xC5)
caption reads scene_data[sectors * 0x800 - 0x2E344]   (reached only from 93 sectors up)

The 0xA8 is fitted: it is one more freed 0x9C node, and without it Gizam Lv3 predicts 114 sectors where the capture reads 115. The formula gives the measured count for all eight captured fights, in both scenes.

What the text routine does with those bytes (simulation). The script scripts/asset-investigation/spikefish_caption_predict.py places the scene's disc bytes at the caption and runs the renderer and expander logic from the disassembly over them:

  • a first byte below 0x20 draws nothing;
  • otherwise it follows the normalisation pass, the token count, the C1..C7 substitution table at 0x80010EC0 and the nested name copy.

A copy that runs past 64 KiB, or touches an address outside RAM and its mirrors (a bus error on hardware), is called a freeze. It needs a battle RAM image as a backdrop for the tables it dereferences; two different backdrops give identical results. The script agrees with all eight captured outcomes, among them the three Barra Lv3 and Aluru + Orb Lv2 freezes, which were predicted before those runs.

Gaza 2 softlock

Reported as
In the Gaza rematch the battle stops and the camera circles the party forever (community report)
Cause
A monster walking to its target is carried only by its Move animation; a leftover flinch flag swaps that animation for idle and the wait never ends
Where
Battle action state machine FUN_801E295C, PROT entry 0898, state 0x19
Affects
Nearly every monster, bosses included, when a melee directly follows a flinch
Status
Explained - traced in full on its own write-up; the key addresses re-read here

This one has a full write-up of its own, with the evidence behind it and the patch. In short: a monster that starts a melee attack out of reach has to walk to its target. Almost no monster has a dedicated approach animation, so the game plays the ordinary Move loop and waits in a state that only re-measures the distance. That state has no movement code and no timeout.

If the monster is still flinching from a hit when its attack begins - a summon is the usual cause - a leftover “go back to idle when this clip ends” flag is still set. The Move loop plays one cycle, the flag swaps it for the idle pose, the monster stops short, and the wait never ends. The camera's idle sweep runs on its own, so the screen keeps orbiting.

  1. FlinchThe hit-reaction stager in FUN_800402F4 queues the flinch clip and raises bit 2 of actor+0x1DC (ori v0,v0,0x4 at 0x80042140).
  2. Attack setup, state 0x14Out of reach, no tag-0x20 walk action (jal 0x80050e2c at 0x801E3268, miss tested at 0x801E327C): the Move clip is staged at 0x801E32B0 and state 0x19 stored at 0x801E32D0.
  3. Mid-clip commitThe Move clip is installed by the path that clears bits 0..1 only (andi v0,v0,0xfc at 0x80047A38). Bit 2 survives.
  4. First natural endFUN_80047430 tests bit 2 (0x80047B38) and queues idle over the Move clip (sb zero,0x1da(s2) at 0x80047B44).
  5. ParkedState 0x19 re-checks the range every frame at 0x801E3594 and falls to the stall label 0x801E35D0, forever.

Conkram mirror freeze

Reported as
The game freezes in present-day Conkram, in the Room of Mirrors scene that sends the party into the past, on many emulators; pressing Cross very fast through the scene's text avoids it (community reports: pSX 1.13 logging r3000: executed illegal opcode in a pSX forum thread, which also reports the PAL release unaffected; ePSXe versions other than 1.7.0; the PS3's PS1 emulation every time; a play guide that moves DuckStation saves to ePSXe for this scene). Nobody reports it on a PS1 or PS2
Cause
The scene runs with almost no free heap. An emulated CPU faster than a console's gives the game more frames per second and keeps more effect parts alive; an allocation fails, the move VM writes animation data through the NULL pointer over the bottom of RAM, and the CPU's exception vector is gone
Where
concnow records P2[17] (the walk-on at the mirror) and P2[18] (the crossing); the unchecked allocation in the move VM's keyframe-pose seat (op 0x3C) at 0x80023CD0..0x80023CE0, SCUS_942.54
Affects
This scene on any emulator that runs the CPU fast enough; how fast depends on the party
Status
Explained - reproduced on two emulators by changing only the emulated CPU speed; why a faster CPU keeps more parts alive is inference
In short
  • What you see. The picture stops during the mirror scene, on some emulators; nobody reports it on a PS1 or PS2.
  • Why. The room leaves the game's heap almost full. An emulated CPU faster than a console's draws more frames and keeps more effect parts alive, and an allocation fails. The code that asked for it never checks, and copies animation data to address zero - over the CPU's exception vector. The next interrupt loops forever.
  • Why some emulators. It depends on CPU speed alone: SwanStation passes at 100 % and froze from 125 % with one save; PCSX-ReARMed freezes at the faster clock it applies to this game by default and passed at the lower clocks tried (30 to 50).
  • What to do. Patch the disc with --conkram-freeze-fix (a checkbox in the browser patcher). Without it, set the emulator's CPU speed to 100 % (on PCSX-ReARMed, a lower CPU clock) for this scene, then put it back.

The players call it an FMV, but no movie plays here: it is an ordinary in-engine cutscene. After the party sets the Seru Flame down before the mirror (“That mirror is the door to the past”), the crossing changes the music, lifts the party off the floor, lights a glow on the mirror, shows three pages of text spoken by Terra, fades to white and loads past Conkram.

The game shares one small heap between the scene, its effects and anything it loads. In this room only about 16.6 KB of it is free before the crossing starts. During the crossing the game also loads the party's battle files ahead of time, and the scene's effect parts each take two blocks: a render node and an animation buffer. On a console-speed emulator the free space bottoms out at about 3.7 KB and the scene finishes. On an emulator whose CPU runs faster, the game draws more frames per second (20 per 30 vsyncs instead of 15 in this room in one capture), more parts are alive at once, and the heap runs out.

The routine that sets up a part's animation never checks whether it got its memory. Handed a NULL pointer, it copies the animation data to address zero. On the PlayStation that is real RAM, and the copy covers 0x80000028..0x8000008C, including the CPU's exception vector at 0x80000080. The next interrupt runs the copied data as code, hits a reserved instruction at the vector itself and raises the same exception again, forever. The picture stops; pSX logs exactly that as a stream of illegal opcodes.

  1. Heap nearly fullAbout 16.6 KB free in concnow; the allocator FUN_8002B468 is best-fit over one free list.
  2. Battle data pre-stagedAfter the music swap the poller FUN_800243F0 (0x80024BB0..0x80024C64) reads 8 sectors through FUN_8003E360, then FUN_80052770 loads the party battle files PLAYER1..3, about 316 KiB in twenty chunks.
  3. Allocation failsAn effect part's keyframe buffer (FUN_80017888(0, count*32|8), 0x88 bytes for four lanes) returns NULL; the failure arm only adds 0x20000 to the PRG ERR counter 0x8007B828.
  4. NULL writeMove-VM op 0x3C stores the pointer with no test and copies through it (sh at 0x80023D30, 0x80023D3C); its retarget op 0x3D writes through it too (0x80023EF4).
  5. Exception loopCause 0x428 (reserved instruction), EPC 0x80000084, on every interrupt.

The fix

The patcher's --conkram-freeze-fix (details) makes both of a particle's memory requests safe, with four same-size edits of SCUS_942.54. Nine words of the move VM's op-0x3C handler are rearranged so a NULL animation block branches to the VM's own halt: the particle ends the way a script ends one and its render node goes back to the heap. A failed render node, which retail leaves as a NULL pointer for later code to write through, gets a shared stand-in node kept in free RAM instead. Both requests go through a quiet copy of the game's allocation routine that has no error counter, so a failure the fix absorbs leaves the PRG ERR readout off the screen. Every allocation that succeeds runs the retail instructions.

Run on the settings that froze, the patched disc passes: both saves reach past Conkram on SwanStation at 200 % and on PCSX-ReARMed's default, with PRG ERR at 0. A write watch on the bottom 256 bytes of RAM in PCSX-ReARMed's interpreter sees no store at all through the scene, where the retail disc makes 72. At console speed nothing fails, and past Conkram loads on the same vsync as with the retail disc.
