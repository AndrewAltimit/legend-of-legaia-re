Items, stats and equipment

Five reported oversights in how Legend of Legaia handles items, stat boosts and equipment, each followed through the game's own code: stacked Speed Elixirs that lock characters out of their turns and hand turns to empty slots, story events that take equipment without telling the rest of the game, enemy items that can be turned on your own party - a Point Card that freezes the battle and a flute that kills an ally who stays standing, a Japanese coin vendor that sells a hundredth item, and a prize that can be announced and never handed over.

How to read this page
Everything here is read from the instructions and data on the disc - the USA disc throughout, plus the Japanese and French discs for the Elixir and coin-vendor sections. Two sections, the Speed Elixir one and the enemy-item one, were also observed in a running game, captured on a poked state: values were written by hand in an emulator, as each section spells out. The other three were not run. Where a section says something "should" happen, that is a prediction that follows from the code and is marked as untested. Each glitch has an address table so another reader with a debugger can check it.

Speed Elixirs and void turns

Reported as
"Stat boost overflow": stacking Speed Elixirs in battle makes the turn-order number go negative. The character stops getting turns and "void turns" appear, where the camera swings to a spot where nobody stands and nothing happens. A dead enemy can take a turn, and the game can freeze (community report)
Cause
The turn-order number rolled from Speed is kept in 16 bits, and the routine that picks the next fighter reads its best-so-far as signed, so a number of 32,768 or more can never win
Where
Key seed FUN_801DA780 and next-actor picker FUN_801DABA4 in PROT 0898; Elixir arm 0x80041464 in SCUS_942.54
Affects
The over-boosted character and every party member seated before them; all three builds read (USA, Japanese, French)
Status
Explained - read from the instructions and captured on a poked state; the freeze is not traced

This entry is about Elixirs used in battle, and about Speed in particular. A Speed Elixir or a Wonder Elixir multiplies a character's battle Speed by 1.2, and nothing stops the next one from doing it again. On the USA disc the Speed stat itself is safe - it stops at 65,535. The overflow is one step further on, in the number the game rolls from Speed every round to decide who acts next.

That turn-order number is kept in 16 bits. The routine that picks the next fighter walks the seats in order and remembers the best number so far, but it reads the remembered number as signed and each new candidate as unsigned. A number of 32,768 or more therefore beats everything when it is the candidate, and then reads as negative when it is the remembered best - so the very next seat takes the lead from it, even a seat whose number is zero.

Two things follow. The over-boosted character can never be picked, and neither can anyone seated before them, because each of those lost the lead to the big number on the way past. And the round still hands out one turn per living fighter, so the turns those characters should have had go to whichever later seats are left. Once those have acted their numbers are zero, every remaining seat ties at zero, and the game draws one at random without asking whether anyone is there:

  • an empty enemy slot - the void turn: the camera frames a spot with nobody in it and the turn ends at once;
  • a fighter who has already acted, who gets a second action;
  • a defeated enemy's slot, which the draw does not exclude.

The effect has a window. From an ordinary Speed it takes roughly 16 to 26 Elixirs on one character to open it, and a few more close it again: once Speed is pinned at 65,535 the sum wraps all the way round to an ordinary positive number and the character simply acts first.

Which party members lose their turns depends on where the boosted one stands: in the first seat only that character is locked out, in the second seat the first two are, and in the third seat the whole party is - and then every freed turn goes to an enemy slot, living, empty or fallen.

What was captured, and how

The mechanism was read from the instructions and then observed in PCSX-Redux on the unpatched USA disc, in a battle of three party members (seats 0 to 2, Speed 331, 364 and 310) against one enemy in seat 3. These are captures on a poked state: Speed, or the turn-order number itself, was written directly in the emulator. No Elixir was used, so the Elixir routine itself was not exercised in a running game, and nobody has yet played the 24-odd Elixirs out by hand. Commands were entered by scripted pad presses (a plain attack for every member).

The Japanese and French builds

The picker is the same in all three builds, instruction for instruction. The Elixir routine is not: the Japanese executable has no ceiling on it, so on the Japanese disc the boosted stat itself wraps round to a small number once it passes 65,535 - an Attack of 300 is 59,077 after the twenty-ninth Power Elixir and 5,356 after the thirtieth. The USA and French builds stop the stat at 65,535. The localisation closed the stat wrap and left the picker as it was.

What it is not

The permanent stat items - the six Waters, Honey and Miracle Water - cannot overflow anything. Each adds a small fixed amount to a 16-bit number and immediately compares the result with a ceiling (999, or 9,999 for HP and 280 for agility), writing the ceiling back if it was passed. The largest value that ever exists before the compare is 1003 (10015 for HP). The Japanese and French builds carry the same compares with the same ceilings. A status-screen figure above 999 is the stored stat plus equipment, summed for display.

The instructions

The Elixir arm (class 7, 0x80041464) computes stat + stat/5 on both halfwords of a battle stat pair and stores 0xFFFF when the sum is larger (0x800414D8..0x80041510 for Speed, actor +0x164 and +0x166). There is no "already boosted" guard. Once a round the key is seeded:

801da800  lhu a0,0x164(a1)        ; SPD
801da840  addu a0,a0,v1           ; + rand() % (SPD/2 + 1)
801da844  addiu a0,a0,1
801da84c  sh a0,0x16c(a1)         ; 16-bit store, no ceiling

The wounded bonus, the halving under Slow (0x801DA908) and Speed Chain's +0x1000 (0x801DAA08) are 16-bit too. The picker starts with seat 0's key as the running maximum and walks seats 1 to 6:

801daca4  sll v0,a1,0x10
801daca8  lhu v1,0x16c(v1)        ; candidate key, zero-extended
801dacac  sra v0,v0,0x10          ; running maximum, sign-extended
801dacb0  slt v0,v0,v1            ; (signed) max < (unsigned) candidate?
801dad60  sb v0,0x274(a1)         ; the chosen seat

A passing test makes the candidate the maximum and restarts the tie list; equality (0x801DACD8) adds the seat to the list; the result is a random member (jal rand at 0x801DAD0C). A key of 0x8000 or more always passes as a candidate and always loses as the maximum, and the equality test never matches it. The loop has no HP test, no "is this slot populated" test and no "key is not zero" test.

The turn start (0x801E2C50..0x801E2D54 in FUN_801E295C) takes the seat from ctx[+0x274] unchecked, loads the actor pointer from 0x801C9370[seat], zeroes its key and calls the camera framing routine (jal 0x801d5854). Unused enemy seats still have zero-filled actor records, so the camera frames an empty seat and the turn falls through to its end.

From the seed formula: below Speed 21,845 the key is always under 0x8000; from 21,845 to 32,766 it passes 0x8000 on some rolls; from 32,767 to 43,689 on every roll; from 43,690 to 65,534 the sum increasingly passes 0xFFFF and wraps back; at 65,535 the key is rand() % 0x8000.

The Japanese Elixir arm (0x800425F0..0x8004295C in SCPS_100.59) ends in a plain store with no compare: lhu v0,0x164(a0) at 0x80042608, addu v1,v1,v0 at 0x80042620, sh v1,0x164(a0) at 0x80042624.

The permanent items are class 6, arm 0x8004112C; the tier byte picks one of seven arms through the table at 0x800151B0, all of this shape:

800411e8  lhu v0,0x6ec(v1)      ; stored ATK (record +0x124)
800411f0  addiu v0,v0,0x4
800411f4  sh v0,0x6ec(v1)
800411fc  sltiu v0,v0,0x3e8     ; below 1000?
80041200  bne v0,zero,<exit>
8004120c  _sh v0,0x6ec(v1)      ; otherwise store 999

The item menu then rebuilds the live stats (jal 0x80042558 at 0x801D8540, PROT 0899), which clamps again at 0x80042C0C..0x80042CE0. One enemy move does wrap a stat - Jugger Power (cast 0xBA, PROT 0945) adds a quarter to ten stat halfwords at 0x801F6DA0..0x801F6E34 with no compare - but it is an enemy self-buff and unrelated to Elixirs.

Verifying it

On a poked state: at the entry of 0x801DA780, write 0x8000 to +0x164 and +0x166 of the party member in seat 1. A breakpoint at 0x801DA84C shows a0 between 0x8001 and 0xC001; a breakpoint at 0x801DAD60 never shows seat 0 or seat 1 as the result, and shows an empty seat once seats 2 and up have acted. Unpoked, use Speed Elixirs on one character up to the count in the table and watch the same two breakpoints.

Still open: the freeze (a battle with one enemy already defeated and a later-seated party member in the lock-out band would show where the action state stops); and a played reproduction with real Elixirs. Related reference: item-effect table, battle action state machine, battle formulas.

Story equipment removal skips the re-check

Reported as
When the plot takes a piece of equipment, its bonus keeps working until something else re-checks equipment (community report)
Cause
The script's take-item instruction empties the equipment slot but never calls the routine that turns slots into bonuses
Where
Field script instruction 4C 52 at 0x801E1ABC (PROT 0897) and FUN_800430AC in SCUS_942.54
Affects
Nine story hand-overs of a wearable item, listed below, when the item is equipped at that moment
Status
Explained - the mechanism and the nine sites; how long the stale bonus lasts is only partly traced

An accessory's effect is not looked up when it is needed. One routine "bakes" it into each character: it reads the eight equipment slots and writes out a set of ability bits and a block of live stats. Battle, the random-encounter roll and the menus all read the baked copy, never the slots.

When the story takes an item, the script instruction first tries the bag. If the item is not there because it is being worn, it clears the equipment slot directly - and stops. It does not run the bake, so the baked copy still says the item is on.

The game does have a script instruction that writes an equipment slot and re-bakes; it is the one that grows the Ra-Seru. The take-item fallback simply does not use it.

How long it lasts

Until the next event that re-bakes. Entering any scene always does. So do using an item or magic from the menu, equipping or removing anything, Best Equipment, and buying-and-equipping in a shop. The field scene is reloaded after a battle, which would limit the stale bonus to a single battle fought before leaving the room; that part rests on the engine documentation and was not re-read. The in-field save screen does not re-bake. Whether simply closing the pause menu does is not traced.

Every reference to it on the disc is a call, and none is in the battle program. Enemy items aimed at an ally

Reported as
An enemy-targeted battle item can be aimed at one of your own characters. The Point Card then hangs the battle (the "Point Card softlock"); the Lippian Flute can kill an ally who stays on their feet ("Lippian Roulette"). Outcome matrix from a community report by ZetaPhoenix
Cause
The target cursor can step onto an ally. The Point Card's damage skips the HP-counter bookkeeping the end of the action waits on; Lippian's kill runs a fade that stands a party member back up at 0 HP
Where
Item window at 0x801D18F8 and the action state machine in PROT 0898; discharge arm 0x8004209C and fade arm 0x80050230 in SCUS_942.54; Lippian cast module FUN_801F6A18 in PROT 0924
Affects
The Point Card and the Lippian Flute, aimed at any party member. Not the Spikefish Flute, which hits every enemy and never opens a cursor
Status
Explained - read from the instructions, matched against the community matrix and captured on a poked state

Two items in the game are meant for one enemy and nobody else: the Point Card and the Lippian Flute. Both can be pointed at your own party, and each does something its designers never drew.

The cursor can reach an ally. When you pick one of these items, the game builds a ring of targets that starts from whoever this character aimed at last. It builds the ring first and only afterwards checks that the last target was an enemy. If the last thing this character did was aimed at an ally - any healing item or Elixir, whose cursor starts on the user - that ally is already in the ring. The cursor is moved to an enemy, but one press of Left steps back onto the ally.

The Point Card breaks the HP display. Its damage routine subtracts HP and does nothing else. Every other routine that changes a party member's HP also notes how far the on-screen number still has to count. At the end of an action the game holds, slowly turning the camera, until on-screen HP equals real HP. Here they differ and nothing is counting, so the hold never ends: the "Points returned" caption stays up, the HP bar keeps its old value and the camera circles for good.

Lippian's kill is written for a monster. It sets HP to 0, starts the target's knockdown and switches on the fade a defeated monster disappears with. On a party member that same fade is the one a revive uses: the body goes black, is put back in its standing pose and fades in again. HP stays at 0, so the character is dead in every way except how they look.

The Point Card hang is the same end state as the first of the two known battle softlocks - the HP-counter wait - reached from the controller on an unmodified disc. It is not the approach softlock described in the endless camera orbit.

  • The dead-ally column holds only when the item user is the first fighter to act that round. For anyone acting later the game re-rolls a dead target among the living party members, the user included, and the living-ally column applies to whoever it picks (D, P).
  • Point Card on a dead ally is not quite nothing. The whole bank, up to 9999 points, is spent, a damage number is shown and the body replays its collapse. Nothing hangs because real and on-screen HP are both 0 (D, P).
  • The Point Card itself is never used up. It leaves the bag when you confirm and is added back when the action starts. The Lippian Flute is consumed (D, P).
  • The kill roll is 1 in 128, not 1 in 126: a random number's low seven bits must all be zero. The number 126 does appear on the item, as the effect class of its descriptor, which is not a probability (D).
  • A standing character at 0 HP is dead to the rules. The command ring skips them, and when the last living member is "killed" this way the battle ends in the ordinary defeat message with the character upright (D, P).

Coin vendor purchases past 99 (Japanese release)

Reported as
On the Japanese release a coin vendor sells an item while 99 are held; the coins go and the count stays 99 (community report)
Cause
The Japanese prize counter only asks whether the coins cover the price; the bag then adds the item and silently trims the stack back to 99
Where
Japanese prize session 0x801DE978 in PROT 0899 and bag add 0x80043210 in SCPS_100.59; USA counterparts FUN_801DC1CC and FUN_800421D4
Affects
Every coin prize row on the Japanese release; equipment bought "to the bag" in a gold shop on every release
Status
Explained - Japanese, USA and French code read; not run

At a coin prize counter the Japanese game asks one question before the Yes / No box: can the coins cover the price? It never looks at how many of the item you already hold. After Yes it adds the item and takes the coins with no further test.

The bag's add routine never refuses anything: it adds, then quietly trims the stack back to 99. So a hundredth copy costs the coins, is added, and is trimmed off again - exactly the report.

The USA build added two things in front of that. The prize list draws a row dimmed when you hold 99, and the confirm step asks "fewer than 99 held?" and buzzes if not. The French build carries both. The purchase itself and the trimming add are the same in every build.

Because only the coin counter was fixed, every release still has a relative of this fault in the ordinary gold shop. Stackable items go through a quantity screen capped at 99 - held, but equipment does not: the buy confirm tests only gold, and the "to the bag" row adds the item and debits gold with no held-count read between them. Buying a hundredth copy of one piece of equipment takes the gold and leaves 99. That is read from the instructions of the USA and Japanese builds (the French build is the USA code relocated) and has not been run.

The instructions

Japanese build - PROT 0899 at load base 0x801D05C8, executable SCPS_100.59. The session's call list is the USA one with a single call missing, the held-count lookup.

801deca8  jal 0x80043210          ; commit: bag add (id, 1), result ignored

After the add the commit subtracts the price from the coin bank at 0x801DECD8..0x801DECF0. The Japanese prize-list row draw (0x801D8014..0x801D80A8) dims a row only when it is unaffordable. The whole Japanese PROT 0899 contains no signed compare against 0x63.

War God Icon can be missed for good

Reported as
The War God Icon is lost for good if the Muscle Dome Expert Course is beaten before defeating Jette (community report)
Cause
The item and its "already awarded" flag are handed out by two different pieces of code that disagree about what clearing the course means
Where
Arena settlement FUN_801D0F60 in PROT 0977; lobby victory scene, record P2[9] of scene koin1 (PROT 543)
Affects
The Master course - not Expert as reported - won while it is still shortened
Status

Correction to the report
The dome's three courses are Beginner, Expert and Master. The Icon belongs to Master. Nothing in the script or the arena program ties it to the Expert course.

The prize is split in two. The item is added by the arena program, and only when the contest ended on its thirteenth fight and an "already awarded" flag is clear. The flag is set by the lobby's victory scene, which only asks: was this a Master contest, and was it won?

Early in the story the Master course is cut short to 8, 11 or 12 fights. Winning the shortened course is still a win as far as the victory scene is concerned, so it sets the flag and plays the presentation - the announcer names the Icon. But the arena saw fewer than thirteen fights and added nothing. From then on the flag is set, and the arena skips the item every time, including after a full thirteen-fight win.

The last of the three story flags that lengthen the course is set inside Jette's Fortress. That is where "before defeating Jette" comes from.

The tests overwrite one another in order, so the last missing flag decides: with all three missing the course runs 12 fights.
