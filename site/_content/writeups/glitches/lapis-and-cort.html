Lapis and Mystic Shield Cort

Three boss oversights, explained from the game's own code on the USA disc: why the super-boss Lapis stops casting Lapis Wave once the party has left and come back, why that fight can be fled at all, and why Mushura and Theeder land extra hits on Cort while his Mystic Shield is up - and why only Mushura's add up to extra damage.

How to read this page
The causes are read from the game's code and the disc's data. The key numbers are also measured in a running game (PCSX-Redux, retail USA disc): the Mystic Shield hit counts and damage, Lapis's stats, the Lapis Wave MP wipe, the MP check and Jedo's kill. None of those measurements is a played fight at the boss; how the captures were made says exactly what was forced or written by hand, and every result below is labelled where one of those applies. Where the page still predicts something, it says so. All addresses are USA SCUS_942.54 runtime addresses.

Shared vocabulary

Each fighter in a battle has an actor record. Pointers to them sit in a table at 0x801C9370: party in slots 0-2, enemies in slots 3-6, a summoned creature in slot 7. The battle's shared state is the context at *0x8007BD24. The battle logic is PROT entry 0898 (loaded at 0x801CE818); each spell and summon is its own small module, PROT entries 0903-0966, all loaded at 0x801F69D8 (see battle and battle action).

How the captures were made

No save in front of Lapis or Cort was available, so the fights were built from a nearby one. Each measurement on this page has this pedigree:

  • Forced formation. Every fight starts from a retail save state at the Queen Bee fight in Rim Elm - a scripted battle about to begin, with Vahn alone. At the instruction that reads the first byte of that battle's formation row (0x801DA600) the row is overwritten in memory: 01 00 00 01 B4 for Cort, and 00 00 00 01 A7 00 00 00 for Lapis, the exact bytes of the disc's own row. Everything after that read is the game's code on the game's data: the flag, the monster load, the stat boost, the boss's turn logic, Cort casting and losing his own Mystic Shield. What is not the real fight: the party (Vahn alone, at opening-chapter stats), the stage (Rim Elm) and the frame load, which is lighter than with three characters on screen.
  • Injected casts. Vahn owns none of these spells. When his turn is set up, his queued action is rewritten to the spell; the game then loads and runs the spell's module itself.
  • Poked values. Vahn's HP is held at 9000 so he survives, and his MP is topped up. For the summon measurements his magic level for the spell is set to 1 - at the default the damage roll saturates at 9999 and hides the shield's halving. For the "no shield" baseline Cort's HP is written to 25000 and the game breaks the shield itself on the next frame. For the Lapis MP check, Vahn's current MP is written to 0 once. "Captured on a poked state" below means one of these.
  • Which runs are cited. A first batch of these runs held Vahn's HP with a write that began before the battle existed, and so also wrote field memory for the first few seconds. Every experiment was rerun with that write confined to the battle. The reruns gave the same counts, and they are the ones cited; the single exception is marked where it is used.

Lapis Wave checks current MP

Reported as
Lapis Wave tests the party's current MP where it was meant to test maximum MP, so Lapis can be killed outright with magic the party is not supposed to have (community report)
Cause
Lapis decides whether to cast by counting party members with current MP above zero; leaving a battle saves current MP only, so a returning party reads 0 of its full maximum and is never targeted again
Where
Lapis's turn logic at 0x801EBC38 in PROT entry 0898; the wave itself at 0x801F6A04 in PROT entry 0943
Affects
The Lapis fight only
Status
explained - from disassembly and disc data; the wave, the check and the Jedo kill each captured in a forced fight, the flee-and-return route not played through

Lapis Wave is not a damage attack. It sets both the current and the maximum MP of every party member to zero for the rest of that battle. That is why MP items do nothing afterwards: there is no maximum left to fill. In the captured fight Lapis casts it before the party can enter a command, and Vahn's MP goes from 27 / 27 to 0 / 0.

The check the report describes is in Lapis's turn logic, not in the attack. Each time Lapis acts it counts the party members whose current MP is above zero and casts the wave only if it finds one. A party at zero current MP is left alone, whatever its maximum is: with Vahn's current MP written to 0 and his maximum left at 27, Lapis attacked physically on both of its turns and never cast the wave (captured on a poked state).

Leaving a battle writes each member's current MP back to their permanent record and never writes the maximum. So a party that takes one wave and flees walks away at 0 MP with its real maximum untouched. On the return visit Lapis sees nobody with MP and never casts the wave; an MP item works again; and a caster who gets a turn before Lapis's next one can cast.

The spell that ends the fight is Jedo. Its module has two outcomes: against a fight flagged as scripted it deals normal rolled damage, against an unflagged one it sets every living enemy's HP to zero with no roll. The Lapis fight is unflagged (next section), so the kill is certain: an injected Jedo took Lapis from 64800 HP to 0 in a single write. A check on maximum MP would have fired on the returning party and closed the route; whether the designers meant that field is an inference, not something the code states.

These pieces were captured one at a time. The wave, the check at zero MP and the Jedo kill are each observed; the flee, the return visit and the MP item between them are read from the code and have not been played through as one route.

  1. Forced ambushBattle setup compares the first enemy id with 0xA7 (Lapis) and forces a back attack, so Lapis acts before the party can enter a command. Captured: the first action of the fight is Lapis's. 0x80051FC4
  2. The checkLapis's turn counts party actors with +0x150 (current MP) non-zero. None: an ordinary attack. One or more, and Lapis holds 255 MP: Lapis Wave. 0x801EBC68
  3. The waveFor every party seat: current MP and maximum MP both written to zero. Captured: 27 / 27 to 0 / 0, and the cast costs Lapis 255 of its 4000 MP. 0x801F6D08, 0x801F6D1C
  4. The exitFleeing copies current MP to the character record. Maximum MP is not copied. 0x801E7C78
  5. The returnThe battle reloads MP from the record: 0 current, full maximum. The check finds nobody and never fires.

Scope

  • The code does not require the escape. Any party that walks in with every member at zero current MP is never waved. Captured on a poked state: with Vahn's current MP written to 0 before Lapis's first pick and the maximum left at 27, Lapis opened with a physical attack, attacked physically again on its next turn and kept all 4000 MP. Those two turns are all the capture covers. The reported route is flee and return, which works because the wave itself produces the zero.
  • One member holding a single point of MP re-arms the wave for everyone. The count has no "is this member alive" test, so a fallen member's MP counts.
  • Lapis needs 255 MP of its own for each cast.
  • That MP items do nothing at zero maximum is a community report consistent with the code; the item routine's cap was not re-read for this page.

Verifying it

Observed in the forced Lapis fight (forced formation, Vahn alone with 27 MP; see how the captures were made):

  • Lapis acts first, with no command prompt before it, and the action is Lapis Wave: the cast loads PROT entry 0943 and costs Lapis 255 MP.
  • Vahn's +0x150 and +0x152 both go from 27 to 0 during the wave. On its next turn, with the party at 0 / 0, Lapis attacks physically.
  • With +0x150 written to 0 and +0x152 left at 27, no wave is cast. The maximum being non-zero when the check runs is what tells the two fields apart.
  • Jedo, injected: ctx[+0x287] reads 0 at 0x801F87DC, the write at 0x801F8814 zeroes Lapis's 64800 HP, and the damage roll at 0x801F8438 never runs.

The run that shows the wave also carries two unrelated one-byte writes to Vahn's character record (a summon level stash and his Ra-Seru item byte), made for a different question.

Still to check in a played game:

  • After a real escape, read the character record: +0x108 unchanged, +0x10A zero.
  • On the return visit, break at 0x801EBC68: +0x150 reads 0, +0x152 the real maximum, and the branch at 0x801EBC90 is taken.

Open questions

  • Whether the check was meant to read maximum MP. The code is equally consistent with a check written for the in-battle case, where both fields are zero after a wave.
  • Whether the restored caster reliably acts before Lapis (speed 451) was not simulated.
  • The route as a whole (wave, flee, return, MP item, Jedo) has not been played through in one capture; the flee's save of current MP and the MP item rest on the code alone.

Lapis can be escaped from

Reported as
The party can run from Lapis, unlike other bosses (community report)
Cause
"No escape" is one byte at the start of the fight's formation row, and Lapis's row has it at zero
Where
Mt. Dhini scene data (PROT entry 0383), formation row 11; read by the escape roll at 0x801E7B14
Affects
Lapis, plus the Rim Elm opening fights and Gold Face at the Shadow Gate
Status
explained - from disassembly and disc data; the clear flag and Lapis's stats captured in a forced fight, the Run command itself not driven

Each scene carries a table of enemy formations, eight bytes a row. The first byte of a row says "this is a scripted fight". When it is non-zero the game sets a flag for the battle, and the escape roll fails whenever that flag is set. Nothing else makes a fight inescapable: not the script command that starts it, and nothing in the monster's own data.

Lapis's row has zero there. The fight is started by a script command exactly like a boss fight, yet the battle treats it as an ordinary encounter, so Run, the escape accessories and the Spikefish Flute all work. A fight started from that row's exact bytes reads the flag as zero from start to finish; choosing Run was not part of that capture.

Where the flag comes from, FUN_801DA51C in PROT entry 0897, at battle launch:

801da5f8  lw  v0,0x94(s1)         ; the formation row

The latch, FUN_800513F0 in SCUS_942.54, 0x80051430..0x80051444: ctx[+0x287] = (flags >> 5) & 4, stored before any monster is loaded. The same routine then clears bit 0x80 of 0x8007BD60 again (0x80051A14), so during a fight only ctx[+0x287] tells a flagged fight from an unflagged one.

The escape roll, FUN_801E791C in PROT entry 0898:

The script command: in the Mt. Dhini scene data the fight starts with 3E FF 0B at offset 0x3840 - "scripted battle, formation row 11". The command itself writes no flag (see encounter records).

The stat boost, FUN_80054CB0 in SCUS_942.54 (s4 is the monster record):

80055234  lbu  v0,0x287(v0)
8005523c  beq  v0,zero,0x800552ac ; flag clear: second block

Mushura and Theeder against Mystic Shield

Reported as
Mushura and Theeder do excessive damage to Cort while his Mystic Shield is up; Crazy Driver racks up hits on the shield, the only place in the game where that happens (community report)
Cause
Both summons rely on the target's flinch animation to stop repeat hits, and Mystic Shield removes Cort's flinch. Mushura then hits on every tick it touches him; Theeder hits twice, which only cancels the shield's halving
Where
Hit tests at 0x801F7870 (Mushura, PROT entry 0915) and 0x801F7CF0 (Theeder, PROT entry 0904); shield setup at 0x801F7690 (PROT entry 0940)
Affects
Cort's first form for his first 25000 HP
Status
explained - mechanism from disassembly; hit counts and damage measured in a forced Cort fight with injected casts

The two summons turn out to be different cases. Mushura's Crazy Driver really does deal excess damage to shielded Cort: measured, 28 run-over hits where an unshielded cast lands 4, and 2252 damage against 784 - about 2.9 times an unshielded cast. Theeder only slips past the shield: its beam lands 2 hits where an unshielded cast lands 1, each at half strength, for 284 damage against 282. The shield is cancelled out, not exceeded.

Element, overflow and a special formula play no part. Every summon uses the same damage roll, and the shield halves every hit on Cort - these two included.

The difference is how these two decide when to hit. Most summons hit on a fixed beat of their animation. Mushura and Theeder are sweeps: on every tick they test each enemy for contact with the rolling body or the swinging beam, and hit whatever is touching. The one thing that stops them from hitting the same enemy again is a test that the enemy is standing idle. A hit makes the enemy flinch or fall, and an enemy in the middle of that reaction is skipped.

Mystic Shield wipes Cort's four reaction animations so that he stands unmoved behind it. Animation number zero is the idle stance. So a hit now tells Cort to play "idle", he is still idle at the next contact, and the sweep hits him again. Mushura rolls back and forth across him and stays in contact for many ticks. Theeder's beam crosses him exactly twice.

Mule, a Ra-Seru summon built the same way, has an extra line at the same spot: if Mystic Shield is up, skip the hit. Mushura and Theeder lack it. Three summons share the pattern and one of them is guarded.

How far the numbers carry
Each figure is one cast in a forced fight with a one-character party. Theeder's count of two depends only on the sweep's step: the same arithmetic gives two cone hits for frame steps of 2, 3 and 4, one at 5 and six at 1 (only 4 was observed; the rest is calculation). Mushura's count is a count of contact ticks, so it moves with the frame rate: the measured cast ran at a frame step of 3 to 4, and the real fight, with three characters on screen, runs heavier frames and should see fewer contact ticks. The 28-to-4 ratio is a measurement of that run, not a constant.

Scope

  • Only Cort's first form (id 0xB4, scene chitei2, formation row 13) while the shield handle is set: his first 25000 of 50000 HP.
  • The cause is "no reaction clips", so any monster whose data supplies no reaction clips at all would be hit the same way by these two summons. That set has not been listed.
  • Each extra hit also earns its own summon experience, applies its own Seru side-effect and shows its own damage number: twice for Theeder, 28 times for the measured Mushura cast.
  • The shield can break in the middle of a Mushura cast. Once Cort is at or below half HP the per-frame pass restores his clips, one more hit lands on the clip that was already staged, and the rest of the cast is one hit per contact window. This was seen in one run from the first batch described under how the captures were made (the batch whose HP hold also wrote field memory before the battle), with the magic level left alone so that each hit took 2499: Cort crossed 25000 on the tenth run-over hit and the cast ended with 13.
  • Mule is the opposite case by its code: under the shield its sweep is written not to hit at all.

Verifying it

The three checks, as run on the forced Cort fight:

  • Execute breakpoints at 0x801F789C (Mushura) and 0x801F7D38 (Theeder) count 28 and 2 with the shield up, 4 and 1 once the game has broken it.
  • +0x1D9 of the actor at *(0x801C9370 + 0xC) reads 0 throughout under the shield; without it, 3 after a Mushura hit and 4 after a Theeder hit.
  • Writing 03 02 04 05 to that actor's +0x1EF..+0x1F2 with the shield up returns the counts to 4 and 1 while the rolls stay halved. The missing reaction is the cause and the shield handle is not.

Still to check: the same breakpoints in the real chitei2 fight, with three party members on screen, give Mushura's count at the real frame timing.
