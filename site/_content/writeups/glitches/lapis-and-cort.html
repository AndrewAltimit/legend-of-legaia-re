Lapis and Mystic Shield Cort

Three boss oversights, explained from the game's own code on the USA disc: why the super-boss Lapis stops casting Lapis Wave once the party has left and come back, why that fight can be fled at all, and why Mushura and Theeder pile hits onto Cort while his Mystic Shield is up.

How to read this page
Every address below is read from the disassembly or decoded from the disc. Nothing here was captured on an emulator: where the page says what a debugger will show, that is a prediction from the code, and each section ends with the check that would confirm it. All addresses are USA SCUS_942.54 runtime addresses.

Shared vocabulary

Each fighter in a battle has an actor record. Pointers to them sit in a table at 0x801C9370: party in slots 0-2, enemies in slots 3-6, a summoned creature in slot 7. The battle's shared state is the context at *0x8007BD24. The battle logic is PROT entry 0898 (loaded at 0x801CE818); each spell and summon is its own small module, PROT entries 0903-0966, all loaded at 0x801F69D8 (see battle and battle action).

Lapis Wave checks current MP

Reported as
Lapis Wave tests the party's current MP where it was meant to test maximum MP, so Lapis can be killed outright with magic the party is not supposed to have (community report)
Cause
Lapis decides whether to cast by counting party members with current MP above zero; leaving a battle saves current MP only, so a returning party reads 0 of its full maximum and is never targeted again
Where
Lapis's turn logic at 0x801EBC38 in PROT entry 0898; the wave itself at 0x801F6A04 in PROT entry 0943
Affects
The Lapis fight only
Status
explained - from disassembly and disc data

Lapis Wave is not a damage attack. It sets both the current and the maximum MP of every party member to zero for the rest of that battle. That is why MP items do nothing afterwards: there is no maximum left to fill.

The check the report describes is in Lapis's turn logic, not in the attack. Each time Lapis acts it counts the party members whose current MP is above zero and casts the wave only if it finds one. A party at zero current MP is left alone, whatever its maximum is.

Leaving a battle writes each member's current MP back to their permanent record and never writes the maximum. So a party that takes one wave and flees walks away at 0 MP with its real maximum untouched. On the return visit Lapis sees nobody with MP and never casts the wave; an MP item works again; and a caster who gets a turn before Lapis's next one can cast.

The spell that ends the fight is Jedo. Its module has two outcomes: against a fight flagged as scripted it deals normal rolled damage, against an unflagged one it sets every living enemy's HP to zero with no roll. The Lapis fight is unflagged (next section), so the kill is certain. A check on maximum MP would have fired on the returning party and closed the route; whether the designers meant that field is an inference, not something the code states.

  1. Forced ambushBattle setup compares the first enemy id with 0xA7 (Lapis) and forces a back attack, so Lapis acts before the party can enter a command. 0x80051FC4
  2. The checkLapis's turn counts party actors with +0x150 (current MP) non-zero. None: an ordinary attack. One or more, and Lapis holds 255 MP: Lapis Wave. 0x801EBC68
  3. The waveFor every party seat: current MP and maximum MP both written to zero. 0x801F6D08, 0x801F6D1C
  4. The exitFleeing copies current MP to the character record. Maximum MP is not copied. 0x801E7C78
  5. The returnThe battle reloads MP from the record: 0 current, full maximum. The check finds nobody and never fires.
  6. The killJedo on an unflagged fight sets each living enemy's HP to 0. 0x801F8814

801ebc68  lhu   v0,0x150(v0)      ; current MP

The wave, FUN_801F6A04 in PROT entry 0943, third arm, once per party seat. The module calls no damage routine anywhere:

801f6cf4  lhu v0,0x150(v1)
801f6cfc  sh  v0,0x178(v1)        ; MP readout drains from the old value
801f6d08  sh  zero,0x150(v0)      ; current MP = 0
801f6d1c  sh  zero,0x152(v1)      ; maximum MP = 0

The exit, FUN_801E791C in PROT entry 0898: actor +0x150 is stored to the character record's current MP (+0x10A) at 0x801E7C78..0x801E7C80. The routine has no store of +0x152.

Jedo, FUN_801F6A58 in PROT entry 0931:

801f87d4  lbu v0,0x287(v1)        ; scripted-fight flag
801f87dc  bne v0,zero,0x801f8844
801f8804  lhu v0,0x14c(a0)        ; each enemy seat 3..6
801f8814  sh  zero,0x14c(a0)      ; HP = 0
801f8820  sb  a2,0x21c(v0)        ; defeat fade

Nighto (PROT entry 0907) also reaches its kill on this fight, as a roll: the resist test is skipped when the flag is clear (0x801F6BF0), the resist roll is rand % (0x13 - magic_level) < 9 (0x801F6C28), and the kill is rand % 8 == 0 (0x801F6B50, store at 0x801F7E58). At magic level 9 that is about 11% per cast.

Scope

  • The code does not require the escape. Any party that walks in with every member at zero current MP is never waved. That is a prediction from the check; the reported route is flee and return, which works because the wave itself produces the zero.
  • One member holding a single point of MP re-arms the wave for everyone. The count has no "is this member alive" test, so a fallen member's MP counts.
  • Lapis needs 255 MP of its own for each cast.
  • That MP items do nothing at zero maximum is a community report consistent with the code; the item routine's cap was not re-read for this page.

Verifying it

  • Break at 0x801EBC68 on Lapis's turn. v0 is a party actor: read +0x150 and +0x152. First visit: execution reaches 0x801EBCB4. Return visit: +0x150 is 0, +0x152 is the real maximum, and the branch at 0x801EBC90 is taken.
  • Write-watch a party actor's +0x152 during the cast; the predicted writer is 0x801F6D1C.
  • After fleeing, read the character record: +0x108 unchanged, +0x10A zero.
  • Break at 0x801F87DC while Jedo is cast on Lapis: ctx[+0x287] reads 0.

Open questions

  • Whether the check was meant to read maximum MP. The code is equally consistent with a check written for the in-battle case, where both fields are zero after a wave.
  • Whether the restored caster reliably acts before Lapis (speed 451) was not simulated.

Lapis can be escaped from

Reported as
The party can run from Lapis, unlike other bosses (community report)
Cause
"No escape" is one byte at the start of the fight's formation row, and Lapis's row has it at zero
Where
Mt. Dhini scene data (PROT entry 0383), formation row 11; read by the escape roll at 0x801E7B14
Affects
Lapis, plus the Rim Elm opening fights and Gold Face at the Shadow Gate
Status
explained - from disassembly and disc data

Each scene carries a table of enemy formations, eight bytes a row. The first byte of a row says "this is a scripted fight". When it is non-zero the game sets a flag for the battle, and the escape roll fails whenever that flag is set. Nothing else makes a fight inescapable: not the script command that starts it, and nothing in the monster's own data.

Lapis's row has zero there. The fight is started by a script command exactly like a boss fight, yet the battle treats it as an ordinary encounter, so Run, the escape accessories and the Spikefish Flute all work.

+0x0000scripted?
+0x0100 00reserved
+0x0301count
+0x04A7Lapis
+0x0500 00 00empty

Lapis: Mt. Dhini (deene), row 11, at offset 0x38C3 of the decoded scene data.

+0x0001scripted?
+0x0100 00reserved
+0x0301count
+0x04B4Cort
+0x0500 00 00empty

For comparison, Cort: scene chitei2, row 13. The only difference that matters is the first byte.

The same flag feeds every other boss protection in the battle code, so the Lapis fight loses those as well. This is what lets the instant-kill summons of the previous section land.

Mushura and Theeder against Mystic Shield

Reported as
Mushura and Theeder do excessive damage to Cort while his Mystic Shield is up; Crazy Driver racks up hits on the shield, the only place in the game where that happens (community report)
Cause
Both summons rely on the target's flinch animation to stop repeat hits, and Mystic Shield removes Cort's flinch
Where
Hit tests at 0x801F7870 (Mushura, PROT entry 0915) and 0x801F7CF0 (Theeder, PROT entry 0904); shield setup at 0x801F7690 (PROT entry 0940)
Affects
Cort's first form for his first 25000 HP
Status
explained - mechanism from disassembly; the size of the excess is an estimate

Element, overflow and a special formula play no part. Every summon uses the same damage roll, and the shield halves every hit on Cort - these two included.

The difference is how these two decide when to hit. Most summons hit on a fixed beat of their animation. Mushura and Theeder are sweeps: on every tick they test each enemy for contact with the rolling body or the swinging beam, and hit whatever is touching. The one thing that stops them from hitting the same enemy tick after tick is a test that the enemy is standing idle. A hit makes the enemy flinch, and a flinching enemy is skipped.

Mystic Shield wipes Cort's four reaction animations so that he stands unmoved behind it. Animation number zero is the idle stance. So a hit now tells Cort to play "idle", he is still idle on the next tick, and the sweep hits him again for as long as it overlaps him.

Mule, a Ra-Seru summon built the same way, has an extra line at the same spot: if Mystic Shield is up, skip the hit. Mushura and Theeder lack it. Three summons share the pattern and one of them is guarded.

  1. Shield goes upThe Mystic Shield module stores its effect handle in 0x8007BD84 and zeroes Cort's reaction clips +0x1EF..+0x1F2. 0x801F7678, 0x801F7690
  2. Every hit is halvedWhile the handle is set, the damage routine halves any hit on an enemy. 0x801DDB98
  3. The sweep tests for idleMushura and Theeder skip an enemy whose playing clip +0x1D9 is not zero. 0x801F7870, 0x801F7CF0
  4. The hit stages a flinchAfter the damage, the module stages the clip id held in +0x1EF - which the shield has set to zero.
  5. Still idleClip zero is the idle clip, so +0x1D9 stays zero and the next tick hits again.
  6. Shield breaksAt half HP the per-frame pass restores the clips to 3, 2, 4, 5 and clears the handle. 0x8004D5C4

The halving, FUN_801DDB30 in PROT entry 0898, 0x801DDB98..0x801DDBC8: with an enemy defender and the handle non-null, the attack value becomes def + (atk - def) / 2.

Scope

  • Only Cort's first form (id 0xB4, scene chitei2, formation row 13) while the shield handle is set: his first 25000 of 50000 HP.
  • The cause is "no reaction clips", so any monster whose data supplies no flinch at all would be hit the same way by these two summons. That set has not been listed.
  • Each extra hit also earns its own summon experience, applies its own Seru side-effect and adds to the damage number.
  • Mule is the opposite case: under the shield its sweep does not hit at all.
The multiple is an estimate
For Theeder, the beam sits inside the hit cone for about 12% of its sweep. If the sweep lasts 64 ticks that is roughly 6 to 8 hits, each halved by the shield: on the order of three to four times an ordinary single hit, where the shield's rule alone would give half. The tick count is an assumption (the real step depends on frame timing) and no hit count has been measured. Mushura deals one eighth of a roll per contact tick; how many ticks it stays in contact has not been worked out.

Verifying it

  • Cast Mushura on shielded Cort with an execute breakpoint at 0x801F789C (Theeder: 0x801F7D38) and count the hits in one cast. Repeat once Cort is at or below 25000 HP.
  • Watch +0x1D9 of the actor at *(0x801C9370 + 0xC) during the cast: predicted to stay 0 under the shield, and to read 3 after a hit without it.
  • With the shield up, write 3 to that actor's +0x1EF before the cast. If the extra hits stop, the missing flinch is the cause and the shield handle is not.

Open questions

  • Hit counts per cast, which depend on frame timing.
  • Whether Theeder is meant to hit again when the beam swings back over a target that has returned to idle. That sets the unshielded baseline.
  • Which monsters, if any, ship with no reaction clips.
