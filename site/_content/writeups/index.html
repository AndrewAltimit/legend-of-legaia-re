Technical write-ups

Long-form walk-throughs of how a piece of the game was reverse-engineered and what the project built on top of it. The rest of the site is reference - per-format byte specs, per-subsystem function maps; these pages are the narrative: the reasoning, the dead ends, and the payoff, told in reading order.

Reading list

Three write-ups so far. Each one starts from something a player has actually seen - a randomizer seed, a battle that never ends, a fish that never bites - and follows it down into the disc. You do not need to have read the reference pages first; each article links out to them where the detail lives.

Patching a sealed disc

What it is about. How the randomizer rewrites a retail Legaia disc with no source and no SDK - told as a ladder of six injection techniques, from same-size byte edits, through editing inside compression and rewriting field-VM bytecode, up to hand-assembled MIPS hosted in a resident overlay's dead space (the in-shop Seru-trading vendor).

Why you might care. If you have ever wondered how a "randomizer" can exist for a game that was never meant to be modified, this is the answer, one technique per page, with the mods as worked examples. Includes a generated mod catalog + roadmap.

The endless camera orbit

What it is about. Anatomy of a retail softlock: the 25-year-old "battle freezes while the camera circles the party" bug, caught live, traced to an approach animation that dies with nothing to re-stage it, reproduced on demand (a summon immediately before the boss's melee), and fixed with a nine-word in-place rewrite that lets the game's own staging state repair it.

Why you might care. If Gaza's second fight ever ate a save of yours, this is what happened - and the fix ships in the browser ROM patcher.

The Spirit fish gate

What it is about. Fishing's rarest catch, decompiled: why the observed Spirit catch rate never matched the published 1-in-32 model. The rare spawn column is unreachable by the random roll - it sits behind a six-condition strike-time gate (a saved cast counter over 50 and even, the right lure and rod, the rare band, then 1-in-16) - and the "Good!" splash fires for four reel cadences of which only the Square-ending one sets the rare band.

Why you might care. Twenty years of player folklore about the Spirit, each observation mapped to the mechanism that produced it, with the real per-catch probabilities.

How to read these

Each write-up leads with the reverse engineering - the static and runtime evidence that pinned a function, an offset, or a structure - and only then describes what was built. Addresses are PSX virtual addresses (0x801C0000+ is the overlay window); function labels are Ghidra's FUN_8xxxxxxx entry points, cross-referenced to the per-subsystem and per-format reference pages. No Sony-owned bytes appear here or anywhere in the repo - only addresses, offsets, and the project's own clean-room code.
