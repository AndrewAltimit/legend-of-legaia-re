Technical write-ups

Each write-up starts from something a player has seen - a randomizer seed, a battle that never ends, a fish that never bites - and follows it down into the disc. The rest of the site is reference; these pages are the narrative: what the finding is, how the mechanism works, and what the project built on it.

Reading list

No reference page is required reading first - each article links out to the byte-level detail where it lives.

Patching a sealed disc

What it is about. How the randomizer rewrites a retail Legaia disc with no source and no SDK - a ladder of six injection techniques, from same-size byte edits up to hand-assembled MIPS hosted in a menu overlay's dead space (the in-shop Seru-trading vendor).

Why you might care. If you have wondered how a randomizer can exist for a game that was never meant to be modified, this is the answer, one technique per page, with a generated mod catalog + roadmap.

The endless camera orbit

What it is about. Anatomy of a retail softlock: the "battle freezes while the camera circles the party" bug, traced to an approach animation that dies with nothing to re-stage it, reproduced on demand, and fixed with a nine-word in-place rewrite.

Why you might care. If Gaza's second fight ever ate a save of yours, this is what happened - and the fix ships in the browser ROM patcher.

The Spirit fish gate

What it is about. Fishing's rarest catch, decompiled: the Spirit's spawn column is not on the dice at all. It sits behind a six-condition strike-time gate, and only one of the four "Good!" reel cadences selects it.

Why you might care. Twenty years of player folklore about the Spirit, each observation mapped to the mechanism that produced it, with the real per-catch probabilities.

How to read these

  • Each page opens with the finding in plain words, then the mechanism, then a How we know table with the function addresses and dump files.
  • Addresses are PlayStation virtual addresses. 0x8000xxxx is the always-resident executable; 0x801C0000+ is the window the game pages its per-mode overlays into.
  • Register-level walkthroughs sit in collapsed Details blocks - open them when you want the proof.
  • No Sony-owned bytes appear here or anywhere in the repo - only addresses, offsets, and the project's own clean-room code.
