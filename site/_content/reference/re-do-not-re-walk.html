Falsified RE readings - do not re-walk

Every reverse-engineering project accumulates readings of the bytes that looked right, were believed, and turned out wrong. This page keeps Legaia's, with the reasoning intact: why each one was convincing, and what finally killed it. Check here before committing to a reading that looks obvious - someone may already have spent a week disproving it.

What a falsified reading is, and why we keep it

A reverse engineer looks at a routine, a table or a container and forms a story about what it does. Most stories survive contact with the disassembly or a runtime capture. The ones on this page did not - but each was a plausible story, usually backed by real evidence that was being read the wrong way. Deleting the row would throw away the most useful part: the shape of the trap, which tends to recur in other subsystems.

Rows here are terminal. If new evidence reopens one, it moves back to open RE threads rather than having its verdict edited in place, because the falsification trail is what makes the row worth reading. The answered questions - and how firmly each is pinned - live on settled threads.

Two traps that recur often enough to name up front

  • VA aliasing. A bare virtual address is not an identity. The game swaps different overlays into the same RAM window (0x801CE818 for slot A, 0x801F69D8 for slot B), so the bytes at one address change with the game mode, and a dump labelled by address can be a different function entirely. See no overlay function lives below 0x801CE818.
  • Ghidra's collapsed switch. A jump table's arms can render in the decompiled C as bare breaks or as fake FUN_x calls, inventing opcode semantics that the raw table does not have. Several rows below were born from exactly this - the cure is always the disassembly, never the C (Ghidra tooling).

World map / kingdom bundles

The overworld you walk between towns is built from three kingdom bundles, and one slot of each - slot 4 - resisted interpretation for a long time. Two readings of it are dead.

There is no slot-4 to cluster-A converter

Verdict: falsified. Slot-4 bytes never become cluster-A TMDs.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The cluster-A mesh pool (DAT_8007C018) is filled exclusively by FUN_80026B4C, reached only from the asset dispatcher FUN_8001f05c at case 0x02 (TMD pack) and case 0x09 (bare TMD). Slot 4's type byte is 0x05, whose case merely allocates the MOVE buffer _DAT_8007B888 and never calls FUN_80026B4C. So the DAT_8007C018 kingdom entries are the scene's own type-0x02 field-file TMD pack(s), installed by the single FUN_80020224 descriptor walk.

Slot 4 is not a coastline wireframe

Verdict: falsified. The bodies are small object-local 3D meshes; treat any future "kingdom border lines" claim with suspicion.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Visual inspection plus the slot-4 record-semantic work refuted the "world-map overlay outlines / coastline wireframe" interpretation. Each 8-byte record is a GTE vertex read in place by the renderer; the full layout is on the world-map overlay page.

Battle / arts / level-up

Battle is where most of the game's logic lives, and most of it sits in RAM overlays that share addresses with other overlays - so it is also where the most readings died. The short rows first, then the ones whose story needs room.

Move-VM op 0x2F has one extension dispatcher, not one per overlay

Verdict: falsified - one copy, field overlay 0897 only. Battle-side move records cannot use it.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The capture-derived _801d362c dumps are identical to each other (0897 observed under world-map / dialog / cutscene scenario labels); the 0897 static dump is a strict subset of them, not a byte-identical twin (Ghidra could not follow the jump-table flow). Every other mapped slot-A overlay and the title overlay carry unrelated bytes at the fixed call VA and no jump table at 0x801CE868, so op 0x2F is executable only while 0897 is resident. See the move-VM overlay extension doc.

FUN_801F3894 is not the spirit/magic damage roll

Verdict: falsified - a VA-aliased dump. The real state-0x3D callee FUN_801F3990 is a cast audio-cue dispatcher; spirit damage is state 0x3E's inline formula.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The overlay_0897_801f3894 dump is FUN_801DD0AC byte-for-byte under a double VA shift, so the already-ported damage kernel surfaces at a fake entry VA. Corollary, widened: 801Exxxx dumps are suspect too, not just the 0x801F band - 801f0348 and 801e23ec are settled casualties, the latter's aliased reading having dropped all three initiative modifier terms; 0x801F1ED4 / 0x801F45A4 are unverified. See battle formulas.

A level-up is not a heal

Verdict: falsified. Levelling up raises HP and MP maxima by the growth amount; the current pools do not move. The "refill" the captures showed is the battle-end resync.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The reading came from a multi-level capture triplet whose third frame writes the live current-pool cells (+0x106 / +0x10A) right after a level-up, and it was written into capture_observations::char_level_up as "the level-up refill". The port encoded it in LevelUpTracker::apply_to_record, which made every level-up a free full heal.

Two independent checks falsify it:

  • Disassembly. FUN_801E9504 stores to exactly eleven addresses: the record window's hp_max / mp_max, its six battle stats, the displayed-level byte and its actor-table mirror, and two globals. No live-window cell is among them, and the routine's only jal is the BIOS rand. Identical in both dumps of the routine.
  • Capture. The single-level noa_levelup_* triplet reads Noa at 164/182 HP and 16/16 MP going into the fight and 164/221 / 16/21 once the L2 to L3 level-up has settled. Both maxima move; neither current moves.

Generalises to: a write that lands near an event is not a write by it. A frame-window capture cannot separate the two on its own - only the store set of the routine can.

The cast-module blocker was named wrong

Verdict: falsified. A party actor can have its animation indices resolved by a streamed signature-attack module; the "no monster block" impossibility hid three real blockers, one of them a silent memory corruption.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The reading was that a streamed module "stages the caster's monster-block entries by raw index, and a party actor has no monster block". The second half is false. FUN_8004AD80 resolves actor+0x1DA down two arms - monster seats through DAT_801C9348[slot-3]+0x4C, party seats through DAT_801C9360[slot] - and the indices PROT 960 stages resolve on both. Its one monster-block access is a hardcoded seat-0 write to a single clip's root-motion field, unrelated to who is casting.

Three real blockers were behind it:

  • That seat-0 read walks past magic_count into words the loader never fixed up, so a seat-0 monster with magic_count <= 13 (Che Delilas has 12) turns the pointer into a bare offset and the following store lands a halfword in PSX kernel RAM. Retail never trips it because the module is only ever reached with Lu (16 entries) in seat 0.
  • Battle state 0x70 re-enters the module every frame and advances only on a zero return; no timer, no bail-out. One phase gate needs a clip of at least 23 keyframes, which Gala's party index 0x0D fails at 17 of 19 equippable section-2 ids.
  • The damage call and both HP writes are hardcoded to actor slot 0, not the chosen target.

Generalises to: a structural-sounding impossibility ("X has no Y") reads as settled and stops the search, so the actual failures never get enumerated. Full account on the randomizer page.

The attack camera was never an arm choice

Verdict: falsified. Every attack-camera arm is timed for a ~20-frame swing and none is spare, so an art that films flat cannot be fixed by pointing it at a "better" arm - only by re-timing (or swapping) in place.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

FUN_801D71B8 dispatches the per-art attack camera through three per-character jump tables; the arms visibly differ in choreography (one shot for the whole swing vs two or three), and 37 of the tables' 54 slots point at a bare return. So a flat-filming art looked like a poor dispatch, and the fix looked like retargeting its slot.

  • Every arm is timed, not merely shaped. A shot change is an slti against the animation cursor actor[+0x22C][+0x68] (sixteenths of a keyframe). The arm span carries thirteen such tests with immediates 64 / 97 / 112 / 144 / 160 / 176 / 192 / 224 / 240 / 272 - keyframe 4 through 17, sized for the ~20-frame swings retail authors. Aimed at a 46-100 frame clip, any arm finishes its choreography inside the wind-up and then holds one shot.
  • Not one arm is spare. The 37 dead slots all point at a single shared epilogue (0x801D828C). The 17 live slots reach 13 distinct arms, no arm is live in more than one character's table, and every one is already some art's camera. A retarget can only alias an arm another art still uses.

What the arms do admit is re-timing in place: scale an arm's immediates and its shots land at the same beats of the longer swing. Where a host art's arm carries no cursor test, the move is a swap of two slots inside one character's table. See Delilas party swap on the randomizer page.

Generalises to: "pick a better one" presumes the candidates differ along the axis the defect lies on. A table that is two-thirds dead slots reads as spare capacity while the things those slots would point at are fully subscribed.

There is no navmesh at 0x80108EA4

Verdict: falsified. 0x80108EA4..0x80109550 is per-scene GPU primitive scratch, not a 24-byte-stride navmesh.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The window differs across area-load saves, which is what a navmesh would do - but pointer hunts find zero RAM cells pointing into it. Real per-scene region / collision / event-trigger data lives in the field-file preamble (a count + u16 offset table + records - not the field-pack schema slots, which are a global-constant template); the collision grid is the +0x4000 MAP region; the encounter-record path lives at actor[+0x94]. Recorded on its own page: navmesh (negative finding).

Op-0x4E sub-op family - every sub-op 0..9 is a compare

Verdict: falsified. The "absolute jump" (sub-ops 5..8) and "rand to next PC" (sub-op 4) readings were Ghidra's collapsed switch; all ten sub-ops load a value and join one 7-byte compare-and-skip.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The raw 12-entry jump table at 0x801CEE30 (field overlay, PROT 0897 file +0x618) routes every sub-op 0..9 to a value loader that joins the shared continuation at 0x801E0B40:

801d58f0 / 801d63b0 are not single shared port blockers

Verdict: falsified - a VA-aliasing artifact. The two addresses host different code in different overlays.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Byte-verified: 80/228/124/308/1 bytes and 208/1036 bytes across 0897 / baka / cutscene / debug-menu / fishing / slot / dance. The port catalog's bare-VA keying aggregated their references into phantom top blockers. Tracked per overlay via overlay_<label>_<addr> identities; catalog ignore category va_aliased_overlay_local. See the port catalog.

The charm softlock is not an unbounded reroll

Verdict: falsified. The reroll loops in FUN_801E7320 cannot spin from any reachable state; the real defect is downstream in the 0x5A victory arm's roster indexing.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The loops are unbounded in isolation, but every reachable caller state has an exit: the scheduler FUN_801DABA4 never seeds a dead actor (predicate +0x14C != 0 && !(+0x16E & 0x4)), the acting 0x380 monster is itself an in-band self-pick exit (0x801E73E8 clears +0x1DE), and a band with zero living members means the previous 0x5A already fired the wipe. Lesson: an unbounded loop hangs only under a reachable all-invalid state - check the predicates feeding it first. See battle.

Gaza 2 0x51 park - the two falsified generators

Verdict: both first-pass "ordinary play" generators of the HP-readout desync are falsified. What remains is recorded on settled threads under the endless camera orbit.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Clamp asymmetry as a standalone generator. The two overkill clamps in FUN_801EC3E4 (accumulator vs displayed bar at 0x801EDB70, live HP vs itself at 0x801EEA10) can only disagree when the bar already lags live HP at action start - and the previous party-targeted action's own 0x51 settle wait guarantees it does not. The "live HP 266 / bar 0 / zero accumulator" exhibit was per-strike phased crediting (paired stores 0x801EDB40/0x801EDB58 credit per strike while live HP commits once), a transient that closed with a death commit ~90 vsyncs later. A per-frame watchpoint cannot distinguish an absorbing desync from the inside of a healthy multi-strike resolution.

The Final Heal revive "at the worst possible moment". The assigning seed (0x800410BC) is real and the discard arithmetic stands, but the timing premise does not survive measurement: credits land per strike early; state 0x50 arrives after remaining targets resolve; the quarter-step drain empties any accumulator within ~35 rendered frames. A three-capture campaign (autorun_gaza2_acc_discard.lua, Lost-Grail-armed party, ~84k vsyncs) drove twelve retail FUN_801E6968 revives across cast-path, kernel-path, single-target and party-wide kills: every assign hit +0x10 == 0, margins 143-280 vsyncs.

Muscle Dome was never a card battle

Verdict: falsified. The arena is an ordinary battle whose command string is bounded by AP; the on-screen number is the opponent's HP percentage; and the four-turn strip belongs to the boss Koru, not the dome.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

"A hand of four cards." FUN_801d388c case 9 builds four slots in a do { } while (< 4) loop, which reads like a deal. The four slots are the four d-pad directions, always command ids 0xC..=0xF, each carrying that fighter's own AP cost. Nothing is drawn, discarded or reshuffled.

"A score of hp * 0x6C / max." The compiler renders × 100 as a shift-add chain: sll 1, addu, sll 3, addu, sll 2 at 0x801d0f38..0x801d0f4c. Stopping at the fourth instruction yields 25, and folding the wrong pair yields 0x6C (108). A multiplier read off a shift-add chain is only correct if you consume the whole chain - and Ghidra's own C prints * 100.

"Rendered in phase 0x6e, per fighter." The computation lives in the phase-0x14 arm; 0x6e only re-stamps the two globals. The record it reads is DAT_801c937c - actor-table index 3, the first enemy slot - so there is one number on screen. What the arm draws is the Turns Left / HP Left strip: 4 - ctx[+0x28a] and the first enemy's HP percentage.

"...and four turns is the whole dome leg." Also wrong. The arm is gated on *(u8*)0x8007BD0C == 0xB6, and that cell is the monster-id formation cell, not a battle-type byte. The dome stages its opponents out of a 29-round table topping out at id 0xAA, so no dome round can reach it. The strip belongs to monster 0xB6 - Koru, whose four-turn timed kill the curated boss table records independently. A byte compared against a small constant is not a mode tag until you have found its writer.

A separate widget must not be folded into this one: FUN_801d8de8 is the shared battle status plate, drawing each fighter's own HP/MP numerals. It computes no percentage and is not dome-specific.

The dome victory caption is not a prize

Verdict: falsified. A won Muscle Dome leg awards nothing; a contest pays casino coins, and the Master-course final fight hands over one item once per save. No Seru is ever captured there.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

FUN_801D8DE8 case 0x59 composes a victory line out of a per-character label from the table at 0x801F4DFC plus a spell name from the shared spell-name table at ctx[+0x269] + 0x80 - the player Seru-magic block. Read on its own that is a convincing award message, and the port credited a Seru capture on a won leg.

The table is shared: 0x801F4DFC is byte-identical across the battle-action, magic-capture, magic-level-up and dome overlays, and the composer is the ordinary cast-caption builder reached by any cast in any battle. Its presence in the dome overlay is residency, not a dome feature. And the whole reward path in PROT 0977 is FUN_801D0F60: it settles the score tally and, once per save on the Master-course final fight, hands over item 0xCD. The tally is paid by the shared minigame-exit routine FUN_80026018 into the casino coin bank 0x800845A4, saturating at 9,999,999.

Lesson: a caption that names a reward is not a reward. Find the writer of the thing being credited.

FUN_801DBC30 is not the battle name plate

Verdict: falsified. Its texture page and CLUT resolve to the effect page's red cross-out X - the mark laid over a command chip the actor cannot pick.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The blit sits in the battle overlay next to the party-name panel's open and teardown leaves, takes an (x, y), and lays down one 0x40 x 0x10 textured quad. A fixed-size strip, drawn at a caller-supplied seat, in the one function group that builds the name buffers - almost irresistible. The port acted on it.

The quad's own constants falsify it: tpage 7 resolves to VRAM page (448, 0) and CLUT 0x7704 to (64, 476). That is the etim effect page, and the texel span (0, 96)-(63, 111) decodes out of a battle VRAM dump as the red X. The real name plates are 3-slice runs off the system-UI sheet's page (896, 256).

Lesson: a primitive builder is identified by the page and palette it samples, not by the neighbourhood it is compiled into.

The party HUD has no gauge bars

Verdict: falsified. There is no bar primitive in either party readout's packet run - retail shows numerals only. Geometry on the battle page.

A kind named by its seat can name the wrong record

Verdict: falsified. Screen-element kinds 0x32/0x33 are not "the roster panels"; the three roster-panel placement records carry kind 0x07.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Before the +0x0E kind byte was resolved as a table index, the open thread listed undecoded values by the surface each one sat under: 0x0303 "message rows", 0x0404 "framed windows", 0x2B2B "the status bar", 0x32/0x33 "the roster panels". Four survive the decode; the panel one does not. 0x33/0x34/0x35 are sibling kinds that add the level / status marker on top of the same panel chain - their chain walks into the same 0x08 to 0x09 plate that 0x07's ends on, so the two draw the same pixels. Only the index arithmetic (0x800732A4 + kind * 0x0C, FUN_8002C69C) separates them. Resolution on settled threads.

The battle message banner has no interior fill

Verdict: falsified. The banner is a border and a glyph run; the scene shows through.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Two live frames carrying the banner (the mid-battle Seru "captured!" line and the level-up banner) draw the class-0 9-slice border sprites and the glyphs and nothing else - no textured fill, no flat quad, no semi-transparent rect. What made "gold border over blue interior" natural is that the framed-window widget records (0x03 / 0x04 / 0x44) carry a 32x32 blue-marbled patch at texels (128, 0), and the framed menu windows do fill with it. The art exists; the battle banner does not use it.

The flash ramp is the Arts announcement banner

Verdict: falsified. FUN_801E2524 / FUN_801E2650 do not fade the screen - they slide the words SUPER ARTS!! / HYPER ARTS!! / MIRACLE ARTS!! / NEW ARTS!! in from opposite sides.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

FUN_801E2650 scales a percent into grey, replicates it into RGB, picks GP0 0x2C or 0x2E, and emits quads whose extent is driven by a level byte. On the arithmetic alone that is a flash, and it was documented as one.

The quads are textured. Every arm writes texpage 0x27 = (448, 0) under CBA 0x7703; decoding that page at 4bpp through that sub-palette shows the emitter's three 24-tall rows are the words SUPER, HYPER, MIRACLE + NEW, directly above the DAMAGE / HIT / TOTAL labels on the same sheet; the second quad's fixed texel rect reads ARTS!!. ctx[+0x28C] is the slide's clock, and the four "layers" are a ghost trail behind the moving word.

Lesson: a routine that emits textured primitives is not characterised until its texels are decoded. See battle action and the effect bundle sheet layout.

Three readings the port already satisfied

Verdict: all three falsified - each described retail behaviour the port was already reproducing.

Why they looked right, and what killed them
  • "The port never latches the art id, so actor[+0x1DB] reads 0 all fight." It latches 0x01, then 0x0D, then 0x0C - the rolled arm swings a basic Attack queues, and a retail mid-swing Attack state reads the same band. 0x1A..=0x2D, the per-art camera's band, is reached by action-constant queue bytes, i.e. an arts chain, so the camera not arming on two arm swings is retail.
  • "The port's 0x51 Done-band residency is unbounded." Bounded at ctx[+0x6D8] = 0x3C, as retail. The 60-70 frames a sample shows is one action's countdown plus the HP-bar settle freeze; a multi-action sample sums several. Counting frames in a band without splitting them by action reads a per-action budget as a park.
  • "The sparring tutorial reopens the command session, so the cursor cannot move." The session is reopened only on a rejected resolution; the cursor walks 0..5. What pins it on screen is retail's own ctx[+0x6B2] box guard, which the port reproduces.

Details on battle action.

An action does not return combatants to their formation seats

Verdict: falsified. Retail leaves each combatant on the ground the action ended on; the seat is committed from the live position at cleanup.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Audio / sound driver

The sound side is PsyQ library code plus a thin Legaia layer, and the library's own naming led two readings astray. The other two are about what a global or an opcode does when its behaviour happens to correlate with something more visible.

FUN_80068D94 is the VAB-open head, not SsSepOpen

Verdict: falsified. The routine validates the VAB magic and patches the program-to-tone-page rank map, not a per-track pointer table.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The plausible part: it validates a magic, reads a count at +0x12, SsSpuMallocs, and patches a pointer table - the shape of a SEP/track loader, with the magic read as 'VAP'. The disassembly refutes it: the compare is 0x564142 against word >> 8 plus low byte 0x70 - pBAV, the VAB magic - and +0x12 is ps. The "per-track pointer table" is the ProgAtr table receiving the program-to-packed-tone-page rank map; the mislabel hid that map, and with it the engine's tone collapse on sparse banks. Correct roles on audio; the map on VAB.

The 256-slot SPU-address run that was really a CLUT

Verdict: falsified. The entry matching "[u32 == 2][u16 x 256] all >= 0x8000" is summon.dat, and the halfwords are a BGR555 palette with the STP bit forced on. monster.snd is extraction 891, a 206-bank multi-VAB archive.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

0x8000 is exactly the boundary an SPU sample address clears once the reserved low region is skipped, so "256 halfwords, every one >= 0x8000" reads as a fully-populated address table, and a leading 2 reads as a format word. One PROT entry matched, and its CDNAME label named the sound cluster.

What it matched is summon.dat (extraction 893, summon / readef), whose header word is a mode 2 and whose next 0x200 bytes are a CLUT with bit 15 set on every non-zero entry. The tell the predicate cannot see: the values repeat (0x8000 0x8000 0x8000 0x8000 0x8001 ...), and SPU sample addresses are strictly increasing. The loader says where monster.snd really is: FUN_8003E104 does li v0,0x37d (raw TOC 0x37D = extraction 891) beside the path string - the same CDNAME +2 shift that mislabels other entries (CDNAME numbering).

Generalises to: a threshold predicate over a fixed-size run identifies a shape, never a format. Where the shape encodes an ordering, assert the ordering.

Op-0x35 sub-op 9 was never a queue

Verdict: falsified. Sub-op 9 is sub-op 1 (start a BGM track) plus a wait on the asynchronous asset load - it is what a cutscene changes music with mid-scene.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The word "Queue" sat in the sub-op table with no body behind it, and it is a reasonable guess: the op appears next to the pause / resume / stop words, it is never the op a scene's entry script uses, and its arm begins by comparing two globals - which reads as "is a slot free yet?".

The arm at 0x801E0224 compares *0x8007BAB8 (the index the resolver produced) against *0x8007BA9C (the index actually loaded); the mismatch branch goes to 0x801DEE4C, the dispatcher's restore-PC idiom, so the script re-runs the same instruction next frame. When it clears, sw v0,-0x4538(a1) writes *0x8007BAC8 = id - the identical store sub-op 1 makes. Sub-op 11 (_DAT_8007BA9C = -1) is the barrier's arming half.

Two things kept the wrong reading alive: a scene-corpus BGM sweep only runs prescripts, which emit sub-op 1 exclusively, and the recording director folded its start and queue hooks into one list. The audible symptom is narrow: the cutscene plays silent and its score starts over the next scene. Full arm on script VM.

Containers / placeholder slots

Most of the game's data sits inside PROT.DAT, a 1233-entry archive whose table of contents gives each entry only a start sector. An entry's size is the gap to the next entry - and for a long time the project's reader computed it differently, reading past the end of one entry into the next. Four of the rows below are the same root cause wearing different symptoms; the rest are neighbours in the same overlay slot.

Assets named by the entry the over-read window started in

Verdict: falsified. The kingdom bundle is PROT 0086 / 0245 / 0392 (not 0085 / 0244 / 0391); the battle-form atlases are eight whole TIMs in PROT 1205 (not seven in 1204 with one truncated and CLUT row 496 skipped); the title TIM ships once, in 0890 at 0x14228 (not three times). Each asset is where it always was - only its name was wrong.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Under the pre-correction entry size a reader positioned on entry N could see entries N+1, N+2... so an asset was recorded as "PROT N offset K" whenever the scan that found it started at N. The coordinate is not wrong about the disc - start_lba(N)*0x800 + K really is where the bytes are - it is wrong about which entry owns them, which is the only thing a correctly-bounded reader can use.

Each wrong name came with corroboration:

  • The kingdom bundle "at 0x1800 of entry 85" had a table there with the right count and the right first descriptor offset. It is entry 86's offset 0 - and the block layout (.MAP / v12 header / prescript / bundle) says entry 85 is the prescript.
  • The battle-pack atlases had a consistent stride from a consistent base, and a truncated last member is a normal thing to find at the end of a container. 0x25804 is 1204's own length plus 4, i.e. entry 1205 offset 4; the "seven" and the "truncation" were both where the window stopped.
  • The title TIM's three "duplicates" were byte-equal - exactly what a multi-bank duplicate looks like, and also what you get when three arithmetics resolve to one absolute offset.

Two lessons: byte-equality between two (entry, offset) pairs is evidence of duplication only after the pairs are shown to resolve to different absolute offsets; and a container's member count is a property of its framing, not of where a buffer happens to end. Corrected coordinates on PROT.DAT.

Pochi-fill slots are not stale mastering scratch

Verdict: falsified. Every one of the 266 pochi slots is exactly one 2048-byte sector of fill and none carries a TIM. The pages that corrupted a ground atlas came from the scene_tmd_stream entry after the pochi slot.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

Reserved-but-unused filler holding leftover bytes from an earlier master is an ordinary thing to find on a PSX disc, and the hazard had a reproducible exhibit: two 64x256 pages uploading to framebuffer (768,0) and (832,0) erased a ground atlas every run, and the sweep was positioned on a pochi slot when it happened.

The over-reading reader made the next entry's bytes look like the current entry's content, so the symptom was attributed to the entry the reader was positioned on. Reproducibility confirmed that something was wrong at that step and said nothing about which entry owned the bytes. What the slots actually hold: pochi.

Verdict: falsified. The "second concatenated sub-stream" was the next PROT entry. 0 of 182 entries hold a second stream.

Why it looked right, and what killed it

The third shape of the same root cause, and the one that got furthest: here the over-read invented a structural feature of the format. 0006_town01 was read as two concatenated [chunk0 TMD][type-0x01 TIM chunks][terminator] sub-streams, the second at 0x14000. Entry 0006 is exactly 0x14000 bytes, so all of that is PROT entry 0007, whose own tail chunks sit at the recorded offsets minus 0006's length.

The plausible part was unusually good: the second block opened on a sector boundary, was preceded by zero padding, and carried a valid TMD plus two well-formed chunks - because that is what a scene_tmd_stream entry looks like, and the next entry was one. The reading even explained a real fact about the walker (FUN_8001FE70 returns param_1 + 1, just past the terminator). And it was "confirmed" against the town0b / town0c clusters - four-entry runs of the same layout, so every over-read spilled into a sibling of the same shape. The replication was the artifact copying itself.

Lesson: a structural feature that only ever appears at the end of a buffer is a claim about the reader's bounds until it is shown somewhere else. sub_streams and WalkSource::Continuation survive in scene_tmd_stream.rs as regression detectors. Corrected layout on scene bundles.
