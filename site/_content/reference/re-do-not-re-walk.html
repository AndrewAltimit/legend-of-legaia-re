Falsified RE readings - do not re-walk

Every reverse-engineering project accumulates readings of the bytes that looked right, were believed, and turned out wrong. This page indexes Legaia's: what each reading claimed, the verdict, and the reasoning - why it was convincing and what killed it. Check here before committing to a reading that looks obvious.

What a falsified reading is, and why it is kept

A reverse engineer looks at a routine or a table and forms a story about what it does. Most stories survive the disassembly or a runtime capture. The ones here did not - but each was plausible, usually backed by real evidence read the wrong way, and the shape of the trap recurs in other subsystems. Rows are terminal: new evidence moves a row back to open RE threads, never edits it in place. The answered questions are on settled threads. Each row opens to its full reasoning - why it convinced, what killed it - and the longer cases sit under Long write-ups in this area. Every area, row and write-up is rendered from docs/reference/re-do-not-re-walk.md when the site is built.

Three traps behind most rows

  • VA aliasing. A bare virtual address is not an identity. The game swaps different overlays into the same RAM window (0x801CE818 for slot A, 0x801F69D8 for slot B), so a dump labelled by address can be a different function entirely. See the overlay band.
  • Ghidra's collapsed switch. A jump table's arms render in the decompiled C as bare breaks or fake FUN_x calls, inventing opcode semantics the raw table does not have. The cure is the disassembly, never the C (Ghidra workflow).
  • The over-reading entry size. A PROT entry's size is the sector gap to the next entry. An older expression read past that end, so a "field at offset K" was really the next entry (PROT.DAT).
