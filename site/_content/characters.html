Field form, battle form, Baka Fighter

The game keeps two versions of each hero: a low-poly field form that stays loaded everywhere, and a higher-detail battle form a real battle assembles piece by piece from the character's equipped gear. The disc also ships a pre-assembled default-gear copy of the battle models for the Baka Fighter minigame; that form shows it in the minigame's colours, with the true in-battle palette one toggle away. Equipment is bone geometry, not a prop

The battle form runs the assembly a real battle runs. Each hero's data\battle\PLAYERn file carries a five-section table - body, head, weapon, Ra-Seru, feet, though not in the same order for every character - and each section lists one entry per equippable item. At battle load the game matches those entries against the equip bytes in the save record and splices the winning sections' 3D objects into a single model. Clicking a piece in the equipment panel does exactly that, on your disc, in the browser.

What comes out is the finding worth stating plainly: there is no item object. Equipping a Survival Knife does not attach a knife - it swaps in a re-authored right-hand object that has a blade built into it (Vahn's hand goes from 40 vertices to 71; a two-hander takes it to 109). Body armour is the same trick and can make an object smaller: it is a re-sculpt, not a layer. A full endgame set re-authors most of the body. So the main .glb download is always the whole character, named for what it is wearing.

The weapon can still be cut out - by palette, not by geometry

"No separate object" does not mean "inseparable". A weapon is drawn from its own palette column, and across all 81 weapon and Ra-Seru records on the disc no primitive mixes the two - the item is an exact primitive subset of the hand object, selected by material. So every equipped section gets a record-keeping download in the side panel: for a weapon, that subset plus the limb it was cut from as two named nodes in one file; for anything with no such boundary, the piece as spliced in. The host limb is ground truth, so it is always included.

Each piece is labelled by how cleanly it came away, in the same words the glTF root node carries. Four cases occur across the disc's 132 equipment records: it ships as its own piece already (6 - retail did the split); it comes away whole, sharing no vertex with the hand (21, all Gala's and most of Noa's); it is welded to the hand at the grip (53, all of Vahn's); or it is fused with the host limb (52 - every armour, headgear and footwear record, plus one Ra-Seru armband). A fused piece exports with the body part it is sculpted onto, because there is no material boundary between them - armour has no surplus object and its palette split is body-versus-trim. The panel says which you got per piece rather than quietly capping a hole or quietly leaving a slot empty; every equipped section gets a download, and completeness beats purity.

The equipment panel and its ⬇ buttons are the second, deliberately opinionated cut: the item alone - the great axe with no hand, the helmet with no face, the robe with no neck. That cut has no exact answer on the disc (nothing in a re-authored hand object says which primitives are the axe), so it is a policy plus a committed per-record rule table: a primitive stays only if it is not the character's own flesh and not an unchanged piece of them - read as colour against the bare limb for held items and headgear (skin, hair and the wrist band are what the bare hand shows), and as geometry-and-colour identity for body and footwear (the default outfit is a real garment, so a dark robe is not "body" just because the default robe is dark). Every card says which reading decided it and whether the rule table has hand-checked that record; the preview toggle in the side panel draws exactly what the file will contain. The record-keeping export is unaffected - it stays the exact palette cut with its ground-truth limb.

The grip is inferred, and the file says so

A welded item leaves the cut with an open grip: the part of the shaft inside the closed fist was never modelled - the fingers covered it - so Vahn's Great Axe comes away as an axe head on a stub of haft and a pommel end, each ending in an open ring where it met the fingers. No cutting strategy recovers what was never drawn, but the two rings say what was there: two coaxial polygon loops of similar size, facing each other across a gap the width of a hand, are the two ends of one straight shaft. The item-alone cut therefore bridges such pairs - a tube lofted between the rims, textured from one of them - and marks the result grip inferred on the card, in the summary, and in the glTF root name. Nine of the disc's 81 held-item records take a bridge (all Vahn's: the axes, maces and the shorter blades). The rule is narrow on purpose: rims that face away from each other (a Ra-Seru armband, a helmet's neck hole) are never joined, a lone rim is never capped, only rims on the same bone are paired, and the record-keeping export is never touched. Bridging is inference about a stretch of shaft between two ends that both exist; capping would be invention about the item's silhouette.

Two readings that look right and are not, in case you try them: connectivity alone misses the item case (the weapon is a separate component for Gala and Noa but welded for Vahn), and "geometry the bare hand lacks" misses it too, because equipping re-textures the whole object - Vahn's bare fist draws from one palette column and his knife-holding fist from two entirely different ones. And a cross-bone bridge (compared in the rest stance) finds no real grip on this disc - Vahn's swords put the blade on the hand object and the pommel block on the forearm, both closed at the fist - while happily joining the elbow-facing rims of two Ra-Seru arm plates, which is why the same-bone rule is data, not caution.

The diff highlight toggle answers the other question - what the loadout changed. It dims the geometry the equipped part shares with the unequipped one, brightens what reaches beyond it, and draws the bare geometry the section replaced alongside in blue. That boundary is an approximate radius-envelope test rather than the exact palette cut, and the table underneath reports the per-object counts it is derived from.

The one-byte equipment swap

Each hero's field model carries two pre-baked variants of its weapon-holding part, flipped by a single byte in the save record - that is the entire field-side equipment mechanism, and the field form's toggle performs exactly that swap. Details: equipment-conditional swap.

asset character-pack … --slot 0 --equip 1 --out vahn_equipped.tmd

The animations are the real in-game clips

Every clip in the Animation panel comes off your disc, and the list is scoped to the shown model. In field form the default source is the party's own locomotion bank (PROT 0874 §1): the capture-pinned Walk and Idle clips plus the not-yet-pinned rest of each character's 7-clip bank, with the per-scene NPC pools still browsable as a secondary group. In battle form the clips come out of the same player battle file as the model - the record[0] action bank plus the four weapon swings, which are spliced from the equipped weapon's section and change with it - and every Tactical Art out of the character's readef.DAT keyframe archive, resolved to its record the same way the arts page does (a multi-strike art such as Hurricane Kick plays its consecutive records as one clip). In Baka Fighter form it is the character's labeled 9-record battle bank (Idle, the three attacks, Special, the hit reactions, Win). The animation data assembles the character's separate body pieces each frame exactly as the game's renderer does, so playback matches what you see in-game; the transport under the viewer plays at the record's own retail rate (¼× and ½× slow it down), and the keyboard drives it (Space, [ ]). The .glb download bakes the same clips as named glTF animations. Encoding: ANM format.
