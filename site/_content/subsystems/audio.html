Audio

How Legaia makes sound. The town theme that starts when you step through a gate, the clang of a sword hit and the shout a character gives when an art lands each take a different road to the PlayStation's sound chip: music is a MIDI-like score played through a sampled instrument bank, effects are one-shot samples keyed on a voice, and voices are streamed straight off the CD. This page follows the three roads; the library catalogues and the port's audio model are on audio internals.

At a glance

Hardware
The SPU: 24 voices, 512 KB of its own RAM, ADSR envelopes, one global reverb. Retail runs reverb Studio C, always on, on every voice
Music
Every real track lives in the music_01 bank as a self-contained [VAB][SEQ] pair; scenes pick one by script (field-VM op 0x35, id 2000 + slot). No BGM table exists
Effects
100 static descriptors at DAT_8006F198; a descriptor's category byte names the VAB slot the cue plays from
Voices
CD-XA streams: arts shouts from XA2 / XA4 / XA6, the blocked-strike grunt from XA30, victory lines from the tail of monster.snd
Libraries
Sony's PsyQ libsnd (sequencer, 0x80061..0x80067) and libspu (0x80068..0x8006D), statically linked and traceable
In the port
crates/engine-audio: a from-scratch SPU, a sample-locked sequencer, the SFX bank, XA mixing; a WebAudio backend for the browser

Three roads to the speaker

The SPU cannot be driven from main RAM directly; games go through Sony's libsnd (sequencer playback) and libspu (voices and registers). Legaia links both statically, so they are part of the game binary and can be traced and named like any other code. Legaia's own layer above them is small: two loaders, one bank installer, one SFX helper, one BGM poller and one CD-XA clip player.

Music: which track plays

Walk into Rim Elm and the town theme starts; walk into a fight and the battle theme takes over. Nothing in the executable maps a place to a tune. The scene's own script says “play track 2016” through field-VM op 0x35, and a per-frame poller turns that id into a disc entry by arithmetic - the archive is the table.

  • Global ids (≥ 2000) are offsets into the shared music_01 bank: id 2000 + i is sound-test track i. Scene-local ids (< 2000) only feed the “did the track change” test - the load itself is overwritten with a global slot, so every scene that starts music picks a global track.
  • The bank is piecewise. In extraction space it runs 988 + i for i ≤ 67 and 990 + i for i ≥ 68, with a two-entry gap at 1056 / 1057; a flat base gets the top of the sound test wrong (music tracks).
  • Each track carries its own instruments. An entry is a chunk header, a pBAV bank, then a pQES score; playing it uploads that entry's VAB. A scene's own sound stream is never its music.
  • Re-selecting the playing track is a no-op, so walking between two rooms that name the same theme never restarts it.

Changing track on cue

A cutscene can choose the exact beat the outgoing score dies on. The poller walks a short ladder - wait for the CD, let the load settle for 30 frames, tear down the old track unless the script owns the swap, load, install the score, latch - and coordinates with the script through one sound-flag word. A swap started by sub-op 9 keeps the old track playing until the script commits it with sub-op 0xA. The flag bits are on internals.

  • Stop, pause, replay. Sub-op 2 stops the track (notes cut, cursor back to the start), sub-op 3 pauses it (notes keyed off, cursor kept) and sub-op 4 replays it from its first event.
  • Hard cuts. Retail never cross-fades between tracks: the old sequence is keyed off (notes release through their own envelopes) and the new one starts that instant.
  • Movies leave the score alone. The movie player never touches the sequencer: a score the script left sounding keeps playing under the movie's audio, and one it stopped stays silent. Only the title attract stops the title theme, and the title screen restarts it from the top.
  • The silent dawn. town01 starts its theme and, while a story flag is clear, pauses it at once; the opening cutscene's own sub-op 9 brings it in.

Sound effects

A footstep, a menu blip, a sword clang: each is one 8-byte descriptor, and the actor or script that wants the sound just names its id. The static table holds 100 descriptors; ids at or above 0x200 come from a runtime bank instead - in battle, bse.dat.

+0programu8
+1tone / region baseu8
+2note levelu8
+3voice count + sustained bit 0x20u8
+4category = VAB slotu8
+5unused (zero)3 bytes
  • Trigger. Move-VM and field-VM opcodes write a sound id and a voice into the actor; the actor tick fires the SFX helper on a one-shot pulse or a staged continuous cue, keying one voice per descriptor count.
  • The cue names its own bank. Before looking the program up, the voice programmer switches libsnd's current bank to the slot the category byte names - so a cue never plays out of the scene's music bank. Layout and the category → slot → PROT map: sfx table.
  • Every battle command press blips. The command flow rings its cues through the dispatcher FUN_8004FCC8, which stores id - 1 for an id below 0x40: a commit, a cursor step and a cancel or refused arm each key one descriptor lower than the id the flow names. The item and magic windows are list windows, and the shared list kernel rings their cues itself. The port derives one blip a frame from each surface’s state before and after its input, and both hosts drain it through the session’s audio director.
  • A sustained cue loops until something releases it. A descriptor with bit 0x20 keys a looping sample on voices from 7 up, and nothing in the voice ends it. The teardown FUN_80017910 keys those voices off; it runs at every mode entry, at a battle actor’s clip commit when the clip changes, in the defeat fade of a monster seat, and from the sound test’s “stop all”. So a monster’s looping attack sound ends on the frame its next clip commits. The port raises the release in call order with the cue writes, so it runs ahead of that frame’s new cues.
History: “a cue plays out of the scene's music VAB”

The current-bank globals are shared with the sequencer, so a save state sampled after a BGM note holds the music bank. That artefact produced the earlier reading; the program-change call inside the voice programmer is what selects the bank per cue.

Sound banks and their slots

An instrument bank (VAB, format on vab) is a set of samples plus the tuning and envelope data that makes them instruments. Legaia keeps several resident at once, each parked in a numbered slot. One installer opens every bank; its argument is the same category byte the SFX descriptors carry.

0PROT 0868 system bank (the shared UI cues)resident from boot

  • Slots 2 and 6 are one physical bank in two modes. They share a header buffer and an SPU address: the field init loads 0876 into slot 6, and battle init closes it before the battle loader fills slot 2. That is why retail needs no extra SPU room for field cues.
  • Monster banks come from one packed file, monster.snd, with a per-monster TOC of sector ranges. Its tail clips are the party's victory-pose voice lines: the spoils routine picks a pose tier from the actor's HP (a status forces the weakest pose) and plays that character's clip.

Battle voices: shouts and grunts

When Vahn lands a Somersault he shouts. That shout is not an SPU sample - it is a short CD-XA clip streamed off the disc, picked at random from a small per-character channel pool so it does not repeat back to back.

CD-XA streaming Confirmed

Cutscene audio and voice clips are streamed CD-XA: the drive plays them directly, interleaved with video sectors, without the CPU or the SPU voices in the loop. crates/xa decodes the 4-bit ADPCM bit-exactly against an external lossless reference; the demuxer splits raw 2352-byte sectors by (file, channel), which a 2048-byte ISO view strips. Format on xa.

Two stages sit between the decoded PCM and the speaker. The CD decoder converts 37.8 kHz to 44.1 kHz with a fixed 29-tap "zig-zag" filter (seven outputs per six inputs), which passes the band at about 0.906 and suppresses the image a linear interpolator would leave. Then the SPU scales the stream by its CD-input volume: every voice cue plays at register 0x75EA (libsnd level 117, about 0.921), set by the clip player's CD callback once the drive starts reading, while a movie plays at full scale. The port runs both stages.

History: the “non-standard interleave” reading

An earlier reading had most XA sound groups failing validation and inferred a bespoke interleave. That was Form-1 truncation damage in the old extractor, not the format. See do-not-re-walk.

What a cold scene entry owes

Music does not start because a scene loads. It starts because the scene's entry script reaches op 0x35, and the id it names reaches a director that knows the track carries its own instruments. Anything that drops the engine into a scene from cold - both play hosts and every headless driver - owes the same three steps in this order, and each one is separately load-bearing.

StepKernelWhat skipping it sounds like
1. Free-roam story stagingWorld::seed_free_roam_story_baselineThe entry script's authored pause parks the track: a sequencer attaches on the first frame and its playhead never leaves tick 0
2. Live field entryBootSession::enter_field_liveLoading the scene alone installs no field record, so the field VM steps nothing and op 0x35 never executes
3. Global-pool start hookBgmDirector::start_owned_vabEvery real music cue is a global id, so a director that leaves this hook on its no-op default plays nothing while looking fully wired

All three failures read identically from outside - silence - which is why a driver missing any one of them reports as “the engine starts no music on scene entry”.

  • Why step 1 exists. A scene-picker entry runs the entry script with none of the story choreography behind it. Rim Elm's script starts its theme and pauses it for the opening's silent dawn; without the cutscene that later restarts it, the pause would hold forever. The staging drops a pause issued inside the entry window on picker entries only - a new game keeps the authored pause.
  • A story twin's flags pick its track too. Rim Elm under attack opens on the town theme and then, on one flag pair, switches to the attack theme over a night grade. A picker entry with neither flag staged would play the wrong tune, so the staging seeds the flag.
  • Battle reuses the same road. An encounter queues an ordinary track start for the battle theme and the fight's end resumes the stashed field track - one dispatch path, no battle-only audio code.

The field script picks the fight's music

Which track a fight plays is the field script's choice, not the formation's. Op 0x35 sub-op 7 stores a battle sound set number, and the scene's map init zeroes it on every entry. The battle intro reads it: set N ≥ 0 loads battle bundle 0x36F + N and starts its score; set -1 loads nothing and leaves whatever the event was already playing. That is how a boss fight keeps the boss theme its cutscene started one line earlier. Each bundle of the battle bank carries a byte-identical copy of one music_01 score.

What pitch a note keys at

Both key-on paths - a sequencer note and a one-shot cue - converge on one arithmetic, and its result is written straight into the SPU voice's pitch register. Nothing rescales it afterwards.

n     = note + 60 - center + carry
pitch = PITCH[(n % 12) * 16 + fine]  shifted by  (n / 12 - 5)

PITCH is a 192-entry table at DAT_8007A940 holding one octave at 1/16-semitone resolution; every entry is exactly floor(0x1000 × 2^(k/192)), so the port computes it instead of carrying it.

  • A tone plays at unity on its own centre note. note == center selects 0x1000, which is 44.1 kHz. There is no separate sample-rate factor: a 22.05 kHz sample is authored with its centre an octave above the key it should sound at, and the same law lands on 0x800. Folding a rate ratio in as well puts every voice an octave low.
  • The tone's shift byte raises the pitch by shift/128 of a semitone, quantised to 1/16. It is a positive fine-tune, not a downward correction.
  • A cue keys half a semitone above a note. The sequencer path forms its fine index as min(shift >> 3, 15) and never carries. The cue path uses (0x40 + shift) >> 3, where 0x40 is a literal at every traced call site, and carries a whole semitone at 16.

The law is measured as well as read: across the catalogued save states, 126 of 128 sounding voices hold exactly the pitch it computes from their staged note against the live bank. The two misses are voices whose bank was swapped after the key-on. Port: compute_pitch in engine-audio::vab_bind.

Three format traps that silence or speed up music

  • A SEQ meta event has no length byte. Legaia's tempo meta is 0xFF 0x51 plus three tempo bytes, and end-of-track is 0xFF 0x2F with nothing after it. Reading the MIDI length byte that is not there drops the first tempo and pins playback about three times too fast against the header's 240 BPM placeholder (seq).
  • A meta is a running status. Retail latches 0xFF, so a data byte after a meta is the next meta's kind. One track's closing tempo depends on it.
  • A VAB arrives as two chunks. Its header and its sample bodies are separate chunks of one stream, and some entries put the score chunk first. Probing fixed offsets finds the wrong bytes; the body origin has to be resolved off the stream (vab).

Scripted field cues ride a four-slot ring

A door creak or a switch click placed by a scene script does not go through the per-actor helper. The field VM's op 0x36 and the motion VM's op 0x09 push the cue id onto a four-slot ring, with an optional countdown, and a drainer keys each one when its countdown ends.

  • Two id ranges, two banks. A ring id below 0x200 goes through the static table and the bank its category names. An id at or above 0x200 indexes the scene's own descriptor record and the side-band bank that record names - slot 3 in a town.
  • The field bank is only there in the field. Most static field cues are category 6, the field bank, which shares its SPU region with the battle bank in slot 2. A category-6 cue therefore keys in the field and is silent in battle, where retail's slot 6 is closed. The port refills that region per mode the same way.
  • Placed sounds follow the camera. A town's river or waterwheel is not a ring cue but a placed emitter part with a box around it: each tick its volume is written straight to its voices, scaled by how deep inside the box the camera focus sits, and it is released when the focus leaves. The port runs the same arm on the same parts; every sounding cue voice in the save-state library, emitters included, matches register for register.
  • Every scripted id on the disc resolves to a row of its own scene's bank, bar one that points one row past its table and keys nothing in retail either.

In the port

crates/engine-audio rebuilds the stack from this page, the libspu API surface and the public SPU register layout - no Sony bytes. It models the 24 voices with Gaussian resampling, the envelope, the ADPCM decoder, the reverb network (set to Studio C on every voice, as retail runs it) and a sequencer counted in whole output samples that spends retail's tick budget once per vsync period, so playback is locked to the audio clock, lands its key-ons where retail's do, and is bit-deterministic for replays. Both play hosts drive it through the same BGM director (engine-session). Module map, the sequencer details and how the port's sound is measured against retail: internals.
