Audio

How Legaia makes sound. The town theme that starts when you step through a gate, the clang of a sword hit and the shout a character gives when an art lands each take a different road to the PlayStation's sound chip: music is a MIDI-like score played through a sampled instrument bank, effects are one-shot samples keyed on a voice, and voices and cutscene audio are streamed straight off the CD. This page follows the three roads and shows how the clean-room engine rebuilds the stack.

How it works

The SPU cannot be driven from main RAM directly; games go through Sony's PsyQ libraries - libsnd for sequencer playback and libspu for voice and register control. Legaia links both statically, so the libraries are part of the game binary and can be traced like any other code. Legaia's own layer above them is small: a path-string cluster, two loaders, one bank installer, one SFX helper, one BGM poller and one CD-XA clip player.

The surprising part is music dispatch: there is no "BGM id to file" table anywhere. A scene's own script writes a track id, and a per-frame poller turns that id into a PROT.DAT entry number by arithmetic over the disc layout. The archive is the table.

Finding the sound files

Retail reads sound data by PROT.DAT entry number. A dev-station path (h:\...) survives in the same functions but is never taken on a retail disc: the split is a build-mode global, and the dev arm opens files through a host trap that has no disc to read.

Sound banks and their slots

An instrument bank (VAB, format on vab) is a set of samples plus the tuning and envelope data that makes them instruments. Legaia keeps several resident at once, each parked in a numbered SPU slot. One installer opens every bank; its argument is the same category byte the SFX descriptors carry, and a 12-byte mixer record per category names the header buffer and the slot.

SlotBankInstalled by

1The current BGM's own VAB (music_01, variable)The BGM poller

  • Slots 2 and 6 share one SPU base. The record initialiser assigns header buffers from one base with four pairs sharing one, and the SPU-base table gives those pairs one address - the field bank and the battle class-2 bank are the same physical bank in two modes. The engine seeds the same twelve-word image at boot and answers slot queries from the other end, with a test that the two agree.
  • Monster banks come from one packed file, monster.snd, with a per-monster TOC of [start_lba, end_lba + 1] pairs. Its tail clips carry the party's victory-pose voice lines: the spoils routine picks a pose tier from the actor's HP (three-quarters, half, quarter; a status forces tier 4), an action id from a per-character table, and a clip byte - Vahn 0xB8..0xBC, Gala 0xBD..0xC3, Noa 0xC4..0xCB.
  • Multi-bank archives sit at extraction 0889 / 0890 (sound_data2) and 0891 (level_up). Slot sizes and the SPU map: sfx table.

Sound effects

A footstep, a menu blip, a sword clang: each is one 8-byte descriptor - which program in which bank, how many voices, how loud - and the actor or script that wants the sound just names it. The static table holds 100 descriptors; ids at or above 0x200 come from the runtime .dpk bank instead.

ByteField
+0Program
+1Tone / region base
+2Note level
+3Voice count, plus a sustained bit 0x20
+4Category - selects the VAB slot the cue plays from
  • Trigger. The actor tick fires the per-actor helper when the actor's one-shot pulse byte is set or a continuous cue is staged; move-VM and field-VM opcodes write the sound id and voice into the actor. The helper keys one voice per descriptor count.
  • Bank is named by the cue. The voice programmer issues a VAB program-change before the program lookup, repointing libsnd's current-bank globals at the slot the category byte names. A cue never plays out of the scene's music VAB.
  • Engine. SfxBank::from_descriptors builds the catalog from the disc table and fires each cue against the bank its category names; SfxScheduler dispatches at the retail timing offset (a hit cue with timing_frames = 1 plays one frame after the strike begins). Implementation crates/engine-audio/src/sfx.rs.
History: "a cue plays out of the scene's music VAB"

The current-bank globals are shared with the sequencer, so a save state sampled after a BGM note holds the music bank. That artefact produced the earlier reading; the program-change call inside the voice programmer is what selects the bank per cue.

Music: which track plays, and how it starts

Walk into Rim Elm and the town theme starts; walk into a fight and the battle theme takes over. Nothing in the executable maps a place to a tune. The scene's own script says "play track 2016" through field-VM op 0x35, and the per-frame poller turns that id into a disc entry:

IdResolved PROT indexBase
< 2000 (scene-local)scene_block + 6 + idThe scene's own bundle (never taken by a field scene)
≥ 2000 (global pool)music_01_base + (id − 2000)Raw TOC 990 = extraction 988
  • The bank is piecewise. Global id 2000 + i equals sound-test track i, but in extraction space the bank runs 988 + i for i ≤ 67 and 990 + i for i ≥ 68, with a two-entry gap at 1056 / 1057. music_labels::prot_entry_for_bgm_id owns that map; a flat base gets the top of the sound test wrong.
  • Each entry is self-contained - chunk header, pBAV body, then a pQES score. Playing a track uploads that entry's VAB into SPU RAM; a scene's scene_vab_stream SEQ is never its music, which is why fingerprinting a playing track against the scene-local corpus fails.
  • Script-selected, not table-driven. Running every scene's prescript and observing the emitted id (bgm_scene_resolution.rs) shows every scene that starts music picks a global-pool id. The sweep sees a scene's entry track only; cutscenes change music mid-scene with sub-op 9 from timeline records.
  • Re-selecting the playing track is a no-op - the resolved index is compared with the loaded one before anything happens.

The track-swap handshake

A cutscene can choose the exact beat the outgoing score dies on. The poller runs a stage counter through a seven-entry ladder - wait for CD idle, arm a 30-frame settle, tear down the BGM slot unless the script owns the swap, kick the load, count down, install the SEQ, re-attach unless paused, latch - coordinated with the script through one sound-flag word:

BitMeaningSet byCleared by
0Script-owned start pending (defer slot teardown)Sub-op 9Poller commit, scene entry
1BGM slot paused / detachedSub-op 2, 3, the dance overlaySub-op 1, 4, 0xA, scene entry, game over
2Script flag (opaque to the sound side)Sub-op 6Field overlay
3Load settled - payload staged and delay elapsedThe pollerPoller commit
4Release-ack - the script has released the old occupantSub-op 0xAPoller commit

After setting bit 3 the poller stalls while bit 0 is set and bit 4 is not: a swap started by sub-op 9 keeps the old track until the script commits with sub-op 0xA. A port that honours the sub-op 2 pause but drops the 0xA commit leaves the music paused after the cutscene; the engine routes sub-op 0xA straight to BgmDirector::unhalt_pause because its BGM bytes resolve synchronously.

  • Entry-script pauses. town01 starts id 2016 and, while a system flag is clear, pauses it at once - the opening's silent dawn, restarted by the cutscene's own sub-op 9. The engine's free-roam staging drops that pause on scene-picker entries only; the new-game chain keeps it.
  • Battle swap (engine). World::set_battle_bgm queues an ordinary sub-op 1 start on encounter and resumes the field track on battle end. Default battle track: global id 2026 (retail's battle_id == 0 bundle, sound-test #26). Retail BGM changes are hard cuts, so AudioOut::swap_bgm key-offs the outgoing sequencer (notes release through their own envelopes) and starts the new one that instant, with a couple of frames of click-guard on the master.
  • Minigame pages take the same path: Baka Fighter loads extraction 1043 (#55), the dance overlay 1048 / 1054 (#60 / #66); the slot machine, fishing and Muscle Dome inherit their host scene's track.

Battle voices: shouts and the melee grunt

When Vahn lands a Somersault he shouts. That shout is not an SPU sample - it is a short CD-XA clip streamed off the disc, picked at random from a small per-art channel pool so it does not repeat back to back. The plain melee swing works the same way, which is why a port that wires up only the SPU stays silent in a fight.

Engine. Executing an art pushes one BattleShoutCue; boot demuxes XA2 / XA4 / XA6 from the raw 2352-byte sectors (disc-image boots only); AudioBgmDirector::play_art_shout resolves the pool pick and AudioOut::play_xa_shout mixes the PCM the way the CD input does, never through the 24 voices. Two timing behaviours model the CD contract: a ~150 ms response delay (the shout trails the animation) and back-to-back requests queue rather than cut. The cue tables are parsed by legaia_art::arts_voice.

Gap. The melee grunt and the impact cue are not yet staged: no XA30 bank at boot, and the live battle loop emits no cue for a plain swing (only the BattleSession path folds strike cues). A whole fight driven by the play window or the play page produces music but zero effect cues.

Sony's sequencer and SPU driver

The music player is Sony's, not Legaia's. libsnd (0x80061B18..0x800681D8) walks the SEQ bytes, keeps per-channel state in 16 slots of 0xB0-byte records, and turns note events into key-ons; Legaia installs two function-pointer hooks to wire the script VM's sequence context in. libspu (0x80068..0x8006D) pokes the chip's registers, runs the four-mode DMA transfer engine and the block allocator behind SsSpuMalloc / SpuFree. Being the same libraries every PsyQ game shipped is what lets the functions be named.

  • Key-on volume chain. Velocity × bank volume × program volume × tone volume widened into the 14-bit register domain, a channel-volume fold, one pan attenuation per source, and - on the sequencer path only - a closing square taper. Engine port VabBank::fire + sequencer::channel_mix.
  • Pitch law. A note is pitched against its tone's center through a two-octave table into a 14-bit SPU PITCH (clamped 0x3FFF).
  • Bank open validates the pBAV magic, builds the program-to-tone-page rank map into the reserved ProgAtr words, allocates SPU memory and stashes per-VAG addresses. Engine port VabBank::upload.
  • Tempo becomes an integer tick step where the wall clock meets the sequencer; the decoder does not consume a whole event per call.
  • Not audio: the 0x801CE628 cluster next door is libpad. And none of this is libgpu - battle and field code fire cues during render passes, which is why the cluster looks renderer-adjacent in call graphs.

Engine port

crates/engine-audio ports the stack as a clean-room model from this page, the libspu API surface and the public SPU register layout. No Sony bytes.

ModuleModels
spu::Spu, spu::voice24 voices with sample address, loop point, pitch, ADSR, L/R volume and reverb send; 4-point Gaussian resampling as the hardware does
spu::adsr, spu::adpcmFive-phase envelope with linear / exponential modes; streaming 28-samples-per-block decoder, one per voice
spu::reverbRegister-driven network (same / different-side IIR, 4-tap comb, 2 all-pass) with the nine standard presets
spu::ram512 KB model, libspu-shaped transfer engine, SpuAllocator
vab_bind::VabBankupload drops every VAG into SPU RAM; play_note turns a MIDI key into voice config + key-on. Programs are slot-indexed
SequencerSsSeqOpen / Play / Close / SetVol / Rewind surface; round-robin voice allocation tracking (channel, key); pitch bend against per-tone pbmin / pbmax; loop markers from CC99 (20 = start, 30 = forever)
AudioOutOne cpal stream draining the SPU at 44.1 kHz; swap_bgm, play_xa_shout. The audio-webaudio feature adds WebAudioOut for the browser
  • Reverb is Studio C, always on. Every captured state - field, town, battle, summon, title, minigames - has the master enable set, the 32 reverb registers byte-identical to the StudioC preset, and 15-22 of 24 voices sent to it. The engine calls Spu::set_retail_reverb() once at init. No SEQ carries a CC91 reverb-depth event.
  • Timebase. The production path ticks the sequencer once per SPU sample with an exact integer accumulator - locked to the audio clock, no drift, bit-deterministic for the replay oracle. The SEQ header tempo is a 240 BPM placeholder overridden by the first body tempo event, which carries no MIDI length byte; mis-parsing it pins playback ~3x fast (seq).
  • Not modelled: pitch modulation, noise and FM (Legaia uses none), asynchronous DMA timing, and the hardware's 39-tap reverb FIR (decimation + zero-order hold instead).

XA-ADPCM Confirmed

Cutscene audio and voice clips are streamed CD-XA: the drive plays them directly, interleaved with video sectors, without the CPU or the SPU voices in the loop. crates/xa decodes the 4-bit ADPCM bit-exactly against an external lossless reference; the demuxer splits raw 2352-byte sectors by (file, channel), which a 2048-byte ISO view strips. Format on xa.

History: the "non-standard interleave" reading

An earlier reading had most XA sound groups failing validation and inferred a bespoke interleave. That was Form-1 truncation damage in the old extractor, not the format. See do-not-re-walk.

Checking the port: the audio-trace oracle

The port's sound is checked by comparing which SPU voices are active, frame by frame, against a real emulator running the retail disc - the audio sibling of the VRAM and mode-trace oracles. Two retail capture shapes emit the same AudioTraceFrame JSONL: a single-cycle snapshot lifted from a mednafen save state's SPU section, and a multi-frame trace from autorun_audio_trace.lua in PCSX-Redux. The engine runs a standalone SPU + sequencer beside a headless boot session; a retail frame converges when some engine frame's active-voice mask is a superset of retail's with every retail voice's start address matching. A missed frame is tolerable drift; a start-address or master-volume mismatch is a hard failure.

CLI: legaia-engine audio-trace --scene NAME with --scenario (snapshot) or --retail-jsonl (multi-frame). Tests audio_trace.rs, audio_trace_multi.rs (needs LEGAIA_AUDIO_TRACE_JSONL_DIR).

What's left
