Audio

How Legaia makes sound. The town theme that starts when you step through a gate, the clang of a sword hit and the shout a character gives when an art lands each take a different road to the PlayStation's sound chip: music is a MIDI-like score played through a sampled instrument bank, effects are one-shot samples keyed on a voice, and voices are streamed straight off the CD. This page follows the three roads; the library catalogues and the port's audio model are on audio internals.

At a glance

Hardware
The SPU: 24 voices, 512 KB of its own RAM, ADSR envelopes, one global reverb. Retail runs reverb Studio C, always on, on every voice
Music
Every real track lives in the music_01 bank as a self-contained [VAB][SEQ] pair; scenes pick one by script (field-VM op 0x35, id 2000 + slot). No BGM table exists
Effects
100 static descriptors at DAT_8006F198; a descriptor's category byte names the VAB slot the cue plays from
Voices
CD-XA streams: arts shouts from XA2 / XA4 / XA6, the blocked-strike grunt from XA30, victory lines from the tail of monster.snd
Libraries
Sony's PsyQ libsnd (sequencer, 0x80061..0x80067) and libspu (0x80068..0x8006D), statically linked and traceable
In the port
crates/engine-audio: a from-scratch SPU, a sample-locked sequencer, the SFX bank, XA mixing; a WebAudio backend for the browser

Three roads to the speaker

The SPU cannot be driven from main RAM directly; games go through Sony's libsnd (sequencer playback) and libspu (voices and registers). Legaia links both statically, so they are part of the game binary and can be traced and named like any other code. Legaia's own layer above them is small: two loaders, one bank installer, one SFX helper, one BGM poller and one CD-XA clip player.

Music: which track plays

Walk into Rim Elm and the town theme starts; walk into a fight and the battle theme takes over. Nothing in the executable maps a place to a tune. The scene's own script says “play track 2016” through field-VM op 0x35, and a per-frame poller turns that id into a disc entry by arithmetic - the archive is the table.

  • Global ids (≥ 2000) are offsets into the shared music_01 bank: id 2000 + i is sound-test track i. Scene-local ids (< 2000) only feed the “did the track change” test - the load itself is overwritten with a global slot, so every scene that starts music picks a global track.
  • The bank is piecewise. In extraction space it runs 988 + i for i ≤ 67 and 990 + i for i ≥ 68, with a two-entry gap at 1056 / 1057; a flat base gets the top of the sound test wrong (music tracks).
  • Each track carries its own instruments. An entry is a chunk header, a pBAV bank, then a pQES score; playing it uploads that entry's VAB. A scene's own sound stream is never its music.
  • Re-selecting the playing track is a no-op, so walking between two rooms that name the same theme never restarts it.

Changing track on cue

A cutscene can choose the exact beat the outgoing score dies on. The poller walks a short ladder - wait for the CD, let the load settle for 30 frames, tear down the old track unless the script owns the swap, load, install the score, latch - and coordinates with the script through one sound-flag word. A swap started by sub-op 9 keeps the old track playing until the script commits it with sub-op 0xA. The flag bits are on internals.

  • Stop, pause, replay. Sub-op 2 stops the track (notes cut, cursor back to the start), sub-op 3 pauses it (notes keyed off, cursor kept) and sub-op 4 replays it from its first event.
  • Hard cuts. Retail never cross-fades between tracks: the old sequence is keyed off (notes release through their own envelopes) and the new one starts that instant.
  • Movies leave the score alone. The movie player never touches the sequencer: a score the script left sounding keeps playing under the movie's audio, and one it stopped stays silent. Only the title attract stops the title theme, and the title screen restarts it from the top.
  • The silent dawn. town01 starts its theme and, while a story flag is clear, pauses it at once; the opening cutscene's own sub-op 9 brings it in.

Sound effects

A footstep, a menu blip, a sword clang: each is one 8-byte descriptor, and the actor or script that wants the sound just names its id. The static table holds 100 descriptors; ids at or above 0x200 come from a runtime bank instead - in battle, bse.dat.

+0programu8
+1tone / region baseu8
+2note levelu8
+3voice count + sustained bit 0x20u8
+4category = VAB slotu8
+5unused (zero)3 bytes
  • Trigger. Move-VM and field-VM opcodes write a sound id and a voice into the actor; the actor tick fires the SFX helper on a one-shot pulse or a staged continuous cue, keying one voice per descriptor count.
  • The cue names its own bank. Before looking the program up, the voice programmer switches libsnd's current bank to the slot the category byte names - so a cue never plays out of the scene's music bank. Layout and the category → slot → PROT map: sfx table.
History: “a cue plays out of the scene's music VAB”

The current-bank globals are shared with the sequencer, so a save state sampled after a BGM note holds the music bank. That artefact produced the earlier reading; the program-change call inside the voice programmer is what selects the bank per cue.

Sound banks and their slots

An instrument bank (VAB, format on vab) is a set of samples plus the tuning and envelope data that makes them instruments. Legaia keeps several resident at once, each parked in a numbered slot. One installer opens every bank; its argument is the same category byte the SFX descriptors carry.

  • Slots 2 and 6 are one physical bank in two modes. They share a header buffer and an SPU address: the field init loads 0876 into slot 6, and battle init closes it before the battle loader fills slot 2. That is why retail needs no extra SPU room for field cues.
  • Monster banks come from one packed file, monster.snd, with a per-monster TOC of sector ranges. Its tail clips are the party's victory-pose voice lines: the spoils routine picks a pose tier from the actor's HP (a status forces the weakest pose) and plays that character's clip.

Battle voices: shouts and grunts

When Vahn lands a Somersault he shouts. That shout is not an SPU sample - it is a short CD-XA clip streamed off the disc, picked at random from a small per-character channel pool so it does not repeat back to back.

CD-XA streaming Confirmed

Cutscene audio and voice clips are streamed CD-XA: the drive plays them directly, interleaved with video sectors, without the CPU or the SPU voices in the loop. crates/xa decodes the 4-bit ADPCM bit-exactly against an external lossless reference; the demuxer splits raw 2352-byte sectors by (file, channel), which a 2048-byte ISO view strips. Format on xa.

History: the “non-standard interleave” reading

An earlier reading had most XA sound groups failing validation and inferred a bespoke interleave. That was Form-1 truncation damage in the old extractor, not the format. See do-not-re-walk.

In the port

crates/engine-audio rebuilds the stack from this page, the libspu API surface and the public SPU register layout - no Sony bytes. It models the 24 voices with Gaussian resampling, the envelope, the ADPCM decoder, the reverb network (set to Studio C on every voice, as retail runs it) and a sequencer clocked once per output sample, so playback is locked to the audio clock and bit-deterministic for replays. Both play hosts drive it through the same BGM director (engine-session). Module map, the sequencer details and how the port's sound is measured against retail: internals.
