Battle formulas

How much a hit does, what a Tactical Art multiplies, what the defender's numbers subtract - and the smaller rolls around them: turn order, fleeing, MP costs, the RNG. Every formula is traced from the game's own code and mirrored in the clean-room engine. The Offense Value / Defense Value shape follows ZetaPhoenix's community analysis, which corrected Meth962's earlier forum write-ups - see credits.

  • Your attack is base ATK (the unequipped number) plus half the gear the command uses, folded at swing time - never at battle load.
  • An Art multiplies by 1.3 (1.4 with War Soul) and takes the element factor twice.
  • Damage = Offense − Defense, capped at 9999 - with an underdog rewrite instead of a floor-at-1 when the defense would win outright.

Physical damage - Offense Value and Defense Value

One routine, two rolls, subtracted. Integer arithmetic throughout; Rnd(1..1.125) is shorthand for x + rand() % (x/8 + 1).

Base Offense = Base ATK + Equipment ATK / 2

Offense = [ Base Offense * Rnd(1..1.125) * Power / 16
          + Current HP / 256
          + Base Offense * Juggle / 64
          + Base Offense * Angle / 65536 ]
          * Arts Modifier * Element * (Element again for an Art)

Defense = Base DEF * Rnd(1..1.125) + Base DEF * Distance / 1024

Damage  = Offense - Defense                 (cap 9999; underdog rewrite below)

Base Offense: base ATK plus half of one slot

Base ATK is the character's ATK with nothing equipped - the battle loader copies it into the actor with no equipment folded in (it folds UDF / LDF / SPD, and skips ATK). The menu's ATK figure is base plus all gear, so the two never match. The equipment half is added at swing time, per command:

The arms are keyed by slot, not item type: Vahn and Gala carry the weapon in slot 2 and the Ra-Seru in slot 3; Noa carries Terra in slot 2 and her claws in slot 3, so hers are swapped. The Art arm sums body and head too - those normally carry no ATK, so in practice it is weapon + Ra-Seru + footwear.

Worked example - Vahn vs Evil Fly

ZetaPhoenix's playtest: Vahn at base ATK 188, weapon ATK 98, footwear ATK 86, Ra-Seru ATK 100, HP 2747; an Evil Fly with UDF 42 / LDF 49, neutral element. The combo is Left arm, Right arm, then Hyper Elbow (a 20-power, LDF-targeting Art); the random draws are the observed ones.

Total 274 + 283 + 536 = 1086, matching the in-game figure. The second hit's juggle 2 is why it out-damages the first from a near-identical base, and the Art takes all three gear ATKs at half and then the ×1.3 - which is where most of an Art's advantage comes from.

The underdog floor

When Offense does not clear the defense, the routine does not floor the hit at 1 - it rebuilds the attack on top of the defense: Defense + (3/4 × Offense + rand() % (Offense/4 + 1)) × Power / 64 plus the juggle terms, re-applies a reduced Art multiplier (×11/10, or ×12/10 with War Soul) and the element pass, and finally a chip floor guarantees a few points (rand()%3 + 3 plain, rand()%4 + 5 for an Art). The "minimum damage" against a heavily-armoured enemy is therefore a fraction of your own roll, not a constant. A petrified defender takes nothing; the whole hit caps at 9999.

The juggle window - what makes a monster "juggleable"

Every hit adds BaseOffense × juggle / 64. The counter climbs by one for each hit that lands while the defender is still reeling from the last, and snaps back to 1 if you miss the window. The bonus is modest - the fourth hit of a juggle carries an extra ~6% - but it is the term that rewards a tight string. Three facts define it:

  • The window is a property of the reaction clip, not the monster's stats: it is open while the defender's current clip is before its first authored event frame. Two bytes of the light-flinch animation entry - a beat and a rate - set its length: window = 4 × beat / rate ticks at 60 Hz in normal play.
  • It restarts on every hit that lands inside it, so what matters is the gap between consecutive hits, never the length of the string.
  • An Art's slow-motion halves or quarters everyone's play speed, which doubles or quadruples every window while it runs - part of why Arts chain so well.

Deep dive: how the reeling byte works

Two writers on the whole disc. Scanning SCUS_942.54 and every overlay image for a store to actor +0x1F7 finds exactly two, four bytes apart, both in the per-frame anim tick FUN_80047430 (0x80047E50 stores 0, 0x80047E54 stores 1); the battle overlay only reads it. A live write-watch across three battle states sees no other writer.

What it stores. Every tick, after advancing the actor's clip cursor: frame = cursor >> 4; byte = (frame < record[+0x10]) ? 1 : 0 - where record[+0x10] is the clip's first event frame, the same list that marks an attack's contact frame. The byte is re-derived each tick from whichever clip the actor is in, not a loaded timer.

On a hit the defender's light flinch (tag 2) is staged with the cursor at 0 and the byte goes up. Each tick the cursor gains speed × rate / 2 sixteenths of a frame (speed = actor +0x21D, 8 in normal play, dropped to 4 / 2 on everyone by Art slow-motion; rate = the entry's +0x78). Hence window = 32 × beat / (speed × rate). A hit inside the window grows the counter and restarts the flinch; a blocking defender is never juggled. Set sites: 0x801ECA20..0x801ECA80.

Live checks. Against Gilium (f8/2) the counter reads 2, 3, 4 on hits 8-12 ticks apart during an Art (window doubled to 32 by slow-motion), 1 on every hit after the drop; Vahn's own f3/1 = 12-tick window measured three times against Gobu Gobu's three hits 24-26 ticks apart, counter 1 each time. Side effects of "whichever clip the actor is in": an actor struck during its own wind-up counts as juggled, and one Cort's beat lies past its 7-frame flinch, so its byte holds for the whole clip.

History: the port once seeded attack from the menu aggregate

The port once seeded a party member's attack from the menu aggregate (base plus every item's full ATK), over-stating Vahn's first hit above from 237 to 472 before the roll. It seeds the base and folds the halved slot per command (World::battle_equip_atk, arms_weapon_atk_fold); physical_predamage ports the roll pair, the Art and element passes, the status scales, the underdog rewrite and the chip floor, drawing rand() in retail order.

Actor stats and the monster record

The per-actor stat block runs +0x14C..+0x16A, each stat a pair of halfwords (working value, base). The loader copies each monster-record halfword into it; the names come from the formulas that consume each slot:

+0x0C+0x14CHPthe applicator's HP delta
+0x10+0x150MPcast costs
+0x0E+0x154AGLthe action gauge the AI spends per action, re-derived each round; "Power Up" raises it
+0x12+0x158ATKattacker's offense

+0x18+0x168INTmagical damage and magic defense; seeds accuracy / evasion

The single "Defense Up" buff raising UDF and LDF together confirms they are two facets of one defense. The parser exposes all six via legaia_asset::monster_archive::MonsterRecord; the enemy table renders them per enemy, along with each monster's castable spell list and AGL costs (decoded from the record's spell-offset array - entry head byte is the spell id, +0x74 the AGL cost the AI checks and spends).

Victory spoils

EXP / gold / drop are inline in each monster record; the spoils routine walks the dead enemies and applies the scaling in the table above:

RecordFieldNote
+0x44 u16base goldscaled, then committed to the wallet (clamp 99,999,999)
+0x46 u16base EXP×3/4, split among living members
+0x48 u8drop item id0 = no drop
+0x49 u8drop chance %per dead enemy, rand()%100 < chance

Runtime-confirmed by write-watchpoint (Gimard credits exactly +15 gold) and cross-checked against the curated game-data tables (Gimard drops Healing Leaf at 10%). The engine wires the scaling into World::apply_battle_loot / apply_battle_xp, so the credited reward is the scaled amount, not the raw record sum; the per-battle no-gold flag on certain scripted fights is the one unmodelled gold gate.

Engine port

crates/engine-vm/src/battle_formulas.rs ports every formula above as pure functions, drawing rand() in retail call order so the RNG stream matches. Live in the battle loop:

  • Melee: physical_predamage + the per-command equipment fold; accuracy rolls; MP-saver accessories.
  • Magic: the faithful summon kernel for player Seru-magic and arts_physical_predamage for monster specials, seeded by the move-power table.
  • Element affinity in both directions - and by slot, not spell: a player Seru cast attacks as the summoned creature, scaling by matrix[creature element][target element]; a monster's element reads record-direct.
  • Finisher: damage_finish - elemental-guard halving, guard halve, the rand()%9+8 floor, the 9999 cap, spirit-gauge accrual.
  • Spoils and escape: the reward scaling and the Run roll.

Every disc-seeded path is gated so an uninstalled table reproduces the placeholder baseline bit-identically - disc-free battles stay deterministic.

Deep dive: the applicator, and what stays open

The applicator FUN_800402F4 (~1,976 instructions) is a selector dispatch, switch(selector) { case 0..0x83 }, over four 8-pointer arrays covering each actor's HP / MP pairs. Selector 0 applies the pending HP delta against the per-party-slot damage-cap table; selector 9 is the accuracy roll; selectors 1..7 are the ×6/5 stat-buff ramps (each touching a working/base halfword pair). Selectors 0x10..0x83 are stat-up animations, status clears, queue-end markers and multi-target item slots - mostly read-only ramps.

Open threads: the retail debuff factor (the port keeps debuffs additive); the interior semantics of the per-spell effect descriptor a monster spell's effect index resolves to; what the kernel does with a negative angle halfword (a strike from behind) - uncaptured; and two juggle loose ends (the four-slot list helper that no disc reaction entry exercises, and whether the parry guard window uses the same beat). See open RE threads.
