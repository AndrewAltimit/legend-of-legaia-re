Dance minigame

Noa’s dance contest in the Sol Fever Disco is a rhythm game. A beat counter advances with the music, a step chart says which button to press on each beat, and a press counts only inside a timing window that opens on the beat. Closing a chain of correct presses pays points and fills a groove gauge, which promotes the dancer to a denser, higher-scoring chart row. Two rivals play the same chart through the same judge, so their scores are real runs rather than a scripted curve.

The dance in one minute

Buttons
Square and Circle are the chart notes. Triangle is a three-per-song wildcard, the “groovy move”.
Timing
A beat is 281 clock units. A press counts from the beat to 210 units after it; the last 71 units before the next beat are dead.
Points
Only two things score: closing a chain of notes, and a Triangle. A matching note that does not close a chain scores nothing.
Groove gauge
0..2999, never lowered. Each 1000 is a level: the dancer moves to a harder chart row whose chains are longer and pay more.
Score cap
999 per dancer.
Song
92 beats (60 in the how-to lesson); the chart repeats every 32 beats.
Outcome
Story flag 0x50A: set on entry, cleared on a loss. The field script that follows reads it.

Overlay: extraction PROT 0980, 0x8000 bytes at 0x801CE818; init FUN_801CEF54, per-frame tick FUN_801CF470, judge FUN_801D1960, award routine FUN_801D1AF4. Parsers legaia_minigame_formats::dance_chart / dance_cast / dance_art; rules port legaia_engine_minigames::dance.

Getting there

The door is the mode-24 minigame warp: field-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 106 (sub-id 6) sets game mode 0x18 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup, overlay-load, return-to-field sequence, so the game returns to the exact field state it suspended. The mechanism is on the field / event VM page.

This is the one sub-id where the loader’s index arithmetic steps: sub-ids of 6 and above add two, so sub-id 6 resolves to PROT 0980 rather than 0978. A disc-wide walk of every scene puts every dance door in the casino scene koin3; the hall’s own scene module other7 carries no genuine warp.

The tick routine has no direct caller: it is the tick word of a static actor template the init spawns, reached every frame through the actor-pool walk.

How a session flows

The count-in

The hall runs at a frame delta of 3, so every state body runs once per three vsyncs. The count-in is 38 READY runs, 11 GO!-in runs and 11 GO!-out runs. There is no 1 2 3 before the song: the digits belong to the finish countdown, four sprite parts spawned at once whose move programs differ only in their wait and their cue.

Four modes

The field script picks the mode by setting a story flag before the warp. Setup tests 0x133 (how-to), 0x134 (qualifier), 0x135 (finals) and 0x428 (free play) in that order, so the last flag set wins; it then clears the first three but not 0x428, the standing free-play state.

The timing window

Every press is judged against one clock. phase is where the clock sits inside the current beat (0..280), and a press counts only while phase ≤ 0xD2 (210). The window is not centred on the beat: it opens on the beat and closes 210 units later. The 71 units before each beat are a dead zone, so a press made early lands there and misses.

  • A chain is lane + 1 matched notes. One note at level 1, two at level 2, three at level 3.
  • A chain must close inside one 8-beat bar. The chain cursor is cleared every 8 beats, so a figure left half done when the bar turns over is lost.
  • There is no Perfect / Good text tier in the judge. Hit quality is carried only by the weight above.
  • A press is judged once. Every judged press sets a short latch, so a held button is not re-judged every frame; the judge reads the new-press pad word.

The note to press is the judged cell

The CPU feed can substitute a Triangle on a combo slot, but the judge always reads the raw chart cell. Because lane 0’s notes all sit on combo slots, the two diverge on exactly the beats that carry notes. Anything that draws the chart for a player must draw the judged cell; the port exposes both (DanceGame::judged_symbol and required_symbol), and the site’s playable dance drives its note highway off the former.

Judge FUN_801D1960(player, lane, pressed): returns 0 miss, 1 matched, 2 chain closed. CPU feed FUN_801D1820, symbol to pad bit FUN_801D4040.

The chart, the lanes and the bonus table

The step chart is three rows of 32 bytes, one byte per beat. A dancer’s row is its lane, gauge / 1000, so the three rows are three difficulty tiers and every dancer, human or rival, reads the row its own gauge has promoted it to. All three tables are baked into the overlay image; nothing is loaded per song.

SymbolMeaningPad bit
0restnone
1Square0x80
2Circle0x20
3Triangle, produced only by the CPU feed0x10

The lane-0 row puts every one of its steps on a 4-beat boundary (0,0,0,1, 0,0,0,2, ...), so its notes sit exactly on the combo slots, the beats where beat & 3 == 3.

Two scoring tables sit beside the chart, both indexed by the dancer’s kind (the same id that picks its mesh):

  • Bonus points, four kinds by four lanes. Each retail row is k, 2k, 3k: the (lane + 1) scaling is baked into the data. Noa’s row (kind 0) is the richest; the competitor kinds are progressively poorer.
  • CPU triangle schedule, four kinds by 16 slots. Entry n is how many combo slots the dancer must bank before spending its n-th groovy move; a huge value means never. Noa’s row is all zeros, because the human’s Triangles are pressed, not scheduled.

Chart 0x801D509C (PROT 0980 file 0x6884), bonus points 0x801D41A4 (0x598C), triangle schedule 0x801D41E4 (0x59CC); shapes pinned by the index arithmetic in the judge and the CPU feed.

Score and the groove gauge

One award routine runs once per frame for every dancer on the floor. The human’s input is the live pad; every other dancer’s is a pad word synthesised from the chart. Score has exactly two sources:

  • Score is clamped to 999 and the gauge to [0, 2999]. Every writer of the gauge in the overlay is an award, a clamp, or a run / tutorial reset.
  • Feedback is the human’s only. The banner, the sound and the face pose fire for player 0; the rivals score silently.

The rivals

The competitors run the same award routine off a pad word synthesised from the same chart. They differ from the human only by kind, which selects their row of the two scoring tables. A rival therefore hits every note its own lane calls for (so its gauge climbs and promotes it), takes the flat table bonus for each closed chain with no accuracy weighting, and throws its groovy moves on the disc’s schedule. Its cadence is limited by the same press latch as the human’s, plus the move clip it is playing.

The groovy move (Triangle)

Triangle matches no chart cell. It has its own branch, taught by the how-to as a wildcard to press with the last command of a combo, three uses a song. The stock is set to 3 per dancer before the song and is never replenished.

  1. GateOnly when the press latch is clear and the stock is non-zero.
  2. SpendThe stock drops by one wherever the press lands.
  3. PayOn a combo slot inside the window: (lane + 1) × 25 and a full +1000 on the gauge. Anywhere else: only (lane + 1) × 3.
  4. MoveThe dancer plays the lane’s groovy-move clip and spins lane + 1 full turns. Nothing it presses is judged until the clip ends.

The finale tier. In the finish countdown and the result wipe (states 11 and 12) the pad is still read, and the same branch pays (lane + 1) × 17 << 1, the ×34 column above, and raises a per-dancer flag whose reader is not in the dump corpus. It is a timing tier, not a “perfect” one.

Award routine FUN_801D1AF4, 0x10 branch; finale arm 0x801D1CE0..0x801D1D30. Lock figures from the clip lengths at the hall’s frame delta of 3.

Playing it well

Each of these follows from a rule above.

  • Press on the beat or just after, never early. The window opens on the beat; the stretch before it is dead.
  • Accuracy only matters on the closing note. A note that does not close a chain scores nothing whatever its timing, and the closing note’s weight sets the whole chain’s bonus, from 50% at the window edge to 100% on the beat.
  • Finish figures inside the bar. The chain resets every 8 beats.
  • Climb early. The gauge never falls, and every payout scales with the lane. Each level costs four closed chains or one landed groovy move.
  • Save Triangles for combo slots, ideally the last note of a combo at the highest lane. Off a combo slot it still spends a use for (lane + 1) × 3.
  • Budget the lockout. At lanes 1 and 2 a groovy move swallows the next combo slot; at lane 2 also the start of the next three-note combo.
  • A Triangle still pays after the song. The countdown and the wipe read the pad, at the ×34 finale tier.

The dancers

The overlay names every dancer through a per-mode spawn table and a five-record kind descriptor table; the bodies and clips come from the dance-hall scene module other7, whose first motion section is a 60-record animation bundle (PROT 1229). The rivals are dedicated dancer NPCs, not party members; the casino scene koin3 places the same NPCs on its own floor.

  • A move is a clip. Each descriptor carries eleven move pairs: the miss reaction for Square and Circle, a chain-complete move per lane, and a groovy-move step per lane. A dancer is judged only while its bound clip is the idle or the dance loop, so any move, the miss reaction included, locks its presses out for its whole length.
  • Faces are stamped, not modelled. Each dancer’s eyes and mouth are two VRAM blits from a per-rig pose bank (open / blink / intense / wink) into the window the head samples. The human’s pose switches on a scoring event.
  • The Disco King is an off-screen voice. He stands on the lower floor so close to the eye that, under the how-to’s fixed camera, he projects below the frame; nothing judges or scores him. A retail lesson shows Noa alone centre-stage, and the port frames it the same way.

Spawner FUN_801D0190, descriptors at 0x801D4E1C, face stamp FUN_801D03C4, per-dancer handler FUN_801D1358. The party-clip-bank bit 0x200 of a descriptor anim word is carried by no clip on the disc.

The how-to lesson

Mode 2 is driven by the Disco King’s own script, a state machine that prints a line, waits for any face button, and resets the shared run state between segments. In order:

  1. PromptA yes / no choice, moved by Left / Right although the options are stacked vertically. “No thanks” skips to an acknowledgement.
  2. Button pagesFour text pages on the buttons.
  3. Square / Circle practiceProbes the press through the real judge and praises a correct one; ends when a progress accumulator reaches 900.
  4. Level page“The level rises automatically”; arms the load gate.
  5. Groovy-move practicePraise or a timing scold off the triangle-landed flag, for the length of the feedback window; shares the 900 gate.

Two countdown holds clear the beat clock before advancing; both seed 0x3C and advance only once the timer goes negative, so they run one frame longer than the seed reads. The port carries the script’s shape only (dance_tutorial): the dialogue is Sony text, so hosts draw placeholder captions at the retail line positions.

Script FUN_801D0750, a 19-slot jump table at 0x801CEEE8, 18 working states.

What you see and hear

The HUD

  • Three score boxes along the top, with the human always in the centre box. Free play draws only the centre one.
  • The human’s gauge and beat track only. Rival gauge rows exist in the code but draw only while a dev counter is raised; retail never draws them. The gauge reads Lv. plus one digit, with no number run.
  • The beat track is twelve body tiles and the next eight chart cells as notes, scrolling under a clipped draw area. Body and caps flash on beat & 7 == 3 (beat & 3 at level 0) inside the first 0x46 phase units. Three stock markers show the Triangles left.
  • The whole HUD draws additively: every widget record carries the additive blend rate.

A closed chain fires no SFX-ring cue: its sting keys two SPU voices directly out of the dance’s own sample bank, one of three random tone pairs. A groovy move plays its cue and a sting at a fixed variant (r = 5) that the random pick (r = 0..2) never reaches. The Chicken!! art on the HUD page has no widget record and no spawner.

The music

Each mode streams its song once the READY counter reaches 0x6F: extraction 1048 (music_01 #60, “Sol disco final 1”) for the qualifier and the how-to, 1054 (#66, “Sol disco final 2”) for the finals, and a rotation over 1065 / 1048 / 1054 in free play. The songs are short loops of about one 32-beat chart cycle. See music tracks.

HUD driver FUN_801D231C over a 34-record widget table at 0x801D46CC; cues are direct stores into the SFX ring (SFX table), descriptor block PROT 1228, samples PROT 1231; sting FUN_801D3D78.

The hall, the floor and the camera

The dance is an ordinary scene load. The hall - the raised stage, the checkered floor, banners, spotlight cones, smoke columns and fixtures - is the other7 scene module’s own field geometry, staged by the SCUS loaders the init calls. The overlay issues no texture or mesh load of its own; its own loads are all sound. Its art pack (PROT 1230, 31 TIMs) carries the HUD page, the three dancer face strips and the venue textures, and 27 of its 31 images match a live retail VRAM capture byte for byte.

Entry and exit are a save / restore pair. The init saves the walked-in scene’s name and PROT block base and points the block base at other7; the teardown restores both and arms a BGM reload, once the results fade completes.

The camera flies a keyframe track. From the entry pose it eases (a half-cosine) from key to key, one segment per 0x152 frames, wrapping from key 13 back to key 1, so key 0 plays only once and the flight is not locked to the beat. The how-to holds the entry pose throughout. On the far poses the eye passes behind the stage-entrance curtain; back-face rejection and the GPU’s polygon-size limit keep the curtain out of the frame, and the port applies both rules.

The floor reuses the field engine. The floor passes walk the scene’s cell grid, and the step-marker tiles are the scene’s own tile-trigger records with clip 6..=9: ordinary field actors that each run a mesh flipbook, four classes out of step. On other7 ten such tiles land on drawn floor cells.

Init FUN_801CEF54, exit FUN_801D414C; camera block 0x801CF51C..0x801CF7D8; floor passes FUN_801D3A2C / FUN_801D2A10; marker tick FUN_801D0640; floor kernel legaia_engine_field::minigame_floor.

Where this is in the port

The dance is fully playable in the port on all three surfaces, with the rules, the cast, the HUD, the camera and the sound all running off disc data you supply.

Play it in the browser
The minigames page runs a qualifier session with the retail cast, the hall and the rivals scoring their own runs. Its camera starts moving with the song.
Reach it in the game
The play page and the native window run the world session: walk to the door in the casino, or on native press K for a qualifier and U for the how-to.
  • Rules are legaia_engine_minigames::dance: the beat clock, the judge, the CPU feed, the award, the count-in and the finish countdown. A press returns the full event (miss, hit, chain with its points, groovy with its landed flag and lock).
  • Floor size follows the mode: three dancers in the contests, one in the how-to, six in free play. The door warp picks the mode from the story flags and the song’s end applies the win-flag rule, in engine-core, so every host that drains the warp gets them.
  • Host difference: both browser pages draw the step-marker tiles; the native window draws the hall but not the tiles.
