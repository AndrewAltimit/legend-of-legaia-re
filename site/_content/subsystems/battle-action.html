Battle action state machine

The layer between “the player picked Attack” and “the sword has landed and HP has been deducted”. An action takes many frames - turn, run in, swing, damage pop, walk back - so the game tracks it as one state byte advanced once per frame, with a band of states per command category. A Tactical Art is not a band of its own: its arrows and art bytes ride in the same action queue as a plain Attack and play out through the attack states, each swing staging its own power byte.

The state machine

The driver runs every frame from the battle main dispatcher. It resolves the active combatant through the 8-slot actor pointer table and pumps the action through three keys:

KeyFieldValues
Action categoryactor[+0x1DE]0 Tactical Arts, 1 Item, 2 Magic, 3 Attack, 4 Spirit, 5 Run / Defend
Execution phasectx[7]the bands in the figure, plus 0xFD (idle hold) and 0xFF (round end); any unhandled byte falls to the shared epilogue as a no-op
Per-actor sub-stateactor[+0x1DC] + scratchflag bits (0x01 windup done, 0x02 advance done, 0x04 exit); +0x1D9 current anim, +0x1DA queued anim, +0x1DF..+0x1F2 the action queue
  • Not a bytecode VM. No opcode table, no program counter. Each state body waits on a per-actor condition - animation matched, timer expired, range check passed - and writes the next state when ready.
  • Magic and Item cross over. Magic seeds 0x28 but a low-class, low-id spell (class byte < 0x14 and id < 0x65) takes 0x3C; Item seeds 0x3C but items 0x98 / 0x99 take 0x28. Player Seru magic (0x81+) always takes 0x28. The choice picks animation and timing, not cost.
  • Run. 0x64 rolls the escape. A failed run returns to 0x50 (turn consumed, battle continues); a success enters 0x66, which stages a 64-frame fade, raises the battle-end signal and parks.
  • Wipe arm. The 0x5A victory arm stages the win pose off the acting actor’s party slot, re-picking a living member only when the actor is dead. Retail is safe because the wipe scan and the scheduler share one liveness predicate; the randomizer’s enemy-ally charm widens that mask and breaks it (charm softlock).
  • Effects are indirect. The machine calls the UI-element spawner with an element id (0x07, 0x0F, 0x34, …) and the effect VM resolves it to sprite spawns. That path draws the 2D layer; the 3D summon is a separate mechanism below.
  • Two known parks. The 0x51 HP-bar settle gate and the 0x19 attack-approach range poll can wait forever under the wrong seeding; both are dissected in the full doc and the Gaza write-up.

One attack, state by state

The attack band is the one most turns take. A party member's committed Attack plays out like this:

  1. 0x14 Faceturn toward the target, range check; already in reach skips to the strike loop
  2. 0x19 Close inhold until the range check passes (a monster walks via 0x15–0x18, its walk clip’s root motion carrying it)
  3. 0x1E Strike loopone queue byte per swing clip: direction, art starter or art; a counterattack can redirect here
  4. 0x1F Recoverrecover pose; wait for the advance-done flag to clear
  5. 0x20 Returnhold for the last swing’s clip, then the target’s reaction; the pass the last art clip ends, every slot’s animation rate returns to normal (FUN_801E93C8)
  6. 0x50–0x52 Doneauto-revive, Spirit +8, HUD teardown, Seru-absorb banner hold
  7. 0x5A Gatewipe check, then the next actor or the round end

Every reaction turns the defender. When a melee strike commits its reaction (0x801EEC34..0x801EECBC), the defender’s heading is set to the bearing of the attacker, block included, and nothing turns it back: a struck monster keeps that heading until its own action recomputes it, and its knockback steps along it. The port does the same in World::commit_melee_reaction.

When the monster’s action table has no walk entry it falls back to the party’s 0x19 path, which has no movement code and no timeout: if nothing else closes the distance it waits forever - the root of the 0x19 park. On arrival a walking monster shoves its target back to the range boundary, not itself forward. A counterattack fires on the attacker’s first frame when the target chose Attack, carries the Counterattack passive and won the picker’s coin flip: the target becomes the active actor and swings first.

Seru-magic summons

When a character casts a Seru spell, the creature that appears is not in the battle overlay: the cast state pages a small per-summon code overlay off the disc into slot B (0x801F69D8, above the resident battle overlay) while the cast band waits. That is why every summon has its own PROT entry and why a mid-cast save state holds an extra chunk of code.

The loader resolves its argument in the raw TOC (+ 0x381), which is extraction entry + 0x37F (PROT). The player and enemy arms of the same spell ship separate stagers. What a stager contains:

  • No mesh geometry. The meshes are the separately loaded 30-entry model library etmd.dat (extraction 0871) that the battle loader registers at battle init; the flame atlas etim.dat is 0870 (effect cluster).
  • Part records - [i16 model_sel][u16 reserved][move-VM bytecode] - handed to the executable’s part-stager, which spawns a part-actor per record: model_sel ≥ 0 seats a library mesh, -1 is a transform node, 0x4000 / 0x4001 are special render modes. Parser legaia_asset::summon_overlay.
  • Motion is geometric. Gimard’s flame is eight RNG-seeded part-actors moving; the palette is byte-identical across animation-distinct frames.
  • The player summon draws as an ordinary battle actor through the TRS-keyframe pipeline, like any monster; the part records drive the stager, not the draw. The port seats each summon’s own body and clips the same way on both hosts (summon::summon_spawn_asset), and streams the cast’s own summon.dat texture pages into VRAM when the stager arms.
Details: stager internals, enemy stagers, the record-table trim

Case 0x29 of the outer machine performs the load when the queued spell id is in the player block: it advances to 0x32, stores the per-summon effect-data pointer (&PTR_s_re_check_801f6734)[id - 0x81] into _DAT_8007BA2C, and calls overlay loader B with id - 0x79. Every player id 0x81..=0x8B and eight of ten evolved ids are capture-pinned mid-cast (0x90 / 0x91 predicted).

Stagers spawn through FUN_80021B04(world_pos, render_slots, record_ptr, 0x1000), directly or via the pool wrapper FUN_80050ED4 (stores the actor in the 0x60-slot pool at DAT_801C90F0). In the spell-0x83 slot (extraction 905), FUN_801F16A0 phase 0 loops eight spawns with rand()-seeded actor fields (+0xB4 = rng % 15 + 16, +0xB6 = rng % 255 + 512); the record pointers resolve to file 0x180C..0x1E00 under the slot-B base. The jal into the move VM lives in the executable’s stager, not the overlay.

A live Gimard cast shows the overlay’s own scene-graph entry at 0 calls while the battle per-actor draw runs in exact lockstep with the TRS-keyframe decoder, once per live actor per frame - the player summon is a battle actor. The active flame is library mesh DAT_8007C018[26], one of ten fire-textured meshes (CLUT row 478), drawn Gouraud-textured off the resident etim page at (832, 256). The enemy Fire Tail is a single move-VM part-actor over a record in the battle overlay’s effect-prototype table (0x801F6324, 61 pointers → 54 unique records); PROT 0900’s screen-widget family stays dormant outside the ending scenes.

Trim before counting. Extraction .BINs for stagers over-read into their neighbours; an entry’s own content is (next_start_lba − start_lba) × 0x800 bytes (unique_content_len; Cort mid-cast saves pin 0938 → 0x1800, 0940 / 0944 / 0961 → 0x2000, 0962 → 0x2800, 0966 → 0x4000). Trimmed, every record’s first word is -1, a small mesh index, or 0x4000. The 0x4000 render-mode records sit in five player stagers (Palma 0928, Mule 0929, Jedo 0931, Aluru 0916, Iota 0921); no live capture has seated one, so those render modes’ draw behaviour is open.

The action queue and Tactical Arts triggers

What the player types on the D-pad becomes a flat list of action bytes before the machine sees it: the per-actor byte stream at actor[+0x1DF..+0x1F2]. Miracle Arts and Super Arts are pattern matches on that list, which is why the right arrows in the right order “upgrade” a chain into a named combo. The queue builder runs both passes, in this order, when the seed state commits the turn:

  1. Miracle Art match. Two conditions, both required. The slot’s Miracle marker ctx[+0x25F + slot] has to be set — it is raised once per battle, when the party actor is seated, from the acting character’s Ra-Seru equipment byte, not by anything the player types. And the command string has to equal the character’s Miracle string (Vahn’s Craze: R D L U L U R D L). Then the whole queue becomes the replacement string: four directions, the SpecialStarter byte, then the arts.
  2. Super Art tail replace. For each chained art the builder walks the character’s five Super find patterns; a match on the queue tail is replaced by the replace tail ending in the finisher. The match needs the find’s last art to be the queue’s last action, and every participating art to have paid AP.

Every string is capture-validated byte for byte against legaia_art’s tables, and a Vahn Tri-Somersault capture’s queue tail 19 27 0F 19 1F 0E 1A 2B 2B 2B equals its modelled replace. The Super pass applies once and takes the first matching row in resident-table order — not to a fixpoint, and not the longest match.

Why a saved chain is not enough. The connector direction after each art is per-combo data - Vahn’s 0x27 is followed by 0F in Tri-Somersault but 0E in Power Slash - so recognising a Super from a list of named arts means tokenising the arrows into arts first and tail-matching the art ordering, not comparing raw strings.

Strikes. The attack chain walks the queue one swing per animation clip. Each swing stages its own power byte, hit timing, hit cue and status effect; damage lands on the clip’s authored hit events through the melee kernel, never in the state body.

History: the ctx[+0x274] queue hypothesis

The queue was once placed at ctx[+0x274]. A capture showed that word is the turn-order active-actor index; the queue is the per-actor stream at actor[+0x1DF..+0x1F2], confirmed by the Noa Miracle and Vahn Tri-Somersault captures (super-art queue capture).

Spirit and Run

Spirit (category 4, states 0x46–0x48) is a gauge move, not an attack. 0x46 stages two targets off the actor’s own AGL and Spirit gauge: next turn’s arts bar becomes min(base AGL × 7/5 + 8, 288), and the Spirit gauge climbs by 32 (35 or 40 with the AP Boost passives), capped at 100. 0x47 grows both bars on screen; 0x48 finishes the ramp. The pending category byte +0x1DE == 4 is also what the damage kernels read as the guard stance until the round’s sweep clears it.

Run (category 5) rolls the escape formula in 0x64. On success every downed party member is floored at 1 HP before the teardown - which is why fleeing cures Stone - and the battle ends with no loot. Chicken Heart (passive 0x34) scales the party roll ×1.5 and Chicken King (0x37) forces a tie, both only from living wearers; the scripted-fight flag ctx+0x287 blocks both.

In the port

engine-battle-vm::battle_action (re-exported as engine-vm::battle_action) keeps one enum value per retail state byte (ActionState), so an engine transition trace lines up against a retail capture line by line. step(host, ctx) runs one frame of dispatch; BattleActionHost carries a callback per helper the state bodies call; validate_action ports the executable’s 18-arm action validator. engine-core::World composes it into the battle tick.
