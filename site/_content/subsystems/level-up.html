Level-Up

The last enemy falls, the spoils window counts out EXP and gold, and sometimes a second window announces that someone's level went up. Underneath, the game splits the EXP among the survivors, compares each one against a threshold curve, and grows eight stats per level crossed. The numbers are tidy: the whole XP curve is a closed form, and every stat's growth is a normalised curve that lands each character exactly on their level-99 ceiling. Fully ported

Two things surprise players: a level-up raises maximum HP and MP but does not heal you, and one big award can jump several levels at once. Dead party members get no XP.

XP curve

The per-level XP delta is ⌊n²/4⌋ + 1 (1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, …, 2402 over 98 levels); the base threshold for a level is the running sum of those deltas. The applier then scales that sum and, for Noa and Gala, corrects it.

StepRule
Base sumsum(level) = Σ delta[0..level]
Scale, level < 17threshold = sum × 9,999,999 / 0x140FE (about ×121.69)
Scale, level ≥ 17threshold = sum × 121
Party-slot correctionNoa subtracts and Gala adds threshold × 20 / divisor(level); the divisor curve runs 125, 251, 376, … so the correction shrinks from ~16% at L1 to ~0.5% mid-game.
Loopdo { level++; grow stats } while (threshold ≤ XP) - several levels per award are possible.

Worked example - the level-2 thresholds a New Game Status screen shows: Vahn and Terra 121, Noa 102, Gala 140. The base curve continues 365, 730, 1338, 2190, … 9,646,483 at level 99. The engine carries the closed-form curve in legaia_save::retail_xp_table() and re-reads the same values from the user's executable at boot, so the two cross-check each other.

Stat gains

Each level crossed grows eight stats - HP max, MP max and the six battle stats - from two tables: three growth curves (one byte per level, each ramp settling to a plateau) and a per-character block of eight {start, max, jitter, row} entries, one per stat.

TermRule
Core gain(max − start) × curve[row][level−1] / 0x24C0
Jitter+ rand() % (2×jitter + 1) − jitter, one BIOS rand() per stat per level
Floor and capsmax(1, core + jitter); then HP ≤ 9999, MP ≤ 999, SP ≤ 280, others ≤ 999
Why 0x24C0Every curve sums to exactly 9408, so the gains accumulate to exactly max − start over 98 levels: each stat lands on its ceiling at level 99.

Worked example - Noa, L2 to L3, HP: (4500 − 150) × 82 / 9408 = 37, jitter ±4; the capture shows +39. Every one of her eight deltas that battle (MP +5, stats +2/+4/+4/+3/+4/+3) sits inside its core ± jitter band. Two per-character facts from the captures: Noa also gains +40 max Spirit per level, Gala gains none.

What a level-up writes

OffsetFieldNote
+0x004Cumulative XPThe value the threshold loop compares against; the Status menu draws it as Experience.
+0x11C..+0x12DRecord-side stat window9 u16: HP max, MP max, a constant 100 at +0x120, six stats. Grown first, then mirrored to the live window +0x104..+0x11B.
+0x120Per-stat cap constant 100Constant across every captured save; not Spirit max. The runtime 999 clamp is a code constant.
+0x130Displayed levelThe byte the Status screen reads as LV and the "Level 99" cheat targets. Incremented once per level-up event, never re-derived from XP.

A level-up is not a heal. The applier stores to eleven addresses and every one is a maximum, a stat or a level; current HP and MP appear nowhere in it, and its only call is the BIOS rand. The capture agrees: Noa enters her L2-to-L3 fight at 164/182 HP and settles at 164/221 - ceiling up, wound unchanged.

The port

  • World::apply_battle_xp splits the reward among survivors (integer divide) and calls LevelUpTracker::grant_xp per slot; multi-level jumps collapse into one LevelUpResult.
  • apply_to_record grows the record window, mirrors it live, and leaves current HP / MP alone. The port keeps its own level byte at +0x100 (always zero in retail) and hydrates the tracker from it on load.
  • Jitter is opt-in (with_level_up_jitter(seed), a faithful BIOS-rand LCG) so replay and determinism oracles stay bit-identical; it is off by default.
  • The banner (LevelUpBanner, 180 frames) and the spoils report are retail's two framed windows, drawn from geometry measured off a capture. Two known gaps: only the last character's banner is shown, and both windows draw over the returned field scene rather than over the battle.

Multi-level captures (vahn_4_level_jump and siblings, codified in engine_core::levelup::observations) pin write regions but not per-level rates; the single-level Noa pair is the arithmetic oracle. A Fire Book I use writes one region, +0x185..+0x188: a count-prefixed list of displayed-skill ids read only by the menu's status renderer (CharacterRecord::displayed_skills). The Seru-struct +0x74 reads are the actor's colour word (FUN_800480D8 stamps 0x00808080), not a growth grant. The grant tables are not in PROT.DAT at all.

Battle-actor stat struct (runtime, distinct from the record)

DAT_801C9370[slot] points at the in-battle actor (8 slots: party 0..2, monsters 3..7). Party actors are initialised by FUN_80053cb8 from the record's live window, monsters by FUN_80054cb0 from the monster archive; both write the same layout. Stats are working / base pairs - the working copy takes buffs and damage, the +2 base copy serves un-buff and percentage math.
