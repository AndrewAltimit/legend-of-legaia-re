Field locomotion

What happens, every frame, between holding a d-pad direction and Vahn taking a step in a town, a dungeon or on the overworld. The controller reads the pad relative to the camera, inches the player forward two units at a time against a per-scene wall grid, settles the height afterwards, and fires doors and story beats on the tile it lands on.

At a glance

Controller
FUN_801D01B0 in the field overlay (PROT 0897), shared by towns, dungeons and the overworld walk. Not the tile-board puzzle mode that shares the overlay.
Collision grid
Per scene, one byte per 128-unit tile: high nibble = four sub-cell wall bits, low nibble = floor tier. Streamed from the scene's .MAP file at load; scripts paint story-conditional changes on top.
Speed
480 units (3.75 tiles) per second walking, 720 running - counted in vsyncs, so the same on a busy frame as an empty one.
Height
A second controller after the walk: floor sampled from the grid's low nibble, or from per-tile ramp records; ledges trigger a scripted hop.
In the port
World::step_field_locomotion (engine-core); collision is retail by default, precise free-angle movement is an opt-in enhancement (below).
Confidence
Confirmed - runtime write-watchpoints on the player position, wall-press captures, disassembly (how we know).

How walking works

One pass of the controller, in order. Everything below the second step is about one question: may the player move two more units this way?

  1. GateSkip everything while the player is held: an encounter queued, a cutscene or talk owns him (flag 0x80000). This also blocks the pause menu.
  2. Camera remapRotate the held d-pad by the camera's eighth-turn so "up" means "away from the camera"; snap the heading to one of eight angles.
  3. Wall slideIf the wanted direction is blocked, probe along the wall and add a perpendicular direction, so the player skids instead of sticking.
  4. SpeedBase step (8 walk, 12 run) × the actor's multiplier × the frame scalar; halved on slow terrain, trimmed by a quarter on diagonals.
  5. Step loopSpend the speed 2 units at a time, Z then X; each axis moves only if its collision probe is clear.
  6. SettleRe-sample the floor height under the new position; hop a ledge if the floor ahead rises a step.
  7. TriggersEntering a new tile checks the trigger block: teleports, door records, story beats, encounters.
  • Direction. The remapped mask names axes: 0x1000 Z+, 0x4000 Z−, 0x2000 X+, 0x8000 X−. A diagonal is two axes resolved independently, so a diagonal into a wall still slides along the open axis.
  • Wall slide. For a blocked cardinal, three probes 62 units ahead (centre, ±33 lateral) sweep an offset table across the travel axis and add the slide direction the sweep's sign picks. Pure diagonals and symmetric dead ends add nothing.
  • Animation follows motion. A committed step selects the walk or run clip; no step selects idle. The pad is never consulted for the clip, and walking and wall contact are silent.
  • Run is an exclusive or. The Field Move option sets the default and the held button inverts it, so with Run selected the button walks. The button is Cross | R1 (mask 0x48), seeded once at new game and never rewritten: retail cannot rebind it.

A walkability tile

The world is cut into 128-unit tiles, and each tile is split again into four 64-unit sub-cells. One byte per tile carries both a wall bit for each sub-cell and the tile's floor height.

bits 7..4wall bits, one per sub-cellread by collision
bits 3..0floor tier 0..15read by the floor sampler
  • Two nibbles, two mappings. The collision probe maps world to sub-cell with a bias (Z floored then +2, X rounded up then −1), which pushes the half-tile-centred player onto the forward tile; the floor sampler reads the same bytes with plain indexing. A sweep that reads the whole byte as a wall mask calls every raised tile a wall.
  • The grid wraps. Both the row and the column are masked to 7 bits, so a Z past the last row reads a real row rather than "no wall".
  • Actors block too. Before the wall probes, three wider probes test other actors: a static prop blocks within ±80 units of its footprint, a moving NPC within ±40 of its live position. A hit links the pair, which is how a door's touch script fires on contact.

How fast is a frame?

Retail runs two clocks: the vsync (60/s) and the game tick, one pass of the master frame driver. A scratchpad byte, the frame scalar, says how many vsyncs the last tick spanned (1..4, floored at 2 in field scenes and 3 on the overworld). Every duration and velocity inside the tick is multiplied by it, so a cadence change moves the sample rate, never the speed.

The overworld step comes from a per-scene MAN flag set on exactly the three kingdom maps, and each kingdom's entry script also sets the player's multiplier to 0xC00, so the overworld walk is (5 × 0xC00) >> 12 = 3 units per vsync. Reading a per-frame number in the wrong clock is where "the player moves 1.67× too fast" comes from; the port ticks once per vsync with the scalar at 1.

The field buffer: where walls come from

Nothing in a town's script says "this is a wall". The scene loader streams the scene's .MAP file into a per-scene buffer, and the collision grid is one region of it.

The .MAP is the first 0x12000 bytes, streamed by CD-DMA at scene load; a write-watchpoint on the live grid catches only the DMA writer. Scripts then paint story-conditional rectangles on top with 4C 7s - Rim Elm's south gate stays painted shut until the story opens it.

Pressing the action button runs a separate probe: a radius-64 compass point in the facing sector against a ±72 NPC box. A hit engages the NPC's script and turns the player toward it. Props divide into two classes on one authored bit: doors open on body contact, cupboards (which run 31 1E in their spawn prologue) only on the button.

Height: two floor models

The walk controller only writes X and Z. After it commits, a second controller re-samples the floor under the new position and glides Y toward it; a third probes ahead along the last step and starts a scripted hop when the floor rises a ledge.

Tile word bit 0x800ModelUsed for
clearBilinear nibble surface: the four corner tiles' tiers through a 16-entry height LUT, weighted by sub-tile positionflat ground, gentle terrain
setElevation override: flat mean of the four corner tiers plus the tile's kind-2 record (−32 per whole-tile step, −16 per sub-cell step). No interpolationramps and staircases

The kind-2 record replaces the bilinear branch; it is not a fast path on top of it. Rim Elm's shore ramps sit on sea-level nibbles and hold their whole elevation in kind-2 records; interpolating their nibbles walks the player under the drawn stairs.

Triggers: doors, beats and teleports

Crossing onto a new tile (tile = (world − 0x40) >> 7, compared against last frame's) looks the tile up in the trigger block. Scene entry marks the compare stale, so the arrival tile fires on the first tick.

KindRecordWhat it does

1, gate 0[x][z][p0_record][0]At scene init, bind a partition-0 record to the object on that tile as its touch script and idle clip - house doors, cupboards

A beat that should play once self-latches: its script sets the very flag its gate lists, so it runs on the crossing that finds the flag clear and blocks itself afterwards. The gate bitmap is the field VM's own story-flag bank, so a set is visible to the next record immediately.

Walking into a house

A town house is not a scene change: the interior is a sub-area of the same 128×128 grid parked in an unused corner, and the door repositions the player. Two door mechanisms exist, and one house can use one of each.

ClassMechanismLives in
Script doorA gate-0 bind whose record moves the player channel 0xF8 on contact - instant teleport (A3 F8), teleport + move clip (CC F8 51) or an animated walk to a tile (C7 F8)MAN partition 0
Map doorA kind-0 teleport tile: no object, no script, no name.MAP trigger block

Map doors are the larger class, and a MAN-only census cannot see them. Vahn's house is the mixed case: script door in, map door out.

NPCs: facing and glide speed

Initial facing

The placement record has no facing byte. At spawn the installer pre-runs the record's leading ops (a 0x24 / 0x25 marker up to the first 0x21), and two of them write the heading from the 8-direction LUT at SCUS 0x80073F04: the NPC move-to-tile op 4C 51 (operand +3, low nibble) and op 0x38's simple path. Town prologues route that leg through story-flag tests, so the fall-through branch is the fresh-game facing. Rim Elm's side-by-side villagers at tiles (29,22) / (30,22) derive indices 6 and 2 - they face each other.

Heading space: retail 0 = Z−, 0x400 = X−, 0x800 = Z+, 0xC00 = X+. After spawn, walking snaps the heading and the rotate ops ramp it; the last writer in the frame draws (motion VM).

Glide speed

An NPC's pace is not the player's walk step - the walk kernel never reads the speed multiplier. Both NPC walk kernels carry the base step in the walk op's own operands, on the ladder numerator >> (2 + bits) units per vsync.

In the port

  • Faithful path. World::step_field_locomotion rings the held pad around the same eight-direction compass, hands the remapped mask to the wall-slide resolver and walks the result through the same per-axis 2-unit collision with the three-probe edge and the actor boxes. Wall-press captures rest byte-exact. Retail picks its rotation step from scene data; a free-orbiting camera has none, so the port rounds the live camera azimuth to the nearest eighth.
  • Enhancements. Precise movement (options::precise_movement, default off): continuous azimuth, true 45° diagonals at unit speed, analog stick angle. --no-edge-collision / --no-solid-npcs fall back to a centre test and walk-through NPCs.
  • Floor. World::sample_field_floor_height carries both height models.
  • Run button. The run mask defaults to the retail Cross | R1 plus Square; which key is which pad button is a host binding (legaia-engine config set --binding W=R1).

Details

Player actor fields

The player actor pointer is _DAT_8007C364. Read +0x14/16/18 as 16-bit: a 32-bit read of +0x14 folds the next word into X and looks like position drift.

+0x26heading, eight angles
+0x72speed multiplier, 0x1000 = 1.0

OffsetTypeMeaning

A placed record becomes an actor through exactly one of two sweeps. The scene-init sweep resolves the record's bind (the gate-0 trigger on its footprint anchor) and skips unbound records; the sub-area window rebuild spawns from the tile words with no bind lookup. The sets are complementary (Rim Elm: 37 bound + 9 unbound = 46), so every placed record draws. A bound record with a clip id is posed by that clip's frame 0 - the cupboard's hinge doors hang through the floor without it. Parser legaia_asset::field_objects.

Scene entry: which .MAP, which script, which encounters
  • The .MAP is the PROT slot at define − 2 from the scene's CDNAME block; its 0x12000 footprint identifies it.
  • The entry script is MAN partition-1 record 0 on channel 0xFB. Its wall paints sit behind story-flag tests: map03 paints tile (66, 102) solid behind flag 0x6C2 and clears three rects behind 0x378.
  • The same MAN carries the random-encounter table; towns have real tables too - Rim Elm declares 7 formations, gated by its region records.
  • On entry the player is seated from the destination-entry pair _DAT_80073EF4 / EF8: the door's operand on a scene change, the saved coordinates on a return from battle, a minigame or an FMV. (0xA40, 0, 0xA40) is where the ambient particle emitter spawns, not Vahn's seat.
The scripted-scene actor

Some field actors are small programs rather than NPCs - the hop-arc controller, door swings, one-shot props. FUN_801D4A60 is a 38-state jump table on the actor's +0x54 whose entry state computes +0x54 = +0x54 + 1 + (+0x50 × 10): +0x50 is a program selector, and the table is four short programs of 5, 5, 6 and 7 states entered at 1 / 11 / 21 / 31. Spawner FUN_801D5A24(program).

History: readings this page replaced

Walls were once thought to be authored entirely by scripts (a static search for CPU stores cannot see the DMA load). The +0x8000 region was read as a terrain-flag grid; it is the object map. The object table was read as NPC spawns rather than buildings. Base step 5 was read as a scripted slow-walk; it is the kingdom overworld's step. The 4C 51 operand was read as a glide speed; it is a facing nibble. See do-not-re-walk.
