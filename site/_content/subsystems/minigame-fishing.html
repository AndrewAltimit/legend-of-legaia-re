Fishing minigame

The party stands on a pond shore, casts with a power meter, coaxes a bite by reeling in a rhythm, then fights the hooked fish on a tension gauge. A landed fish pays fishing points, which the venue’s prize counter trades for items. All three hosts of the port run the whole game as one session type.

Getting there

The door is the mode-24 minigame warp: field-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 100 (sub-id 0) sets game mode 0x18 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup, overlay-load, return-to-field sequence, so the game returns to the exact field state it suspended. The mechanism is on the field / event VM page.

A disc-wide walk finds the door at exactly two sites, both signboards on the overworld. Which one the party came from picks the venue: state 1 compares the departure scene’s id against two immediates and selects Buma or Vidna, which in turn selects the prize page and the spawn page. Both pond areas are in the bundle’s one map.

The warp is the last op of the signboard’s own talk record, which the action button runs like any town conversation. Before it, the board refuses on a frozen lake (Karisto only, system flag 0x378 clear), on no fishing rod and on no lure; then it asks whether to fish, and a yes fades the screen to white and waits 48 frames before the warp. The record is laid out on the world-map internals page.

Fishing loads no music of its own; it keeps whatever the departure scene was playing. The driver has no direct caller either - it is the tick word of an actor template the init spawns.

State machine

Reel input

InputRetail meaning
Crossreel A; also casts and locks the power meter
Squarereel B; also casts and locks the power meter
R2reel-direction mirror, read alongside the reel buttons
Circle, L2the abandon edge - not a cast
Triangle, Selectopen the hub menu from the idle shore

Holding both reel buttons resolves to reel A. The cadence recogniser keeps a 16-entry ring of {button, held frames} and matches a window of that history against the rhythm templates decoded from the disc. A match selects a cast band directly; with no match the band is the current frame’s random roll.

The port’s cast input differs from retail by choice: Circle casts and locks the meter, Cross and Square only reel.

No roll and no template maps to band 4. The rarest band has its own gate, which is what the Spirit Fish write-up follows. A matched template holds its band for the next 64 frame-steps.

The fight

While hooked, a small behaviour machine (run, dart left, dart right, dive) moves the fish and modulates its pull, re-rolling from the species record’s cutoffs when each behaviour’s timer runs out.

Scoring and the prize counter

points = fish_base_value * (strength + 0x9C0) / 0x32000

The points are added to the saved counter, clamped to 999999, and a catch that beats the saved best replaces it. A per-catch latch scores a fish once.

The prize counter spends the pool on six rows per venue. A row is a 12-byte record with a limit - 1 for a one-time prize latched in a saved bitfield, 99 for a repeatable one. Row 0 is hidden until strictly affordable, so each venue’s top prize only appears once the pool exceeds its price.
