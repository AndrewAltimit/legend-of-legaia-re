Fishing minigame

The party stands on a pond shore, casts with a power meter, coaxes a bite by reeling in a rhythm, then fights the hooked fish on a tension gauge. A landed fish pays fishing points, which the venue’s prize counter trades for items. The points, the best catch, the equipped lure and rod and a lifetime cast counter live in the save block and survive between sessions. This page walks through the rules with the game’s own numbers: what makes a fish bite, which fish it is, what breaks the line and what a catch is worth.

At a glance

  1. CastStop the bouncing power meter; a longer line is the only line that can bite.
  2. CoaxReel in one of four rhythms. A matched rhythm picks the fish band and boosts the bite.
  3. StrikeEach frame with a reel button held rolls for a bite; the band and your lure pick the species.
  4. FightHold reel to pull, release to ease. Pinned at the top of the gauge, the line can snap.
  5. ScorePoints scale with the fish’s value and the fight’s strength; spend them at the counter.

Two overworld signboards: Buma (from Karisto’s map) and Vidna (from Sebucus’s map)

Three lures (Light / Normal / Heavy, items 0x9D..0x9F) and three rods (items 0xA0..0xA2)

Getting there

The door is the mode-24 minigame warp: field-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 100 (sub-id 0) sets game mode 0x18 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup, overlay-load, return-to-field sequence, so the game returns to the exact field state it suspended. There is no menu entry and no dedicated opcode; the mechanism is on the field / event VM page.

A disc-wide walk of every scene finds the door at exactly two sites, both signboards on the overworld. The warp is the last op of the signboard’s own talk record, which the action button runs like any town conversation. Before it, the board refuses on a frozen lake (Karisto’s map only, system flag 0x378 clear), on no rod and on no lure; then it asks whether to fish, and a yes fades the screen to white and waits 48 frames before the warp. The record is laid out on the world-map internals page.

The departure scene picks the venue, and the venue picks the spawn page and the prize page. Both ponds are halves of one map in the bundle.

  • Your tackle is corrected on entry. The init keeps the saved rod when the party still holds it, otherwise steps forward to the next one it owns. The lure check does the same to the saved lure, so the HUD’s lure label can change without the player touching the menu.
  • No music of its own. Fishing keeps whatever the departure scene was playing; the reel, water and cast sounds are a positional voice on top.

The hub menu

Triangle or Select at the idle shore opens a five-row picker: back to fishing, help (two pages), the rod / lure select, the prize counter, and leave. The tackle screen opens on the equipped lure; accept equips the highlighted lure or rod (rods are listed only when owned). The help pages are 14 and 15 lines of text read from the overlay.

Provenance: jump table 0x801CEBE0; picker FUN_801D0474, help FUN_801D72A0, tackle FUN_801D0F5C; abandon edge _DAT_8007B874 & 0x21 at 0x801D0318. State 0x2D (a miss / retry timer) has a case but no store on the disc ever selects it.

Reeling and the four rhythms

ButtonRetail meaning
Crossreel A; also casts and locks the power meter
Squarereel B; also casts and locks the power meter
R2reel-direction mirror, read alongside the reel buttons
Circle, L2the abandon edge - not a cast
Triangle, Selectopen the hub menu from the idle shore
D-padaim at the shore; with the lure in the water, down lifts the rod and left / right roll it

The game reduces the held pad to one reel state: Cross wins, Square alone is reel B, neither is idle. Holding both is reel A. A cadence recogniser then keeps a 16-entry ring of {button, frames held} and compares the recent history against four rhythm templates, allowing ±10 frame-steps of slack per step. A full match resets the ring and chooses the cast band: the template’s number is the band.

Provenance: decoder FUN_801D7450 (m & 0x40 ? 1 : (m >> 6) & 2), recogniser FUN_801D3DB4, ring reset FUN_801D746C, templates at DAT_801D87D4 (four 0x40-byte records).

What makes a fish bite, and which fish

While no fish is hooked and the line is out past a record of 500, the bite check runs every frame. It has three parts: the band (a spawn-table column), the strike roll (does anything bite this frame), and the species lookup.

species = spawn_table[venue][lure * 8 + band]     lure 0..2, band 0..4

The band

A matched rhythm stores its band and holds it for the next 64 frame-steps, and also fires the strike splash. With no match, the band is re-rolled from a 12-bit random number every frame, so an unmatched band is only ever the current frame’s roll.

Roll rBandRolls of 4096Share
r <= 0xC0033073~75.0%
0xC00 < r <= 0xE702624~15.2%
0xE70 < r <= 0xF381200~4.9%
0xF38 < r0199~4.9%

The strike roll

strike  <=>  a reel button is held  and  rand % denom < credit

credit = (band countdown + 2)          0x40 on the frame a rhythm matches
       + water bonus                   0x1E / 0x14 / 0x14 by water class
       + 1 per pad edge this frame     D-pad left, D-pad right, a reel button

The line-length readout sets the denominator, and a short line kills the roll outright:

Worked from those numbers at a readout of 201 or more: with no rhythm running the credit is about 2, a one-in-500 chance per held frame before water and pad bonuses. On the frame a rhythm matches the credit jumps to 0x42 (66 in 1000) and then decays with the countdown, so a strike is roughly twenty times more likely while the matched band is held.

The water class is read from the pond map under the lure. Position and bait-twitching change how often a strike is rolled, never which species results; the class’s second half is the size of the fish (below).

Band 4: the rare-fish gate

Band 4 is only reachable through a gate inside the hook-success branch, checked just before the species lookup. All conditions are required:

The cast counter goes up once per landed cast. Each venue’s gate hardwires its lure row, so the band-4 cells on the other rows are dead data. A debug shortcut in the same branch (the debug print flag set, R1 held at the strike) stores band 4 unconditionally; it is not reachable in a retail session. The hunt for species 9 is followed end to end in the Spirit Fish write-up.

Fish weight

At the hook the game sums a fight strength from four terms, which the score and the rank plate read later:

strength = rand1 % water_modulus              10 / 100 / 300 / 500
         + (line_record >> 5) + 10
         + rand2 % 600
         + 100 * (rand3 % (rod + 1)) + 50 * (rod + 1)   rod = 0 / 1 / 2

The last term is the rod you fish with (index 0..2, read from the save block), so a better rod raises both the floor and the spread of every catch. strength + 0x400 is also the hooked fish’s render scale, so a better water cell or rod can produce a visibly larger fish. The weight touches neither the species roll nor the strike credit.

The sum is stored once and the fight never changes it. Besides the score and the rank plate, the landed fish’s lift reads it (its sparkle bursts climb the same thresholds as the plate), and its +0x400 copy is the bias of the fish’s per-frame pull. The port sums it at the strike the same way, off three draws of the same random stream in the same order; the pull bias is still the engine’s own, because retail scales the pull per behaviour and by the line depth before the tension gauge sees it, and that step is not ported yet.

Venue spawn tables

Each venue has an 8 × 8 table of species ids: the row is the equipped lure (rows 3 to 7 are zero padding), the column is the band (column 4 live only on the gate’s row - Normal at Buma, Heavy at Vidna). The ids themselves decode from the player’s disc and are not reproduced here.

Provenance: band and strike in FUN_801D26CC; readout ladder at 0x801D3284 (six slti / bne pairs, four arms unreachable); water class through func_0x800180EC at 0x801D3384; gate on _DAT_80084460 (cast counter, written at 0x801D2954..0x801D296C); weight stored to DAT_801D91B8 at 0x801D3814; spawn tables 0x801D8334 (Buma) / 0x801D8434 (Vidna).

Details: the lure drifts off walls, alternating by cast

The bite tick probes the walk grid's high nibble under the lure each frame (FUN_801D7030). On a hit it nudges the lure's x by frame_delta << 11 in 24.8 fixed point, adding on an odd cast counter and subtracting on an even one, so the drift direction alternates between casts. A capture across two full casts found the probe returning zero on every call: the pond never triggers the drift on its own.

The fight and the tension gauge

A hooked fish is a tug-of-war on one gauge from 0 to 0x1000. Holding a reel button raises it by the fish’s pull divided by a rod-dependent divisor; letting go lowers it at a rod-dependent rate. The gauge is clamped at both ends.

RodReel A divisorReel B divisorRelease per frame-step
first (index 0)352574
second (index 1)4431138
third (index 2)5337202

The three rows are the gauge formulas above evaluated at rod index 0, 1 and 2.

While hooked, a small behaviour machine (run, dart left, dart right, dive) moves the fish and modulates its pull, re-rolling the next behaviour from the species record’s cutoffs when each one’s timer runs out. Holding a reel button also lowers the line depth a little each frame.

Reel A is the safe button

Measured over the port’s whole parameter space (both venues, three lures, three rods, reel held throughout), reel A (Cross) never reaches the ceiling - its peak is 2017 of 0x1000 - while reel B (Square) does, on the hardest-pulling common fish. Reel A also recovers line faster.

The port’s fight model

The port carries the gauge divisors, the release rate, the clamp and the species table’s pull, dart and sink formulas. The line break is retail’s: a hooked frame at the ceiling draws one rand() % 10, and a zero breaks the line and spends one equipped lure from the bag. The glue between them (reel-down rates, the AI’s branch order and re-roll interval) is an engine-side reading, listed under Internals.

Provenance: gauge DAT_801D9168 updated at the tail of FUN_801D4004; exits in FUN_801D26CC at 0x801D3AFC..0x801D3CD4.

What each species record controls

What a catch is worth

points = species_value * (strength + 0x9C0) / 0x32000

The points go into the saved pool, clamped to 999,999. A per-catch latch scores a fish once. A catch that beats the saved best replaces the best value and its fish id. The result plate fades up as the fish is lifted, with the species name rising into view, and its rank badge is chosen by the fight strength:

Fight strengthRank plate
below 0xC9 (201)plate 0x15
from 0xC9 (201)plate 0x13
from 0x259 (601)plate 0x12
from 0x321 (801)plate 0x11
from 0x4B1 (1201)plate 0x14

The payout is linear in strength + 2496: a strength of 0 still pays value * 2496 / 204800, and a strength of 2496 doubles it. Better water raises the strength term through its modulus, and a longer line record adds record >> 5.

Provenance: FUN_801D5298 (the result actor’s tick); rank ladder at 0x801D5448..0x801D54B4; best catch _DAT_80084458 / _DAT_8008445C.

The prize counter

The hub menu’s prize row spends the point pool. Each venue has six rows; each row is a 12-byte record of limit, price and item. A limit of 1 is a one-time prize, latched in a saved bitfield (Buma bits 0 to 5, Vidna bits 8 to 13); 99 is repeatable. Both venues spend from and latch into the same globals.

  • Row 0 is hidden until strictly affordable. Each venue’s top prize appears only once the pool exceeds its price; exactly equal is not enough.
  • A row shows white (available) when affordable, the item count is not 99, and a one-time row is not latched.
  • “Trade how many?” caps the quantity at min(points / price, limit - owned).

Tips that follow from the rules

  • Cast long. A line readout under 200 cannot bite at all.
  • Reel in a rhythm, and keep holding. A matched rhythm makes a strike roughly twenty times likelier for 64 frame-steps, but a strike only lands on a frame with a reel button held. Completing the band-0 rhythm (idle, Cross, idle, Square) and holding the final Square is the best sequence the game offers: band 0 pinned, bite boosted, reel held by the same press.
  • Twitch the bait. Each D-pad left / right or reel press on a frame adds one to that frame’s strike credit.
  • Fight on Cross. Reel A divides the pull harder; Square is the button that can pin the gauge and snap the line.
  • Use the best rod you own. It softens tension both ways, and the third rod is required for both rare-fish gates.
  • Rare fish. At Buma: over 50 lifetime casts, an even count, the Normal Lure, the third rod, band 0, then 1 in 16. At Vidna: the Heavy Lure instead and no cast threshold, then 1 in 4. Every landed cast flips the parity, so only every other cast is eligible.
  • Water matters for size, not species. A better water cell raises both the bite chance and the fight strength, which feeds the points and the rank plate.
  • Save for the hidden rows. Points are one pool across both ponds; the top prize at each counter appears only once you have strictly more than its price.

Where this is in the port

The port runs the whole game as one session type, PondSession (crate engine-fishing), on all three hosts: the native play-window, the browser play page, and the site’s minigames page, where you can fish in the browser against your own disc. No game data is baked in: the species, spawn, rhythm, sprite, prize and caption tables are all read from the disc you supply.

  • Controls differ by choice. In the port Circle casts and locks the meter, Cross reels (reel A) and Square reels harder (reel B).
  • The door warp and both play hosts’ launchers (L in play-window, the Fish button on the play page) enter the same session; leaving banks points, best catch, tackle, cast count and prize bits back into the world’s saved words.
  • The prize counter opens from the hub’s prize row (or P in the native window): Up / Down move, Cross or L1 trades one, Circle or L2 closes, and Left / Right switch the venue page (a port affordance).
  • The shore party, the venue camera, the scrolling sky strip, the rod model and the fishing line are drawn from the retail rules on every host; the details are under Internals.

Kernels: CastPower, TensionGauge, FishAi, ReelCadence, BandCheck, band4_gate, FishingRecord, PrizeExchange; draws in engine-ui::fishing.

Internals

For modders and anyone reading the dumps: the actors, the HUD, the geometry helpers, the save-block words, and what the port still reconstructs.

Actors and the scene: shore party, camera, sky, rod and line
  • The shore party. Setup spawns three actors on the venue’s anchor tile: Vahn in the middle, the second member 0x60 to his right, the third 0x60 to his left and a step nearer the camera. The lead’s tick rolls a point out in the water every so often and spawns a ripple there; nothing moves him.
  • The camera is a level view behind the party with H = 0x140 in place of the field’s 0x200; its yaw and focus follow the lead as the D-pad aims him.
  • The sky is not geometry. It is a screen-space strip of six 128 × 256 textured quads drawn behind everything, positioned on the projected horizon and scrolled by the lead’s aim, over a blue clear colour (0x17, 0x50, 0xA0).
  • The rod is one of three untextured 42-vertex models (scene models 0x19..0x1B, one per rod), posed in view space and bent by a morph. D-pad down lifts it, the sides roll it, a held reel or a fish on bends it; a hooked rod bent past its cap creaks.
  • The line is one Gouraud line packet a frame from the lure (dark end) to the rod model’s vertex 37 (light end), clipped to the 320 × 224 drawing area.
  • The floor is the shared minigame ground solver: a 128-unit cell grid with a 16-step height ramp, bilinear blending and step-layer patches, also used by the slot machine and the dance floor.

Rod actor FUN_801D1C5C, lead FUN_801D2050 / FUN_801D2278, flanking members FUN_801D70EC, line / landed-fish actor FUN_801D4948, sky FUN_801D24EC, floor pass FUN_801D6BBC, ground solver FUN_801D6028.

HUD: gauges, readouts and banners

The persistent HUD draws the best catch, the point total (capped at 999,999), the equipped lure’s label and the number of that lure left. While a cast is out, the catch HUD adds the line length, the cast-power bar, the depth gauge and, once hooked, the tension gauge.

length  = max(record - 300, 0) * 100 >> 9  +  max(lure_height >> 9, 0)
          drawn as  length / 10 . length % 10
power % = power * 100 >> 12

The tension gauge fills along a ramp against its complement; the depth gauge is constant red against the ramp. Five one-shot banners share one motion: slide in at 8 px a frame, hold at the centre from frame 0x14, slide off from frame 0x8C, gone at frame 0xC8.

caughtfrom the rightreel-in complete with a fish on

strike splashrises one pixel every 32 frames, fades in and outa rhythm match before the hook

Every HUD record is a semi-transparent quad whose palette carries no STP bit, so on the GPU every texel draws opaque. The catch HUD relies on it: a stray m glyph drawn over the line readout’s units digit is hidden under the plate’s opaque band. A renderer that blends every texel of a semi-transparent quad shows 22m4 m for 22.4 m, which is why the port’s screen-primitive passes split them per texel.

Persistent HUD FUN_801D13F0, catch HUD FUN_801D1580, bars FUN_801D1870 / FUN_801D1A90, digit field FUN_801D76E0, sprite emitter FUN_801D63B0 over the 29-record table at 0x801D8590.

Geometry helpers and VA aliasing

The overlay carries its own copies of four small geometry routines, because the field overlay is not resident while it runs: a walk-grid wall probe on the terrain bytes’ high nibble (the lure drift), a sub-cell separation of two globals, a 2-D segment clipper (the line) and a 3-D segment transform with depth clip (no caller). All four are confirmed at their printed addresses by disassembling PROT 0972 at its base.

The dumps covering 0x801D1xxx and 0x801D6F00..0x801D78FF are runtime captures whose overlay labels are unreliable: a save-state slice can keep bytes from a previously resident overlay. Functions here are attributed by content - the lure item ids, the DAT_801D9xxx globals - not by filename.

Open: what the port still reconstructs
  • Line break. The ceiling roll and the spent lure are ported; the off-screen latch, the far-record strain and the no-lure screen after the last lure are not.
  • Cast camera. Retail cuts from behind the party to a low shot over the water; those camera writes are not decoded, so the port keeps the shore view throughout.
  • Fight pacing. The reel-down rates, the AI’s branch order and re-roll interval, and the pull bias are engine-side readings.
  • Cast. The meter’s per-frame step (hosts use 0x80), the flight timing and the power-to-line mapping are approximations.
  • Result plate. The fish’s lift before the plate and the recast gate on the plate’s accept word are not modelled.
  • State 0x2D has no writer anywhere on the disc; every store to the state word resolves to another value.
