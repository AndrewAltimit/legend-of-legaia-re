Per-actor motion VMs

The interpreters that walk NPCs around. When a villager wanders inside an invisible box, an actor turns toward a target, a cutscene marches someone across the screen, or a scene flips a story flag as its choreography plays, one of two small bytecode VMs in the main executable is stepping that actor. This page covers which VM does what, how an actor's facing changes, and where the bytecode lives on the disc.

At a glance

An actor is the engine's record for one on-screen entity (the player, an NPC, a prop, a camera target). Every frame the field actor tick visits every actor and, depending on flag bits in its word at +0x10, hands it to one of two interpreters.

Two traps

The NPC turning to look at you when you press the action button is neither VM - it is a single store in the dialog state machine (talk-time facing). And the ambient VM's bytecode arrives as disc data, so it has a static decoder beside its interpreter: engine-vm::ambient_motion runs it, engine-core::man_field_scripts::npc_motion answers questions about a stream without running it, and engine-vm::motion_vm is the other VM entirely.

Both are distinct from the window-widget VM and the move-table VM; the whole family is on the VM family table.

The driver: one tick, two arms

The height arm runs first for any live actor inside the camera's visible window. It writes the actor's Y position, not its heading: a stored override, a hold, a per-frame clamped step toward the sampled floor, or a snap onto it. Bit 1 of the flag word is a visibility cull rewritten every frame by the pre-update, so an actor outside the window holds its Y. The dispatch arm then fires the routines in the figure, unless the global suppress bit (_DAT_1F800394 & 0x400) skips it wholesale.

The pursue VM

This is the walk kernel the field VM hands its motion ops to. When a script runs 0x37, 0x41 or 0x47, it parks the op's address on the actor and sets the halt bit; each frame this VM interprets those same bytes in place until the leg finishes, then clears the halt bit so the script can resume. There is no separately synthesised motion bytecode.

With the high bit set, the target resolves first: 0xF8 means the player, 0xFB the system actor, and any other id matches the actor's placement bind index at +0x50. Each tick spends the frame scalar's worth of progress; the leg returns "still running" or "done".

The opcode numbers are the field VM's own: the jump table is indexed (op & 0x7F) − 0x37 over 0x37..0x4C, and every slot not listed above ends the leg.

The 0x47 approach mode. While both axes are still open, the high nibble of the mode byte decides which ones a step moves. Mode 0 closes the dominant axis alone, clamped to the difference, until the two deltas are equal, then cuts the diagonal. Any other mode masks the open axes at the full step: 3 walks the diagonal from the first frame and finishes on the longer axis, 1 closes X first, 2 Z first. The two laws end an uneven leg facing different ways - mode 3 along the longer axis, mode 0 on the diagonal - and cutscene walks carry 3.

How an actor's facing changes

Watch a villager walk and they only ever face one of eight directions, yet a scripted turn in a cutscene glides smoothly. Those are two different laws, and which one an actor gets is a property of the bytecode, not the situation.

  • Snap (walking). A 0x47 leg reduces its step to axis signs, maps them through the eight-entry compass LUT at 0x80073F04 and writes the heading outright. Entry i is i × 0x200, so a walking actor's facing is always a compass point, one value per leg - there is no walk-turn interpolation anywhere in retail. A cold-boot town sample confirms it: every on-field heading is a multiple of 0x200.
  • Ramp (the rotate ops). 0x38 and 0x4C interpolate over a frame budget: each tick, heading += arc × speed / remaining measured from the live heading, and the terminal frame snaps exactly onto the target so rounding never leaves the actor short.

Talk-time facing is not this VM

The NPC turning to face you on the action button is easy to mis-attribute to Face target, and it is not that op. It is a save / snap / restore triple in the per-actor dialog state machine: the heading is saved aside, one store snaps it toward the player in a single frame (no ramp), and the exit paths write the saved heading back. It runs only for moving-class actors - static props never turn.

Port trap

The dialog snap calls the bearing helper with the endpoints swapped relative to Face target, and without Face target's +0x800 half-turn - the swap is the half-turn, so the two agree. Copying the Face target convention here and keeping the +0x800 faces the NPC exactly backwards.

The ambient motion VM: town life and story flags

This is the interpreter behind a town's ambient life - villagers wandering inside an invisible box, idle turns, waits, tile teleports - and, less visibly, the story flags a scene flips as its choreography plays: op 0x07 sets a story flag and op 0x08 clears one, in the same bank the field VM uses.

Most ops do not end the frame. The interpreter is a loop that runs until an op says it did work, so a stream can raise a story flag, swap a model and start three tweens between two frames. Only the walks, the waits, the facing ramps and the bit-wait stop it.

Dispatch is a 32-entry jump table at 0x80010FE8 indexed op − 1; slots 0x1A..0x1F point back at the loop test and re-dispatch forever, as a rejected byte does.

  • The disc carrier is MAN tail-section 1 - a record chain in the scene's script-and-data bundle that binds each stream to a placed actor at scene entry. It is the only disc source of this bytecode.
  • Walk ops collide with the player only - not the walkability grid, not other NPCs. A wanderer can stroll through a fence; it cannot stroll through Vahn.
  • The binding byte is a suppression mask, not an enable. With bit 0 set the stream defers while the player is engaged (talk, menu, beat), while the actor is busy, or while it is parked off-map; a zero byte keeps choreographing through a cutscene.
  • The ambient facing ops have no shortest-arc override, so an authored idle turn can deliberately take the long way round.
Stream binding, speed encoding, op 0x17 and the touch post

Binding. FUN_8003AEB0 walks the MAN's tail-section chain and FUN_8003A9D4 walks section 1 as [u8 count][s16 next_delta][count × (u8 actor_id, u8 mask)][motion stream]. 0xF8 binds to the player, 0xFB to the world-map entity node, anything else matches actor +0x50 - which the spawner writes as N0 + placement_index (N0 = the MAN's partition-0 record count), so binding 0x30 in town01 (N0 = 36) is placement 12. Streams open with a flag-selected variant header re-evaluated every tick. The stream is read at *(u32*)(actor+0x80) + *(u16*)(actor+0x84).

Speed encoding. All walk ops step on the 0x80 >> (2 + bits) per-frame ladder; directional steps carry bits in operand byte 1's low nibble, while 0x06 and 0x18 scatter a 4-bit selector over their operand bytes' high bits. The axis-bitmask table sixteen bytes past the compass LUT (1 = +Z, 2 = −Z, 4 = +X, 8 = −X) is why diagonal steps move both axes equally.

Op 0x17 and the touch post. [17, move_id, anim_id] writes the per-actor default-move table 0x801C6470[bind × 4], guarded < 0x8C. When two actors overlap, the collision probe posts the touched actor's bind index into a one-slot mailbox the VM's wait arm consumes; the post is dropped when the record's class byte is 0x8C, so a placement with no move assigned can be walked into without waking its script.

Flag census. legaia-engine man-scripts --motion-flag-census sweeps every scene MAN for op-7 / op-8 sites. Disc-wide the surface is overworld walking-band choreography plus one town clear; the chapter-spine gate flags appear in no motion stream - their writers are field-VM script bytes.

In the port

  • engine-vm::motion_vm is the pursue VM, with one LUT module for the facing law so spawn-time and runtime facings cannot drift apart. Two deliberate departures: LUT indices 8..=15 are no-ops rather than retail's over-read into adjacent executable data, and an unrecognised Face-target sub-mode ends the leg instead of yielding forever.
  • engine-vm::ambient_motion runs the whole ambient op table.
  • NPC pacing is decoded, not guessed: each placement's glide speed comes from its real walk operands (field locomotion).
