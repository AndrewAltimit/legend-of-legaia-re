Baka Fighter minigame

The Sol casino’s one-on-one fighting cabinet. You pick Vahn, Noa or Gala in their battle forms and climb a ladder of CPU opponents in best-of-three matches. Every exchange is rock-paper-scissors between three attacks, damage scales with each fighter’s stats, HP and combo, and a downed opponent is finished by an automatic special. Won rungs build a coin pot you can bank or risk on the next rung. This page is how the cabinet works, with the numbers it uses.

At a glance

  • Buttons: Square, Circle and Cross are the three attacks. Circle beats Square, Cross beats Circle, Square beats Cross; the same button on both sides is a draw.
  • A match is best of three rounds. Both fighters start every round on 0xC80 (3200) HP.
  • Damage grows with the winner’s attack, the loser’s defence, both picked by HP tier, plus 0x40 per extra hit in a streak. Below 0x280 HP a fighter can roll a critical.
  • The special is never a button. Whoever leaves the foe at 0 HP throws it automatically, and its last strike wins the round.
  • The ladder serves twelve opponents with rising prizes. Two secret opponents appear only when your running score clears 250,000 or 700,000 at the right rung.
  • The pot pays into the casino coin bank when you choose PAY OUT. Losing a match forfeits all of it.

Getting there

The three Baka Fighter cabinets in the Sol casino (koin1) are interchangeable doors. Each sits behind an entry gate: an inventory compare and a one-coin fee before the fade and the warp. Failing the gate skips the warp. The door is the mode-24 minigame warp, field-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 104 (sub-id 4), which sets game mode 0x18 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup, overlay-load, return-to-field sequence, so the game comes back to the exact field state it left (mechanism on the field / event VM page).

A disc-wide walk of every scene finds a fourth site, on an overworld map. It is dev residue: a bare record with nothing but the warp, in a slot no reachable script spawns.

The seat

All six casino cabinet records (three Baka Fighter, three slot machine) share one choreography, written into the cabinet’s own script:

  1. Seat flagone per cabinet: 0x433..0x435 for Baka Fighter
  2. Walkthe player walks onto the stool tile and the record waits until they land
  3. Turnthe player turns to the machine over 23 frames
  4. Sit + feethe sit clip, then the coin gate and the fee
  5. Washa subtractive white held at full: the screen goes black
  6. Warpthe door warp into mode 24

A second pass after the warp walks the wash out, clears it, stands the player up and glides them off the stool. Nothing in the record reaches it on its own: the scene’s system script loops on the six seat flags, clears the one it finds and re-engages that cabinet once the field is back.

The port runs both passes on both play hosts through the field-VM talk runner, and emits no field colour push in a minigame mode, so the wash never draws over the duel.

A visit, start to finish

Above the fight sits the cabinet: one state word with 37 branch targets covering the whole run - attract, player select, per-rung setup, the round bracket, the duel, the win / lose / all-clear sequences, the NEXT GAME / PAY OUT menu, the pause menu and an unreachable developer menu. Only state 0x64 is “a round is live”.

Front end

  • Attract. The title card animates on a clock of its own while PRESS START blinks. Start, Cross or L1 leaves it.
  • Player select. Vahn, Noa and Gala stand in the walled arena; left / right moves the cursor (wrapping both ways) and the two fighters not under it dim halfway toward black. Confirm is Cross or L1.
  • The pick is a roster record, not a skin. The chosen party member brings their own stats, action table, strike clips and special camera into the duel.

NEXT GAME / PAY OUT

After a won match and its tally, a two-way menu offers NEXT GAME (fight the next rung, keeping the pot on the line) or PAY OUT (bank the pot and leave). The pot shows under it as a GET COIN readout. The menu’s cursor wraps both ways, and the sheet vanishes the frame NEXT GAME is taken.

The cabinet reads the packed Legaia pad word, not the raw PSX layout:

The player-select and NEXT GAME / PAY OUT cursors wrap both ways. The three-row pause menu and the developer menu fold with an unsigned modulo, so they wrap at the bottom and stick at the top: -1 as a u32 is divisible by both 3 and 5.

The epilogue carries two draw flags: the arena pass is off on the attract, the tally, the last all-clear beats and the exit; the duel HUD draws only in 0x36..0x38, 0x64, 0x65, 0x6E, 0x96 and 0x97. So the tally and the NEXT GAME / PAY OUT menu show no duel HUD.

The round and the exchange

A match is first to two round wins. Inside a round, each fighter commits at most one attack per exchange; the exchange stays undecided until both sides have committed, and is booked on the frame the winner’s strike lands.

Who wins an exchange

P1 \ P20 idle1 Square2 Circle3 Cross4 special
0 idleundecidedundecidedundecidedundecidedP2
1 SquareundecideddrawP2P1P2
2 CircleundecidedP1drawP2P2
3 CrossundecidedP2P1drawP2
4 specialP1P1P1P1P1

The resolver tests in a fixed order: a special on either side wins outright (P1 checked first); both idle, or either side still mid-commit, is undecided; equal types draw; otherwise the beats relation. A draw books damage to both fighters. An attack never lands on a fighter who has not thrown. The resolver also has a settle timer, but it is vestigial: nothing in the overlay ever stores a positive value to it, so exchange pacing comes from the two per-fighter cooldowns.

Damage

hit = power + power * ATK / 100
dmg = (hit * (200 - (mod + mod * DEF / 100)) * 0x20) / 100  +  (combo - 1) * 0x40

critical pending on the winner:  dmg = power << 7
InputWhere it comes from
powerthe winning attack’s action record +0x18
ATKthe winner’s roster record, one of three values picked by the winner’s HP tier
DEFthe loser’s roster record, one of three values picked by the loser’s HP tier
modthe loser’s roster damage modifier +0x24
combothe loser’s count of consecutive hits taken; it rises after every hit and clears when that fighter wins an exchange
  • The comeback critical. For the rolled fighter: only while its own HP is above 0 and below 0x280, rand() % 100 below its roster record’s +0x34 chance sets a critical-pending flag. The next exchange that fighter wins does power << 7 instead of the formula.
  • The streak term. Each extra consecutive hit on the same loser adds 0x40 (64) on top: the second hit of a streak gets +0x40, the third +0x80.
  • Hit reactions. One application decrements HP (floored at 0, and only while HP is above 0), pushes the loser back 0x20 world units and plays their hit clip, or the knockdown when the landed keyframe is the special’s last.
  • The special’s HP delta is zero by construction. Its power is 0 for all seventeen fighters, and it only ever runs against a foe already at 0 HP, so the formula’s bare combo term never lands. Its whole payoff is the round win, credited on its final keyframe. The port applies exactly 0 damage for a special-won exchange.

Damage FUN_801D3B18, critical FUN_801D6660, resolver FUN_801D3A14. The constants (0x20, 0x40, << 7, the HP thresholds) are immediates in the dump; a debug printf labels the +0x38 family atk, which pins ATK against DEF.

The player’s throw comes from the edge-triggered pad word, so a throw is a press, not a hold. The special is not a button: it fires automatically for a fighter whose own HP is not zero, whose foe’s HP is zero, in a round not already decided. It plays its clip, spawns the afterimage and drops the duel into slow motion.

The strike clock

An exchange is booked on the frame the winner’s strike keyframe is crossed, not when both fighters have chosen. Every attack and special in the action tables carries at least one strike frame, each in 0..=64 clip frames, in order. Each tick the clip cursor advances by the action’s speed; the range it covered is checked against the strike frames, and a hit arms the booking. So a slower attack lands later even when it wins.

  • Slow motion. A special commit lowers the rate divisor from 8 to 4, which halves both fighters’ steps for the rest of the round. The impact effects run on the same divisor and slow with them.
  • What a fighter shows is a separate display clip. An attack plays out to its last frame even after its exchange is booked, then drops to the idle; a hit reaction plays out, holds one tick and idles; a knockdown holds until the next round setup.

How the CPU picks

Each opponent mixes random throws with a canned pattern: a NUL-terminated byte list in its roster record at +0x4C. Every pick:

  1. Rollroll = rand() % 6
  2. Pattern running?if the pattern cursor is not zero, step it back one and throw (pattern[cursor] - 1) % 3 + 1, whatever the roll
  3. Randomotherwise, a roll below 3 throws roll % 3 + 1: one of the three attacks, uniformly
  4. Start the patternotherwise, if the pattern is not empty, set the cursor to its length and throw from its last byte; an empty pattern falls back to random

So an idle cursor gives a three-in-six chance of a random attack and a three-in-six chance of starting the pattern, which then plays back to front to exhaustion regardless of later rolls. The CPU never opens with a special; its specials are the same automatic finisher as yours. A developer branch, unreachable on retail, can instead remap the opponent to counter the player’s committed input.

Combat tick FUN_801D3F44 (slot-0 branch for the buttons), keyframe lookup FUN_801D6E5C, CPU pick FUN_801D487C; port BakaFight::ai_pick on a BIOS-rand stream.

The ladder, the secret rungs and the pot

  • Every visit starts at roster 5. The stage counter is seeded to 2, steps once per cleared stage, and on reaching 0xE raises the all-clear flag and wraps to 0.
  • The secret opponents are score-gated, not lap-gated. After a won match the cabinet tests the stage just cleared against the running high score: stage 5 above 250,000 arms roster 3, stage 13 above 700,000 arms roster 4. Both comparisons are strict, so a score exactly on the threshold does not qualify. An armed override suppresses the stage advance and inserts the secret rung instead of replacing a rung; it is consumed when the rung installs.
  • Only the tally adds to the pot. A won match hands the rung’s coin prize (roster record +0x20) to the tally, which drains it into the pot. The match-won arm itself never touches the pot.
  • A mid-run loss forfeits the pot. GAME OVER zeroes the accumulator on its first frame, before its banner spawns.
  • The pot is coins, not gold. The exit pays the accumulator into the casino coin bank. There is no save point inside the minigame, so the coin bank is the only result that outlives a visit.

The fighters’ names, prizes, stats and patterns decode from the disc (roster parser legaia_minigame_formats::baka_opponents) and are not reproduced here; the minigames page reads them off your disc.

Score and the tally

A won match ends on a tally of four counters. Three feed the running high score (which is what the secret-rung gate reads); the fourth is the coin prize, which feeds the pot.

RowWhat it addsDrains into
Match bonus30,000 when the opponent won no round (a shutout), else 20,000high score
Combo bonusper resolved hit, a table cell keyed by the longest streak you have landed, clamped at 0x13 (20 rows)high score
HP clear bonusper resolved hit, 50,000 at full HP 0xC80, else a table cell keyed by hp / 0x140 (11 rows)high score
Coin prizethe rung’s prize, roster record +0x20the pot

The rows drain strictly in sequence, each starting once the rows before it are empty, with a tick blip every step. A row moves a fifth of what is left per frame while more than 5 remain, half while 3 to 5 remain, and exactly one below that. Pressing any face button snaps the tally to its end: the fast-forward latch short-circuits the step to the whole remainder.

The combo row reads the running maximum of the opponent’s hits-taken counter, which the HUD latches once a frame - so the score’s combo term tracks the longest streak you land.

Tally FUN_801D239C, drain step FUN_801D6710, per-exchange accumulator FUN_801D2A28, combo / HP tables DAT_801D70C4 / DAT_801D711C (overlay rodata, no terminators). The same drain step is linked into the Muscle Dome hub.

Playing it well

Each of these follows from a rule above.

  • Learn the counters. Circle answers Square, Cross answers Circle, Square answers Cross. A draw hurts both of you.
  • Read the pattern. Half the time an idle CPU starts its pattern, and once started it plays the whole list back to front whatever it rolls. A repeated sequence from an opponent is its pattern; counter it in order until it runs out.
  • Keep a streak going. Every consecutive hit on the same fighter adds another 0x40, and the longest streak you land also feeds the combo score row. Losing a single exchange ends it: the counter belongs to the fighter taking the hits and clears when that fighter wins one.
  • Low HP is dangerous both ways. Below 0x280 HP a fighter rolls for a critical at its own roster chance - including the opponent. Finishing a foe who has dropped into that band removes the comeback threat.
  • Don’t drop a round. A match where the opponent won no round tallies 30,000 instead of 20,000, and a round in which you take no damage raises PERFECT!!.
  • Bank before you gamble the run. NEXT GAME keeps the whole pot at stake; a single lost match zeroes it. PAY OUT is the only way coins reach the bank.
  • Chasing the secret opponents means banking score early: the running high score must be above 250,000 when you clear stage 5 (the fourth opponent) and above 700,000 when you clear stage 13.
  • Speed: hold any face button through the tally to skip its drain.
  • A curiosity: hold Triangle while a round sets up and a ring girl walks on, turns to the camera, poses with a wink and walks off.

The arena in 3D

Everything the duel draws in 3D is placed by immediates in the overlay. The fighters are full battle-form meshes: the party’s are the same high-detail TMDs the turn-based battle uses, and each ladder opponent ships its own mesh, 256x256 atlas and 8-clip animation bank.

  • The camera. Every round opens on an oblique shot that swings a full turn while backing off, then settles side-on. Nothing moves it during the round except a special commit, which glides it to that fighter’s special framing - a per-party-member table for the player, one fixed record for any opponent.
  • The player is on the left of the screen. The settled camera rotation is the identity and the projection is unmirrored; the player stands at X = -(stand_off + 200), the opponent at +(stand_off + 200), where stand_off is the roster record’s +0x44 (0 for the first three ladder rungs, up to 140).
  • The room. One wall model is drawn four times as a closed room; a wall draws only when it lies past depth 0x2710, which drops the one between the camera and the fighters. The floor is the battle ground-grid emitter relocated into this overlay.

In the port, one 3D surface (engine-minigame-scenes::baka_duel_scene) draws the duel on all three hosts: fighters, ghosts, walls, floor, impact parts and the round-start cameo, plus the cabinet’s front-end lineup. The actor drop shadow is the one piece of the arena it does not draw.

Effects

The impact pair

When an exchange books, two effects spawn at the winner’s strike offset - the fist, on the frame the strike lands: an additive flip-book flash (eight cells along one row, seven along the next) and a stage-pack mesh that fades in and out, yawed to mirror the flash across the arena. A draw spawns both fighters’ pairs.

The finishing special’s afterimage

The special draws two ghosts of its thrower, three and six frames behind the body. They are translucent: the colour word’s mode byte raises the semi-transparency bit with additive blending, so each ghost is the fighter’s texture at half, then a quarter, of its weight added over the frame. The far ghost is the fainter one - a glow that thins away from the body, not a dark copy.

  • The ghosts outlive the round. The afterimage is its own pool actor, run every frame whatever the round is doing. The special’s last strike decides the round while the ghosts are still mid-clip, and they keep playing through the round-over hold until each runs past the end of the clip and drops out, the nearer one first; the next frame retires the actor. The impact parts run out the same way.
  • The port once stepped them only while the round was open, so the deciding special’s ghosts froze in place for the whole hold. They now run out as retail’s do and draw additive on every host: the native window blends the untextured prims through the same colour-mesh blend word it uses for the other minigame surfaces, and the minigames page’s fallback builds its ghosts from the same colour word.

The round-start cameo

Held Triangle at a round setup spawns the stage pack’s fourth model, a ring girl: she walks in side-on, turns to the camera, strikes a pose, turns back and walks off. At the pose a VRAM cell blit swaps her eye sprite from open to closed and leaves it swapped - a wink. A capture with and without Triangle held confirms she spawns only in the held run.

Impact pair FUN_801D4DF8, afterimage FUN_801D49E8, cameo FUN_801D6310 + blit FUN_801D65F8; ports in baka_fighter_chrome and baka_impact_fx.

The duel HUD and the banners

One renderer draws the duel HUD every frame. Its own pieces sort one layer under the round banners, so a ROUND or FIGHT! card always draws over it.

  • VITAL bars. One pixel per 32 HP, the player’s filling leftward from the centre, the opponent’s rightward, inside a three-cell frame (116 pixels round the 100-pixel bar). The bars are textured: each samples a tiny grey patch of a system texture page, so the red comes out at 160 for a vertex red of 188, as a retail capture measures.
  • Round-win pips, filled one per round won.
  • Combo counters are crossed: each side shows the other fighter’s hits-taken count with “HIT!”, which is the streak that side is landing.
  • Attack-icon columns. The row of the attack a fighter just chose brightens, dims for three frames, then brightens again, off a timer the combat tick runs from the commit - a flash on every fresh throw.
  • Top strip: STAGE and its number, the blinking PRESS SELECT TO MENU prompt, and the running high score.

Where this is in the port

The port is from-scratch Rust: the rules crates carry no world, scene or renderer, and the same cabinet, chrome and 3D duel run on all three hosts. Play it on the minigames page with your own disc.

The play hosts run the real ladder inside one visit: NEXT GAME seats the roster record the rung fold names, the tally drains into the same winnings accumulator retail keeps, and the exit’s return warp pays it into the coin bank. Leaving the duel before the tally finishes banks the remainder.

Known differences from retail

  • Exchange recovery is immediate, where retail sequences it through the per-action keyframes; cooldowns pace re-entry.
  • The result banners last for the round-over hold, not retail’s spawn template (no library capture holds a result screen).
  • The play hosts draw the tally’s digit strips as font glyphs, and the VITAL bars untextured with the system texel’s grey folded into their colour.
  • The minigames page draws the duel HUD page-side rather than through the shared builder.
  • The drop shadow is not drawn; the developer menu, keyframe editor and slot helpers are not ported.

Developer screens (unreachable on retail)

Retail carries a developer menu (0xC8), an in-game action-table keyframe editor (0x190 / 0x191) and an action-table dump to ot5stat.txt. The pause-menu edge routes to the developer menu only when a word that boots to zero and is only ever cleared is non-zero, so none of them opens. The editor is linked, not dead: it is the callback of one of the overlay’s eight actor prototypes.

Shared code outside this overlay

The 24-instruction tally drain step here and the one in the Muscle Dome hub are the same routine linked into two overlays; only the pair of instructions that loads its bypass flag differs. Dumps named overlay_baka_fighter_801f.... are a dump-corpus artifact: those addresses hold resident field-overlay code, including the casino coin counter (which credits coins and debits 100 gold per coin) and the op-0x49 submode family.
