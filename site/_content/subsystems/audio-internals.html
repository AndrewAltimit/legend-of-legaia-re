Audio: internals

The reference half of the audio tour: how the sound files are found, the track-swap handshake bit by bit, Sony's sequencer and SPU libraries function by function, the port's audio model, and how the port's sound is measured against a retail emulator.

Finding the sound files

Retail reads sound data by PROT.DAT entry number. A dev-station path (h:\...) survives in the same functions but is never taken on a retail disc: the split is the build-mode global _DAT_8007B8C2, and the dev arm opens files through a host trap that has no disc to read.

The track-swap handshake

The BGM poller FUN_800243F0 runs a stage counter through a seven-entry jump table while a track change is in flight: wait for CD idle and arm a 30-frame settle; tear down and close the BGM slot unless bit 0 below is set, then kick the async load; count the settle down; install the score; re-attach the slot unless bit 1 is set; latch. The sound-flag word _DAT_8007B750 coordinates it with the script:

BitMeaningSet byCleared by
0script-owned start pending (defer the slot teardown)sub-op 9poller commit, scene entry
1BGM slot paused / detachedsub-op 2, 3, the dance overlaysub-op 1, 4, 0xA, scene entry, game over
2keep the audio across the next scene load: the scene initializer skips its music level ramp and its effect-voice cutsub-op 6field overlay
3load settled - payload staged and delay elapsedthe pollerpoller commit
4release-ack - the script has released the old occupantsub-op 0xApoller commit

After setting bit 3 the poller stalls while bit 0 is set and bit 4 is not, so a swap started by sub-op 9 keeps the old track until the script commits with sub-op 0xA, which detaches and closes the old occupant, sets bit 4 and clears bit 1. A port that honours the sub-op 2 pause but drops the 0xA commit leaves the music paused after the cutscene.

The libsnd calls under the control words settle their meaning: the pause raises a status flag and keeps the cursor; the play core rewinds the cursor to the start for every mode before it plays - which is why sub-op 4 is a replay from the first event, not a resume.

Sony's sequencer and SPU driver

libsnd (0x80061B18..0x800681D8) walks the SEQ bytes, keeps per-channel state in 16 slots of 0xB0-byte records, and turns note events into key-ons; Legaia installs two function-pointer hooks to wire the script VM's sequence context in. libspu (0x80068..0x8006D) pokes the chip's registers, runs the four-mode DMA transfer engine and the block allocator. Being the same libraries every PsyQ game shipped is what lets the functions be named.

  • Key-on volume chain. On the sequencer path (FUN_80067550, in retail’s order and integer steps): the channel volume (CC7) folded into the velocity as vel × chvol / 127; velocity × bank volume × program volume × tone volume widened into the 14-bit register domain; the per-side sequence volume / 0x7F, which the game sets to 107 for every BGM sequence ((215 << 15) >> 16 through FUN_80064890); one pan attenuation per source (tone, program, channel); and a closing square taper v × v / 0x3FFF. A mid-note CC7 or CC10 re-runs the whole chain for every sounding voice on the channel. Against three retail save states, every sounding BGM voice’s pitch, envelope, left / right volume and reverb send matches what the engine programs.
  • Monaural. The options screen’s mono mode sounds each voice at the larger of its two volumes on both sides (0x800677F4..0x80067818), not at their average.
  • Pitch law. A note is pitched against its tone's center through a two-octave table into a 14-bit SPU PITCH (clamped 0x3FFF).
  • Bank open validates the pBAV magic, builds the program-to-tone-page rank map into the reserved ProgAtr words, allocates SPU memory and stashes per-VAG addresses.
  • Tempo becomes an integer tick step where the wall clock meets the sequencer; the decoder does not consume a whole event per call. A tempo set (the open FUN_80062410 and the tempo meta FUN_80061954 share one tail) stores the integer 60000000 / us_per_qn as the BPM and installs a budget of tenths of a tick per call, resolution × bpm × 10 / (divisor × 60) rounded to nearest, with the divisor 0x801CD2BC reading 60 in every catalogued save state. A track therefore runs at budget × 6 ticks a second rather than at its written tempo - 0.44 % off on average over the music_01 scores, 120 BPM exact, 24.83 BPM playing 3.4 % slow.
  • Not audio: the 0x801CE628 cluster next door is libpad. And none of this is libgpu - battle and field code fire cues during render passes, which is why the cluster looks renderer-adjacent in call graphs.

The port's audio model

crates/engine-audio is a from-scratch model; engine-session's AudioBgmDirector turns field-VM sound events into sequencers and fires SFX cues against the banks their category names, generic over the output so the native window (cpal) and the browser (WebAudio) share it.

ModuleModels
spu::Spu, spu::voice24 voices with sample address, loop point, pitch, ADSR, L/R volume and reverb send; 4-point Gaussian resampling as the hardware does
spu::adsr, spu::adpcmFive-phase envelope with linear / exponential modes; streaming 28-samples-per-block decoder, one per voice
spu::reverbRegister-driven network (same / different-side IIR, 4-tap comb, 2 all-pass) with the nine standard presets
spu::ram512 KB model, libspu-shaped transfer engine, block allocator
vab_bind::VabBankUploads every VAG into SPU RAM; turns a MIDI key into voice config + key-on. Programs are slot-indexed

sfxThe cue catalog built from the disc's descriptor table, fired against the bank each cue's category names, through a frame-accurate scheduler
AudioOut / WebAudioOutOne output stream draining the SPU at 44.1 kHz; hard-cut track swaps with a few frames of click guard; CD-XA clips mixed outside the 24 voices
  • Reverb is Studio C, always on, on every voice. Every captured retail state - field, town, battle, summon, title, minigames - has the master enable set and the reverb registers byte-identical to the Studio C preset, with all 24 per-voice enable bits set. The output depth, which no preset carries, is a measured fixed pair. No SEQ carries a CC91 reverb-depth event.
  • Timebase. The sequencer ticks once per SPU sample with an exact integer accumulator - locked to the audio clock, no drift, bit-deterministic for replays. It spaces events at retail's quantised rate (above) by default; Sequencer::set_exact_tempo(true) plays the score as written. One difference is kept on purpose: retail fires an event at the first SsSeqCalc after it falls due, so every onset lands on a vsync, while the port fires it on its own sample - the rate matches, the jitter does not. The SEQ header tempo is a 240 BPM placeholder overridden by the first body tempo event, which carries no MIDI length byte; mis-parsing it pins playback ~3x fast (seq).
  • Not modelled: pitch modulation, noise and FM (Legaia uses none), asynchronous DMA timing, and the hardware's 39-tap reverb FIR (decimation + zero-order hold instead).

Measuring the port against retail

The audio-trace oracle compares which SPU voices are active, frame by frame, against a real emulator running the retail disc. Two retail capture shapes feed it: a single-cycle snapshot lifted from a mednafen save state's SPU section, and a multi-frame trace captured in PCSX-Redux. A retail frame converges when some engine frame's active-voice set covers it with every start address matching; a start-address or master-volume mismatch is a hard failure, and so is an engine trace that sounded nothing where retail had voices. CLI: legaia-engine audio-trace.

  • A save state cannot say which voices belong to the score. A voice leaves its off phase on key-on and returns only when a release runs all the way down, so a save taken mid-playthrough reports nearly every voice non-off - the residue of every cue since boot. Deciding which voices the music is sounding needs the per-vsync trace.
  • Compare the same track over the same stretch. An engine trace plays the track the scene's entry script selects; a retail save plays whatever the playthrough left loaded. Paired on one track and window, the two sides hold the same number of concurrent voices - nineteen at the peak of the measured track, which is exactly what its score decodes to.
  • What remains is one per-voice difference: retail occasionally doubles a note across two voice slots, where the port plays each note once.
History: “the engine is much the quieter side”

The comparison once read as the engine routing no reverb and sounding about half retail's voices. Both were instrument defects: the reverb figure was read from the SPU control register rather than the per-voice reverb-enable mask, and the oracle had built a bare SPU where the shipped host sets retail reverb. The voice-count gap was two different pieces of music - a town save reached from the world map still holds the overworld track.
