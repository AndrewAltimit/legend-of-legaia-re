Muscle Dome

An arena contest fought as a ladder of ordinary battles. Pick one of three courses and fight its fixed run of rounds (8, 8 or 13), one real monster per round, with one party character. Between rounds the arena shows a tally that heals the fighter and adds casino coins to a running total, and finishing the contest pays the total out. This page covers how a contest runs, what each screen and rule does, and what that means when you play it.

Three things it is not:

  • Not a card battle. The four “cards” are the four direction commands, always the same four, each at that fighter’s own AP cost. Nothing is drawn, discarded or reshuffled.
  • Not turn-limited. A round ends on a knockout and on nothing else. The “Turns Left / HP Left” strip in the same overlay belongs to the Koru boss fight (below).
  • Not part of the minigame-hub family. It shares no controller code with fishing, the slot machine, the dance or Baka Fighter.

Contest init FUN_801CEA6C, per-frame hub FUN_801CF870 (state byte DAT_801D1A78, 51-entry jump table at 0x801CE990), opponent installer FUN_801D1510, settlement FUN_801D0F60, all in PROT 0977; round driver FUN_801D0748 and action SM FUN_801E295C in PROT 0898.

Getting in

Every dome door on the disc is in one record of the koin1 scene: the course menu. Each of its three arms takes its entry fee, sets its own course flag and clears the other two, then runs the mode-24 minigame door warp (field-VM op 0x3E, sub-id 5), which loads the arena overlay. So exactly one course flag is set when the arena starts, and that flag is the course pick. The taiku scene, despite its label, carries no arena door.

The warp is the ordinary minigame round trip: the field is backed up, the overlay runs, and the game returns to the exact field state it left. The mechanism is on the field / event VM page.

Two pieces of code run the contest, one inside the other. The contest hub owns the ladder: which course, which round, the intro / INTERVAL / ROUND screens, the tally and the payout. Each round is a normal battle on the shared battle round driver, which every battle in the game steps; the battle’s exit selector sends a finished round back to the arena (mode 0x18) instead of to the field when the contest bit 0x100 is set in the special-battle word _DAT_8007BAC0.

How a contest runs

The cursor

The whole ladder position is one byte. The low byte of the special-battle word _DAT_8007BAC0 packs (course, round): course = ((word - 1) & 0xFF) >> 4, round = (word - 1) & 0xF. Each finished round adds one. The bits above the low byte are the course’s restrictions and survive the whole contest unchanged.

“Survived” is one bit, DAT_8007BD60 & 0x80: the battle’s party-wipe scan clears it, and the shared minigame-exit routine raises it again on the way out, so on re-entry it reads “the party is still standing”.

The intermission is per fight, not per turn

The INTERVAL screen comes between fights. Inside a round, the end of a turn sends the battle straight back to its own command menu: the game is in battle mode, and the hub, which is the only thing that draws INTERVAL, is not running. There is no beat between turns.

The three courses

Each round’s opponent is a real monster id from a roster table in the arena overlay: 29 rounds in all, 8 + 8 + 13, one monster per round with no formation variety. The installer writes that id into formation slot 0, clears the other three slots, and sets the game mode to battle init; that is the arena’s whole handoff to the battle.

How long Master runs

Beginner and Expert always run their 8 rounds. Master is 13 rounds long only once the story has caught up: three story flags each cut it short, and each is checked only when the run reaches its threshold.

What each course restricts

The restrictions are bits of the special-battle word, seeded at the arena’s first entry from whichever course flag the menu set:

Accessories and the Seru setting are never touched, so a stripped fighter keeps its accessories. Everything comes back at the end: settlement restores the whole party block saved at entry. The curated course rules in casino.toml (no equipment, no items, magic forbidden on Master) are walkthrough labels that agree with these bits.

Ladder tables in PROT 0977: score table 0x801D1860 (3 x 16 i32), round roster 0x801D1920 (29 x {name_ptr, monster_id}), course descriptors 0x801D1A08 (3 x {round_count, first_round}). Master clamp at 0x801CED28..0x801CEDA4; course seed at 0x801CEB8C..0x801CEBC8.

A round is an ordinary battle

Nothing in a round’s damage, input or ending is dome-specific. The fighter is the lead party member alone, in the normal assembled battle form; the opponent is the rung’s monster, acting on the ordinary monster AI from its own action entries in the monster archive (PROT 867). There is no dome-specific AI table and no dome-local damage scaling.

A turn

  1. Begin | Runevery turn opens on this prompt with the far camera
  2. Command ringAttack, Item, Ra-Seru, Spirit; the highlight starts on Attack
  3. Auto | CommandAuto replays the saved string; Command opens direction entry
  4. Direction entryeach press spends that command’s AP; ends by itself when nothing is affordable
  5. Reviewthe committed bar of pennants
  6. Begin | ReselectBegin plays the turn; Reselect reopens the ring
  7. Playbackeach fighter’s whole string, then back to the prompt

The round driver steps through phase bytes 0x1E (prompt), 0x28 (ring), 0x78 (Auto | Command), 0x50 (entry), 0x5A (review), 0x6E (confirm), 0xFE / 0xFF (playback) and 0x14 (turn top) - a chain pinned by a phase-byte watch across a driven round. The turn-top arm is the only writer of the prompt phase and stores it unconditionally, which is why every turn opens on Begin | Run.

What ends a round

A knockout, and nothing else. The battle’s end-of-action gate walks the actor table; when one side has nobody standing it raises the battle-end signal (party wipe or monster wipe), and the exit selector sends the game to the arena. The battle’s turn counter is a counter, not a budget: its readers pick scripted per-turn enemy behaviour or draw Koru’s countdown, and none of them reaches the battle-end signal.

Run gives the contest up

Run is on the prompt every turn, and in the dome it always ends the contest. The fight start marks each round as a scripted fight, and the escape roll lets a scripted fight flee only through its forced arm, the special-battle word’s 0x100 bit. Every course the koin1 menu opens carries that bit, so Run gets out. The round driver’s one contest-gated arm then stores leg outcome 4, which the re-entered arena settles as a give-up: the running total is zeroed.

Running on purpose

Giving up on round 1 also sets story flag 0x130 + course. Doing it on all three courses is the trigger for the Muscle Paradise / Chicken King secret (“run from the first battle in all three difficulties”).

Fight start FUN_801D1510 stores DAT_8007BD60 = 0x80 (scripted fight); escape roll FUN_801E791C, forced arm 0x801E7978..0x801E7A14; the contest-gated flee arm is at 0x801D3228..0x801D328C in FUN_801D0748, skipped for monsters 0xAF / 0x3D..0x3F, none of which is on the ladder.

Commands, AP and the ring

On screen a round looks like any battle with the course restrictions applied: the gold Begin and name chips top-left, the blue-marble Attack / Ra-Seru / Spirit plates with the Item chip crossed out by a red X, the pointed blue status plate (name, HP and MP as cur/max) with the AP plate above it on the right. A committed string that performs an art raises the art banner (“HYPER ARTS!!”) during playback. As in any battle, the enemy’s HP is not shown.

The four direction commands

Attack opens the Arts input: four direction commands, the same four every time. Each press debits the command’s AP cost from the turn budget and appends the command to the fighter’s action queue. The cost is per character and per weapon, copied at battle load from the equipment’s swing records:

DirectionAP cost (retail values)
The weapon’s favoured direction30 (0x1E)
Off-class42 (0x2A)
Far54 (0x36)
  • A pennant per press. Pennant n sits at x = 16 + (cost of the earlier pennants) and is cost - 6 wide, so the bar fills in proportion to AP spent; a 100-AP pool spans 128 pixels.
  • Entry ends itself on the press that leaves no command affordable; there is no confirm step there.
  • Arts. What plays out is the battle’s tokenised queue, so a string that spells an art plays the art. A Hyper Art collapses for a fighter carrying a Ra-Seru, as in any battle. Triangle pages the learned-arts list.
  • The AP plate on the right reads the Spirit gauge and does not move during entry; the budget’s visible form is the bar of pennants.

Auto

Auto is not a picker. The queue is already loaded from a 16-byte saved string in the character’s record; Auto skips the editing screen and flags the fighter so each round rebuilds the queue. Confirming the entry screen with nothing entered replays the saved string, and confirming the review screen writes the string back. Two saved strings exist, one per AP band.

A Ra-Seru cast spends MP, not AP: it replaces the direction string with the spell id, at the spell table’s cost discounted by the fighter’s ability bits (one halves it, one takes a quarter off).

Swing power and the action queue

Selection appends raw direction ids (0x0C..=0x0F) to the actor’s queue at +0x1DF. Arts and magic route through FUN_801E09F8 to FUN_801DD0AC, which indexes the move-power table at 0x801F4F5C by a byte map at 0x801F4E63. That map sends every bare swing to row 0, which is all zeros, so a bare swing’s strength is the melee kernel’s per-command scalar 12 / 18 / 20 / 22 / 28. The map and the tables are disassembly; that swing damage is therefore the melee kernel’s is inference.

The INTERVAL tally: healing and coins

After each survived round that is not the last, the INTERVAL screen counts up four rows. Three of them are healing, each a percentage of the fighter’s max HP; only the fourth is coins.

RowValueAs % of max HPGoes to
Roundround * 2 * max_hp / 1002% per round numberHP
Turnsmin(turns, 8) * max_hp / 1001% per turn taken, up to 8%HP
Outcome[8, 12, 4, 2][min(outcome, 3)] * max_hp / 1008, 12, 4 or 2%HP

The three HP rows drain into one accumulator, and the restore state adds it to current HP, capped at max. So a later round can open hurt, and the longer the run, the more each interval gives back. Each row drains one step per frame with a voice blip per step; one button press fast-forwards the whole roll.

The contest-start refill

  • HP, MP and SP are refilled to max when the contest starts.
  • On Expert and Master only, the four gear slots (armour, head, weapon, leg) are emptied first. Beginner keeps its gear.
  • It is one-shot: the refill sits on the fresh-contest side of the arena init, so a round boundary never refills MP or SP. Between rounds the only healing is the tally’s HP.

Row maths FUN_801D1184 (the 0x51EB851F reciprocal is the / 100), tally screen FUN_801CF074, outcome weights DAT_801D1A5C, HP accumulator DAT_801D1AC8; start refill FUN_801D0ED8, gear clear behind bnez at 0x801D0EE8.

Payout and prizes

A round pays nothing; a contest pays coins. Each score cell is counted once: the non-final rounds through the INTERVAL tally, the last one at settlement. Settlement first restores the saved party block, then settles the running total:

The hub screens and their timing

Each hub screen is a fade-in at its own rate, a hold, and a fade-out. Every step is scaled by the adaptive frame-skip factor, so the figures are ticks: frames at the normal cadence. Brightness climbs to 0x80, a PSX textured primitive’s neutral level.

  • Tally fast-forward is a latch. A press during the roll raises the step scaler’s bypass, after which each lane moves its whole remainder in one step. Only the arena init clears it, so one press rolls out everything left on that screen.
  • Tally cues. The INTERVAL screen queues four “ka-ching” cues (ids 0x202, 0x202, 0x202, 0x203) staggered 0 / 30 / 60 / 90 frames, played from the arena’s own sound bundle.

The ringside still

Between rounds the hub does not show the brick wall of the first visit. It shows a pre-rendered still of the party at the ring fence, streamed into VRAM at (384, 0) at the battle’s teardown. Two stills exist, and the loader picks between them on the lead character’s live HP: int.tim (extraction 1221) normally, int2.tim (1222), the different-reaction version, when current HP is below half of max. A re-entry latch set on every return decides still versus wall. The format and the draw are on the ringside still page.

Music, announcer and sound

The battle theme waits for the fight

The arena loads its music itself and plays it only once a fight starts:

  1. Releasearena init stops both BGM sound sources: whatever was playing stops
  2. Loadthe standard battle theme (extraction 1014, sound-test #26, global 2026) is opened, not played
  3. Playthe fight start resumes it, past the ROUND card’s fade-out

The arena init runs on the door and again after every round, so the Welcome card, the course card, every ROUND card and every INTERVAL tally play with no music, and each round’s fight starts the theme afresh. On the way out the venue (koin1, whose casino-floor track is global 2018) gets its own music back.

The announcer

Two XA voice lines belong to the hub: the Welcome line on the Welcome screen’s first tick, and a ROUND line on each ROUND card’s first tick, whose XA channel is the round index. The ROUND card state is the same on a first visit and after every round, so every round’s card speaks its number.

That the channel is the upcoming round’s index on a re-entered hub is inference from the first-visit value.

The Koru strip is not the dome’s

The “Turns Left / HP Left” strip lives in the same battle overlay, and its turn-top code runs in every battle, but it draws only when formation slot 0 holds monster 0xB6, Koru, the game’s one turn-limited boss fight. The dome cannot raise it: its highest roster id is 0xAA.

  • Turns Left = 4 - turn counter; HP Left = the opponent’s HP as a percentage of its max.
  • Nothing compares the turn counter to a bound. The four turns end in Koru’s own AI: rounds 0 to 3 cast spells 0xA2..0xA5 and round 4 casts 0xA1, the all-party finisher.
  • The strip is up from each turn’s start until Begin, and never shares a frame with the per-actor name plate (retail capture: of 27 checkpoints, the three at a Begin / Run prompt show the strip).

Tips that follow from the rules

  • Losing still pays half. A knockout halves the running total; Run voids it. Unless you want the Chicken King flag, never Run once coins are banked.
  • Only HP comes back between rounds. MP and SP are refilled once, at the contest start. Budget Ra-Seru casts for the whole course.
  • Long rounds heal more. The turns row gives back 1% of max HP per turn, up to 8%, and the round row 2% per round number, so late rounds are the most forgiving between fights.
  • Plan Expert and Master without gear. Armour, head, weapon and leg slots are emptied at the start; accessories stay, so they are the only build you bring.
  • Master needs the story. Missing story flags end Master early at 8, 11 or 12, and the War God Icon needs the 13th fight.
  • Skipping screens. The Welcome hold (123 ticks) cannot be skipped; the course card and every ROUND card hold can, and one press during the tally rolls it out.
  • Saved strings carry over. Auto replays the last confirmed string, and a bare confirm on the entry screen replays it too.

Where this is in the port

The port models the same two layers, and the same rules run on the native play-window, the browser play page and the site’s minigames page, where you can play it.

  • A round is the ordinary battle on both play hosts. The fight start becomes a one-slot formation, a scripted-fight flag, the arena stage (extraction 1225) and a battle that returns to the dome; the command ring, Arts input, monster AI, hit sparks, victory pose and battle camera are the battle’s.
  • Run raises the contest’s “ran” outcome and settles it as given up.
  • The theme is held through the hub screens and started on the tick the hub hands the round over.
  • The minigames page runs the contest on the play page’s runtime (“in the game’s battle”): it opens koin1, makes the course menu’s pick and arms the same door warp. The party level defaults to 1, matching the one retail dome capture (Vahn alone, level 1). The dome panel stays for free play against any archive monster.
  • Contest, ladder and tally are one model (engine-minigames::muscle_dome: DomeContest, settle_contest, the hub envelopes), with no per-host ladder rule.

Where the leg session still runs a round on its own (a monster with no catalog entry, a world-only harness, the free-play panel), a few things are host models rather than retail: the escape roll is not modelled (Run ends the leg on the press), the Item arm always refuses, Spirit commits an empty string, and the Ra-Seru list is a status row outside the game picture.

HUD chrome texture sources

The chrome resolves to five disc sources and one layout table, pinned from a live dome battle with every texture page byte-matched: the screen-element placement table at SCUS 0x80076C10 (24-byte records); the boot-gap widget TIM for chips, plates, the D-pad glyph and the AP plate; the boot-gap ASCII font; the menu-glyph atlas for small digits; the etim page for the red X, the art banner words and the damage numerals; and extraction 1220 for the hub strips. The dome’s command cluster is exactly the battle’s (centre (228, 70)). The AP meter has no source rect: retail draws it as two untextured gouraud strips.

Shared battle code under the overlay_muscle_dome_* dump names

The overlay_muscle_dome_* dumps are the entire battle-action overlay (PROT 0898) dumped while the arena was resident; the prefix is not dome evidence.
