Muscle Dome

An arena contest fought as a ladder of ordinary battles. The player picks one of three courses and fights its fixed sequence of rounds, one real monster per round, with one party character. Between rounds the arena shows a tally that heals the fighter and banks casino coins; a finished contest pays the coins out.

Three things it is not

  • Not a card battle. The four “cards” are the four direction commands, always the same four, each at that fighter’s own AP cost. Nothing is drawn, discarded or reshuffled.
  • Not turn-limited. A leg ends on a knockout and on nothing else. The “Turns Left” strip in the same overlay belongs to a timed boss fight.
  • Not part of the minigame-hub family. It shares no controller library with fishing, the slot machine, dance or Baka Fighter.

Getting there

The door is the mode-24 minigame warp: field-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 105 (sub-id 5) sets game mode 0x18 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup, overlay-load, return-to-field sequence, so the game returns to the exact field state it suspended. The mechanism is on the field / event VM page.

Every dome door on the disc is in one casino record - the course menu. Each arm debits its entry fee and latches a course flag before its own warp. The battle exit selector is the second of only two writers of game mode 24: with the contest bit set it stores the arena mode instead of the field’s, which is what returns a finished round to the arena.

The contest hub

What a leg’s end routes to

LegNext hub state
survived, course not exhausted0x0A - the between-leg tally
survived, course exhausted0x32 - settle, continuing
not survived0x32 - settle
ran0x32 - settle, gave up

“Survived” is a single bit test on re-entry. “Continuing” is derived, not prompted - there is no continue prompt; the latch is raised on the one path where the course is exhausted and the fighter survived.

The tally heals

RowValueDrains into
roundround * 2 * max_hp / 100the HP accumulator
turnsmin(turns_taken, 8) * max_hp / 100the HP accumulator
outcome[8, 12, 4, 2][min(outcome, 3)] * max_hp / 100the HP accumulator
scorescore_table[course][round - 1]the coin tally

Three of the four lanes are healing, not score; only the fourth is coins. So a contest costs no permanent HP. The lanes drain one after another, one scaled step per frame with a voice blip per step.

The roll can be fast-forwarded. The tally arm opens by testing the press mask against 0xF4 and latching DAT_801D1AB4 (0x801CFD60..0x801CFD7C). That word is the step scaler’s bypass: while it is up a lane moves its whole remainder in one step, so one press rolls out everything left. Its only clear is the arena init.

Hub screens are envelopes

Each hub screen is a fade-in at its own rate, a hold and a fade-out. The counters are brightness levels clamped at 0x80 - a textured primitive’s neutral modulation - not durations, and every step is scaled by the adaptive frame-skip factor. The two card holds end early on any bit of pad & 0xF4. A port that counts frames ends the screens at the wrong instants and cannot be ended early by a button; one that draws them at 0x100 is twice retail’s brightness.

A leg plays out as a battle

Nothing about a leg’s choreography is the dome’s own: a turn acts out on the battle’s animation and locomotion code, and the port plays it on the same kernels its battles use.

PieceWhat decides it
Clip speedthe anim tick’s rate law: rate / 4 keyframes a vsync at the normal speed byte
Where the walk stopsthe range law FUN_8004E2F0: the attacker’s live position against the target’s body pair, the monster’s size class and the party reach offset
Traveleach clip’s own root speed and end step; a reaction slides its body back
The hitthe swing’s event frame; a flinch, or a knockdown on the killing hit
Walking homenone - the next string starts where the last one left the pair

A walk that stopped on a margin of its own rather than on the range law is what left daylight between a fist and its target.
