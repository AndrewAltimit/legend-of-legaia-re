Casino slot machine

Three reels of ten pictographic symbols. Every spin costs a flat three coins and plays all five paylines; a matched line pays a per-symbol amount, and a line of either jackpot symbol opens a bonus round on the same reels, where the three numbers you stop on are multiplied together. Behind the face sits a feature roll that pays back what the machine has taken, a stop that nudges a reel only a few rows, and a press latch that punishes mashing. It is a 3D scene, not a sprite collage: the reels are textured cylinders, the paylines are 3D line segments and the cabinet is a mesh.

Not the prize counter

Trading coins for items is a separate static table and a separate screen at the koin1 / balden prize counters. Buying coins with gold is a third screen, in the field overlay. The slot machine only pays into the coin balance; the exchange spends it.

Getting to the machine

A disc-wide walk of every scene finds five slot doors: three in the Sol casino (koin1) and the Vidna cabinet, present in both variants of that scene. The three Sol cabinets are interchangeable doors to one machine; the script byte that names the cabinet is never read.

  1. Door gateThe cabinet record compares the casino coin bank against 1. With no coins the script jumps to the refusal line and the machine never loads.
  2. FeeA casino-coin debit runs next. At a slot cabinet it is zero (a Baka Fighter cabinet takes one coin), because the machine charges its own bets from the balance it copies in.
  3. SeatThe player walks onto the stool, turns to the machine, plays the sit clip, and the screen washes white.
  4. WarpField-VM op 0x3E with op0 = 103 (minigame sub-id 3) switches to game mode 24 and loads the overlay. It is the ordinary scene-backup / overlay-load / return sequence, so you come back to the exact field state you left.
  5. Entry seedThe slot generator is seeded with the literal 0x6C0A2AF0, the machine’s balance is set equal to the coin bank, and the state word is cleared. A launch outside the casino door path falls back to a 70-coin dev balance.
  6. Return passEvery exit - the quit row, an empty machine, the port’s Start escape - lands in a second script pass that clears the wash and stands the player up, as at the Baka Fighter cabinets.

The mechanism behind the warp is on the field / event VM page.

The picker cursor wraps with an unsigned remainder: Up on row 0 stays on row 0, Down on row 2 wraps to row 0. Pressing cancel and confirm on the same frame on the rules row lands in a spin-up with no bet charged - a retail collision the port does not reproduce.

The reel strips

Each reel is a 20-slot strip. Init builds two source strips per reel in one interleaved pass: for each slot it draws a random position mod 20 and probes forward to a free one (step 13 for symbols, step 1 for numerals). So every symbol appears exactly twice on every reel, at scattered positions, and so does every bonus numeral. A third strip, the display strip, is the only one the win evaluator and the renderer read.

The display strip is refilled one row per reel per frame, nine rows ahead of the payline, from whichever source the feature mode names. That is how a bonus round swaps numerals onto the reels: they rotate in from off-screen as the reels turn, and rotate back out when the round ends. Because a reel has to travel about nine rows before the swap reaches the payline, the spin-up is forced 24 frames longer on the first spin of a bonus round and on the first spin after one.

The two generators and the feature roll

Two independent generators feed the machine:

  • The slot generator builds the strips, picks reel landings and rolls whether a feature stays on. It is one self-contained word, so reel outcomes are reproducible from the seed state: 
    x = x * 5 + 1
x = (x << 16) + (x >> 16)        fold the 16-bit halves
  • The BIOS rand drives the per-spin feature roll and the landing-line pick - the parts that should not be replayable from the visible reels alone.

At every spin start, in this draw order, the roll picks the payline a forced stop will land on (rand % 5), the normal-mode target symbol (rand % 6 + 2, so symbols 2 to 7), and, if no feature is already running, rolls for feature modes 1, 2 and 3:

widen = press_latch ? rand % 100 + 200 : 0
mode 1 if rand % (widen + N1)  == 0
mode 2 if rand % (widen + N2)  == 0
mode 3 if rand % (widen + 600) == 0

N1 and N2 are bracketed on the machine’s net-take counter, not on your balance. The counter gains 6 per normal spin and 1 per feature spin, and every bonus payout is subtracted from it: the machine pays back what it has taken.

The spin-up press latch

Pressing any face button while the reels are still spinning up raises a latch, and the next spin’s roll reads it and clears it. A raised latch adds rand % 100 + 200 to every feature denominator: in the lowest bracket, mode 1 goes from 1 in 700 to between 1 in 900 and 1 in 999. Mashing through the spin-up makes the reach and hot modes rarer.

What a stop press does

A stop never yanks a reel to a result. It searches a short window of rows past where the reel is when you press, for one target symbol; if the target is in the window the reel lands it on the spin’s chosen payline, and otherwise the reel simply stops on its next natural row.

The payout table is exactly ten bytes, one per symbol, and the byte scales with the symbol’s rarity. The values come off your disc: the machine’s own rules screen shows them as its second page (each symbol’s three faces beside its line payout).

Symbols 0, 1 and 2 are one piece of artwork recoloured three ways, as are 4 and 5; the palette is what tells them apart on the reel.

The bonus round

  1. SwapThe reels rotate onto the numeral strip: the values 1 to 10, each in its own colour, each twice per reel.
  2. StopYou stop each reel with no help from the machine: mode 6 searches zero rows, so the reel lands on its next natural row. The three numbers are your timing and nothing else.
  3. PayThe spin pays the product of the three numbers on the centre line, from 1 (1×1×1) to 1000 (10×10×10). No match is checked and no payout byte is read; the middle lamp lights.
  4. Count downEach bonus spin still costs 1 coin. When the earned rounds run out the feature ends and the next spin runs long enough to rotate the symbols back on.

The marquee strip across the top reads 0 x 0 x 0 at the start of a round and fills in each column’s number as its reel is stopped; between spins it shows the rounds still owed as three pips. The number drawn on the reel, the number in the tally and the factor in the product are one byte read through one bias, so the three can never disagree.

The reach tease

While exactly two reels are stopped, a scanner checks every line for an equal pair of punches or kicks among the landed reels. A pair scrolls the reach legend across the marquee and plays the reach sting once per spin. It is presentation only: it sets no payout and changes no odds.

Provenance: win evaluation FUN_801D13E8, payout bytes DAT_801D3598 (PROT 0975 file 0x4D80), reach scanner FUN_801D1AF4, tally composer FUN_801CFFF0. Confirmed structure; the payout values are disc data.

Coin economy

EventBalanceNet-take counter
normal spin (modes 0..3)−3+6
feature spin (modes 4..6)−1+1
line win+payout byte-
bonus-round spin+product−product
  • Two words hold coins. The machine plays from its own balance; the casino coin bank is touched exactly twice, both by assignment: bank → balance on entry, balance → bank once the exit fade completes. It is never debited or credited per spin.
  • The not-enough gate tests for 3 coins before it looks at the mode, so even a 1-coin feature spin needs 3 on the machine. An empty machine shows its prompt and can only leave.
  • The balance is capped at 9999999 in the tally path; the HUD displays at most 99999.
  • A win is tallied over time: 11 coins per odd frame while more than 20 are owed, then 1 at a time, with a tick sound per move.
  • The well-known infinite-coins code pins the bank at the casino, but does not make spins free: betting decrements the overlay-local balance.
The coin-exchange counter (buying coins with gold)

Coins are bought at a counter, not inside the slot overlay. The counter is a field-overlay screen: field-VM op 0x49 sub-op 6 at the koin1 / balden counters. Its entry panel shows the coin bank, a six-digit entry field, party gold and the total cost; the cost is entry × 100 gold, drawn in the warning pen when party gold is below it or the entry exceeds min(gold / 100, 9999999 − bank). A Yes / No confirm opens over the entry panel, which stays drawn underneath it.

Provenance: submode handler FUN_801F0ADC and entry panel FUN_801E6F70 in PROT 0897. Port engine-field::submode_hub_actors::coin_exchange, drawn on both hosts through one builder.

What the rules imply

Following from the traced rules

  • Don’t press during the spin-up. Any face button there raises the latch that widens every feature denominator by 200 to 299 on the next spin.
  • Bonus rounds are pure timing. The machine searches zero rows in mode 6, so where you press is where the reel lands. Watch for the 10s: three of them pay 1000.
  • Normal stops are nudges, not results. A normal stop searches only 2 to 4 rows for one of symbols 2 to 7 and never steers toward the jackpot symbols; only the reach modes target the kick and the punch, over 6 to 9 rows.
  • The machine gets looser the more it has taken. The reach modes are four times likelier once the net-take counter is above 2000, and a big bonus payout pulls the counter back down.
  • Cash out to keep it. Winnings live on the machine until the exit fade writes them back to the coin bank.

A 3D scene

Every element on the machine’s face is a quad in a 3D scene, projected through the geometry coprocessor and depth-sorted. The overlay carries no coprocessor instruction of its own - it reaches it through shared executable wrappers - which is why a scan for one inside the overlay wrongly calls the machine 2D.

The reel faces are depth-cued off their distance: the shade peaks (a 1.41× brighten) exactly on the payline face and falls to black within about 48 degrees either side, which caps each reel window top and bottom. The camera faces the machine head-on with a 2:1 horizontal scale, the pixel aspect of the 640-wide video mode the machine runs in.

Projection, reel geometry and the marquee in detail

The captured camera state while the machine draws reduces every path to one mapping on the 640×240 framebuffer:

view   = (6x - 1440, 3y + 20, 3z + 24480)
screen = (320 + 1024 * vx / vz, 114 + 1024 * vy / vz)

-z is toward the viewer: the glass (paylines, medallions, lamps, pedestals, marquee) sits at z = -768 / -800, the reel cylinders are centred on z = 0 with the payline symbol at z = -512, behind the glass.

The payline face is the fifth of a reel’s eight faces, not the first; a renderer that indexes the strip straight off the face index draws a payline whose three symbols are not the three that paid. Symbol art is arithmetic: a strip value of 0x10 or more switches that row to the numeral page, which is why one strip can carry both kinds mid-rotation.

The marquee is rebuilt from scratch each frame from a 21-record message bank. One occupant wins each frame, in priority order: the payout caption (sliding down into place over 13 frames, leading zeros suppressed against the whole figure), then the bonus tally or round pips, then the attract legend.

Provenance: reel renderer FUN_801D0FA8, paylines FUN_801D3380, billboards FUN_801D08E4, marquee FUN_801D0E1C, HUD widgets FUN_801D2CC0; camera matrix and offsets read from the minigame_slot_machine capture’s coprocessor registers. That the cabinet’s per-frame draw is the shared mesh renderer is inferred, not traced to a GPU word.

The art pack

The init loads extraction PROT 1200: a container of five textures, the cabinet mesh and a two-record animation bundle. Each texture’s framebuffer destination is the texture page the renderers sample, and every image block is byte-identical to a retail VRAM dump at the machine.

The two jackpot symbols are told apart by their art cell: blue-dominant for the kick (8), red-dominant for the punch (9). The neighbouring PROT 1198 / 1199 entries are the machine’s sound bank, not backdrop art.

The reel motor is not a cue: the bet keys a voice directly, the reach scanner re-keys it on a second tone for the reach loop, and the evaluation releases it. The machine’s cues resolve through its own effect bank (PROT 1199) against its VAB (PROT 1198).

The machine starts no music. It inherits the host scene’s track: in Sol, the casino floor’s Sub-game theme (sound-test slot 18, the same track the Baka Fighter cabinet and the Muscle Dome inherit); in Vidna, the town theme, or the mist theme during the mist story state. See music tracks.

Where this is in the port

The rules engine (engine-minigames::slot_machine) runs on all three hosts: the native play window, the browser play page and the standalone minigames page, where you can play it now with your own disc. Walking up to a cabinet in either play host runs the record’s script through the field VM, including the coin-bank compare that refuses an empty bank. The standalone page racks its own 60 coins.

  • Faithful: strip construction in retail’s draw order, the feature roll with its exact draw order and bracket edges, the bet charge and net-take accrual, the stop plan and landing search including the bonus round’s free stop, the five-line evaluation and bonus product, the claimed-column tally and marquee, the cash-out picker and the bank commit on exit.
  • Drawn as retail draws it: both play hosts build the whole frame through one primitive builder sampling the art pack from VRAM; the standalone page rasterises the same primitive list on the CPU.

Where the port differs

  • The spin-up length and reel speed are engine-chosen; retail’s magnitudes are not pinned.
  • The BIOS rand stream is a deterministic generator, so replays stay bit-identical.
  • Hot (3) and hold (5) feature modes fall back to the normal landing plan.
  • A press collects the rest of a payout tally at once.
  • The picker’s cancel + confirm collision and a press latched on the spin-up’s last frame are not reproduced.

For testing, play-window’s O key arms the same door warp without the door gate, and --cheat-coins N (or the play page’s Cheats panel) sets the bank.
