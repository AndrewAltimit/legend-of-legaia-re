Battle subsystem

How Legaia runs a fight, from the flash of an encounter to the spoils screen. The town logic is paged out of RAM, the battle overlay (a chunk of code streamed in on demand) is paged in, and the field you were standing on stays loaded underneath as the 3D backdrop. This page is the guided tour; the per-field tables live on battle internals, the measurements on capture notes.

How a battle works

Three systems share the battle overlay. The scene loader pulls the stage, effect and monster assets off the disc. The menu state machine walks the player through Begin / Run, the command ring and the target cursor. The action state machine plays each committed command out over many frames - wind-up, strike, damage popup, recovery - and hands the turn on. The effect VM draws sparks and spell visuals but never decides anything; the move VM turns a Tactical Arts input into keyframes one layer below the action machine.

Getting into a fight

Three doors lead into the same loader:

  • Walking. Every step onto a new 128-unit collision tile rolls against the region's rate; a hit picks a formation row from the scene's own table (see encounter record).
  • World map. Each region rolls the same way against its own slice of rows (world map).
  • Script. Boss rows sit outside every rollable slice and are reached only by the field-VM scripted-battle op 3E FF <row> (any op0 < 100 runs the same body). The row's non-zero header byte is what gives a scripted fight its particle intro and second audio cue - and the same byte marks the fight no-escape.

Whichever door opens, the field scene is not unloaded: the scene-name table is identical before and after the switch, and the battle draws on top of the resident terrain. The formation ends up in one four-cell array of monster ids that every later stage reads (internals). Battle setup also rolls for a back attack or a pre-emptive strike by comparing the two sides' mean SPD; the side caught out faces the wrong way and loses its first-round turns (formula).

Details: who carries the boss op, and the forced-battle id nobody writes

The garmel fights ride partition-2 beat records spawned by the gated record dispatch. The Caruban op lives in a partition-1 boss-stager placement: a parked special-model actor whose record opens on a park-gate flag test, stations itself at the nest tile, self-suspends on a halt-acquire, and carries the beat body (staged-marker SET, then 3E FF 11). No script-side un-halt exists, so walking up to it is what resumes it.

A "forced battle id" global exists but has no writer anywhere on the disc: set, it would seed the formation cells from the id itself and exit to the debug menu. It is dev-harness residue, like the mode-18/19 game-over rows. Full trace in battle.md.

Loading the fight

Before the first prompt the loader ticks through an asynchronous ladder, one PROT.DAT entry per state:

  1. Stage packthe paired battle stage pack · PROT 0869 / 0875
  2. Texture packetim.dat, the effect textures · raw TOC 872
  3. Model + VDF packsone read: etmd.dat into the effect window, vdf into its pointer table · raw TOC 873 + 874
  4. Effect scriptsefect.dat, then the runtime effect 2-pack · raw TOC 875
  5. Stage overlayonly for a non-zero stage id: per-stage code, not geometry · PROT 967..969
  6. Side-band streamssummon.dat / readef.DAT for arts poses and summons · PROT 893 / 894
  7. Party + monstersparty meshes assembled from equipment; one archive slot per distinct species · PLAYER1..4, PROT 867

The befect_data rows are numbered in the loader's own raw TOC space, the space its li constants live in; the extraction entry is two lower, so raw 872..875 are extraction 870..873 - etim / etmd / vdf / efect. Reading a raw index as an extraction one is what once fused the vdf pack with the character pack that carries extraction label 874.

  • Party meshes are assembled, not loaded whole. The player battle files (data\battle\PLAYER1..4) carry per-equipment sections; the loader splices the equipped pieces into one mesh per member and relocates its textures into the seat's VRAM band. Details: battle data pack, character mesh.
  • The heap is the real formation limit. Everything the loader places comes from one ~1.23 MB best-fit heap, and the monster streamer copies through the returned pointer unchecked. About 145 KB is left for distinct monster blocks; retail never authors more than two distinct species per formation and the randomizer enforces the same cap. Ledger on internals.
  • Duplicates are free. Only the first occurrence of a species id streams; repeated seats share the record and mesh.
  • A stage overlay takes the fight over. Only the Tetsu sparring tutorial and the Cort fight page one in. The evolved-Cort intro leaves the command-flow byte on a value the menu has no handler for, so the menu idles while the module runs its own camera move, banner and countdown, then writes the flow byte back with the intro timer already zeroed. No button press hurries it.

The arena: backdrop, camera and seats

There is no arena. The fight happens on the ground you were walking on; the camera swaps from the walk camera to a slow orbit around the midpoint between the rows, and the actors and HUD are layered on top. An overworld fight adds two layers so the horizon reads complete from every angle: a flat procedural grass grid under the actors and the map's sky dome, drawn twice (a half turn or an X mirror, chosen per stage by an authored table).

The orbit camera is pinned exactly: pitch 32 (of 4096), a fixed translation 7680 deep, a 4x world scale composed under each actor. Constants on internals; the per-phase framing (far shot for the menu, close-up for arts input) is on battle action.

Seats are authored data. Two static tables of (x, y, z) entries, picked by combatant count, are stamped into every actor at setup. The party faces +Z, the monsters -Z.

An alternate monster family (used by the pincer and scripted seatings) is selected by the same flag bit that marks a fight no-escape.

Who is fighting

Every combatant is one actor struct reached through an 8-entry pointer table, and the slot number is identity for the rest of the fight: the action machine, the range check, the AI and the HUD all say "slot 4" rather than "the Gimard". Four records feed those structs:

RecordWhat it holdsWhere it comes fromTables
Actor structPosition, HP / MP / AGL pairs, status halfword, action queue, mesh pointerBuilt at setup from the records belowactor struct
Battle contextMenu cursor, action cursor, acting slot, action parameters, message buffer, intro timerAllocated per battlecontext struct
Character recordThe persistent 0x414-byte per-character block: stats, spells, equipment, nameThe save filecharacter record, save record
Monster recordStats, three castable spell ids, gold / EXP / drop, mesh + texture pool, action entriesMonster archive slot, one per speciesmonster record

The party side is read straight out of the character record and written back (HP / MP and the Spirit gauge) when the fight ends. The monster side is decoded once per species: the loader keeps the record pointer in a side table so the spoils routine can read gold, EXP and the drop roll without copying them into the actor. Each monster's mesh is a Legaia TMD inside the same block, textured from a 15-palette pool that the loader relocates into the seat's own VRAM column - which is what the monster viewer reproduces.

A round, turn by turn

The command UI is not one menu. The menu machine walks a byte cursor through three small D-pad surfaces - each a cluster of plate chips around a D-pad glyph - and every round opens on the same prompt:

Flow stateOn screenWhat the player does
0x0A / 0x0BParty plates + formation bannerWaits out the intro timer (a back attack skips the party's first round)
0x1EBegin / RunLeft begins the round, Right opens the flee confirm
0x28Command ringUp = Item, Left = Attack, Right = the Ra-Seru (magic), Down = Spirit; one member at a time
0x78Auto / CommandAttack is a door: Auto goes to the target cursor, Command to directional arts entry (an option byte can skip this prompt)
0x3C / 0x46 / 0x50 / 0x5AItem window / magic window / arts entry / target cursorPicks the payload and target
0x6E then 0xFE-All members committed; the frame is handed to the action machine

The action machine then resolves the round. It keys on its own byte, walks a band of states per command category (Attack, Magic, Item, Spirit, Run), and ends each action at a gate that counts who is still standing. When every living actor has acted it writes the menu machine back to turn setup and bumps the round counter - the two bytes hand the round back and forth. State bands are on battle action; the arithmetic is on battle formulas.

  • Turn order is an initiative key per living actor, seeded from SPD plus a random spread and re-rolled each round; the highest key acts, ties break at random.
  • Monster AI is two routines: a picker that rolls between a physical strike and the record's castable spells (MP-gated, targeted by the spell's shape byte) and can be overridden by a per-monster-id script, and a target resolver that only runs for a charmed party member. A monster fills its turn by paying each move's cost out of its AGL gauge - the same budget the party's arts bar spends.
  • Status effects are bits in one halfword on the actor, set only by the battle overlay's two infliction legs. Poison ticks never kill (they bottom out at 1 HP); Stone counts as defeated. Per-status table on internals.
  • AP is two gauges. The arts bar's per-turn pool is the character's AGL, spent by direction presses; a named art is paid from the Spirit gauge, which Spirit charges and which carries from fight to fight. A Spirit turn also lengthens the next arts bar and raises a guard stance. Both are on arts command gauge.
  • Items come from the same 256-slot bag the field menu uses - there is no battle inventory. The bag and its "split bag" window are on inventory.
  • Capture-class casts (Seru capture, the Amulet, the Delilas signature attacks) page a small dedicated module into the side overlay window and tick it every frame until it reports done. See internals and cast modules.

Ending the fight

OutcomeTriggerWhat happens
VictoryEnd-of-action gate finds no living monsterGold is summed halved and halved again (x1.25 with the gold passive); EXP is summed x3/4 and split among the living; every enemy rolls its drop chance, the last winner decides, and at most one item drops per battle (three times in four nothing does, unless a chance is 100% or Items Up applies). Win pose, spoils banner, back to the field. Formula: victory spoils.
Seru captureA party member's killing blow on a monster with a Seru idChance = the record's catch-rate percent, +30 flat with Magic Boost, one d100. No HP-fraction term - "weakened Seru" is only the killing-blow condition. An unknown Seru is learned in the same action: the Done band teaches its spell and raises the learn banner (a Seru the character already knows, or a character without their Ra-Seru, absorbs nothing). The catch-rate byte is the ROM patcher's --seru-catch-rate target.
EscapeRun at the round prompt, or an escape item / spellParty (SPD*3)/2 + missingHP/16 vs enemy SPD + missingHP/32, one random draw each; Chicken Heart x1.5, Chicken King or a pre-emptive strike forces the tie; a scripted fight always catches you. Success ends the battle with no loot and downed members floored at 1 HP; failure spends the turn. Worked example.
Party wipeEnd-of-action gate finds no living, non-petrified party memberThe battle exits like any other, into MAIN INIT; there a gate reads a party-survived latch and sends the game to the CARD screen - the title screen with the cursor on Continue. No "GAME OVER" art is drawn on the reachable path.

Two things about the wipe catch people out. A scripted-loss battle (the Rim Elm ambush) raises a story-flag latch first, so its wipe returns to the field like a win; the same gate sets a second flag on the survived path, which is how a scene script can score a scripted battle. And the game-over artwork overlay that exists on the disc (mode 18/19) has no writer anywhere - it is a dev harness. Latches and stores on internals.

After every battle retail reloads the field scene and re-runs its entry script, which is what dispatches post-battle beat records (the rikuroa progression gate, for one).

Details: the charm battle softlock (randomizer interaction)

The randomizer's enemy-ally feature widens the monster-wipe scan's down-mask so a living charmed monster counts as "down". That breaks a retail invariant: the wipe scan and the initiative scheduler gate on the same predicate, so in retail a living acting actor at victory is always a party member. With the widen, a charmed ally's own killing blow makes victory fire with a monster as the acting actor; the win-pose staging reads its character id past the 3-byte party roster and arms a garbage side-band stream request - the battle wedges at the hand-off. (Both reroll loops terminate with probability 1; an "unbounded reroll" is not the cause.) The disc-side fix, legaia_patcher::charm_fix, is a one-word detour at the keep-branch.

In the port

The engine keeps the battle simulation renderer-free in engine-core and drives it from one per-frame World::tick, so walking, being ambushed, fighting and returning to the field all happen from a single call on both hosts. Where to look:

  • Encounters and entry: engine-core::encounter, World::begin_encounter_battle, battle_seats.
  • Menu half: battle_input, battle_flow, with the submenus in battle_arts, battle_magic, inventory_use, target_picker.
  • Action half: engine-vm::battle_action (the state machine) and battle_formulas (the arithmetic).
  • Monsters, spoils, wipe: monster_ai, World::apply_battle_loot, LevelUpTracker, game_over::GameOverSession.

Retail's render and animation systems clear two gates the action machine waits on - the advance-done flag at attack recovery and the caster's spell iterator at magic sustain - so a renderer-free port has to retire them itself. Post-battle HP / MP is written into the character records before the field snapshot is restored. The per-monster Seru attachment table is an engine-side approximation (SeruRegistry::vanilla), Inferred, not Confirmed.
