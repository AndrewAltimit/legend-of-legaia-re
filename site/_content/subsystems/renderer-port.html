The port’s wgpu renderer

How the Rust port draws a frame: which PlayStation rasterisation traits are always on, the ones that are opt-in, how per-texel semi-transparency becomes two passes, the enhancement passes that leave the faithful render bit-identical when off, and the screen-space overlay that stands in for retail’s ordering table. Retail’s own renderer is on renderer; how a pixel gets its colour is on shading.

The screen pass dithers its Gouraud quads. The GPU dithers a POLY_G4 or POLY_GT4 as it writes it, opaque or not, and a flat quad not at all. PSX mode does the same on both hosts for a screen quad carrying screen_prim::FLAG_GOURAUD, from one rule (screen_prim::psx_rule_flags). The case is PROT 0898’s band wash, two opaque POLY_G4 behind every band-mode cast: undithered, its dim colours fall to the bin below and Big Wave’s upper frame reads darker than retail.

The play page’s PSX frame is single-sample. The page’s context has MSAA, which cannot change after creation and which WebGL cannot turn off on the canvas’s framebuffer. So a PSX frame draws into a single-sample framebuffer of its own (site/js/webgl-psx-target.js) and is copied to the canvas once; on the canvas a polygon edge would resolve to a blend of partly covered samples, which neither retail’s rasteriser nor the native renderer has. The enhanced mode keeps its MSAA.

There is no synthetic light. Retail runs no light source on the field path: shading is baked into each primitive’s colour word and the only colour operation is the depth cue. A missing colour stream defaults to white, and white reads as too bright, not unlit - the usual sign that a draw lost its colours.

Semi-transparency is two passes

Whether a texel blends is decided per texel: bit 15 of the palette entry picks blend or opaque inside one semi-transparent primitive. So the opaque pass draws everything and discards those texels, and a blend pass re-draws only the semi-transparent triangles, keeping only them.

A whole draw can be made semi-transparent too. Retail reads the top byte of an actor's colour word as primitive flags and ORs them into every packet, so one byte turns every primitive of the actor into a blended one. The port carries that byte per draw: the opaque pass treats every primitive of the draw as semi-transparent, and the blend pass re-draws the whole mesh in the forced mode. Underground Octam's corridor pillars use it (field-VM 4C D6).

Order mirrors the ordering table at per-primitive granularity. Every blended primitive is keyed by its centroid depth and the whole list blends far to near regardless of draw boundaries; equal keys draw later-submitted-first, as a bucket of the retail table does. Untextured primitives have no per-texel gate and blend every pixel.

The field sorts by each primitive’s link

In PSX mode the field pass draws each primitive at the depth of its ordering-table link rather than per pixel (Renderer::set_field_prim_order). Retail has no depth buffer, and three rules set the link:

The three rules hold against a state’s table: on koin1_field_route_walk every quad of the floor sheet sits at the bucket of its farthest corner, and the terrain’s wall quads at their mean, ten buckets behind the sheet’s rule. The mean alone, with no record flags and no ground exception, hides floors and cabinets. With them concend_field_door_hop reads 1.000 where per-pixel depth reads .942: the sky’s quads link among the far ridge’s and cover them.

An NPC takes 0x800000 where a seat samples the floor under it: op 4C 51 calls the floor sampler on the seated actor, which sets the bit on a cell without object-grid bit 0x1000. dolk2’s audience seats King Drake in his throne this way: the seated pose’s pelvis stands inside the throne’s back, and only the ten buckets put him in front of it.

A clean frame keeps per-pixel depth with one exception, the near shift: a field actor carrying the near-bucket bit (the player on a cell without 0x1000, an NPC a seat or a prologue gave it) tests each vertex’s depth ten buckets nearer than it stands. Those cells are the ones a placed object’s mesh floors – a dais, a ledge, a bridge – and per-pixel depth alone buried King Drake in his throne and Vahn in the cliff behind rikuroa2’s chest ledge, where retail paints both over it.

A walking NPC carries 0x800000 from the cell it stands on: the field actor tick FUN_8003BC08 runs the floor sampler on every placement it neither culls nor holds at a script height. Not modelled: a posed prop’s record flag 0x800, and the order inside one bucket. Links FUN_80043B58, FUN_80045194; actor base FUN_8001ADA4, FUN_8001B964; floor sampler FUN_80019278.

Enhancement passes

  • Enhanced lighting shades the scene under a time-of-day mood, adds lamp point lights, draws emissive-tagged primitives at a gain and composites glow sprites and light shafts. A light is as bright as its emitter, so a faint additive sheet casts faintly and a green glow casts green; an emissive primitive ignores the point lights, since a light derived from a glow would otherwise light that glow with itself; and a lamp whose cell the visible-tile crop leaves out keeps shading the cells that draw but has no halo, which would float over the clear colour. The derivation lives in the shared kernel so both hosts light from the same values; per-light shadows are the part only wgpu has.
  • Camera-occlusion fade dissolves environment fragments that stand between the camera and the player into a 4x4 screen-door pattern, armed only when a five-point ray-cast finds the character completely hidden by geometry the frame actually draws (a wall the visible-tile crop or the actor cull dropped does not count). It is a fragment discard, not a per-body cull.
  • Volumetric ground fog is separate from the retail fog pool and never touches it. It lies on the ground the scene actually draws: a coverage field rasterised once per scene from the static geometry stops the bank over open air (the Sky Gardens' tower top no longer carries a flat sheet across the sky), and past a rim the bank spills over the edge as slow, broken fog - wisps and tendrils with gaps between them, some stretches of rim pouring and others barely, falling almost straight down the wall face with a third of the bank's weight - above a faint haze far below the rim, so the drop into the void reads as misty rather than ending on nothing. Only what is on screen shapes it: objects a script hid, near-invisible cutouts and blended veils are not ground, and an invisible marker does not part the bank. In battle it drops to a thin, ankle-high haze - a third of the field opacity - so the stage floor stays readable, and it eases in rather than popping at the cut.
  • Ambient occlusion (SSAO, on by default; Home or --no-ssao turns it off) darkens creases and contact points from a depth and normal pre-pass of the opaque scene, blurred along depth and normal edges and multiplied in right after the opaque draws, so water, glass, halos, effects and the HUD never darken. Characters receive none - the estimator would read an arm against a torso as a crevice - but still occlude, so the ground under their feet keeps its contact shadow. In battle the stage counts as environment: the backdrop shell, the ground grid and the backdrop’s ribbon take the same AO a field floor does. It runs where frames are drawn through the scene camera with that actor split - the field, expansion maps, the world map and a battle - and nowhere else: a minigame venue (the Baka duel, the Muscle Dome, the fishing pond, the dance hall, the slot machine) owns its frame and camera, and both hosts ask one shared scope test before staging the pass.
  • Bloom (off by default) lets the bright parts of the 3D frame - lamp halos, glowing props, lit windows, sunlit sky - bleed a soft glow. The scene renders offscreen; a quarter-resolution pass keeps only what is already bright (a soft-knee threshold), a two-round Gaussian blurs it, and a composite adds it back under every screen layer, so the HUD never blooms. The threshold and strength follow enhanced lighting’s mood - darker moods bloom lower and stronger - from one shared kernel, and the browser draws the same passes from a GLSL twin held to the WGSL by a parity test.

Each is identity when disabled: the shader helper returns its input when the uniform enable is zero, so the disabled path is pixel-identical to the faithful render.

The screen-space overlay

A screen primitive is a textured quad or a flat quad carrying an ordering-table index. The pass reproduces the retail walk - farthest bucket first, later-submitted-first inside a bucket - and draws coalesced runs by blend class. Primitives are authored in the 320x240 display space every retail emitter clamps against, and the overlay stretches over the surface.

Its consumers are the things retail draws as 2D packets into the 3D table: battle streaks and trails, the field-to-battle transition styles, billboards. The corner projection is the GTE’s own, including its reciprocal and its saturating screen coordinates, so a corner behind the camera smears the way hardware does.

Backdrops draw first. A screen primitive whose four corners sit at the far plane is one retail links into the farthest bucket - the cast colour wash and the overworld sky band. Composited after the 3D pass, a blended primitive writes no depth, so a wash drawn there would erase every additive effect over the sky. Both hosts split those backdrops off the under list and draw them in the scene pass, after the clear and before the first 3D draw.

The battle sorts by each primitive's link. A battle has one ordering table, and the bodies, the after-image ghosts, the effect parts, the draw-kind-4 nodes and the backdrop shell all go through the same per-primitive leaves, which link each primitive at its corners' mean depth. Nothing on the GPU tests depth, so a monster and the additive ring around it sort by link, not by pixel: on Dohati's Chaos Breath the ring's packets sit in buckets nearer than every packet of the body, and the ring paints over the belly even where the belly stands in front of it. In PSX mode both hosts draw every battle mesh primitive at its corners' mean depth, as they do on the field; an effect part whose colour word carries the far-corner bit sorts at its farthest corner, as a field prop does (Inferno's tunnel wall stays behind its flames that way); the ground grid keeps its far bucket, and the screen primitives (trails, streaks, cast-arm packets) still composite over the 3D frame. Clean rendering keeps per-pixel depth: a mean pops as the camera turns, which the retail frame shows and the clean frame need not.

Landing a frame back in VRAM

The renderer only ever pushed the software page to the GPU. The capture path is the missing direction: it quantises a readback to 15-bit colour and writes it into a rect of the CPU-side page, so the result is visible to every reader of that page. The field-to-battle transition needs it - the tile shatter and the curtain sample the field frame that was on screen when the encounter fired. The frame read back is the one the window presents, screen layers and enhancement passes included, less the transition’s own primitives.

Two retail nuances are stated rather than hidden: the transition’s depth-cue bank has no channel in the overlay, and retail subtracts from the display buffer each frame instead of clearing it, so its strips leave fading trails where the port’s leave black.

A cast's frames build on earlier ones

A retail battle frame's farthest bucket holds an opaque black rect, and a cast arm that arms its subtractive wash has that quad take the rect's place, so the frame's additive layers sum over the frames before it. That is why Ozma's fans read white on retail while a frame rasterised from the same packets on black is dark teal with thin red rays.

The native target is the one target that outlives its frame, so it reproduces this: while a cast's arms keep the frame, each frame loads an earlier one instead of clearing, and a capture seeds from the same one. Retail's earlier frame is the same display page two game frames back, so the renderer keeps a ring of presented frames and reads the one twice the running battle frame's span back: eight vsyncs at the frame step a heavy arm runs at, ten when a streaming clip runs each pass a vsync past its step, as Genocidal Cannon's arm 6 does. Building on the last frame instead fills the buffer eight times too fast. The ring fills from the moment a cast module is resident, not from the first frame that keeps: an arm arms the wash a pass after the arm before it hands over, so its first kept frames read pages from before the arm. Started on copies of the last frame, Thunder Storm's second pass of arm 4 built on its first pass's white where retail's other page still held the dark frame before the arm. The play page does the same under PSX rasterisation (LegaiaPsxTarget in webgl-psx-target.js holds the same ring and reads it as deep as the engine says; the ring's size and the read are one shared kernel, render_kernels::frame_persist), and bloom stands the frames down on both hosts, since it reads the frame it adds to. The window's own target and the browser's canvas still clear, and there the additive arms draw dimmer than retail.

Frame profiler

LEGAIA_PROFILE=1 prints a rolling one-second timing breakdown from the native window: frame average and percentiles, draw counts, and the tick / pose / draw-list / encode / submit / present split. It is free when off - every entry point short-circuits on one cached flag. scripts/ci/frame-bench.py and frame-bench-web.mjs run it, and the browser's own counters, over a fixed scene set (frame benchmark).

Two things the benchmark drove: a rebuilt mesh writes over last frame's buffers, since a buffer dropped within two frames of its birth goes back to a pool that the next mesh of its exact size draws from; and the shadow stage draws only what each light reaches, since a draw wholly outside a lamp's frustum writes no depth into that lamp's layer.
