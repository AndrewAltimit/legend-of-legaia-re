Battle actor rendering

How a combatant gets on screen: which mesh it draws, which clip poses it, what colour and blend the draw takes, and the trails a swing leaves. Party members are not stored as finished models - each is assembled per fight from equipment sections - and every clip an actor plays goes through one staged byte.

The draw pipeline

One staged clip byte

Which clip an actor plays is a single byte, actor[+0x1DA]. Every producer writes it and the last writer wins; the commit FUN_8004AD80 snaps the playing id to it. A hit reaction is not a mode the actor is in - it is only the current value of the staged byte, and the next thing staged replaces it.

Flinch or knockdown is decided in the damage arm: flinch only when the actor has no get-up entry and the damage is survivable. Every player battle file carries a get-up entry, so a party member takes the knockdown arm on any hit.

When a staged clip commits. Mid-clip, commit-flag bit 0 commits at once and bit 1 commits once the cursor is past the entry’s gate frame. Otherwise the staged byte waits for the natural end. A clip left staged behind itself re-commits from frame one, which is how a loop loops.

The clip-tag ladder

A monster’s death runs through an earlier arm of the same routine: the knockdown is held on its last frame, the spoils run, and the actor takes the capture / defeat fade. With a Seru staged for capture the monster is stood back up for the absorb.

Slow motion and after-images

actor[+0x21D] scales how far the animation cursor moves per frame: 8 normal, 4 half speed, 2 quarter, 0 a freeze. Every animation-driven edge - swing pacing, root motion, the strike loop’s gate - stretches with it. A Stone or Numb body on its idle takes no step at all: the animation tick skips it and the body holds the keyframe it stood on.

The mesh after-images behind an art are two ghost copies drawn from the actor’s pose-history rings (FUN_80049348), additive, only while the ring id marks an art.

Tint and blend

The tint pass packs the actor’s colour lanes into the render node and the draw pass stages them as the GTE far colour and IR0. The prim’s modulation colour becomes

baked + (tint - baked) * blend / 0x1000

and the GPU still multiplies the texel through it. With no blend weight set, the same word is derived from depth instead: the distance fade. A colour word of zero is the summon-hide’s “not drawn” value, not a black tint.

  • The capture / defeat fade ORs additive blending into the node’s mode word, so the fading body is never an opaque black silhouette. It runs after the near-camera ghost pass, which clears the same bits, so the fade’s OR is what the draw reads.
  • The near-camera ghost pass (FUN_8004DC68) makes a body that crowds the lens translucent.
  • Shading itself is on shading and palettes.

Where the weapon trail lands. The sweep is sampled off the pose-history ring: step k is the pose 2k frames ago, retail’s rewind at a constant rate. Each control point is turned by the body’s facing and set on its slot’s ground base with retail’s integer arithmetic (products round toward zero, no base height), then placed the way the hosts place a battle body - the frame flip and the 4x world scale - and projected through the battle view-projection.

Both hosts call one kernel for that, engine-world-views::screen_passes::weapon_trail_prims, and hand it nothing but their battle view-projection, so a trail cannot land in one place on the page and another in the window. Tests pin the native composition to the page’s matrix and a trail point onto the body’s own drawn point under both. PROT 0904’s Theeder beam projects through the same point kernel (render-kernels::battle_trail::project_battle_point). The bands composite over the scene; retail’s ordering-table interleave with scene depth is not reproduced.
